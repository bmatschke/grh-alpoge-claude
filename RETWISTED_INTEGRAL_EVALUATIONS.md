# The full integral evaluation lattice on a retwisted column

Fix one marked core of the enlarged column, with small geometric coefficient field $F$ and ring of integers $O$. At row $j=i+1$, write $C_j$ for its $F$-space of cusp sections of the prescribed weight. Let $M_j^F$ consist of the sections whose value belongs to the valuation ring at **every** integral framed point over a complete discrete valuation ring with finite residue field, over every finite place of $F$. Here a test includes its coefficient embedding $\iota:F\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Frac}V$, a positive ramification index $e$ with $v_V(\iota(a))=e v_\lambda(a)$ for $a\in F^\times$, and a framed point of the source test class (`FromTest` and `Integral`). Finite residue field is its additional predicate `IsAlg`. Then $M_j^F$ is a full $O$-lattice. Given any finite set of vectors in $C_j$, one nonzero element of $O$ puts all of them in $M_j^F$. The bound is qualitative. The construction below gives the spanning half for general cusp sections without an expansion in short theta generators. The argument below uses the marked integral model and the source's criterion at degenerating frames. The [all-row proof](PEL_ALL_FRAME_DENOMINATORS.md) gives an independent construction using the supplied generic marked row, its graph theta line and the bounded complex cusp rule. Finite generation is the complementary assertion already established in ICL8. [Exact valuation class][vals] · [Definition of fullness][full] · [Previous spanning inputs](integral_cusp_lattices_v46/main.tex#L1136-L1162)

On an enlargement with the same small field, level and full preliminary parameters, this is the **same** lattice of geometric sections. In the coordinates of the two fine data the enlarged small-field lattice is the coefficientwise image of the old one. Each allowed new tuple has its own analytic reading into the common target. The conclusion holds on the stage chosen for the enlargement and survives restriction to its active coefficient places. ## One denominator on the locus of good reduction

The marked core has $M=g_0g_1$ with distinct odd primes and auxiliary full level $A=1$. Thus $M/\ell^{\operatorname{ord}_\ell M}\ge3$ at every rational prime $\ell$. The row has positive rank $p_j+q_j=k+m-1$. Use the normal projective $O$-model $Y$ of the source, equipped with its semiabelian family, CM action and rigidified line. The generic abelian locus is the given marked moduli scheme after extension to $F$. The source supplies this global model at precisely these level hypotheses. Lan's Theorems 7.14 and 11.2 give the local toroidal models and degenerating PEL families also at ramified characteristics. The projective global model here is the project's all-prime construction, with the prescribed half-level and theta line. [Definition of the model][model] · [Global model at this exact level][global] · [Lan, *Compactifications of PEL-type Shimura varieties in ramified characteristics*, §§7 and 11][lan]

For a good integral frame over $V$, three families enter the comparison. The definition of `Good` gives a proper semiabelian witness $A_0$, hence an abelian scheme. The definition of `Integral` gives a possibly different semiabelian witness $G_1$ bearing the actual tangent bases and theta generator. Properness of $Y/O$ gives a third family $G_Y$, pulled back along the unique extension to $Y(V)$ of the marked generic point. Here $O\to V$ is supplied by the coefficient embedding above $\lambda$. The three generic groups are identified through the source's Cartesian maps to the same marked generic object. Lan's Proposition 3.3.1.5 extends each generic isomorphism and its inverse over the noetherian normal trait $\operatorname{Spec}V$. Uniqueness identifies the composites with the identities and intertwines every endomorphism of the CM order. Thus both $G_1$ and $G_Y$ are abelian schemes, and the trait maps into the open abelian locus $Y^a$. This argument applies to a DVR with any residue field. [Independent properness and integrality witnesses][goodtest] · [Cartesian maps with CM action and rigidified cubical line][sabmap] · [Lan, Lemma 3.3.1.4, Proposition 3.3.1.5 and Corollary 3.3.1.6][lanbook]

Under the group identification, the generic line comparison supplied by those maps respects the prescribed rigidifications at the identity. It extends with exactly that normalization. Indeed, view it as a rational section of the difference of the two lines on the common abelian scheme over $V$. This scheme is regular and its special fibre is geometrically integral. The divisor of the comparison therefore has the form $mA_s$. Dividing locally by the $m$-th power of a uniformizer makes it a unit, so its restriction along the identity also has order $m$. Both rigidifications identify that restriction with $1$ on the generic fibre. Hence $m=0$, and the comparison extends invertibly. In particular the generator borne by $G_1$ is transported by the same line comparison that the integral framed point uses on its generic fibre. The graph formula from the source's CM line identity gives the same rigidified comparison. [Rigidified theta line](PEL_THETA_LINE.md) · [Generic compatibility with the actual generator][tests]

The global model specifies its rigidified line $L$ on the semiabelian family and the marked polarized object on its generic interior. To compute its direct image on $Y^a$, the restricted group $p:A\to Y^a$ is an abelian scheme by the fibre criterion. The generic fibre of $Y^a/O$ is exactly the moduli scheme $X_F$, where $\phi_L$ is a principal polarization. Flatness over $O$ makes this fibre dense and each connected component meets it. The locus where $\phi_L$ is a polarization is open and closed. Its degree, the rank of the finite locally free kernel, is constant on each connected component and equals one there. The inducing line $L$ is therefore relatively ample and principal throughout $Y^a$. Fibrewise vanishing and Riemann–Roch give $H^{>0}(A_s,L_s)=0$ and $h^0(A_s,L_s)=1$. Cohomology and base change now give an invertible $p_*L$ commuting with arbitrary base change. Its generators on a trait are precisely the integral theta frames. [Model and generic abelian locus][model] · [Polarization in families, Proposition 2.3.13][pol] · [Theta cohomology and base change](PEL_SECTION_LAWS.md)

The tangent eigenspaces can meet modulo a ramified prime. Here is a finite-type integral set of coordinates containing *every* prescribed good integral eigenframe. Choose a primitive integral element $b$ of $E/\mathbb Q$, and choose $0\ne d\in O$ clearing the denominators of the spectral idempotents
$$
 P_\tau=\prod_{\nu\ne\tau}\frac{\iota(b)-\nu(b)}{\tau(b)-\nu(b)}
$$
on the Lie bundle over the generic fibre. The field $F$ contains all images of $E$, and one may take the product over all ordered pairs of distinct conjugates in the denominator. The endomorphism $dP_\tau$ is thus an integral polynomial in the CM action in every local trivialization. If $H$ is the Lie lattice of a good test, then $dP_\tau H$ lies in $H\cap\operatorname{im}P_\tau$. Indeed $\iota(\tau(a))\in V$ for every CM integer $a$, and $H$ injects into its generic fibre. Thus the simultaneous integral eigenequations in `IsEigV` cut out exactly $H\cap\operatorname{im}P_\tau$. The uniqueness and existence of integral coordinate combinations in the definition give a basis of this intersection, with the prescribed multiplicity. The sum $\sum_\tau P_\tau=1$ implies that the matrix $U$ formed by joining its columns has
$$
                        d H\subseteq U V^{\dim H}\subseteq H.
$$
Use a trivialization of $H$ to express this as $U B=d\,1$ with $B$ integral. In the generic fibre $B=dU^{-1}$. This bounds the defect of the combined eigenframe uniformly even at primes where two embeddings agree on residue fields. It does not require the combined columns to be a basis of $H$ there.

Cover $Y^a$ by finitely many affine opens trivializing the Lie and theta direct-image bundles. This is possible because $Y$ is noetherian. On each, adjoin the entries of $U,B$, impose the eigenvalue equations for the columns and $UB=BU=d\,1$, and adjoin the coordinate of a theta generator and its inverse. A chosen trivialization of the Lie bundle identifies $U$ with a square matrix. The equations define an affine finite-type $O$-scheme. On the generic fibre $d$ is invertible, $U$ is a basis and $B$ is determined. This is exactly the corresponding part of the generic eigenframe and theta-generator scheme, with columns ordered within the prescribed eigenspaces. In particular the value of an algebraic cusp rule $f$ there is a regular function. The rules in the project are natural under base change on reduced test algebras, so one may equivalently apply the rule to the universal generic frame on the reduced affine chart. The chart can be replaced by the reduced closure of its generic fibre. Every valuation-ring point above a good test factors through this closure, since a relation vanishing at its generic point vanishes in the valuation ring. [Generic frame scheme and section laws](PEL_SECTION_LAWS.md) · [The test frames before taking their valuations][tests]

For an affine chart $\operatorname{Spec} B_0$ so obtained the generic function belongs to $B_0\otimes_O F$. It becomes an element of $B_0$ after multiplication by some $s_{f,B_0}\in O\setminus\{0\}$. Take the product of these finitely many scalars. For any good test, choose a chart containing its closed point. The whole trait then lies in that chart, and the preceding matrix construction and the specified theta generator give a valuation-ring point of the framed chart. Consequently the single scalar $s_f$ gives
$$
                     s_f\, f(y)\in V
$$
for every good-reduction integral test $y$, simultaneously at all residue characteristics, with arbitrary ramification index. This argument even admits good tests with infinite residue field. In particular, at a fixed prime its estimate applies to the good-reduction Gauss valuation used in the boundary argument.

## From good reduction to the full geometric lattice

Apply Roadmap B8.43n to $s_f f$ on the actual marked row, separately for each coefficient place. The core supplies a valid uniformization at each designated complex embedding, its row weight is dominant and nonnegative, and its theta degree is nonnegative. Membership in $C_j$ supplies cuspidality at those embeddings, and the prime-to-$\ell$ level condition holds at every $\ell$ as above. This criterion tests a degenerating frame after a finite extension on a one-parameter toroidal degeneration. In its integral tangent and theta frame the evaluation is $h(t)/(\pi t)^m$. Boundedness of the unit-frame cusp reading and the norm comparison along the complex period arc kill the negative $t$-powers. At the Gauss valuation given by $\pi$, the family is abelian. The preceding good-frame estimate therefore gives divisibility of every coefficient of $h(t)/t^m$ by $\pi^m$. Substitution $t=\pi$ is integral. Descent along the finite extension preserves the valuation inequality. The marked arc and its comparison with the actual theta generator are precisely the input of B8.43n used here. The usual Fourier expansion principle on a smooth model by itself only controls its own chosen expansion charts. [Boundary criterion][boundary] · [Pinned theorem with all trait quantifiers][bddall] · [Marked toroidal arc and its two divisibilities][arc] · [Integral test on that arc][arctest]

Thus $s_ff\in M_j^F$. Taking products proves simultaneous clearance for finitely many sections. The definition of $M_j^F$ is closed under addition and multiplication by $O$, by the nonarchimedean triangle inequality. Applied to a basis of the fixed finite-dimensional $C_j$, clearance shows that this submodule spans. ICL8 supplies finite generation by evaluation at finitely many separating algebraic frames whose values for integral forms lie in fixed fractional lattices. The argument there applies to the present signature and field and includes the zero space. Hence $M_j^F$ is finite, torsion free and spanning, exactly the three properties needed here (in particular it is projective over $O$). This supplies the qualitative full-lattice conclusion without the quantitative bounds on individual short theta coefficients in ICL12–19. The common denominator just proved can depend on the chosen core and sections; the definition of `LatFull` allows that dependence. ## The same lattice on the actual enlarged column

Let $z$ be the prescribed initial fine datum satisfying its lower-row laws. Take any pinned retwist of it with the specified preliminary parameters, the same ordered marked primes and the required inclusions of embedded coefficient fields. Write $L,L'$ for the large fields, $a:L\to L'$ for the inclusion preserving the distinguished complex readings. Their embedded small fields are equal, so they give exactly $F$ and $O$ above. The [fine-extension construction](THETA_FINE_EXTENSION.md) copies the level, moduli scheme, coefficient datum, uniformizations and weights. Thus $C_j$, the integral test frames over every $\lambda\subset O$ and the function $y\mapsto f(y)$ for $f\in C_j$ identify on the two sides. This proves equality of the two intrinsic integral lattices, testing the very same sections at every valuation, including the degenerate tests.

Denote the small-field coordinate maps of the old and enlarged geometric ties by $e_j,e'_j$. The construction gives $e'_j(f)=a_*e_j(f)$, with $a_*$ acting coordinatewise, and the point equivalences intertwine the prescribed local norms. In each row the small-coordinate module called $M_K$ consists exactly of those $e_j(f)$ with every norm at most one. Equivalently it is the image of $M_j^F$. The equalities are therefore
$$
  M_{K,j}'=a_*M_{K,j},\qquad
  O_{L'}M_{K,j}'=O_{L'}\otimes_{O_L}(O_L M_{K,j})
$$
in the matched ambient coordinates. For the second equality the coordinate family for $C_j$ remains linearly independent on extending either large field. Since rings of integers extend flatly over Dedekind domains, the indicated tensor maps injectively and its image is the stated span. There is no condition of unramifiedness of $L'/L$ in these equations. They use the ring-of-integers *span* of the geometric small-field lattice prescribed in the source. [Coordinate identification][coords] · [Transport of all point norms][norms] · [Definitions of the small lattice and the two large-field intersections][spaces]

At either marked level the large target lattice is the intersection of this span with the required type subspace, and for the good row also with the image of the good idempotent. In a full lattice the intersection with a vector subspace spans that subspace, since a denominator clears each vector in a basis, and it is saturated because the quotient injects into the complementary vector-space quotient. Pullback through the prescribed coordinate isomorphism gives the full lattice on the actual marked row at that level. For a type transported from the old stage, intersection also commutes with flat extension by taking the kernel of the map to the ambient quotient. The same full-intersection argument applies directly to a new type defined over $L'$. This concerns the full **target row** for each permitted reading. An integral-image estimate for an individual lifted source vector uses the subsequent theta and congruence comparisons. [Definitions of type and good lattices][spaces] · [Both level-coordinate pullbacks](integral_cusp_lattices_v46/main.tex#L1091-L1133)

Finally, on the retwist chosen with a prescribed initial core, finite exclusions and advance final-field request, Green's construction supplies the later stage with the true finite group and the prescribed group comparison. The chosen Galois stage supplies the volume identity for that same stage. These are exactly the hypotheses, on the enlarged side, of the extension with fixed old datum. On the initial side use the saturated fine datum with all seventeen lower-row laws. The seven analytic inputs of the extension theorem supply the nonreference models and their standard readings, and preserve the designated reference phase on the geometric core just identified. The qualifying enlarged datum $z^+$ therefore exists on this column, with the full lattice proved above. The comparison of the nonzero reference indices and the forced content identifies its active places with the inverse image of the prescribed ones. Restricting $z^+$ to them preserves the same cusp spaces, lattice and point norms. Each of the two standard reading formulas composes its tuple parameter with this restriction, so the conclusion is simultaneous for every surviving tuple. This discharges the compatible fine-extension and full target-lattice step on the requested columns under the marked-model, boundary and analytic inputs specified here. The comparison with localized Galois representations and good weighted points is a subsequent obligation.
