Fix the enlarged column and its restricted fine datum $z_W$, before applying the bottom-row test to produce $P'$. Fix a realized grid, one of its tuples $t$, and a square $(i,j)$. Denote the source ranks by $a>a'$ and the common target rank by $d=a+a'$. At **each ramified place of this square**, on both source spaces, the kernel of the prescribed local matching map on the *whole* Schwartz space is the span of finite source differences. The two quotients are the same normalized parabolic induction. These statements permit every conductor allowed on this grid. With B8.11 on these triples and the project’s local theta and Siegel-set inputs, the last section derives the full twisted-square theorem B8.16, including the real quotient chosen before the finite twists. ## Why the character at a bad place suffices

The actual grid characters are $\alpha=\alpha(G,t,i,j)$ and $\beta=\beta(G,i,j)$. The square construction gives their rank parity, real exponent gap, two Weil splittings and the finite alternatives. At each $v$ in the prescribed bad set $T$, the place splits in $E/F$, is prime to the absolute different of $F$, and $\nu=\alpha/\beta$ ramifies on both branches. Off $T$ both characters are unramified. Indeed $\nu$ is trivial on the diagonal $\mathbb A_F^\times$ by parity, so the two split restrictions are inverses. The finite construction also gives the additive character conductor $\mathcal O_v$ at the ramified places. Thus on the prescribed split branch the multiplicative character in the rectangular source action is nontrivial on local units. Inverting this character in a change of convention preserves this fact. This check takes place on the square's own tuple, including tuples with new ray characters. [Grid realization with the actual ranks and characters][realize] · [Placewise alternatives][places] · [Source and target signatures][triple]

Put $k=F_v$, $\mathcal O=\mathcal O_v$, $n=a$ or $a'$, $G=\mathrm{GL}_n(k)$ and $K=\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathcal O)$. In the split matrix chart the source acts, writing $A$ for its transposed matrix, by
$$
 (R_A f)(Y)=w(A)f(AY),\qquad
 w(A)=\nu(\det A)|\det A|^{d/2},\qquad Y\in M_{n,d}(k).
$$
Transpose reindexes all source elements and compact averages. Write $D$ for the span of $R_Af-f$ over all $A,f$. Normalize $\operatorname{vol}(K)=1$. The compact average $E_K f=\int_K R_u f\,du$ is a finite average on each smooth test and has the same class as $f$ modulo $D$. If $Y$ is singular, the saturation $\operatorname{im}(Y)\cap\mathcal O^n$ is a proper direct summand of $\mathcal O^n$. Take an automorphism acting identically on this summand and multiplying one complementary basis vector by any $b\in\mathcal O^\times$. It fixes $Y$ and has determinant $b$. Compact invariance gives
$$
 (E_K f)(Y)=\nu(b)(E_K f)(Y).
$$
Choosing $\nu(b)\ne1$ proves vanishing on the entire singular set. Since $E_K f$ is locally constant with compact support, it has compact support *inside* the open full-rank locus $U_n$. The operation works on the input test in the given model and commutes with the target action. [Split action on tests][action] · [Coinvariant comparison in B8.11f][b11f]

## The exact kernel, including the larger rectangle

The orbital part of the native transform at a full-rank frame $Y_*$ has weight $w(g)\,dg$. The normalized transform also includes the specified target determinant factor. On the big side the definition sums integrals over determinant Cartan double cosets. Inside each $K$-double coset, left averaging under $K$ replaces $f$ by $E_K f$ without changing that coset integral. For fixed $Y_*$ the orbit map $g\mapsto gY_*$ identifies $G$ with the frames of a fixed row space. This fiber is closed in $U_n$. Hence the part of the fiber meeting $\operatorname{supp}(E_K f)$ is compact, and only finitely many Cartan cosets contribute. In particular summing the shell integrals of the original big test gives the absolutely convergent orbital integral of its compact average.

For completeness, source invariance on the whole test space follows without an unrestricted change of order on the big integral. Insert $|\det g|^s$ in its weight. In a right half-plane the integral of $f(gY_*)$ converges absolutely. The entries of $g$ are bounded on its support by using a right inverse of $Y_*$. Relative to additive matrix measure the determinant exponent in absolute value is $\Re(s)+d/2-n$, which can be taken nonnegative. Averaging over $K$ identifies the answer with the integral of $E_Kf$, a finite Laurent polynomial in $q^{-s}$ for $q=|\mathcal O/\varpi|$. Change of variables in the half-plane and evaluation at $s=0$ now give invariance under all source translations for the prescribed transform. This also proves compatibility with its shell definition at $s=0$. [Native small integral and big sum][transforms]

On $U_n$ the map $Y\mapsto\operatorname{rowspan}(Y)$ is the frame bundle over the Grassmannian. In a minor chart write $Y=gY_L$ using the section with an identity pivot minor at the row-space point $L$. Weighted integration along $g$ gives a locally constant section of the line bundle with the corresponding transition character. A compactly supported test on these coordinates is a finite sum of products of compact open indicators after refining the chart. On the group factor, multiplication by $w(g)$ converts weighted translates to ordinary translates. The kernel of Haar integration on $C_c^\infty(G)$ is spanned by differences of translates. Indeed, after choosing a common right compact open stabilizer, the function is a finite linear combination of indicators of left cosets of that stabilizer. All have the same integral, and a zero-sum combination is a sum of their differences. Applying this in disjoint compact open base charts proves the same assertion for the frame transform with source differences on $U_n$. Conversely a compact open group factor of weighted integral one lifts each local section, and the compact Grassmannian admits a finite disjoint refinement of the chart cover. The transform on $U_n$ is therefore onto its induced function space.

If the transform of an arbitrary $f$ vanishes, $E_K f$ has zero transform and full-rank support. The preceding argument expresses it as a sum of source differences on $U_n$. Subtracting the compact average expresses $f$ as a sum of source differences on the entire matrix space. This proves both identifications
$$
 C_c^\infty(U_n)/D(U_n)\ \simeq\ C_c^\infty(M_{n,d}(k))/D
 \ \simeq\ \operatorname{Ind}_{P_{a,a'}}^{\mathrm{GL}_d(k)}
       \big((\alpha_v\circ\det)\boxtimes(\beta_v\circ\det)\big).
$$
Here induction is normalized. In the big transform the target acts by inverse transpose in the frame argument and in the small transform by the given matrix. Complementary row spaces and the two target prefactors give the same rule on block diagonal entries $A,B$, namely $\alpha_v(\det A)\beta_v(\det B)|\det A|^{a'/2}|\det B|^{-a/2}$. The induction is irreducible by Bernstein's theorem on normalized induction of unitary representations of general linear groups. Both inducing characters are one-dimensional, smooth and unitary. This identifies the maps for arbitrary tests at ramified primes even when the intermediate groups are indefinite at infinity. ## One full-rank representative operator at the designated good place

B8.16 also uses a good place $v_1$ outside $T$. Here $\nu$ is unramified and the rank-deficient stabilizer average by itself no longer removes singular matrices. The following formula works for every test at this *fixed* place. Use the primed rectangle $n=a'<d/2$, $K=\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathcal O_{v_1})$, $\kappa=\mathcal O_{v_1}/\varpi$ and $q=|\kappa|$. For every subspace $W\subseteq\kappa^n$ take its inverse image lattice $L_W$ and choose $g_W$ with $g_W\mathcal O^n=L_W$. Put $c_W=n-\dim W$ and $\mu_W=(-1)^{c_W}q^{\binom{c_W}{2}}$. For $\bar f=E_K f$, define
$$
 (\mathcal H f)(Y)=\sum_{W\subseteq\kappa^n}\mu_W\,\bar f(g_W^{-1}Y).
$$
The function $\bar f$ is now invariant under integral row operations, so the formula is independent of the choices of $g_W$.

If the columns of $Y$ span a proper subspace $U\subset k^n$, put $U_0=U\cap\mathcal O^n$ and let $\bar U_0$ be its reduction in $\kappa^n$. The value $\bar f(g_W^{-1}Y)$ depends only on $W\cap\bar U_0$. Indeed this intersection determines $L_W\cap U$. For two choices with the same intersection, the map between their inverse images of $U$ identifies the primitive sublattices there and carries the first matrix to the second. Extending bases of those primitive sublattices gives an integral change of basis of $\mathcal O^n$. For each $V\subseteq\bar U_0$, the sum of $\mu_W$ for $W\supseteq V$ is zero by Möbius inversion on the subspace lattice, since $V$ is proper in $\kappa^n$. A second inversion inside $\bar U_0$ shows that the sum for $W\cap\bar U_0=V$ is zero. Thus $\mathcal H f$ vanishes on every singular $Y$ and is a compactly supported full-rank test.

Modulo source differences its action is multiplication by
$$
 \sum_{W}\mu_W\bigl(\nu(\varpi)q^{-d/2}\bigr)^{c_W}
   =\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}\bigl(1-\nu(\varpi)q^{i-d/2}\bigr).
$$
The first equality of classes undoes the determinant multiplier of $g_W^{-1}$. The product follows by grouping by codimension and using the finite $q$-binomial identity. Every factor is nonzero since $|\nu(\varpi)|=1$ and $n<d/2$. Dividing $\mathcal H$ by this product yields a single degree-one operator on tests, independent of their conductors, ball decompositions or exterior endpoints. Compact averaging here and at a ramified prime is a finite combination of source translations on each smooth vector. The constructions require complex coefficients. ## Deriving B8.16 from ordinary matching on the realized triples

Assume B8.11 on the Schrödinger triple attached to **each** square in this range, including its unrestricted big convergence, full-rank primed decay and nonzero matched scalar. Keep the local Weil and Howe hypotheses and the unitary Siegel-set estimate. Then **all the convergence, existence, real-factor and proportionality clauses of B8.16 follow on this range**. The following argument also covers matching maps supplied independently of the maps used in B8.11. [Precise clauses of B8.16][b16] · [Precise ordinary input][b11] · [Native implication on general matching maps][nativeglobal]

**Transfer and convergence.** In the given frames replace the two source forms by $J_W,-J_{W_1}$ and the target form by the reindexed skew form $\xi_0 J_X^t$. The target isomorphism conjugates coordinates and reindexes them. With the conjugate complex embeddings the positive real multiplier $i\bar\tau(\xi_0)$ keeps the target signature, the second source signature reverses, and the exponent gap becomes $2(b_\tau-a_\tau)>d$. The splitting pairs agree under this change, including the inverse target determinant under conjugation. The adelic Schrödinger-to-theta realization sums Heisenberg translates over the rational polarization. Fourier inversion on the compact additive quotient recovers the Schwartz test. The two maps thus identify polynomial Schwartz tests with the *whole algebraic* Fock spaces, intertwine the finite and Lie actions, and give the same theta kernels pointwise. The prescribed pushed-forward measures give the integral identities. Archimedean and finite coordinate transformations bound the new target height polynomially in the old one. [Space, character and model identifications][transfer] · [Algebraic tests, pushed measures and the two splittings][nativemodel]

B8.16 also gives an absolute bound for every permitted test when $d_X<2(n-p)$ at one real place of source signature $(n-p,p)$, for either sign. Let $r$ be the rational Witt index. Rational isotropic planes embed at that real place, so $r\le p$. In Witt torus parameters $t_0,\ldots,t_{r-1}$ the Schwartz lattice bound costs $\prod_i t_i^{[F:\mathbb Q]d_X}$ times a polynomial in target height. Multiplication by the unitary Siegel density gives powers $t_i^{A_i-1}$ with $A_i=[F:\mathbb Q]\, (d_X-2n+4i+2)$. On the ordered cone, the partial exponent sums are $[F:\mathbb Q]\, (j+1)(d_X-2n+2j+2)<0$ for $0\le j<r$. Successive integration gives a finite constant. For $r=0$ the cone contributes one. This proves the absolute moderate-growth assertion. It applies to the larger rectangle on the prescribed grid by its rank gap. On the primed full-rank subspace the bound of B8.11 with decay exponent zero, times the finite source quotient volume, gives the absolute moderate-growth bound at every target argument. [Siegel estimate and integrated cone powers][cone]

**Which irreducible quotient is used.** On each source side, quotient the algebraic test space by the source differences and archimedean Lie relations. The result is $M_\infty\otimes\bigotimes'_v M_v$, the restricted algebraic tensor product of the local source coinvariants. Only finitely many components of an adelic group element change a given pure tensor, so its difference telescopes into single-place differences. Conversely every such single-place relation is a global relation. Connected real maximal compacts on the polynomial orbits implement the corresponding compact relations by their Lie actions. At infinity, real Howe duality gives finite length and a unique maximal proper submodule $N_\infty$ on each side. A nonzero jointly simple quotient $Q$ of the tensor is semisimple for the real target action: some fixed-finite-factor image of $M_\infty$ is nonzero of finite length, so it contains a simple submodule. The sum of all such submodules is stable under the commuting finite actions and equals $Q$. Maps into any of these simple summands kill $N_\infty$.

At a split place with nontrivial source character the coinvariant factor is already simple, by the computation above in the ramified case and the good split calculation of B8.11f in the unramified nontrivial case. For the remaining finite places the two spherical calculations say that $M_v$ is generated by its vacuum $s_v$ and the corresponding weighted compact average $e_v$ has image $\mathbb C s_v$ with $e_v s_v=s_v$. The unique maximal proper submodule is
$$
 N_v=\{z:e_v(gz)=0\text{ for all }g\in G_v\}.
$$
Indeed, any submodule containing a vector outside $N_v$ contains $s_v$ and hence is the whole module. In the global simple quotient $Q$, the common kernel of $e_vg$ is stable under the other actions as well. It is proper because the vacuum generates the local factor and therefore some tensor with $s_v$ has nonzero image. Thus the common kernel on $Q$ is zero. Every map obtained by fixing the other factors kills $N_v$. These arguments use the specified spherical and real local facts. The native proof also uses Mínguez's type-II local correspondence on the split side. [Nontrivial split character][b11f] · [Inert averages][inert] · [Remaining spherical averages][spherical] · [Mínguez, *Correspondance de Howe explicite*][minguez]

It follows that both $Q$ and the quotient supplied by B8.11 factor through the restricted tensor of these simple local quotients, using the nonzero unramified vacuum classes. That tensor is simple. Each factor has at most countable complex dimension (at a finite place take a countable compact-open basis of the Schwartz space), so a commuting endomorphism of a simple factor is scalar by the countable-dimensional Schur lemma. Jacobson density on a finite set of factors isolates pure tensors and gives simplicity of each finite tensor. A nonzero vector and any desired vector of the restricted product occur at a common finite stage after inserting the vacuum in unused factors. Finite-stage simplicity now proves the claim. Consequently the two surjections have the same kernel on each source side. The descended isomorphisms $i_A,i_B:Q\to\Pi_{\rm Sch}$ are nonzero and differ by one nonzero scalar $s$, since $Q$ itself is countable-dimensional and simple. This proves uniqueness of the comparison even on the primed full-rank vectors, which still map onto the quotient by the degree-one replacement. It gives the joint irreducibility required in B8.16 when one takes the B8.11 quotient and transports its maps. [Detailed local-to-global comparison][scalar] · [Finite-stage simplicity on the target](../construction/ROW_STAGE_STANDARD_TESTS.md)

**One real choice before all finite twists.** Fix the frames, standard additive character and infinite exponents of the square. The real source relations and real target actions on the two polynomial spaces depend only on these data. Take their simple quotients $P_A,P_B$ just constructed. Any one matching datum at these exponents identifies them up to isomorphism, since its real summands are copies of both. Fix one such isomorphism once, use $P_{\rm real}=P_A$, and let $q,q'$ be the two resulting quotient maps. In the realized case there is a witness by the preceding construction. For exponents with no matching datum the zero choice satisfies the empty requirement. This choice is independent of the tuple subsequently presented, including its finite conductor. For any match $p,p'$ onto $Q$ put $P_{\rm fin}=\operatorname{Hom}_{\rm Lie}(P_{\rm real},Q)$. Each permitted finite test $f$ defines an intertwiner $r(f)$ by $r(f)(q(P))=p(\operatorname{Fock}(f,P))$, and similarly for $r'$. As a real module $Q$ is a direct sum of copies of $P_{\rm real}$. Evaluation on any nonzero generator of this simple module shows every intertwiner has image in a finite subsum. The scalar commutant then identifies evaluation $P_{\rm real}\otimes P_{\rm fin}\to Q$ as an isomorphism. Each side's pure Fock images span $Q$, so under evaluation the span of the $r(f)$ is all $P_{\rm fin}$. Linearity makes $r$ onto, and the same proof applies to $r'$. This gives both tensor formulas with the same $q,q'$. [Common real quotient and multiplicity space][realuniform] · [Native real comparison][nativereal]

Finally, fix **any** admissible matching maps, source measures and domains. If $\Phi'$ is any algebraic primed Fock vector, apply the degree-one operator above to its finite decomposition in the Schrödinger model, then transfer back. The resulting $\Phi_0$ is algebraic, lies in the required absolute moderate-growth class, and $\Phi'-\Phi_0$ is in the span of finite source differences. Its image under $p'$ equals that of $\Phi'$. The number $c=s\,c_{\rm Sch}\ne0$ was chosen before $\Phi'$ and works for every unprimed algebraic $\Phi$ satisfying $p(\Phi)=p'(\Phi_0)$, at every target point. The rule compares the integral of the **convergent representative** $\Phi_0$ even in cases where the unaltered primed test lacks an absolute bound. ## Applying the square theorem to the restricted retwist

Fix any permitted enlarged column, its restriction $z_W$, and the temporary stage with division parameter $6x^{-C_F}$ and zero auxiliary row operators. Its marked-group copy has the same analytic maps and a Hida setting with the required row sizes and finite tuple set. On **every** normalized grid over this copy, choose any square $(i,j)$, any tuple $t$ including the reference tuple, and any admissible square measures. The size hypotheses give the host split cutoff at least $11$ and $\mathfrak c_K=\mathcal O_K$. The defining congruence of the distinguished prime $q_0$ makes it coprime to $Q_S q d_{\rm host}$. Each of the four marked level primes is coprime to this integer by its split class and the level guard. At the extra halves of $t$, the level exclusions give the same coprimality. Since $N\mathfrak c_K=1$, this proves the full avoidance condition defining $\mathrm{SquareStanding}(G,t)$. The proof holds for all $t$ on the chosen grid. The two character formulas give $T=S_0\cup Q$, where $Q$ is the set of places under the twist halves with $S_0$ removed. On $T$, the places split, the absolute different is a unit and $\alpha/\beta$ ramifies at both halves. Off $T$ both characters are unramified. The global additive character has the prescribed real restriction and therefore equals the standard character composed with the trace, by density of $F+F_\infty$ in $\mathbb A_F$. Its conductor is the inverse absolute different. At an inert place $v$, $E/F$ is unramified, $v\nmid2\mathfrak D_{F/\mathbb Q}$ and $\xi_0$ is a unit. Also $Nv>3$. The host contains an integral square root of $2$, which cannot reduce into a field of three elements. This argument includes residue characteristic three whenever its residue degree exceeds one. These facts verify the local hypotheses for the transferred lattices and additive character in B8.16d. A completely split place outside the union of the finite conductor sets can be used for priming. The grid realization produces the two sources with defects $p=\lfloor(m-j-1)/2\rfloor$ and $q=\lfloor(m-j)/2\rfloor$ for the native zero-based $j$. It gives $n,(a-n)/2>2(p+q)+1$ for the small and large ranks $n,a$, and $m_{\alpha,w}-m_{\beta,w}>a+n$ at each real place. The transferred model uses the square's actual Weil representations for $(\beta,\beta/\alpha)$ and $(\alpha,\alpha/\beta)$, its measures and its entire algebraic Fock spaces.
