# The discrete series inside an interior theta kernel

Why can one choose one real operator for every finite twist in a step of the tower? At a real place the two groups act on the same oscillator representation. The two discrete series specified in the construction occur **together as a closed Hilbert subspace**. Projecting a polynomial onto this subspace and contracting one tensor factor gives the operator. Its construction uses the archimedean parameters, which are fixed before the finite twist.

## Reading the four lists of weights

Fix a step and a real place. Denote the smaller and larger ranks by $n$ and $m=n+t$. The positive odd integer $t$ is the length of the new string of weights. Let $B_t=((t-1)/2,(t-3)/2,\ldots,-(t-1)/2)$. Write $\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta$ for the four decreasing lists in the chain condition, of lengths $x,y,z,w$. The lists $\alpha,\gamma$ have positive entries, and $\beta,\delta$ have negative entries. All old entries are distinct, lie in $(t+1)/2+\mathbb Z$, and satisfy $|u|\ge(t+3)/2$. Empty lists are allowed. The semicolon separates positive and negative signature spaces in a Harish-Chandra parameter. [Chain condition and exponents][chain] · [Lists, string and separation][lists]

| Real group | Harish-Chandra parameter before the uniform character shift | Uniform shift |
|---|---|---|
| $G=U(p,q)=U(x+y,z+w)$ | $\lambda_0=(\alpha,\beta\,;\,\gamma,\delta)$ | $a_T$ |
| $G'=U(r,s)=U(x+w+t,z+y)$ | $\lambda'_0=(\alpha,B_t,\delta\,;\,\gamma,\beta)$ | $a_S$ |

Here $a_T=-e_k$ at even $k$ and $a_T=-e_k-1/2$ at odd $k$. The other shift is $a_S=-e_k-1/2$ at even $k$ and $a_S=-e_k$ at odd $k$. In Ichino's notation use $m_0=2a_T$, $n_0=2a_S$. The character on $\mathbb C^\times$ indexed by the larger space is $(u/|u|)^{m_0}$ and splits the action of the smaller group. The character indexed by the smaller space has exponent $n_0$ and splits the action of the larger group. Indeed the original rank starts odd and increases by odd $t$, so $m_0\equiv m$ and $n_0\equiv n$ modulo two. This also makes each shifted row an ordinary discrete-series parameter. Use the real character $u\mapsto e^{-2\pi i u}$ and the signatures in Ichino's §4.1. [Definition of infinity exponent][exponent] · [Rank and parity in the tower](BASE_THETA_CONSTRUCTION_STEP.md) · [Ichino, *Theta lifting for discrete series representations of real unitary groups*, §4.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.09148#page=7)

## Why the two discrete series occur together

Let $\pi$ be the discrete series of $G$ with parameter $\lambda_0+a_T$. In Li's nonvanishing criterion, in the form stated in Ichino's Lemma 8.2, the two signature requirements are $x+w\le r$ and $z+y\le s$. The second is an equality here. The numerical bound on the absolute value of an old coordinate is $(m-n+1)/2=(t+1)/2$, which follows from the displayed gap. The local theta lift to this particular $G'$ is therefore nonzero. Ichino’s discrete-series paper, Remark 4.2, explicitly attributes the parameter formula in this gap range to Li’s theorem. [Scope of Li’s result, Remark 4.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.09148#page=8) [Ichino, *Theta lifting for tempered representations of real unitary groups*, Lemma 8.2 and its proof](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2008.06174#page=35)

The explicit upward-lift formula now identifies the representation. Its parabolic is determined by the signed sequence $(\alpha,0^t,\delta\,;\,\gamma,\beta)$. Its only possible nontoral Levi factor is $U(t)$ in the positive compact block. In the good range the resulting representation is discrete series. One can check its Harish-Chandra parameter directly in the formula. In the full decreasing order the $j$th new zero occupies position $x+z+j$, so the half-sum of positive roots contributes $(m+1)/2-x-z-j$. The inducing weight on these coordinates before the common shift is $x+z-n/2$. Adding gives $(t+1)/2-j$, for $1\le j\le t$. At every old coordinate the corresponding correction in the inducing weight cancels its half-sum of positive roots. For example at $\alpha_i$ the position is $i+\#\{k:\gamma_k>\alpha_i\}$ and the correction is its negative half-sum. Thus the answer is $\lambda'_0+a_S$, with $\delta$ in the first signature block and $\beta$ in the second. Write $\pi'$ for this discrete series. [Ichino, discrete-series paper, Theorem 4.1(i) and its good-range calculation in §6.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.09148#page=7) · [Worked calculation of the infinitesimal character](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.09148#page=17)

The Hilbert-space assertion uses an additional theorem. Sakellaridis decomposes the unitary oscillator representation over the Plancherel measure of the smaller group, with fiber $\theta(\sigma)\widehat\otimes\sigma$ in larger-group-first order. A discrete series is a point of positive mass, its formal degree for the chosen Haar measure. An almost-everywhere assertion applies at such a point. For clarity about real places, Ichino's Proposition 7.1 proves that the oscillator matrix-coefficient pairing at $\pi$ is nonzero exactly when its theta lift is nonzero, for $m\ge n$. In Sakellaridis' formula (3.2) the diagonal sum is the trace of the positive Fourier transform of an oscillator autocoefficient. Some such operator is nonzero by Ichino and polarization, so the positive trace is nonzero here. The singleton contributes a closed direct summand isometric to $\pi\widehat\otimes\pi'$ in smaller-group-first order. In the direct-integral norm a vector placed at an atom of mass $d_\pi$ is scaled by $d_\pi^{-1/2}$ for this isometry. This step gives an actual bounded Hilbert-space embedding. [Sakellaridis, Theorem 3.0.2, (3.1)–(3.3) and Remark 3.0.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1610.06202#page=18) · [Ichino, tempered paper, Proposition 7.1 and Lemma 7.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2008.06174#page=25)

## Removing the determinant characters and returning to the framed row

On $\widetilde U(p,q)=\{(g,v):v^2=\det g\}$ put $z(g,v)=v$. Tensoring with $z^b$ adds $b/2$ to all Harish-Chandra coordinates. Pull back the split oscillator action to the product of the two covers and remove $z_1^{m_0}z_2^{n_0}$. The two constituents now have parameters exactly $\lambda_0,\lambda'_0$. [Classification on the determinant double cover](../../../archive/rounds/v90/continuation_v90/discrete.tex#L3-L64)

The choice of polarization in this statement can be checked on the vacuum. In the source's tensor-matrix Fock coordinates the line $(a,c)$ has sign $+1$ for equal signature signs and $-1$ otherwise. Its diagonal operator on a monomial of exponent $h$ on that line has eigenvalue $\pm(h+1/2)$. On the vacuum the smaller group therefore has weight $(r-s)/2$ on its positive block and $(s-r)/2$ on its negative block. The larger group has the corresponding weights $(p-q)/2,(q-p)/2$. Ichino's Lemma 7.10 gives both of these weights plus, respectively, $m_0/2$ and $n_0/2$. It also gives the same passage $(A,B;C,D)\mapsto(A,D;C,B)$ on the nonzero parts of the compact highest weights. [Polynomial operators and vacuum weights][cartan] · [Ichino, tempered paper, Lemma 7.10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2008.06174#page=30)

Here is a check on the **full** group, where agreement of compact types alone would leave a gap. Index the tensor lines by $i$ and write $\epsilon_i$ for their signs. Use the raising and lowering pairs $(R_i,L_i)=(X_i,\partial_i)$ on a positive line and $(-\partial_i,X_i)$ on a negative line. The source's operators are $Q_{ij}=R_iL_j+\delta_{ij}/2$. The relations $[L_j,R_i]=\delta_{ij}$ give
$$
 [Q_{ij},R_k]=\delta_{jk}R_i,\qquad
 [Q_{ij},L_k]=-\delta_{ik}L_j.
$$
Ichino's tensor form in his signed bases is $B(u,v)=-2\operatorname{Im}\sum_i\epsilon_i u_i\overline v_i$. The skew-adjoint Heisenberg infinitesimal operator
$$
 D(u)=\sqrt{2\pi}\sum_i (u_iR_i-\epsilon_i\overline u_i L_i)
$$
satisfies $[D(u),D(v)]=-2\pi iB(u,v)$. For $X$ in the unitary Lie algebra the two displayed commutators give $[Q(X),D(u)]=D(Xu)$, where $Q(X)=\sum X_{ij}Q_{ij}$. Thus the polynomial action implements precisely Ichino's tensor symplectic action with his additive character. The abstract Hilbert Fock data in the source have dense polynomials with monomial squared norms $\prod_i h_i!$. Each $Q(X)$ on degrees at most $d$ has norm at most $C_X(d+2)$, by the normalized creation and annihilation formulas. Its $j$th iterate on a degree-$d$ polynomial is bounded by $C_X^j\prod_{l=1}^j(d+2l)$ times the polynomial norm. Hence these are analytic vectors. The operators integrate to the same one-parameter actions in any such realization by their Taylor series on this dense subspace. [Ichino's two forms and tensor action, §§2.1–2.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.09148#page=4) · [Fock norm, tensor embedding and determinant square roots][fock]

Stone–von Neumann uniqueness identifies the Heisenberg models. Two unitary lifts of the same tensor action then differ by a scalar character on the product of the determinant covers. The vacuum weights computed above give this character on the compact tori as $z_1^{m_0}z_2^{n_0}$. They determine it on the whole group. Its differential kills Lie brackets and is determined on the compact center, and each positive-rank determinant cover is connected (rotating one diagonal entry through a full turn interchanges the square roots). This also accounts for both deck transformations. Removing the character therefore gives the source's untwisted Fock action. [Global model and prescribed splitting](WEIL_AUTOMORPHIC_MODEL.md)

Let $H_1,H_2$ be these two unshifted covering discrete series. For the isometric embedding $T:H_1\widehat\otimes H_2\to\mathcal F$ just obtained, set $T_yx=T(x\otimes y)$ and $B(P,y)=T_y^*P$. Then $B$ is linear in the polynomial $P$, conjugate-linear in $y$, and has norm at most $\|P\|\|y\|$. For every $y\ne0$ some polynomial has $B(P,y)\ne0$, since the polynomials are dense and $T_y$ has norm $\|y\|$ on each unit vector. Its value is compact-finite in the first group because that compact group preserves polynomial degree. This gives a smooth vector. The construction uses only the isometry and equivariance of $T$ in the source's local contraction theorem. Its further assertion testing the projection of a particular highest polynomial is unnecessary for this use. [The exact local use of the Hilbert embedding][contraction]

The automorphic row stores the *dual* of each shifted parameter. On the small factor first pull the row action back by matrix conjugation in its signed frame. On the large factor take the conjugate Hilbert space in its plain frame. Both operations undo the dual parameter. Removing $z_1^{m_0}$ and $z_2^{n_0}$ then gives $H_1,H_2$ by the discrete-series classification. Thus one can choose a linear isometry $U$ on the first factor and an antilinear isometry $U'$ on the second, as specified in the [two frame comparisons][frames]. For the polynomial reindexing $\kappa$ to the signed frames the sought bilinear map is
$$
 A(P,y)=U^{-1}B(\kappa P,U'y).
$$
On passing back to the row variables the two trace corrections in its oscillator differential are $-a_T\operatorname{tr}X$ on the small group and $+a_S\operatorname{tr}Y$ on the large group. They are precisely the two scalar terms in the theta model. [Prescribed differential on the realized functions][real] · [Frame and differential calculation](../../../archive/rounds/v77/continuation_v77/interior.tex#L65-L98)

Tensor the $A$ over the real places. The realized polynomials on any prescribed step vacuum have the same compact and differential actions. The product bound gives the weak Hilbert coefficient bound on pure inputs. The verification of the two Lie and the two group identities, including group elements carrying one realized polynomial to another, is given in I77A2. It uses injectivity of polynomial realization on a nonzero vacuum and the fact that if a noncompact move carried a nonzero polynomial to a polynomial, the conjugated compact generator would preserve a finite-dimensional space, while its iterates would have unbounded degrees. Thus on an original step carrying the prescribed split Weil data, vacuum laws and framed models, a single nonzero real intertwiner works for every allowed finite twist. The construction specifies a line up to the choices of the embeddings and isometries. The [global factorization proof](../geometry/THETA_CUSPIDAL_FACTORIZATION.md) uses the intertwiner actually stored in the weak realization, at every twist and on all smooth input vectors.
