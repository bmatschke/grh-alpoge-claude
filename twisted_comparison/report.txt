Outcome and status
==================
TX1–TX16 reconstruct the variable-twist terminal chart, all middle
charts, their reference comparison, the common penultimate character
and finite carrier, the integral zero-step identification, the actual
all-twist chains and level operators, the row comparison, and the full
terminal period/point join. The endpoint is the literal
colsTwPTiedS_std supplied-data theorem. Its NChoice, TupTied and
tp.xi=I.xi inputs remain explicit. The geometry through TX15 does not
use those arithmetic objects.

These are human proofs, with exact immutable source locators. No Lean
source was changed, no Lean compilation or kernel replay was performed,
and no PDF was produced. The component does not assert existence of the
supplied host/TC.Ok/Pins/Later or completion of RH or GRH.

TX1 — rational intersection is essential
========================================
The completed subgroup is not the entire completed lattice stabilizer.
It is H times the specified principal congruence subgroup. A rational
matrix in it has a rational discrepancy from H that lies in the
principal subgroup, so it belongs to H. This exact intersection is
what turns an adelic level condition into an actual permitted rational
frame move. Character well-definedness follows from the same
discrepancy, on which the rational character is trivial. Normality,
multiplicativity and finite exponent are proved before the chart join.

The inequality on d is in additive valuation language: its valuation
is at least the required principal depth. This is the source's
multiplicative norm inequality in equivalent notation. The subgroup
always includes integrality of both the matrix and its inverse.

TX2 — prescribed powers and split conjugates
===========================================
The full terminal scalar is dTop=qOf(P)^(topDep+1), with that exact
power. Its additive P-valuation depth is N_P=v_P(dTop), not necessarily
topDep+1: qOf(P) is not assumed to be a uniformizer. The row scalar
in TX13 is the first power of qOf(P), whose positive valuation puts
its congruence subgroup inside the depth-one residue kernel.
These exact scalar choices are deliberately different.
The paired-prime diagonal is a finite unitary adele, not a globally
unitary rational diagonal. The prescribed component at P determines
the inverse conjugate-transpose component at bar(P). Q has no conjugate
pairs and is disjoint from type/auxiliary primes, so no prescriptions
conflict. Pi is a unit at Q, and therefore both paired matrices preserve
the integral lattices and all reference congruence-kernel data.

TX3 — inverse conventions
========================
Frames act on the right by theta^{-1}. Thus the completed discrepancy
is A^{-1}A'=theta^{-1}. The top character is defined using the inverse
of the product of completed rational characters, so it contributes
lambda(theta). Dropping either inverse gives the wrong scalar.
Conjugation by t leaves the reference type/auxiliary level and its
finite reduction unchanged because t belongs to its congruence kernel.
At Q the reference companion is identity, identifying rho(t^{-1}xt)
with the local component of (kt)^{-1}x(kt).

TX4 — actual double-coset surjectivity
====================================
DR3 supplies a realizer for each given valid framed point. It alone
does not prove that every adelic double coset comes from a rationally
framed point. That converse is supplied by rational approximation
inside open sets of lattice bases and level/coupling conditions, then
paired-prime splicing. For the initial adele y one approximates
the columns of y*k*t, not y alone: x=kt*z*(kt)^(-1) lies in the
level and y*x*k*t=y*k*t*z has the chosen rational frames. The small
discrepancy lies in the prescribed principal subgroup. The chart representative is the realizer times
the inverse of the same companion. The lattice and stabilizer
identifications consequently concern that exact representative.

TX5 — full extension preserves Q frames
=======================================
The IsExt equations with z0=pi*e_last force the block extension
diag(g',1), since pi is nonzero. The source point frame is
diag(A,pi), obtained by multiplying diag(A,1) by t at Q. This differs
from an acting block matrix diag(gamma,1). Type-prime functional and
auxiliary reductions are handled by the original standard companion
and pi congruent to one at auxiliary primes. The root exponent is a
product of positive exponents for the reference and frame characters.
Two realizers of exactly the same point yield a level element with
character one; the orientation is g^{-1}y.

TX6–TX7 — middle fibres and reference comparison
================================================
The chart index includes the raw index plus both auxiliary and twist
coupling cosets. Their stabilizers, rather than only the raw index
group, cut the level. The actual nonempty twist coset is
z+q_P^M O_(P)^n, with z in q_P^(-M) O_(P)^n, because locLat's
positive exponent is its direction and its negative exponent bounds
the centre. Its finite parameter quotient has additive depth
2M*v_P(q_P). A deep principal congruence subgroup preserves each
coset. The uncut level is integral by the literal LevI definition;
ThetaOpen/lvQ_open supplies a principal subgroup on which its
character is trivial. Concretely choose the depth beyond the
denominator bounds for h and h^(-1): the conjugated zero extension
satisfies the defining local congruences and has identity residue,
while its integral phase is killed by the local additive character.
Ok's actual group/character readings then give membership and value
one. Increasing the depth to preserve the coset proves the required
cut ThetaOpen. Finite order by itself would not prove this assertion. The auxiliary
identity-double-coset statement comes from actual nonvanishing and
finite-sum transport, reconstructed independently in CI. The resulting
conjugation matches group and character simultaneously.

At the penultimate stage the raw representatives are identity, so
only the actual coupling vectors need matching. One constructs a
reference point with the same vector residues and then uses its
preferred moving element to conjugate the stabilizer. Away from
that stage the coupling set is empty. Integral lifting is local at
each selected split prime and paired adelically; it is not an
unjustified surjectivity claim for global GL over O_E.

Functor normalization changes the value module and functional so
that the latter kills the base lattice. It does not change chart
groups, characters, representatives,
or coupling data. The source SameCar assertion therefore follows
on the same objects.

TX8 — why a common character exists
===================================
Individual characters do not in general extend to the join of
subgroups. TX8 supplies a common ambient group NearSet. Rational
middle-level witnesses have integral matrices and inverses; their
values are unique under the actual q_P^D(P) congruence. At the
penultimate index the tail is empty, the n1Arg phase is absent, and
the remaining lamRow character factors through residue matrices;
positive D(P) therefore kills the discrepancy character. The identity
(AB)^{-1}(CD)=(B^{-1}D)(D^{-1}(A^{-1}C)D) proves closure at that
same depth. The local common character is valV^{-1}, including the
inverse required by the level convention.

The proof that a chart lies in NearSet uses its actual level and
coupling dictionary and rational approximation. The equality of its
character with valV^{-1} uses uniqueness of rational witness values,
so no arbitrary choice of a lift survives. There is at least one
penultimate chart: last_uniq provides the index and the base
decoration with the zero-centred actual locLat coset provides a point.
Its principal subgroup is contained in W_v, proving openness.
Finite index follows
from action on a finite lattice quotient, not from finite generation
of an infinite local group.

TX9 — the finite joint carrier
==============================
The level is the reference common level away from Q and the local
join W_v at Q. Its character is the reference character on the
component with Q replaced by identity, multiplied by the product
of local inverse-value characters. These factors recover every chart
character. The stabilizer equation uses both the auxiliary vector
and the Q cosets. Its finite carrier may be realized as a disjoint
union J/H_beta; retaining beta in the Q-coordinate prevents accidental
identification of distinct chart orbits. The pair of coordinate maps
is injective because equality of both coordinates is precisely
membership in H_beta. This proves the stronger joint data, including
Q-trivial invariance of y_Q and the exact HSym auxiliary reduction.

TX10 — integral Law0
====================
At zero, full rational integral frame groups absorb any discrepancy
between two frames of the same lattice. Forgetting frames is thus
an orbit bijection. The preferred decoration is the same canonical
decoration under this bijection; this is stronger than mere isometry
of lattices and is needed for exact transport comparisons. Trivial
stabilizers make the actual rational form space a space of arbitrary
coefficient functions. Pullback gives a linear equivalence, and the
pointwise integral Delta membership is identical on corresponding
classes. Both directions of the lattice equivalence are proved.
Embedding injectivity makes the reference-twist comparison literally
the specified identity. More generally its embedding clause forces
every permissible Law0 witness to equal the same reindexing, so the
proof applies to any supplied Law0, as the source theorem requires.
No rank-only or rational-only inference
is substituted for integral equality.

TX11–TX12 — same-object chains and level operators
=================================================
The full chart is selected before the step family, so its TopBase
and extension witnesses remain those used in the final period map.
The source class lattice is I.latAt and all underlying frame maps
are identity. The exact type-prime and residue maps can also be
identity. Diamond transport at step zero follows from freeness of
the underlying decoration action after restoring the allowed frame
correction; its frame character is trivial. CV9 supplies complex,
rational and integral values on these charts, FC7 their actual
metrics, and FC18 their diamonds.

The level projections are the actual quotient maps. NB moves are
used with DR3 realizers and the same supported splice, preserving
all frame/coupling labels. Step-zero comparison includes invalid
branches of the total up operator: congruent representatives give
identical raw lattices, so validity and fallback coincide. All
transport functions agree, not just their underlying class maps.
The B_w assertion uses equality of the pointwise Delta lattices
of the same local kernel and the two-inclusion localization argument.

TX13–TX14 — row chart and arithmetic values
==========================================
The row chart uses k=t=1 and d=qOf(P), standard base lattice, empty
type-prime set, unique index and no coupling. Its character still
has the inverse twist factor. The reference equality is local away
from Q and pointwise for matrices identity at Q. The constant weight
shift leaves the polynomial natural weight and integral Delta
lattice unchanged, but the determinant factor stays in the frame map.

For localization at a fixed coefficient prime, finite-class
denominator clearing chooses a multiplier outside that prime using
finite prime avoidance/CRT, while satisfying all bounds at the other
finitely many bad primes. Its integrality preserves every already-good
place. Merely saying that the multiplier is integral would not prove
the localization statement. The
pairing has the source Hermitian order nfAll(Q,P), weighted by inverse
stabilizer size. The embedding/norm argument uses the actual rational
basis over the conjugation-stable coefficient field as in FC7,
never continuity of arbitrary embeddings or arbitrary complex
coefficient interchange. Coefficient conjugation supplies the
semilinear period map and its exact image.

TX15 — the same moving element throughout
========================================
gamma is the rational component of the original preferred-point
moving element. Its action gives the exact integral lattice
N_row+O*z0. The full-dual functional formula is checked after
multiplying by bar(pi), then cancelling its unit residue at the
selected type prime. The source intermediate N, L and Lsharp are
kept distinct.

The exact matrix is x=y_M^{-1} rat(gamma)^{-1} ext(y_P), and ka=x^{-1}.
Hence rat(gamma)y_M=ext(y_P)ka and chi(ka)^{-1}=sigma(chr(move)).
Conjugation of the actual stabilizer map by gamma gives the block
extension of the row stabilizer. The same scalar and inverse-frame
action prove evaluation for arbitrary frame functions. No new
representative or moving element is chosen to repair the final join.

TX16 — endpoint and retained scope
==================================

The bad-prime predicate is identified through actual ramification,
