1. FINITE CYCLIC COUNTING AND THE ACTUAL SEGMENTS [GII1]

Let T be a subset of {0,...,n'-1}, and write
  W(i,L)={(i+j) mod n':0<=j<L}.
Assume r,u>=1, G=(M+5)ell, 3G<=n', and the source's good-orbit conditions:
  (A) every W(i,L2) contains at most K0 selected points;
  (T) among the M+1 successive complete tiles beginning at any tile index,
      at least one is marked, with tile indices read modulo t=floor(n'/ell);
  (I) every W(i,G) contains at most N1 selected points.
A complete tile has R cells of length u. In a marked tile the odd-numbered
cells are empty; each even-numbered cell contains exactly one selected point,
and that point is even. Thus it has 3r selected even points.

There is an even selected point at positive cyclic distance at most G from
EVERY residue i, including points in the incomplete remainder of the period.
Here is a short proof which keeps that remainder. List the marked complete
tile indices increasingly. Applying (T) immediately after a marked index
shows that the next one is at tile distance at most M+1. Lift their starts
periodically to the integers. If n'=t ell+R0, 0<=R0<ell, the physical gap
between consecutive starts is D ell, or D ell+R0 at the wrap. It is therefore
strictly less than (M+2)ell. Choose the first such start B strictly after i.
The selected point in its second cell lies in [B+u,B+2u). Its distance from
i is positive and <(M+2)ell+2u<G. This also proves existence of marked tiles:
t>=3(M+5)>0 and (T) applies. This is the actual NextEvenTop argument; it does
not replace n' by t ell at the wrap.

For each i choose such a top z and distance d in {1,...,G}. The map
(z,d) -> z-d modulo n' covers all residues. Consequently
  n' <= |T|G.                                                   (GII1.1)
For the upper bound, sum (I) over ALL n' cyclic starting positions. Because
G<=n', each fixed top belongs to exactly G of these windows. Hence
  |T|G <= n'N1.                                                (GII1.2)
The source uses the weaker |T|G<=N1(n'+G), by covering with intervals
beginning at multiples of G. Our cyclic double count is an explicitly
identified sharpening under the present 3G<=n' hypothesis. Both imply
the source SegmentCountBounds. We retain its downstream constant 40.

For the rest of adm1, take K0=r-1 and L1<=L2<u. The previous L1 positions
before a top inject into the L2 window starting at i-L1. Thus fewer than r
selected even predecessors occur there. Choose the LEAST positive distance
to an even selected successor. It is <=G; its open interior is contained in
W(i+1,G), so contains at most N1 selected points. These are the literal
EarliestMaximumFar and ConsecutiveMaximaClose clauses.

The same proof gives the exact OrbitSegs required by the Type-I branch. There
is an even selected point, and its even successor is distinct since G<n'.
Thus numSeg, the number of even selected tops, is at least two. In the cyclic
increasing enumeration of even tops, the next member minimizes the positive
distance; its gap segG is <=G. The source segDists lists the strictly interior
selected odd positions, whose distances are distinct and lie in the same
G-window. Therefore segS<=N1. This is precisely EvenGapHyp, not a different
segmentation. Source counterparts: bs_numSeg, bs_start_mem, bs_min, bs_segs
and orbit_segments in TopChoiceBounds. The stronger r-plane frames have
already been proved in FG1–FG8.

9. CONDITIONAL GOOD-ORBIT PROBABILITY, NOT JUST WITHIN-ORBIT COUNTS [GII9]

Select every point of a finite ambient group H independently with probability
delta. Fix a step of order n'>=x^(epsilon/4) and a point t, and CONDITION on
t being selected. Its orbit coordinates are independent Bernoulli(delta)
except for one forced index i0. This is the exact finite conditional measure
condProbAt: a subset S containing i0 has weight
delta^(|S|-1)(1-delta)^(n'-|S|). Summing over points outside this orbit
contributes 1, because each independent outside factor is delta+(1-delta).
Thus the unconditioned event {t selected and orbit bad} has probability
delta times this conditional bad probability. The injective orbit map is
(GII3.1); no representative is declared uniformly random.

Assume delta<=1/10, L2<=Lb, n'<=n<=x^(h+epsilon/8), and 3G<=n'. We claim
  Pr(bad orbit | t selected)<=Cb x^(-2),
  Cb=(Lb+1)2^Lb+2.                                          (GII9.1)
For the actual family use the fixed Lb=2ceil(620/zeta) from (GII4.4).

Short windows. At most L2 starting positions yield an L2-window containing
i0: the starting point is determined by its offset 0,...,L2-1. Failure of A
means at least K0+1 selected points. A window containing i0 must then contain
a selected K0-subset of unforced points; a window not containing it must
contain a selected (K0+1)-subset. A union bound gives
  L2 binom(L2,K0)delta^K0
    +n' binom(L2,K0+1)delta^(K0+1).                           (GII9.2)
Since delta^K0<=x^(-(h+2)), and binom(L2,j)<=2^Lb, these two terms are at
most Lb2^Lb x^(-2) and 2^Lb x^(-2), respectively. Keeping the extra delta
in the second term is essential: it cancels the epsilon/8 in the ambient
upper bound. A crude n' binom(L2,K0)delta^K0 would lose that margin.

Marked tiles. Write u=ceil(1/delta). The integral inequality
log(1-delta)>=-delta/(1-delta) gives
  (1-delta)^u>=exp(-(1+delta)/(1-delta))>=1/4.
The last step uses delta<=1/10 and e^(4/3)<4. Also
floor(u/2)delta>=(1-delta)/2>=9/20. A cell is empty with probability at
least 1/4. Its event of exactly one selected even point has probability at
least floor(u/2)delta(1-delta)^(u-1)>=9/80>1/10. Complete cells are disjoint,
so a tile is marked with probability at least P0=10^(-(6r+1)).

For each starting tile, its M+1 cyclic tiles are distinct because
floor(n'/ell)>=M+1. At most one contains the forced point i0. Discard it;
at least M unaffected disjoint tiles remain. Their marking events are
independent under the conditioning. Therefore failure of Tiles has probability
at most n'(1-P0)^M<=n'exp(-P0M)<=n'x^(-(h+3))<=x^(-2).
The remainder interval causes no difficulty: if i0 lies there, no tile is
affected. The parity of the interval start only improves the lower bound
floor(u/2) on the number of even points in a cell.

Long windows. Put B=N1-1=ceil(max(6eGdelta,(h+3)log_2x)). After conditioning,
each G-window has at most one forced point. Failure of I is dominated by
the event that at leastB points are selected in G independent trials. For
B>0 its probability is at most
  binom(G,B)delta^B<=(eGdelta/B)^B<=2^(-B)<=x^(-(h+3)).
The first bound is the union bound over selected B-subsets; the factorial
bound B!>=(B/e)^B follows, for example, by integrating log on [1,B]. If B>G
the event is empty. As B>=6eGdelta>0, these cases cover every possibility.
