This report reconstructs RestMem 1088, RestInd 1088,
GoodPathWeight 1088, BetaRatioBound 1088, and RestSeg 1088 under the
literal Dat.Hyp hypotheses and CLa,Ck>=1088. It uses the integral
signed-path basis and its prefix bounds proved in IP2–IP6, the
tableau/Weyl dimension formula and elementary residue count proved in
v0.6 CB/SD, and the coarse-degree bounds proved in this version's
RS2–RS3. None of the source's path-probability, logarithmic-deficit,
RestMem, RestInd, or BetaRatioBound statements is left as an assumed
input. This is a segment theorem; the other residue gluing and
congruence results are needed to obtain RestCount. It is not by itself
a proof of RH or GRH.

The source's formal proof is packaged in a D7/AdmB interface. Where it
calls BracketPolynomialSpan, DefiniteRankOne, or NestedTypeBound,
we use the particular already-proved IP facts explicitly stated below.
We do not assume an integral direct sum of rational isotypic pieces.
The counting argument is a finite weighted count, not a probabilistic
independence assumption. The only probability measure, if desired, is
the endpoint Weyl dimension divided by the exactly computed total.

GP1. THE ACTUAL SEGMENT AND ITS NUMERICAL DICTIONARY

Fix a segment j. Write its increasing selected letters as
  x_0<...<x_(N-1),    n0=N-r,    k=r+K.
The first r letters form its old frame W; the last r letters form its
current frame, denoted tau_0,...,tau_(r-1). The intermediate letters
are LL_j. Even letters are vectors and odd letters are covectors.
Frame validity says that |LL_j|>=r and that its first and last r
letters are vectors. Thus N>=3r, the first r and last 2r letters of
the segment are vectors, and N<=Ns. For every i>=r, o(i) is the
index of the r-th most recent vector among x_0,...,x_(i-1).
It exists because the first r letters are vectors. There are exactly
r vectors in [o(i),i), and o(i)<n0 because the last r preterminal
letters are vectors. These are exactly the source's oldest indices.

Use the notation P_i=Pz(j,x_i), Q=Qc_j, m=k Ns+1, and
  c_i=a'_(pos x_i)/P_i,
  Bw_i=floor(c_i)-(k+r+1).
For path counting assume only AdmLL: c_i-k<b_i<=c_i on LL_j.
For membership and the rank comparison additionally assume BOK:
b vanishes off the segment and b_i>=Bw_i on W. No other values
of b enter the path-abundance argument. Set, for i<n0,
  B_i=(Bw_i-m)_+       for i<r,
  B_i=b_i             for r<=i<n0,
  beta=sum_(i<n0) B_i,
  R=product_(i<n0) binom(B_i+k-1,k-1).
All positive parts above are integer positive parts. RS2–RS3 give
  (9/10)a/P_i <= c_i <= 2a/P_i,
  c_i >= (36/5)Y k^2 Ns >= 36 k Ns.
Consequently
  c_i-2kNs <= B_i <= c_i,        k<=B_i<=2a/P_i<=2a.       (GP1.1)
For old-frame letters, floor(c_i)-k-r-1-m>0 follows from this
same margin, so Bw_i>=m and no positive-part ambiguity remains.
For the lower bound, floor(c_i)>c_i-1 and
k+r+3<=kNs, since Ns>=6 and k>=r. On LL_j use b_i>c_i-k.

Since dist(j,x)=z_j-x on this segment, i<i' gives P_i>=pP_i';
if the letters have the same parity, P_i>=p^2 P_i'. Combining
(GP1.1) with B_i'>=c_i'-2kNs>=(17/18)c_i'>=(17/20)a/P_i'
gives, with considerable room,
  (p^2/5)B_i <= B_i'  for i<i'<n0 of the same sign.        (GP1.2)
Also, writing bbar_i=a/P_i, for l<i and l<n0,
  B_l <= 2 bbar_i/p <= 4 bbar_i/p.                       (GP1.3)
In particular, the reverse-chronological lists of either sign decrease
by a factor of at least p^2/5>=169/5>4.

The literal size hypotheses imply a>=416 Ns>=3, log a>=1, and
k>=1088 Ns^2. Put h=K-2N=k-r-2N. Then h>=k/2>=1. We will use
  L=log(1+2aN),   L<=2 log a,
  min(La,k)>=1088 Ns^2 log a,                            (GP1.4)
where La=CLa Ns^2 log a. The logarithmic estimate follows already
from a>=4Ns and a>=3: 1+2aN<=a^2. These deductions verify every
numerical premise used below from Dat.Hyp; there is no large-a
asymptotic qualification.

GP2. THE EXACT PATHS AND THEIR TOTAL WEIGHT

A path is a sequence of dominant integer k-tuples mu^0,...,mu^N,
starting at zero. A vector step adds a horizontal strip, and a
covector step removes one. For an addition alpha->alpha', this means
  alpha_l<=alpha'_l,    alpha'_(l+1)<=alpha_l,
and the strip size is sum_l(alpha'_l-alpha_l). Removal reverses the
two tuples. The first n0 sizes are B_i. The last r sizes are allowed
to vary, with the literal head condition
  mu^(n0+t)_l=beta    for 0<=l<t,  1<=t<=r.              (GP2.1)
This is the source's IsPATH, with no change to its selected family.
Write nu^i=(mu^i_0,...,mu^i_(r-1)) and
kappa^i=(mu^i_r,...,mu^i_(k-1)). Give the path weight
  w(mu)=d_K(kappa^N)
       =product_(0<=u<v<K) (kappa^N_u-kappa^N_v+v-u)/(v-u).
By the previously proved tableau dimension formula, this is precisely
the number of standard normal column arrays I for that path.

The total weight is exactly R. Indeed the signed Pieri identity from
IP3 gives, at each fixed-size preterminal step,
  sum_(alpha') d_k(alpha')=d_k(alpha) binom(B_i+k-1,k-1).
For a covector step this follows also by reversing and negating the
weights. At stage n0+t remove the first t forced beta entries and
call the remaining tuple eta^(n0+t). Its length is k-t. The terminal
strip condition is exactly
  eta^old_l >= eta^new_l >= eta^old_(l+1).
Conversely each such interlacing tuple gives a valid terminal strip:
beta is at least the largest preterminal entry and remains so under
interlacing. The one-row GT branching identity
sum_(eta' interlaces eta) d_(m-1)(eta')=d_m(eta) now cancels all
r terminal steps. Starting from d_k(0)=1 proves
  sum_paths w(mu)=R.                                     (GP2.2)
All these sums are finite. A preterminal entry lies between minus
the sum of preceding covector degrees and the sum of preceding vector
degrees. Terminal interlacing gives the same bounds for the unforced
entries; the forced entries equal beta. In particular every absolute
entry is at most beta<=2aN. At each stage there are at most N positive
and N negative entries, and all other entries are zero. Each of the
first N and last N slots has at most 1+beta possible values, using
nonnegative values for the former and nonpositive values for the latter.
They do not overlap since k>=2N. Hence
  number of paths <= (1+beta)^(2N^2) <= exp(2N^2 L).        (GP2.3)
For the positive slots one may use the larger bound beta even at
terminal stages. The zero initial stage contributes no choices.

GP3. INTERLACING PRESERVES TAIL DEFICITS

For a dominant tuple eta of length d define
  T_+(eta,u)=sum_(l>=u) max(eta_l,0),
  T_-(eta,u)=sum_(l>=u) max(-eta_(d-1-l),0).
At stage i use eta^i=mu^i with its first (i-n0)_+ forced entries
removed. Let V_i be the reverse chronological list of the B_l at
vector letters l<min(i,n0), and C_i the analogous covector list.
Let S_+(i,u) and S_-(i,u) be the sums of these lists after their
first u terms have been deleted.

We claim, at every stage and every u,
  T_+(eta^i,u)<=S_+(i,u),   T_-(eta^i,u)<=S_-(i,u).         (GP3.1)
For a preterminal vector step, horizontal-strip interlacing gives
  T_+(eta^new,u+1)<=T_+(eta^old,u),
  T_+(eta^new,0)<=T_+(eta^old,0)+B_i.
The latter holds because positive-part growth is at most the total
coordinate growth B_i. Its positive reference list changes from V
to B_i::V. Negative tails can only decrease, and their reference list
does not change. Covector steps give the two reversed statements.
At a terminal step, eta^new interlaces eta^old in one smaller
dimension. The positive tails decrease. Reversing and negating the
interlacing inequality shows that the negative tails also decrease.
Neither reference list changes. These observations prove (GP3.1)
by induction, including u larger than the list length, where both
reference sums are zero.

The same proof preserves a deficit, with no attenuation. If at some
stage i and index u one has T_+(eta^i,u)<=S_+(i,u)-X, X>=0,
then at the final stage the same deficit holds at index u plus the
number of subsequent preterminal vector steps. At each such active
step use the shifted-tail inequality; at any other step use monotonicity.
The reference-list suffix, including its first entry when nonempty,
is unchanged by these shifts. The negative version counts subsequent
preterminal covector steps. This proves the entire PathTailBounds
mechanism directly.

GP4. A LOGARITHMIC BINOMIAL DEFICIT

Let f_h(x)=sum_(t=1)^h log(1+x/t), x>=0. At integral x it equals
log binom(x+h,h). It is increasing and concave, with
  f'_h(x)=sum_(t=1)^h 1/(x+t),
  h/(x+h)<=f'_h(x)<=h/(x+1).
Suppose a reverse-chronological reference list b_0,...,b_(s-1)
satisfies b_l>=h and b_l>=4b_(l+1). For any a_l>=0 whose every
suffix sum is bounded by the corresponding b suffix, put
  Delta_u=sum_(l>=u)(a_l-b_l)<=0,
  g_u=f'_h(b_u),    g_(-1)=0.
Concavity and finite summation by parts give the exact useful estimate
  sum_l[f_h(a_l)-f_h(b_l)]
    <=sum_l g_l(a_l-b_l)
     =sum_u(g_u-g_(u-1)) Delta_u.                        (GP4.1)
The derivative bounds and b_(u-1)>=4b_u>=4h imply
g_(u-1)<=g_u/2. For u=0, g_u-g_(u-1)=g_u. Therefore every
coefficient on the right is nonnegative and at least h/(4b_u).
The expression is always <=0; if Delta_u<=-X at one index, it is
  <=-X h/(4b_u).                                        (GP4.2)
If a list is shorter than the tuple to which it is compared, (GP3.1)
at its length forces every remaining positive/negative part to be zero,
so padding by zero causes no error. Empty lists have zero contribution
and cannot possess a positive deficit. This handles all endpoints
without division by a zero entry.

GP5. WEYL DIMENSION WITH A SHORTER ERROR ESTIMATE

For lambda=kappa^N, let P and M be its numbers of positive and negative
entries. Both are <=N. Every nonzero absolute value is <=beta, and
lambda_max+(-lambda_min)_+<=beta, by terminal interlacing and the
preterminal positive/negative degree sums. Expand log d_K(lambda)
as a sum over coordinate pairs.

Pairs with two nonzero entries contribute at most log(1+beta) each;
there are at most binom(2N,2)<=2N^2 such pairs. For one fixed
positive entry lambda_u=x, its interactions with zero entries have
the form log(1+x/t), with distinct distances t in {1,...,K-1}.
The terms with t<=h=K-2N are bounded by f_h(x); there are at most
2N remaining distances, each contributing <=log(1+beta).
Exactly the same argument applies to a negative entry, using zero
entries to its left and its absolute value. There are at most 2N
nonzero entries altogether. Pairs of two zero entries contribute zero.
Consequently
  log w(mu) <= sum_l f_h(max(lambda_l,0))
               +sum_l f_h(max(-lambda_l,0))+6N^2 L.       (GP5.1)
This direct endpoint estimate is slightly stronger than the source's
general-purpose 9N^2 L estimate. It uses exactly the same h and
actual path family. For numerical compatibility we may weaken 6 to 9.

Since k-1>=h and each B_i>=0,
  log R=sum_(i<n0)log binom(B_i+k-1,k-1)
       >=sum_(i<n0)f_h(B_i).                             (GP5.2)
Apply GP4 separately to the positive and reversed negative parts of
lambda, using V_N and C_N. Their suffix bounds are GP3. Thus
log(w/R)<=6N^2 L, and any positive or negative suffix deficit X at
reference entry b improves this to
  log(w/R)<=6N^2 L-Xh/(4b).                              (GP5.3)
This proves all the dimension estimates consumed here without
assuming a concentration theorem or using a large-rank approximation.

GP6. THE ACTUAL BAD EVENTS HAVE SMALL TOTAL WEIGHT

For i>=r put Xi_i=La bbar_i/(p k). Call a path typical when
  (a) at a covector step, positive mass decreases by at most Xi_i;
  (b) at a vector step i>=r, negative mass decreases by at most Xi_i;
  (c) mu^i_(r-1)>=(2/3)B_o(i).
Positive/negative mass here means the sum of coordinate positive/
negative parts of mu. For (a) all covector steps are preterminal;
for (b) forced terminal heads are nonnegative, so stripping them
does not change negative mass. This is the precise source Typ, and
agrees with its generic Typical after the stage-coordinate translation.

If (a) fails, the positive reference list is unchanged at this
covector step. By (GP3.1) the positive tail at u=0 after the step
has deficit at least Xi_i. Propagate it to the final tuple. The
reference entry at its shifted index is some earlier B_l. By
(GP1.3), B_l<=4 bbar_i/p, so (GP5.3) gives the penalty
  Xi_i h/(4B_l)>=La h/(16k).
If this reference list is empty the positive deficit is impossible,
so the denominator is positive. The same reasoning with negative
tails treats (b), including terminal vector steps.

For (c), remove the t=(i-n0)_+ forced heads. Then mu^i_(r-1)
is eta^i_(r-1-t), and the entry of V_i at index u=r-1-t is B_o(i).
This is because the terminal heads account for exactly t of the
last r vector letters. All these plane entries are nonnegative:
after at most N covector steps the last N coordinates contain every
possible negative entry, while N+r<=K. Splitting the positive tail
at u into its first entry and the tail at u+1 shows that failure
of (c) creates a deficit B_o(i)/3. Propagating it and applying GP5
gives the penalty h/12. Thus every atypical path satisfies
  w(mu) <= R exp(6N^2 L-min(La h/(16k),h/12)).             (GP6.1)
Since h>=k/2, the minimum is at least min(La,k)/32. Multiplying
by the count (GP2.3) yields the stronger direct bound
  sum_atypical w(mu)
     <=R exp(8N^2 L-min(La,k)/32)
     <=R exp(11N^2 L-min(La,k)/32)
     <=R/a^(Ns^2).                                      (GP6.2)
For the last step use (GP1.4): the exponent with 11 is at most
(22-34)Ns^2 log a=-12Ns^2 log a, which is <=-Ns^2 log a.
The source asks for the final, weaker bound; no source constant
has been reduced or silently changed.

GP7. TYPICAL PATHS ARE THE REQUIRED GOOD PATHS

For every i>=r, horizontal-strip interlacing gives
  mu^i_(r-1) <= B_o(i)+sum_(l<o(i), vector) B_l
              <=(1+5/(p^2-5))B_o(i)<2B_o(i).             (GP7.1)
To see the first inequality, move backwards from coordinate r-1
through the r-1 vector steps strictly between o(i) and i, decreasing
the coordinate index at each such step. Covector steps only decrease
coordinates, so they do not obstruct this upper bound. The result is
the first coordinate immediately after step o(i), bounded by the sum
of vector degrees up to that point. All those steps are preterminal.
The second inequality sums the geometric series from (GP1.2).

For a vector step, the positive part of the normal-coordinate mass
can increase by at most mu^i_(r-1): summing
mu^(i+1)_(r+c)<=mu^i_(r+c-1) telescopes after taking positive parts.
Its total increase is this positive-part increase plus the decrease
in the whole negative mass. Thus typicality implies
  sum_c(kappa^(i+1)_c-kappa^i_c)<=Xi_i+2B_o(i).           (GP7.2)
For a covector step, the decrease in plane mass is at most the
decrease in the whole positive mass, since the plane entries remain
nonnegative. It is therefore <=Xi_i. Finally condition (c) is the
required plane minimum.

The literal source threshold thc is (2/3)(Bw_old-m) when the oldest
letter lies in W, and (2/3)b_old otherwise. These equal (2/3)B_o(i)
here; even the source's positive-part comparison suffices. Its
ordinary-degree allowance is
  oc_i=La a/(p k P_i)+1_(vector i) 4a/P_o(i).
Equation (GP1.1) gives 2B_o(i)<=4a/P_o(i). Hence (GP7.2) and its
covector counterpart are exactly IsGood. The set T of a good path
and a standard normal array I is finite and, by (GP2.2) and (GP6.2),
  |T| >= (1-a^(-Ns^2)) R.                                (GP7.3)
This is GoodPathWeight 1088, with the original product of LL
dimensions and old-frame dimensions because those are exactly the
two factors in R.

GP8. THE ACTUAL FAMILY LIES IN M_j(b)

For a good path mu and standard normal array I, choose nonnegative
integers u_(t,c), 0<=t,c<r, such that
  u_(t,c)+2beta<Q,
  d_(n0+t)+sum_c u_(t,c)=b(tau_t),                        (GP8.1)
where d_(n0+t) is the actual terminal strip size. These are exactly
the source Idx conditions. Put
  C_b=det(X_(old a,c))^m
       product_(a<r) X_(old a,0)^(b(old a)-Bw(old a)),
  U_u=product_(t,c<r) X_(tau_t,c)^u_(t,c),
  A=C_b U_u Psi_plane(nu,Ican),  G=Psi_normal(kappa,I),
  F_(mu,I,u)=A G.                                        (GP8.2)
Variables are embedded in their actual disjoint plane/normal coordinate
sets and renamed by x_i. This is literally famEl, not another generator
family. The old exponents are nonnegative by BOK.

Here are the IP facts used. Splitting the first r and last K rows of
a horizontal strip gives two signed paths. Their degrees add to d_i,
and their crossing inequalities are
  kappa^(i+1)_0<=nu^i_(r-1) for vector i,
  kappa^i_0<=nu^(i+1)_(r-1) for covector i.                (GP8.3)
Each standard polynomial has its path's row degrees and, at every
prefix, minimum generic torus exponent at least its last path entry
and maximum exponent at most its first path entry. These are the
precise integral filtered-basis bounds of IP, over the given DVR O.

The first r full-path steps are vectors; hence their normal paths
are zero and G has no old-frame variables. Every prefix used at a
non-old letter contains the whole old frame. The determinant in C_b
has generic rank-one exponent exactly 1 on any such prefix, so its
m-th power contributes m. All other new multipliers use only vector
coordinates and have nonnegative minimum exponent. Consequently,
with M_i=kappa^(i+1)_0 for vectors and kappa^i_0 for covectors,
  max_(<=x_i)(G)<=M_i,   min_(<x_i)(A)>=M_i+m    (vector),
  max_(<x_i)(G)<=M_i,    min_(<=x_i)(A)>=M_i+m   (covector),
and min_(<x_i)(A)>=thc_i by IsGood. Thus A,G satisfy every
literal generator condition in XX'. At vector x_i its ordinary
degree is the normal row degree; at covector x_i it is the plane
row degree. The multiplier does not alter either, since its extra
coordinates are all plane variables at vector letters. IsGood gives
the required ordinary-degree bounds.

The total row degrees are exactly b. On an old-frame row they are
  (Bw-m)+m+(b-Bw)=b;
on LL they are B_i=b_i; at a terminal row use (GP8.1).
Finally every terminal strip has d_(n0+t)<=2beta, by the already
proved SD6 interlacing estimate. Thus every terminal plane-coordinate
exponent in F is at most d_(n0+t)+u_(t,c)<Q. This checks the actual
resSet cutoff, not merely a row-total cutoff. Therefore
  F_(mu,I,u) belongs to M_j(b).                            (GP8.4)
This proves all four source RestMemA–D clauses and RestMem 1088.

GP9. A SPECIALIZATION PROVES INTEGRAL INDEPENDENCE

Remove the common nonzero factor C_b; multiplication by it is injective
because O and its polynomial ring are domains. For each current-frame
row t, retain only its antidiagonal plane variable
  Y_t=X_(tau_t,r-1-t)
and set its other plane variables to zero in the path polynomial.
Call this substitution KILL. It acts on the path polynomial but is
not applied to U_u at this intermediate stage.

For every positive column index c<=beta, let h_c(i) be its plane
column height. Plane entries lie in [0,beta], and the terminal plane
shape is the rectangle (beta,...,beta). At a terminal step every
column height either stays put or increases by one. Let J_c contain
the coordinate r-1-t precisely when the height increases at terminal
step t, and put Icut_c={0,...,r-1}\J_c. Let nu_cut agree with nu
through stage n0 and remain constant thereafter. Expanding each
terminal exterior multiplication along the retained single coordinate
gives the exact identity
  KILL Psi_plane(nu,Ican)
    =sign(mu) [product_c product_(r-1-t in J_c) Y_t]
                Psi_plane(nu_cut,Icut),                  (GP9.1)
where sign(mu) is +1 or -1. This holds over O with no denominator.

For completeness, Icut is a standard array. It has column size
h_c(n0), because each terminal rise adds one to reach height r.
For every 0<=t<r it satisfies the exact counting identity
  |Icut_c intersect {0,...,t}|+r
      =t+1+h_c(n0+r-1-t).                                (GP9.2)
Heights are nonincreasing in c, which is precisely the required
standard-column inequality. Columns c<=0 or c>beta are empty in
this nonnegative plane situation. Formula (GP9.2) also shows that
nu_cut together with Icut determines the whole plane path: Icut
records all J_c, hence the time of every terminal height increase;
a dominant tuple is determined by its column heights.

The products Psi_plane(nu_cut,Icut) Psi_normal(kappa,I) are members
of the tensor product of the two integral path bases from IP. They
use no current-frame plane variables. They are therefore independent
even when multiplied by distinct monomials in those frame variables.
For the specialized family U_u KILL(Psi_plane Psi_normal), the
resulting monomial exponent is u plus the terminal exponents in
(GP9.1). If two basis labels and monomial exponents agree, (GP9.2)
first recovers nu, then kappa and I recover mu,I, and subtraction
of the common terminal exponent recovers u. Thus this specialized
family is O-linearly independent.

To lift this to the original family, scale all killed variables by
an indeterminate T. Suppose there is a nontrivial finite relation
sum_q a_q U_(u_q) W_q=0, where W_q=Psi_plane Psi_normal. Among
indices with a_q!=0 choose the least total degree delta of U_(u_q)
in the killed variables. The coefficient of T^delta is exactly
  sum_(q with degree delta) a_q U_(u_q) KILL(W_q)=0.
Higher degrees cannot contribute; lower degrees have zero coefficient
in the original relation. Independence of the specialized family
forces all these a_q to vanish, a contradiction. Restoring C_b proves
the literal RestInd 1088. This argument uses an integral path basis;
it never invokes integral semisimplicity.

GP10. THE COARSE RATIO, WITH ITS CONSTANTS

Set D=dist(j,tau_0), g=z_(j+1)-z_j, R0=R_j, R1=R_(j+1).
Frame validity gives a gap greater than L1 before tau_0; hence
  dist(j,x_i)>=D+L1+1  for every i<n0.
All these distances are distinct integers. Therefore (GP1.1) and
the geometric series give
  beta <= 2a sum_(i<n0) p^(-R0-dist(j,x_i))
        <=4a/p^(R0+D+L1+1).                             (GP10.1)
RS3 gives the exact, untruncated scale formula
  Q=p^(g+R1-R0)
and the lower adjacent coarse-power bound
  p^R1 > a/[8pYk^2Ns p^(D+g)].
Thus
  Q > a/[8pYk^2Ns p^(D+R0)],
  beta/Q < 32Yk^2Ns/p^L1 <= 1/(4pY) <= 1/(4Y).         (GP10.2)
The penultimate inequality is exactly
128k^2NsY^2<=p^(L1-1). In particular
  4Y beta<=Q.                                            (GP10.3)
This proves BetaRatioBound directly and is slightly stronger than the
source target. It makes explicit both the direction of the adjacent
coarse-scale inequality and the extra factor p. The integer positive
part in Q's definition is removed only using the RS3 proof that its
exponent is positive. All denominators in (GP10.1–3) are positive.

GP11. RESIDUE PADDING OF EVERY GOOD PATH

Fix a path in T and target residues rho_t modulo Q. For one terminal
row t, count u_c in [0,Q-2beta) with
  d_(n0+t)+sum_c u_c congruent rho_t modulo Q.
Among all tuples in [0,Q)^r exactly Q^(r-1) satisfy the congruence.
For each coordinate, each of its 2beta forbidden values occurs in
exactly Q^(r-2) solutions, since r>=2. The union bound gives at least
  Q^(r-1) max(0,1-2r beta/Q)
   >=Q^(r-1) max(0,1-r/(2Y))                             (GP11.1)
allowed tuples. GP10 implies Q>2beta (also if beta=0), so these
intervals and the forbidden count have their literal meanings.
The rows are independent choices. Define
  e_t=d_(n0+t)+sum_c u_(t,c).
The SD6 bound d_(n0+t)<=2beta and u_(t,c)<=Q-2beta-1 give
e_t<=2beta+r(Q-2beta-1)<rQ. Its residue is rho_t. Changing the
terminal degrees of b to e leaves B_i,beta and the good-path
conditions unchanged: all oldest letters occur before n0. We obtain
the exact source indices Idx(j,restr(setTau(b,e))). The construction

  sum_(0<=e_t<rQ, e_t congruent rho_t mod Q) |Idx(b[e])|
    >= |T| [Q^(r-1) max(0,1-r/(2Y))]^r.                  (GP11.2)
This is RestMon, using the already-proved elementary SD6 count but

GP12. RESTSEG, WITH THE ACTUAL FUNCTION G

Set G_j(b)=|Idx(j,restr b)|. It is nonnegative and depends only on
the late row degrees, because restr sets all other degrees to zero.
In fact Idx(j,restr b)=Idx(j,b): the old-frame B_i and thresholds
use Bw_i and m, not b_i; all other queried row degrees are late.
Thus its indices can be used in famEl with the full BOK degree b.
For BOK b, GP8 and GP9 embed a free module with this many generators
in M_j(b). That module is finite: its polynomials have finitely many
variables and prescribed row degrees, so it is a submodule of a
finite free O-module; a DVR is Noetherian. It follows that
  G_j(b)<=rank_O M_j(b).                                  (GP12.1)
Equations (GP7.3) and (GP11.2) give, for every admissible intermediate
degree choice b and every residue vector rho,
  sum_e G_j(b[e])
   >=(1-a^(-Ns^2))
      [product_(x in LL_j) binom(b_x+k-1,k-1)]
      [product_(x in W_j) binom((Bw_x-m)_++k-1,k-1)]
      [Q^(r-1) max(0,1-r/(2Y))]^r.                        (GP12.2)
The e sum is exactly that of GP11. These are all clauses of the
source's SegOK/RestSeg, under the original C0=1088. Together with
the separately reconstructed RestLam, RestPad and RestSum, this is
the segment-function input required in RestCount.
