0. SCOPE, STATUS AND WHAT IS NEW

This note reconstructs the actual ProjectionDerivativeEstimate used in
CL9 of v0.4. It follows the substantive proof through MonomialOrderSegments,
not merely the final conjunction/transport wrappers. The principal result
is the construction G=p^N2 diag(g_minus) Pi diag(g_plus) Z, with the
specified signed-chain and link conditions and a controlled nonzero
Fock pairing. The source proof actually chooses its error polynomial R=0.

The elementary derivative expansion, weighted-Fock estimates, marked-column
lifting, composition of projectors, unit conjugations, and final error
cancellation are proved below. Two representation results and one digit
result are shared with the companion v0.5 work:

  IP: the integral signed path-column basis and its prefix adaptation;
  IW4: the universal integral isotypic-projector bound;
  DT: integral binomial-polynomial approximation of factorial digit units.

Their exact required forms are stated below. IP3--IP5 prove the integral
basis, prefix adaptation and column clauses; IW4 proves the universal
projector bound; DT1--DT5 prove the factorial-unit approximation. The
IP2--IP5 and IW invariant/projector arguments have also been independently
read by this note's author. These are companion proofs, not definitions
being relabelled as theorems. The arbitrary unit gamma in a SignedChainData
remains part of the datum and carries its explicit approximation hypothesis;
DT supplies it in the actual factorial application. The incoming assertion
MemAt, putting the selected input polynomial in XX, remains separate from
the Gram theorem and is explicitly retained in DT11.

The exact source ledger accompanies this report. Its statuses describe
the human reconstruction, not Lean axioms. No Lean source was edited, no
local compilation was attempted, and no kernel replay is claimed.

1. DATA AND THE TARGET, WITHOUT PACKAGING [PG1]

Let O be the source complete mixed-characteristic DVR, K its fraction
field, and p>=5 a prime. Normalize v(p)=1. The actual standalone source
allows arbitrary ramification e=v_w(p), where w is a uniformizer; it does
not assume w^2=p. The later Cloud application has that additional relation.

There are J>=1 cyclic segments and r>=1 coordinates per letter. Segment j
has a finite set L_j of base letters, each designated vector or covector,
and r additional vector letters z_(j,m), 0<=m<r. A letter has r coordinate
variables. Prescribe a nonnegative multidegree d_x at every original
letter x. Put d_L,j=sum_(x in L_j)d_x and P_(j,m)=p^(g_(j,m)).

The model ring has the base variables, and three copies of each vector
letter: labels a_(j,m), carries u_(j,m), and tangents tau_(j,m). Tangents
with m=0 are absent. The substitution phi fixes base variables and sends
each coordinate a_(j,m,c) and tau_(j,m,c) to z_(j,m,c), and u_(j,m,c) to
z_(j,m,c)^P_(j,m).

A model monomial x^zeta is good if:
  (a) every label exponent is <P_(j,m);
  (b) its base-letter degrees equal d_x;
  (c) label degree + P_(j,m)*carry degree + tangent degree = d_(j,m);
  (d) its tangent degree at (j,m) is <=d_L,j, and is zero for m=0.
These are exactly the GoodE conditions, with the missing m=0 tangent
represented by exponent zero in the larger temporary model.

For a set S of letters define ord_S(F) as the least Laurent exponent t
after applying a generic conjugate of diag(1,...,1,t) to vector letters
in S and the corresponding dual action to covector letters in S. Set
ord_S(0)=infinity. This order is algebraic, not the p-adic coefficient
valuation. For each j choose strictly nested subsets C_(j,i) of L_j,
i=1,...,n_j, and set

  S_(j,i)=C_(j,i) union {all carries at j-1},
  T_(j,m)=L_j union {original z_(j,m'):m'>m},
  Y_(j,m)=L_j union {all carries at j-1} union {tau_(j,m'):m'>m}.

The link conditions are imposed only for m+2<=r. Their thresholds thetaL
are positive in the source. The original SignedChainData chain also
contains all its r frame letters; the above C removes those frame labels
before shifting them to carries from the preceding segment.

Digit component D_rho(F) means: retain monomials whose exponent at each
coordinate z_(j,m,c) is congruent to rho_(j,m,c) mod P_(j,m), replace those
exponents by their quotients, and rename the resulting coordinates as
carry variables. No label monomial is included in D_rho(F).

The input is a polynomial F of multidegree d with

  b(F)=min_alpha(v(F_alpha)+v_p(alpha!)/2)=0,
  ord_(S_(j,i)) D_rho(F) >= theta_(j,i), for every rho,j,i,
  ord_(T_(j,m)) F >= thetaL_(j,m).

The coefficient operator Gamma is diagonal: (Gamma F)_alpha=gamma(alpha)
F_alpha, where gamma(alpha) is a unit. For every positive integer h,
gamma and gamma^(-1) each have approximations modulo p^h of the form
sum_beta c_beta product_x binom(alpha_x,beta_x), with c_beta integral and
sum beta_x<=6h. This is precisely the ga_dig clause of SignedChainData.

Write

  T=sum_x d_x,
  kappa=sum_x r(log_p(d_x+r)+1),
  eP=r^2(log_p(2T+r)+1),
  Dm=eP sum_j n_j,    Dl=eP J(r-1),
  n0=v_p(product_x d_x!)/2,   E=Dm+Dl+2kappa.

This kappa is the source kp, not Cloud's top/flat comparison exponent.
Assume c>=1,

  c>4(Dm+Dl+3kappa),
  r*d_L,j/P_(j,m)+CH*r*c<=s_(j+1,i),  CH>=26.       (1.1)

Put h=ceil(c), an integer. Since c>=1, c<=h<=2c. Thus (1.1) implies the
same slope inequality with 13rh in place of CH*r*c, and
h>4(Dm+Dl+3kappa). These are the parameters of LiftBoundUniform.

We construct an integral good G satisfying

  ord_(S_(j,i))G>=theta_(j,i)-s_(j,i)-12h,
  ord_(Y_(j,m))G>=thetaL_(j,m)-30h,
  b(phi G)>=n0-kappa,
  v(<F,Gamma(phi G)>)<=n0+Dm+Dl+3kappa.             (1.2)

Every supported monomial has the correct degree after phi, by goodness.
Replacing 12h,30h by 24c,60c gives the exact endpoint constants. Its
remainder R may be zero, so all requested coefficient lower bounds for R
hold automatically. The endpoint upper bound is n0+E+kappa.

2. FACTORIAL NORMS AND INTEGRAL PROJECTORS [PG3]

The coefficient Fock form is <F,H>=sum_alpha F_alpha H_alpha alpha!.
Its elementary nonarchimedean inequality is

  v(<F,H>)>=b(F)+b(H).                             (2.1)

Legendre's factorial formula, or the count of carries, gives for a
multinomial of total degree d in r coordinates

  0<=v_p(d!/product alpha_a!)<=r floor(log_p d),

with the right side zero at d=0. Consequently, for exponent alpha of
multidegree d,

  2n0-kappa<=v_p(alpha!)<=2n0.                     (2.2)

The deliberately larger kappa makes this valid uniformly including
d=0. In particular, coefficient valuations >=u imply
b(F)>=u+n0-kappa/2, whereas b(F)>=u implies coefficient valuations
>=u-n0. For a split alpha=A+M of multidegree d, apply the same estimate
and the binomial-factorial identity to obtain

  v_p(A!)+v_p(M!)>=2n0-2kappa.                     (2.3)

The weighted lattice B_d={F of multidegree d:b(F)>=0} is full and finite
free over O. For arbitrary ramification e, a monomial basis is
w^(-floor(e*v_p(alpha!)/2)) x^alpha. It is torus-graded. For a vector
letter, the divided root operator E_ab^[q] sends x^alpha to
binom(alpha_b,q) x^(alpha+q e_a-q e_b). The change of its weighted
coefficient valuation is

  [v_p binom(alpha_b,q)+v_p binom(alpha_a+q,q)]/2>=0.

For a covector the root is the signed transpose, giving the same formula
with a,b exchanged. The divided power of a sum over letters is the sum
of products of the divided powers on the separate letters. Hence B_d is
stable under every integral root divided power. Diagonal binomial
operators act by integers, even at negative integral weights. This is the
actual lattice hypothesis needed for IW4; no square root of p or integral
semisimplicity is being assumed.

Here is the exact application of IW4. On the S-letter representation,
each coordinate weight lies in [-D_S,D_S], where D_S=sum_(x in S)d_x.
Thus the difference of the SAME coordinate in any TWO weights has
absolute value <=2D_S. IW4 implies that every isotypic projector e_lambda
and every sum of such projectors satisfies

  p^A e_lambda B_d subset B_d,
  A=binom(r,2) floor(log_p(2D_S+r-1)),             (2.4)

with the source Nat.log convention at zero. The parameter in (2.4)
bounds coordinates of Hom weights; it is not a bound for differences of
two coordinates within one Hom weight.

For completeness, ord_S on a nonzero isotypic component of highest weight
lambda is lambda_r. Indeed all coordinate weights are >=lambda_r, while
the weight lambda_r occurs; the orbit of a nonzero vector spans an
irreducible factor, so its projection to that lowest-coordinate weight
is nonzero after a generic change of basis. Apply a multiplicity-space
linear functional to reduce the isotypic case to the irreducible case.
Different isotypic components cannot cancel in their direct sum.
Thus the cutoff ord_S>=theta is the sum of the components with
lambda_r>=ceil(theta).

Its projector pi is Fock self-adjoint. The root actions satisfy
E_ab^*=E_ba for the coefficient Fock form. Equivalently, extend the
rational polynomial model to C and use its positive Fock Hermitian form:
inequivalent unitary isotypic components are orthogonal. The resulting
identities are rational and descend to K. This uses ordinary
characteristic-zero representation theory, not a positivity assertion
about a p-adic field.

For S subset T, pi_S commutes with the T-action: E_T=E_S+E_(T\S), and pi_S
commutes with both terms. Consequently it commutes with the T-isotypic
projectors. For disjoint S,T the actions commute directly. It also
commutes with every coordinate Euler operator on a letter outside S.

To get a bound for arbitrary F from (2.4), scale F by p^ceil(-b(F)). The
scaled polynomial lies in B_d. Undoing the scalar gives

  b(pi F)>=b(F)-A-1>=b(F)-eP.                    (2.5)

The final inequality follows from D_S<=T and the definition of eP.
Zero polynomials cause no difficulty. The extra one avoids a false claim
that b(F) itself is always attained in the value group. A product of t
commuting cutoff projectors therefore loses at most t eP, fixes every
polynomial satisfying all the cutoffs, and preserves any further cutoff
whose letter set is nested with or disjoint from every set in the product.
These facts prove the source prodProj properties by induction.

3. THE DERIVATIVE CONGRUENCE FROM A GENERATING FUNCTION [PG2]

Let P=p^g, 0<=rho<P, and put

  a_l=[z^M](1+z)^rho ((1+z)^P-1)^l.

Expansion of (1+((1+z)^P-1))^beta gives the exact finite identity

  binom(rho+P beta,M)=sum_(0<=l<=M) a_l binom(beta,l).   (3.1)

The coefficient a_l vanishes if l>M or rho+Pl<M. In characteristic p,
(1+z)^P=1+z^P, so over Z we have (1+z)^P-1=z^P+pQ(z), with Q integral.
In a contribution to [z^M] of its l-th power, at most floor(M/P) factors
can contribute z^P. All remaining factors contribute pQ. Therefore

  p^(max(0,l-floor(M/P))) divides a_l.              (3.2)

For several coordinates take products of (3.1). Retain only indices l
with M_a<=rho_a+P l_a for all a and
sum_a max(0,l_a-floor(M_a/P))<h. Every discarded term is zero or divisible
by p^h, uniformly for all beta. Every retained index satisfies

  |l|<=|M|/P+h.                                    (3.3)

This proves the precise multivariable coefficient congruence. To read it
as a differential operator, split rho+Pl-M=rho'+P delta coordinatewise,
with 0<=rho'<P. Its l-term is

  a_l x^rho' (x^P)^delta partial^[l](x^beta)|_(x -> x^P).

If beta has a coordinate smaller than l, its binomial coefficient and
divided derivative are both zero. Thus there is no illegal negative
exponent. The remainder has coefficients divisible by p^h for every
monomial input. This is the source DER statement with integral
coefficients and derivative cost (3.3). The proof actually works for every
prime p, although we preserve p>=5 in the surrounding theorem.

4. ORDER CALCULUS AND BINOMIAL DIAGONAL OPERATORS [PG3,PG7]

The elementary V2 rules can be seen directly from the generic matrix
defining ord_S. Its vector entries have t-order >=0 and its inverse entries
have t-order >=-1. The covector uses the inverse-transpose. Thus multiplying
by a vector coordinate never lowers order; multiplying by a covector
coordinate lowers it by at most one. The chain rule uses the inverse
matrix: divided differentiation at a vector letter by order q loses at
most q, while differentiation at a covector letter loses no order. Using
divided derivatives changes only scalar binomial factors and not t-orders.
These rules also follow at once by introducing Taylor variables and
comparing their coefficients.

Adding vector letters to S cannot lower ord_S: apply their matrices,
which have nonnegative t-orders, to the coefficients of the previous
substitution. Removing vector letters of total fixed degree d loses at
most d, by using the inverse matrices. Addition takes minimum lower
bounds, multiplication adds them, and extracting coefficients in letters
outside S preserves an order bound. Individual multidegree components
also preserve it, because the substitution preserves every letter degree.

The diagonal operation multiplying coefficient alpha by binom(alpha_x,q)
equals x^q partial_x^[q]. Whether x is a vector or covector coordinate,
the preceding bounds give a loss at most q. Hence an integral binomial
polynomial in the exponents of total degree b has order loss at most b.
Products add such degree budgets; sums take their maximum.

Affine substitution alpha=rho+P beta does not increase that degree or
destroy integral binomial coefficients. Indeed binom(rho+P n,q) is a
degree-q polynomial integral at every nonnegative integer. Its Newton
coefficients are its finite differences at zero, hence are integers,
and vanish above q. Apply this coordinatewise. These observations
justify every 6h or 12h order loss used below without an operator-word
denominator assumption.

5. THE FIRST DETECTING TEST [PG4]

Choose alpha with v(F_alpha)+v_p(alpha!)/2=0 and write a0=v_p(alpha!)/2.
Let Pi_l be the product of the original link cutoff projectors. Their
sets T_(j,m) are nested within a segment and disjoint between segments.
There are at most J(r-1) projectors. Since F satisfies all the link
conditions, H0=Pi_l(x^alpha) satisfies

  <F,H0>=F_alpha alpha!,   v(<F,H0>)=a0,
  b(H0)>=a0-Dl,
  ord_(T_(j,m))H0>=thetaL_(j,m).                   (5.1)

Choose an integral binomial-polynomial approximation g to gamma^(-1)
modulo p^h with degree<=6h. Then Gamma diag(g)H0=H0+diag(epsilon)H0,
where every epsilon coefficient has valuation>=h. By (2.1) the error in
pairing with F has valuation>=h+a0-Dl>a0. Thus the new pairing still has
valuation a0. The integral diagonal operation cannot lower b, and loses
at most 6h from each link order.

Set N1=ceil(n0+Dl-a0), which is nonnegative since a0<=n0. The polynomial
H=p^N1 diag(g)H0 has integral coefficients: their valuations before the
scaling are >=a0-Dl-n0. Its pairing with Gamma F, equivalently with F and
Gamma H, has valuation

  a0+N1<n0+Dl+1,

and its links are at least thetaL-6h. This is the actual source StepB,
with no nondetection or cancellation choice hidden in its conclusion.

6. THE MARKED-COLUMN LIFT AND DIGIT SPLIT [PG5]

The exact IP input is the following signed path-column basis. In any
ordered list of letters of fixed degrees, there is an integral basis
Psi_b=product_k C_bk, whose columns are multilinear on their occurrence
sets. The number of columns containing letter i equals d_i. The basis is
adapted simultaneously to every prefix order cutoff: an integral
polynomial satisfying cutoff theta_i has an integral expansion supported
on paths with last weights lambda_b,i,r>=theta_i.

A column has integer index q and prefix height H(i), with

  colVal(q,H,r)=1_(q>=1,H=r)-1_(q<=0,H<r),
  ord_prefix(C)>=colVal,
  lambda_b,i,r=sum_k colVal(q_k,H_k(i),r).

For q>=1, H(i) is at most the number of positive-vector occurrences of
that column preceding i. These are the exact SPAN clauses used here.

For segment j order the base letters first, then frames in decreasing
index m=r-1,...,0. Thus T_(j,m) is a prefix. A column is marked when it
contains any base letter. In a marked column rename each frame m>0 as
the corresponding tangent; retain frame m=0 as a label. In an unmarked
column leave all frames as labels. Base variables are unchanged.

This operation has four properties.

First, substituting tangent=label=z restores each original column and
therefore the original polynomial. Second, every tangent letter occurs
at most once per marked column, and there are at most d_L,j marked columns:
each marked column accounts for at least one occurrence of a base letter,
whose total number is d_L,j. Thus the tangent-degree cap is obtained by
construction. No tangent of index zero is introduced.

Third, the link orders are preserved. In a marked column the renamed
link has exactly the same prefix preimage, so the column order bound is
unchanged. In an unmarked column no variable belongs to the renamed link,
so its order is >=0. Its old colVal is <=0: if q>=1, the positive
occurrences before the cut are among fewer than r frame letters, so
H<r; if q<=0, the definition already gives colVal<=0. Sum the column
bounds and use prefix adaptation of the integral expansion of H.

Fourth, integrality is preserved even without separately assuming every
column integral. The Gauss coefficient valuation satisfies
v_G(product C_k)=sum v_G(C_k), and injective variable renaming preserves
v_G. Hence the renamed product has the same nonnegative Gauss valuation
as Psi_b. The multiplicativity is elementary: reduce normalized factors
modulo the maximal ideal, or choose the first minimum-valuation monomial
in a monomial order. Products of their nonzero leading terms cannot
cancel. The integral basis coefficients then preserve integrality of the
whole lift.

Perform this operation segment by segment, coefficientwise in the other
letters. Other segments' orders are preserved because their substitutions
act on disjoint variables. Finally write every label exponent as
rho+P beta, introduce its carry monomial u^beta and retain a^rho.
The substitution phi is unchanged. Coefficient extraction in labels
preserves the link orders, and all new carry variables are positive
vectors, so their insertion into Y_(j,m) cannot lower those orders.
We obtain an integral good Z with phi Z=H and link orders thetaL-6h.
This proves StepC from the explicitly stated IP basis, rather than
retaining a separate mysterious lifting theorem.

7. THE CHAIN PROJECTOR AND ITS COEFFICIENT COST [PG6]

Let Pi be the product of the cutoffs ord_(S_(j,i))>=theta_(j,i)-s_(j,i),
defined separately on every model multidegree and extended linearly.
Within a segment these chain sets are nested; between segments they
are disjoint. Each is contained in its segment's Y-set and disjoint
from all other segments' Y-sets. Consequently Pi preserves all link
orders and has image in all the weakened chain conditions.

All labels and tangents lie outside every chain set. Since Pi commutes
with their individual coordinate Euler operators, a monomial can only
map to monomials with exactly the same label and tangent exponents.
Its individual letter degrees are also unchanged. Call this a fixed
label/tangent block. It follows directly that Pi preserves goodness.

For a good x^zeta, the sum of model letter degrees is at most T, so (2.5)
gives

  b(Pi x^zeta)>=v_p(zeta!)/2-Dm.                  (7.1)

If zeta' occurs in Pi x^zeta, the label and tangent factorials cancel in
zeta'!/zeta!. The remaining base and carry letters have fixed degrees,
bounded by their original degrees. Legendre's multinomial bound gives

  |v_p(zeta'!)-v_p(zeta!)|<=kappa.                (7.2)

Thus every coefficient of Pi x^zeta has valuation>=-Dm-kappa/2.
For any integral good Z this remains true by linearity and the
ultrametric inequality. Scaling by p^N2, N2=ceil(Dm+kappa/2), clears all
these denominators. Also, for every model polynomial A of the same
multidegree satisfying all weakened chains,

  <A,Pi x^zeta>=<A,x^zeta>.                      (7.3)

This is the cancellation identity used below.

8. DIAGONAL FACTORIAL CORRECTIONS [PG8]

For good zeta write its label exponents rho, carry exponents beta, and
tangent exponent M. Let A be the original exponent formed from its base
exponents and rho+P beta, so phi(x^zeta)=x^(A+M). The digit factorial
identity is

  A! M! = C_B gamma_dig(zeta) zeta!,
  C_B=product_t (P_t!)^(|beta_t|),                (8.1)

where gamma_dig is a p-adic unit. This explicit C_B is constant on a fixed
label/tangent block. The source's more general kapC formula is the same
quantity after inserting its explicit digit constants.

By DT, gamma_dig and its inverse admit integral binomial-polynomial
approximations modulo p^h of total degree at most 6h (DT proves a stronger
bound, but we preserve the pinned constants). By the input gamma
hypothesis and the affine-binomial argument in Section 4, gamma(A) and
its inverse also have cost at most 6h on each fixed label block. Hence

  gamma_*(zeta)=gamma(A) gamma_dig(zeta)

has integral diagonal approximations g_plus,g_minus to its two signs
modulo p^h, each costing at most 12h in every order whose set excludes
labels. Treat each label coefficient separately to combine the
approximations into one diagonal operator. On invalid label blocks one
may use the constant 1; only good blocks enter the proof.

Define Omega=diag(g_minus) Pi diag(g_plus). All these operations preserve
goodness. Their coefficient estimates show that p^N2 Omega maps integral
good polynomials to integral good polynomials. The chain orders of Omega Z
are theta-s-12h: Pi resets the chain condition, so only the outer diagonal
operation costs anything there. For a pre-existing link condition of
level theta, the two diagonals cost 12h each and Pi preserves it. Thus
the link level is theta-24h, and for our Z it is thetaL-30h.

9. THE CONJUGATED DERIVATIVE SATISFIES THE WEAKENED CHAINS [PG7]

Let M be supported on the original vector-frame coordinates, with total
degree at each frame t=(j,m) at most d_L,j. Define

  F_M=Gamma^(-1) partial^[M](Gamma F).             (9.1)

We prove that each digit component of F_M is Q+E, where Q satisfies all
chain levels theta-s and every coefficient of E has valuation>=h-n0.
This is not an additional representation assertion.

Initially F has coefficients>=-n0 by (2.2). Replace Gamma by its degree
6h binomial approximation. This loses at most 6h in every digit-chain
order, and introduces error coefficients>=h-n0. For a single frame t,
Section 3 approximates its divided derivative by integral operators on
the corresponding carry, of order<=|M_t|/P_t+h. These only affect chain
sets in segment j+1, the sets containing that carry. Multiplication by
the residual carry monomial is a positive-vector operation and has no
negative order cost. All other chains are unaffected.

Compose over all frames. The errors retain coefficient valuation>=h-n0:
all derivative and approximation operators have integral monomial
coefficients, and the true divided derivatives have integer binomial
coefficients. Finally replace Gamma^(-1) by its own 6h approximation.
The total order loss at a chain in segment j is at most

  12h + sum_(m=0)^(r-1) (|M_(j-1,m)|/P_(j-1,m)+h).    (9.2)

For each m, hypothesis (1.1) in its integer-h form says
r*d_L,j-1/P_(j-1,m)+13rh<=s_(j,i). Divide by r and sum over the r frame
letters. As |M_t|<=d_L,j-1, (9.2) is <=s_(j,i), because
12h+rh<=13rh for r>=1. This proves the claimed Q+E decomposition,
uniformly for every allowed M and every digit class. Extracting digit
components never decreases a coefficient valuation lower bound.

10. THE CENTRAL BLOCK CANCELLATION [PG9]

Put Lambda(W)=<F,Gamma(phi W)>. Fix a good model monomial zeta with
label rho, tangent M, collapsed exponent A, and C_B as in (8.1). An exact
coefficient computation gives

  Lambda(x^zeta)=C_B gamma_*(zeta) (F_M)_A zeta!.  (10.1)

Indeed (F_M)_A=gamma(A)^(-1) gamma(A+M) F_(A+M)
binom(A+M,M); combine binom(A+M,M) A! M!=(A+M)! with (8.1).
Nothing in this identity suppresses a factorial or a factor of two.

Use Section 9 to decompose D_rho(F_M)=Q+E. Multiply Q by the fixed label
and tangent monomial, then take its component of the same individual
model-letter degrees as zeta; call the result A_Q. It satisfies all the
weakened chains. Label/tangent multiplication has nonnegative order,
and homogeneous component extraction preserves order. Therefore (7.3)
applies to A_Q.

For zeta' in the same block, set

  epsilon_(zeta')=g_minus(zeta') gamma_*(zeta') (F_M)_(A')
                    -(A_Q)_(zeta').

Every such epsilon has valuation>=h-n0. To see this, gamma_* is a unit,
g_minus differs from gamma_*^(-1) by valuation>=h, the coefficients of
F_M are >=-n0, and the remaining difference is exactly the corresponding
coefficient of E.

Apply (10.1) to Pi x^zeta and to x^zeta. The main terms are
C_B<A_Q,Pi x^zeta> and C_B<A_Q,x^zeta>, hence cancel. Every remaining
summand is C_B u epsilon_(zeta') zeta'!, where u is the relevant
coefficient of Pi x^zeta or x^zeta. Write a=v_p(zeta!), a'=v_p(zeta'!).
By (7.1), v(u)>=a/2-a'/2-Dm; this also holds for the identity term.
Moreover (8.1), the unit property, and (2.3) give BOTH inequalities

  v(C_B)+a>=2n0-2kappa,
  v(C_B)+a'>=2n0-2kappa.

Averaging these two inequalities yields

  v(C_B u epsilon_(zeta') zeta'!)
    >= h-n0-Dm+v(C_B)+(a+a')/2
    >= h+n0-2kappa-Dm.                            (10.2)

This averaging is a short human simplification of the source's split
according to a<=a' or a'<a. It is the cancellation of the large
factorials which makes the final loss logarithmic.

We have proved the same lower bound for
Lambda(diag(g_minus)Pi x^zeta)-Lambda(diag(g_minus)x^zeta).
To introduce g_plus, use the exact identity

 Lambda(Omega x^zeta)-Lambda(x^zeta)
 =g_plus(zeta)[Lambda(diag(g_minus)Pi x^zeta)
                      -Lambda(diag(g_minus)x^zeta)]
  +(g_plus(zeta)g_minus(zeta)-1)Lambda(x^zeta).

The first coefficient is integral. The second scalar has valuation>=h,
because both approximations are correct modulo p^h. By b(F)>=0 and (2.2),
