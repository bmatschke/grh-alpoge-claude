AC1 — ONE SHIFT CONTROLS EVERY ROOT, NOT ONLY ITS PARITY
For a number field E let w_E=|mu(E)|. This is Units.torsionOrder E. Finiteness
can be seen directly: if z has exact order h, the cyclotomic polynomial gives
phi(h)<=[E:Q], and phi(h)^2>=h/2. Hence h<=2[E:Q]^2. Only finitely many h
occur, and X^h-1 has at most h roots. A finite subgroup of a field's units is
cyclic: combining maximal prime-power-order elements produces an element of
order equal to the group exponent e; all elements are roots of X^e-1, so the
group order is at most e and hence equal to e. This is the same elementary
background justified in SZ10.

Given a finite family of embeddings phi_i:E->F, integer weights a_i, and one
chosen index i0, take a generator zeta of mu(E). The product
  U=product_i phi_i(zeta)^(a_i)
has w_E-th power one. Since phi_i0 is injective, phi_i0(zeta) has exact order
w_E and its powers exhaust every root of X^w_E-1 in F. Select the unique
q in [0,w_E) whose power equals U^(-1). The modified product with exponents
  a_i+q*indicator(i=i0)
is one on zeta and therefore on every power of zeta, which is every root of
unity of E. No division by a possibly zero root occurs. This proves the
source ExistsShiftWeightCongruenceLtTorsionOrder theorem and its host wrapper.

AC2 — PROVE THE NORM-ONE FACT USED BY PARALLEL WEIGHTS
Rather than assume HostDer.norm_mu, prove the stronger elementary statement:
if d>3 is prime, E contains s with s^2=-d, and 4 divides [E:Q], then every
root of unity has norm one from E to K=Q(s).

The basis 1,s of K shows that a rational number which is a square in K is
either a rational square or -d times a rational square. Consequently -1,-2,
-3 and 2 are not squares in K. For the negative numbers the first case is
impossible by sign; the second would say c=d*a^2 with 1<=c<=3 and a rational.
The d-adic exponent of the right side is odd while that of c is zero. For
2, sign excludes the second case and irrationality of sqrt(2) excludes the
first.

K has no nontrivial root of odd order. Otherwise an odd prime divisor p of
that order gives a primitive p-th root whose cyclotomic degree p-1 is at
most [K:Q]=2. Thus p=3, but a primitive cube root v makes (2v+1)^2=-3.

Now let y have 2-power order. If y^2=1, then y=+/-1 and its E/K norm is one
because [E:K] is even. Otherwise choose a power i of y with i^2=-1 and put
K1=K(i), a quadratic extension. The element v=N_{E/K1}(y) has 2-power order.
If its order exceeded four, a power u would satisfy u^4=-1. Then
(u-u^3)^2=2. Writing u-u^3=a+b*i shows a*b=0; hence either 2 or -2 would be
a square in K, impossible. Thus v belongs to {1,-1,i,-i}. Each has norm one
from K1 to K, so norm transitivity yields N_{E/K}(y)=1. For arbitrary z,
write a killing exponent as 2^a*o with o odd. Applying this result to z^o
gives N(z)^o=1, and the odd-root result in K gives N(z)=1.

This follows the separate literal source NormRootOfUnityQuadraticSubfield,
including the key 2-primary argument. It avoids treating a host-derived norm
field or a class-number result as the proof. General field norm identities,
the quadratic basis criterion, and cyclotomic degree are standard algebraic
background; no analytic existence is invoked.

AC8 — ACTUAL INTEGRAL NORM-FORM EQUIVALENCE
NormOne(d,M,p) is the assertion of integers A,B with
  A^2+d B^2=4p, M|B, 2M|(A-2-B).
With d=3 mod4 let e=(d+1)/4 and omega=(1+sqrt(-d))/2; omega is integral
because omega^2-omega+e=0. Write B=M*u and A=2+M*u+2M*v. Then
  pi=1+M(v+u*omega)=(A+B*sqrt(-d))/2
is in O_E cap K, has pi*bar(pi)=p, and is 1 modulo M O_E. Expansion gives
  p-1=M[u+2v+M(eu^2+uv+v^2)],
so the integer prime is also 1 modulo M. This is the exact algebraic
content consumed by normOne_host.

For the converse take pi=1+M*y, where y is integral and M>0. Since pi is in
K, so is y. Write y=a+b*sqrt(-d) with rational a,b. Its rational trace and
norm are integral, so t=2a is integral and d(2b)^2 is integral. If 2b=u/v in
lowest terms, v^2 divides d; since d is squarefree v=1. Thus 2b=u is an
integer. The identity t^2+d*u^2=4N(y) forces t,u to have the same parity.
Set A=2+M*t, B=M*u and take norms; the exact NormOne congruences follow.
For M=0 both sides force pi=1,p=1 and the solution A=2,B=0 works. Divisors
M' of M preserve both integer divisibility conditions. This proves the
source equivalence and its weaker-modulus corollary as well.
