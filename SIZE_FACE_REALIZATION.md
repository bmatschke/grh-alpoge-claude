# A linked bundle at every prescribed size on the weighted point

Fix the original geometric parameter of S77C2, including its strengthened version with the extra twist condition. Work under the published inputs of R73P5 and the local Langlands input of that parameter. In the eventual range of contexts in CSR22, let $q$ be **any** of the parameter's weighted points and $R$ **any** reciprocity datum with $\mathrm{RecPinE}(q,R)$. Put $D=q.w.D(q.Z)$. For each real $c$ and each pair $(\Delta,h_0)\in\mathbb N^2$ with $D.s.\mathrm{Del}=\Delta$ and $q.O.L.\mathrm{etaSz}=D.R.m\,h_0$, impose the original scale equality $q.O.L.\mathrm{sc}=K.s$ and valuation condition $\mathrm{Val}(K.C,K.c_D,K.c_h,q.O.L)$. Then there is a bundle $b$ of size $c$ on $(D,q.O)$ satisfying
$$
                 b.R=R,\qquad \mathrm{BWit}(q,b)\text{ is inhabited}.
$$
This is the original $\mathrm{BunU}$ implication. Combined with the [fixed reciprocity datum](COMMON_DESIGN_EXISTENCE.md), it supplies the linked bundle for every prescribed **good** point in S77C5. Producing good points throughout the required family remains a separate assertion. ## Division compatibility on the updated face

CSR13 used the first output of the division comparison. Here is its construction on $R',F',Y',Z'$. At either the sharp or the base level, take a level vector $v$, and let $v_Q$ be the vector read back from its quotient coordinates $P_qv$. Both lie in the rational level space, and $v-v_Q$ is orthogonal to the coordinate subspace by the defining quotient pairing. The theta image of their difference lies in the level target by the downward level comparison. For any vector $u$ in that target, the upward level comparison and the rational range identity put $\Theta^*u$ in the complex span of the quotient coordinates. By adjointness $\langle u,\Theta(v-v_Q)\rangle=0$. Taking $u=\Theta(v-v_Q)$ gives $\Theta v=\Theta v_Q$. The composite row map at each level is the same theta map on the good projection. Indeed the successive inner theta steps preserve the level, the final step lands at its prescribed level, and the row projector agrees there with the good projector. The maps reading the two level targets into their Hilbert models are injective. Applying this argument at each level yields the two required identities for $\iota$, with the respective maps $j_A,j_{A,0}$ and their own $P_q$. The form and lattice clauses of both row definitions are already fields of $Y'$. [Projection proof][proj] · [Successive theta steps at both levels][composite] · [The two row identities][rows]

For the lift-space clause, let $J$ be the updated lift coordinate map $j_W$, and $E$ the embedding $Y'.\mathrm{mj}.eW$ at the chosen step. The norm identity with positive level factor makes $E$ injective. By the reading, the images $EJ(e_a)$ are the prescribed good forms. Their independence and spanning give independence and spanning of the $J(e_a)$ in the native lift space, using the equality of the good-level range with the image of $E$. For the integral comparison the auxiliary integral model supplies a lattice in the rational form space. Its good part is cut out by the base level character and the good projector. The coefficient-field pins put the twist character, level character and projector coefficients in the prescribed field. Integral descent identifies the coordinate lattice as the integral span of the old integral points in that good part after scalar extension. Under $E$, the native lift lattice reads as precisely those embedded old integral points. Generating with the target's ring of integers and taking additive spans on the two sides now gives the same subgroup: multiplying an integral linear combination by an allowed coefficient still gives an integral linear combination. Pull back along $E$. This is the lattice comparison in the lift-space clause of $\mathrm{DivDef3}$. ## Eigensystems and the scalar at every embedding

On the new face, both stored series identifications on $q$ still give the local series projectors of its distinguished primes. The group and level character identifications in the updated reading carry the good central characters to the same product of generic characters as in R73P1. For each good $\chi$, the coefficient at the identity of $e_\chi e_{\psi_0}$ is $\chi(1)/|G|$. It is nonzero in the coefficient field. This proves the updated genericity input. [Finite character calculation](continuation_v73/reading.tex#L3-L39) · [Specialization to the new face][generic]

At the reference tuple the conductor set is empty, so the excluded places for the spherical operators are exactly the marked set specified in the row. For each occurring type $\pi$, use the first lift at the base level. Genericity gives a nonzero level vector there. At each allowed prime, the spherical operator of a given double coset acts by a scalar on the type's hyperspecial-fixed vectors. Different choices of representatives give the same sum on these vectors, by hyperspecial invariance. This gives a single eigenvalue system $\alpha_\pi$, including the source's value zero for an absent operator. The identification of the occurring type is the one supplied by $Y'$. [The base-level specialization][eig] · [Existence, representative independence and scalars][sphere]

A nonzero occurring row vector of this system lies in the span of the labelled row parts by the primitive decomposition. Each such part is a sum of simultaneous eigenspaces of systems carried by its label. A joint eigenvector in this span lies in a system with its own operator values. Indeed each vector is a finite sum, and a product of linear factors in distinguishing operators isolates its character on that sum. The model embedding is injective, so this gives a carrier label $p$ of $\alpha_\pi$. For every such carrier, the principal-level type map places the native $\pi$-subspace at each step in the full $\alpha_\pi$-eigenspace, hence in the sum $W'_{i,p}$ for that carrier. The base and lift polynomial labels also agree at every allowed split prime by Hecke equivariance and injectivity of the adjoint on the nonzero part. Restricting the full eigenspace to these split operators gives the two projected part containments required in CSR19. These comparisons keep the same carrier on the row and lift sides. [A carrier from the row vector][carrier] · [Containment for every lift step][iso] · [Base part][pbase] · [Lift part][plift]

It remains to identify the scalar at every embedding. Write $I_{i,\pi}=D.\mathrm{isot}(i+1,\pi)$, let $p$ be any label such that $I_{i,\pi}\subseteq(E'_i)^{-1}W'_{i,p}$, and suppose $I_{i,\pi}\ne0$. At the distinguished embedding the lifted theta and its adjoint on this part give the operator with eigenvalue $\lambda'_{i,\mu_0,p,v_0}$. Cuspidal decay and level equivariance put the adjoint vector back in the base level. Pairing it against every source vector using the tied theta reading identifies it with the native adjoint. The original eigenvalue characterization on the nonzero type part thus gives $\mathrm{lamP}(\mu_0,i+1,\pi)=\lambda'_{i,\mu_0,p,v_0}$. Taking logarithms gives the base formula by $q.O.X.\mathrm{logLam\_base}$. [Adjoint and eigenvalue comparison][base]

For any other coefficient embedding $e$, first pull the containment back to the original row using the equality $E'_i=E_i$ and the equality of the part spaces under the scalar update. In that row the complex span of the labelled rational lift vectors equals $E_i^{-1}W_{i,p}$. For this equality the rational space maps into the intersection of the complex label space with the level range. The dimension comparison for its injective coordinate map gives the reverse containment. Thus a label carrying the finite type gives its rational containment in each lift degree, as required by the old scalar reading $q.x.\mathrm{ox}.\mathrm{lam}$. Let $d$ be the label that reading assigns to $\pi$. The nonzero type part under consideration lies over both $p$ and $d$. Distinct labels have disjoint lifted joint eigenspaces by separation of their operator data, and $E_i$ is injective. It follows that $p=d$. Moreover the old row space at $p$ is nonzero. Otherwise every system carried by $p$ would have zero row eigenspace, contrary to the definition of carrying, and its lift-part sum would be zero as well. Injectivity and $I_{i,\pi}\ne0$ exclude this. On this nonzero label the scalar-update identity from CSR11 applies even when $e$ and the distinguished embedding induce the same infinite place. The old scalar formula therefore yields
$$
 q.O.X.\mathrm{logLam}(i+1,\pi,e)
       =\log\lambda'_{i,\mu_0,p,[e]}.
$$
This proves CSR.40 with its guard and for the designated embedding map, including its conjugate. [Rational span and dimension comparison][span] · [Existence of a type label][typelabel] · [Disjointness under the reading][unique] · [Use of the original scalar and transport to the new face][off]

Take the finite image of $\pi\mapsto\alpha_\pi$ and select one carrier for each image value. Two types with the same eigenvalue system have that common carrier. The formula just proved, applied twice at every step at which both parts are nonzero, proves the required constancy of their logarithms. The two part containments and the tuple-character transport give CSR19 on $y$. The selected-prime comparisons of CSR20 use the host datum already constructed on $R$. Finally CSR21 transports the realization on the face, period and scale together and uses $R$ as the first of the seven bundle fields. All fields of the witness use this one bundle and the originally specified columns. The only eventual frame cutoff was taken before $q,R,c$, which gives the claimed $\mathrm{BunU}$ quantifiers. At a good point, S77C5 transports the whole bundle by the equality of its point with $(D,q.O)$, obtaining the original linked-existence implication.
