# A common starting column for the first pass

The first pass in the construction of a good arithmetic point starts with a coefficient field, a frame and untwisted data. The marked finite places, the coefficient field for the theta derivatives, and the local character tuples must all occur on one column. The eventual existence of this starting column was an input in [P78C7](continuation_v78/producer.tex#L218-L276). It follows from the mass and window estimates already reconstructed, together with the canonical column theorem.

**Proposition.** In the original eventual range, impose the two finite field requests described below. For every base and frame satisfying the source's exact small-field and frame condition `KPx` and its forced-prime condition, and every admitted twist datum, there is a column with all the fields of `ColPin`. For untwisted input this column satisfies `Start`. In particular the pinned-column existence assumption in the first pass of P78C7 is discharged. The statement is relative to the admissible bases in the original eventual quantifier. The third- and fourth-stage records used to convert a starting column into a good point have additional requirements. ## How the mass ratio supplies the ranks and primes needed for a column

Write $x$ for the scale, $L$ for its logarithmic window parameter, $B=\exp L$, $m$ for the chain length, $N_m$ for the last rank, and $f=[E^+:\mathbf Q]$. The standard unitary mass formula of Gan, Hanke and Yu at the unramified CM host gives the successive-rank ratio with the full integral stabilizer. The [mass comparison](REMARK_NORMS.md#a-short-literature-proof-of-the-successive-rank-mass-ratio) verifies its local factors and functional-equation normalization. Inserting that ratio in QW3–QW9 gives, for every word $u$, the lower bound $mL$ for the logarithm of the lower endpoint of its type-prime window. The chosen prime $T_u$ therefore satisfies $T_u\ge\exp(mL)\ge B^2$. This is the application of item 9 of Alpöge's remark here. [Gan–Hanke–Yu, §4, Propositions 4.4–4.5](https://web.math.princeton.edu/~jonhanke/Web-02/Mass-Formula-of-Shimura/shimura-mass.pdf#page=9) · [Window with its original quantifiers](quantitative_windows/main.tex#L332-L366)

Take the auxiliary size $A=B^2/5$. Eventually $f\le x^3$, $N_m\le x^{402}$, and $B\ge x^{406}$. The marked primes have $g_i\ge(kB)^8$. Hence $2fk<g_i-1$ and $2fN_m+1<4A<T_u$ for every $i,u$. There is also one bound for the spread of every weight. If $U_0$ is its first top entry and $e_{m-1}$ its last endpoint parameter, monotonicity of the unshifted weights and the half-sum shift give
$$
     \operatorname{spread}(\lambda_{u,j,\tau})\le U_0+e_{m-1}+N_m .
$$
The place inequalities give $e_{m-1}\le 2B$. In their first alternative $U_0\le 2xB$. In the other, $U_0\le 3k(4\Delta)$ and $k(4\Delta)\le b_0/x\le 2B/x$, where $b_0$ is the first lower-block entry. Thus the right hand side is at most $2A$ for large $x$, at every place and step. Every word has a different coded weight since a changed bit changes an entry by the nonzero spacing $\Delta$. The host gives the norm factors for $g_i$, the factors of $T_u$ with their congruences, splitting at the two auxiliary twisting primes, the unit ideal condition, and the required parity. Together these give all the preliminary data called `PreX`. Taking maxima of the finitely many scale thresholds after the window parameters are fixed preserves the order in QW9. ## One choice of halves at every residue prime

Let $K$ be the distinguished imaginary quadratic subfield of $E$ and $F=E^+$. For each rational prime $p$, choose one prime $\mathfrak p(p)$ of $K$ above it. For every $v\mid p$ of $F$, choose $w$ of $E$ over $v$. If $w$ has the wrong contraction to $K$, replace it by $\bar w$. Conjugation fixes $F$, and the only case in which a different contraction exists is when $p$ splits in $K$ and the two primes are conjugate. Thus $P_v$ obtained this way lies above $v$ and has contraction $\mathfrak p(p)$ for every $v\mid p$. Restrict this same selector to the two special twisting primes to obtain both of the required marked selections. This resolves their simultaneous agreement even when several $F$-places have the same rational residue characteristic. Separately, for the two $g_i$ use the primes specified by the given frame. The conjugate factors of a norm-$g_i$ generator in $K$ generate primes of norm $g_i$. Choosing the factor or its conjugate orients its conjugate ideal at the prescribed prime and preserves its integer congruence. ## Coefficients before the column is chosen

The first field request contains a generator of the common frame-value field for the indicated coefficients. The second, which may depend on the finite allowed character set $\Xi$, includes the period-field generators, the two marked square roots, the finite character and residue roots and the generators for the normalized jets of CA12. More explicitly, for a character of order $d$ include $\zeta_d$ and, for the absolute norm $q$ of each ramified prime, $\sqrt q$ and $\zeta_{q^{2m}}$. If a prime $P$ is selected by a tuple, its underlying $F$-unit subgroup is detected by that tuple. Some prime $P'$ above the same $F$-place must then be ramified for the corresponding character, since otherwise every local unit factor would be trivial. The degree-one assertions for these places give $N P=N P'$. The requested higher root thus supplies the required primitive root of order $N P$ in the **given** embedded field. Every unramified character value lies in it as a power of $\zeta_d$. [Field witnesses and residue norm equality](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/IwasawaDecomposition/ExistsColumnInputAssembled.lean#L126-L193)

The normalized derivatives involve infinitely many potential lattice representatives. For each realizable choice of word, step and bad prime sets, CA11 puts the allowed normalized values in one number field. CA12 forms the union over the indices for which that field property holds and verifies that every actual step is included. There are finitely many words and steps. The prime sets range over subsets of the primes above the word and marked primes and the ramification of $\Xi$, hence over a finite collection. Take the union of finite generating sets for the realizable jet configurations as in CA12. Membership in the field is invariant under the canonical choice of the ambient model and uses the actual modulus and total degree. Consequently this second request applies before a particular lattice or column is chosen. Both requests are finite algebraic sets, and the extra prime and avoidance requests for this step can be empty. Apply CA13 to this selector, this preliminary datum and the given frame. Its required inclusion of the reference-frame vectors is exactly the second clause of `KPx`. Its type generators are the ones from the context, so $c_u\pi_T(u)=\pi_T(u_0)$ for every word. Its character range is $\Xi$, its excluded set is the prescribed one, and its local index records, normalized jets and laws are on the same column. At step zero the source bijects decorated classes, preserving their lattice decorations. Stabilizers are trivial there, so reindexing functions by this bijection identifies both the rational functions and their integral sublattices, and commutes with their embeddings. [Step-zero identification](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/IwasawaDecomposition/FormsEquiv.lean#L20-L71)

Keep the field record witnessing the first clause of `KPx`, including equality with the prescribed small field. CA13 gives the period-field and frame-value comparisons on all later assemblies. These data form the original column, and the prime and field arguments above give its complete pin. For untwisted input the support alternative holds by definition. All place assignments in its coherence test are constant and the root needed there has order $p^0=1$. This gives the required starting column. The next first-pass operation uses this particular column to produce the third-stage record. The second pass uses the enlarged base supplied by retwisting.
