NOTATION AND EXACT ROLE OF r
The edge rank is 1. The ambient vector dimension is k=m+1, while the segment
polynomials used after splitting off the edge coordinate have r=m coordinates.
Thus r in the gamma calculation below is NOT the edge rank. The source assumes
2*N1+2<=m; this is a genuinely high-dimensional polynomial construction even for
rank-one edges. In the formulas below v=v_p is normalized by v(p)=1. For the
ramified coefficient DVR O the chosen uniformizer w satisfies w^2=p, so v(w)=1/2.
Fock means the bilinear, not Hermitian, form B(x^alpha,x^beta)=1_{alpha=beta} alpha!.

TI1. THE ACTUAL INTEGRAL SEGMENT FAMILY

Source: GrowthBounds/ObjSeg.lean, BlockData.gens/SSZ, blockData, specConst,
bbSpec/blockLat, segLat/segForm (exact ranges in provenance.json).

For a signed chain x_0,...,x_{N-1} of r-dimensional rows, a tie joins adjacent
vector/covector rows at positions a_i,a_i+1. Add scalar pairs Z_i,S_i and an
auxiliary r-dimensional covector xi. The actual invariant contractions are

  E_i = Z_i S_i + sum_c x_{a_i,c} x_{a_i+1,c},
  E_+ = Z_+ S_+ + sum_c x_{u,c} xi_c.

For legal nonnegative levels j_i,j_+ the generators are

  (product_i E_i^{j_i}) E_+^{j_+}
  (product_i Z_i^{z_i}) Z_+^{z_+} f(x) g(xi),                 (TI1.1)

where f lies in the integral signed-prefix space of the residual row degrees,
and g has degree B-j_+. Legality means each removed level fits both tied row
degrees and j_+<=min(d_u,B). The prefix spaces impose the source's largest
GL-stable first-coordinate weight cutoffs, not merely a monomial inequality.
SSZ is the O-span of exactly these generators.

For a segment with s odd sites and sigma early sites, P_i=p^{G-d_i} and Q=p^G.
The digit rows rz_i,ry_i have r+1 coordinates and entries in [0,P_i). Write
  A_i=(b_i-sum rz_i)/P_i, C_i=(a_i-sum ry_i)/P_i,
  oEff_i=w_i+sg_i (i<sigma), o_i (i>=sigma),
  A0=floor((a-E0)/Q), cap(l)=A0+sum_{i<l}(A_i-oEff_i).
The divisibilities defining A_i,C_i are part of DigitsOK. The chain vector
degrees are w_i early and o_i late; covector degrees are C_i-sg_i early and
C_i late. Its terminal vector degree is oPlus. Ties occur only at late sites;
their scalar shifts are z_i=A_i-o_i, and zPlus=aPlus-oPlus. The terminal
auxiliary degree is bPlus. The exact retained predicates Kept are:

  i>=sigma: o_i<=A_i, o_i<=cap(i)+Lambda*A_i/(r+1), w_i=sg_i=0;
  i<sigma:  o_i=0, w_i<=A_i, sg_i<=C_i,
             w_i+sg_i<=A_i*(1+4/p^2)+Lambda*cap(i)/(r+1),
             sg_i<=2*Lambda*A0/(r+1);
  oPlus<=cap(s)+(Lambda/(r+1)+4/p^2)*sum_i(2*a/P_i)
                     +Lambda*bPlus/(r+1).

The actual factorial multiplier is sp=product_{i<sigma}(A_i-w_i)! sg_i!.
The block lattice is not SSZ itself. It is

  L = span_K(SSZ) intersect {F: v(F_alpha)+(v(alpha!)+v(sp))/2>=0
                                  for every alpha}.           (TI1.2)

Equivalently w^{v_p(alpha!)+v_p(sp)}F_alpha belongs to O. Blocks are put in
orthogonal direct sum over the retained digit/level data; their form is sp*Fock.
The fixed prefix margin is imposed by lowering the source thresholds before
forming SSZ. These details matter for integral primitivity and cannot be
replaced by the rational polynomial span alone.

TI2. FULL LATTICES, PRIMITIVE TESTS, AND TENSOR PERTURBATIONS

The span in (TI1.2) has bounded total degree: every legal level is bounded and
the rows have fixed degrees. Choose a finite monomial support containing a basis.
The ambient coefficient condition on that support is a free O-module with
basis w^{-v(alpha!)-v(sp)}x^alpha. Its intersection with a rational subspace is
saturated, finite and torsion-free, hence free; clearing finitely many coefficient
denominators shows that it spans the entire rational subspace. For F,G in L,
each term sp*F_alpha*G_alpha*alpha! has nonnegative valuation. Thus the actual
block pairing is integral. This proves the source's finiteness/fullness step.

For any finite free O-lattice L with integral bilinear form, take Smith bases
e_i,f_i with B(e_i,f_j)=delta_ij d_i, including d_i=0 if necessary. For real D,
let g=#{i:v(d_i)<=D}. The span of those e_i is saturated; every primitive
vector u in it has a unit coefficient, and the corresponding f_i tests u with
v(B(u,f_i))<=D. Conversely a saturated sublattice of rank>g has a nonzero
rational vector whose first g Smith coordinates vanish. Clear denominators and
divide out its common uniformizer power inside the saturated lattice. The
result is primitive and pairs with every vector with valuation>D. Therefore
the maximum good rank is exactly g. The source's badShare is (rank L-g)/rank L
(and zero at rank zero). This is an actual integral primitive-test statement.

Tensor good bases from several segments. If maps into the actual two edge
lattices produce a pairing matrix
  M=diag(kappa_beta product_j d_{j,i_j})+w^c R,  R integral,
and every displayed diagonal entry has v<=D<c/2, then M=diag(d)(I+E), where
every entry of E is in the maximal ideal. Thus I+E is unimodular, and these
vectors have full claimed rank and all pairing elementary divisors<=D. This
proves the linear-algebra part of PairLiftAt -> liftVecAt_of. Constructing the
maps with this identity is a separate source input, not supplied by this lemma.

TI3. THE ACTUAL GAMMA POLYNOMIAL: DEGREE AND LEADING FORM

Work over any characteristic-zero field. Let a signed chain have N+1 rows,
row degrees d_i, total S, and last row w of vector degree o. Fix a coordinate
rho, a nonnegative integer scalar shift z, and append a new covector y.
Here r>=1 and every d_i is a nonnegative integer. Define the substitution by

  vector x:   x_rho -> sum_c x_c y_c,   x_c -> y_rho x_c (c!=rho);
  covector x: x_rho -> y_rho x_rho,     x_c -> y_rho x_c-x_rho y_c (c!=rho).

It preserves every old row degree, gives total y-degree S, is integral, and
Fcal(f)|_{y_rho=1,y_c=0(c!=rho)}=f. For n>=S put fr_n(f)=y_rho^{n-S}Fcal(f).
Set
  E=sum_c w_c y_c, F=sum_c partial_{w_c} partial_{y_c}, A=o+n+r+z-1,
  Phi_{o,n,z}=sum_{j=0}^o (-1)^j E^j F^j/[j!^2 binom(A,j)].
The exact scalar used by the source is

  Gamma_{f,g}(n)=binom(A,o) B(Phi_{o,n,z} fr_n(f),fr_n(g))
                       /[(n-S)! S! product_i d_i!].             (TI3.1)

For nonzero f,g, let ord_rho(f)=e, ord_rho(g)=e' be their least powers of
w_rho. The case where either polynomial is zero has Gamma=0 and is immediate.
Then Gamma
extends to a polynomial of degree at most S+o-max(e,e'). If e=e',

 [T^{S+o-e}]Gamma_{f,g}(T)
   = e! binom(z+e,e) B(in_e f,in_e g)/(o! S! product_i d_i!).   (TI3.2)

Here in_e extracts exactly the w_rho-degree e terms. These are precisely the
source's GamDeg and GamLead, with no change of variables or normalization.

Proof, including the cancellation responsible for the low degree.
The commutator [F,E]=r+Euler_w+Euler_y gives the rational harmonic decomposition
of bidegree (o,n) into E^l ker(F) at bidegree (o-l,n-l). To see existence and
directness without an integral semisimplicity assertion, repeatedly subtract
E^l times harmonic pieces, starting with the largest nonzero F-power; on such
a piece F^l E^l is the nonzero scalar l!(o+n-2l+r)_l. Thus induction solves
the decomposition over the characteristic-zero field.

On E^l h with Fh=0, Phi acts by binom(z+l,l)/binom(A,l). The finite-sum identity
follows by multiplying by binom(A,l) and taking the coefficient of X^l in
  (1+X)^A (1-X/(1+X))^{A-z-l}=(1+X)^{z+l}.
Consequently, for e<=min(o,n),
  Phi_{o,n,z} E^e = [(z+1)_e/A_under_e] E^e Phi_{o-e,n-e,z+e},  (TI3.3)
where (z+1)_e is rising and A_under_e falling. All denominators here are
nonzero for n>=S>=o and r>=1.

Write f=w_rho^e h, q=Fcal(h), v=Fcal(g), and m=n-S. Then q has y-degree S-e,
v has y-degree S, and fr_n(f)=E^e y_rho^m q. For fixed q,v and nonnegative
s,j, the function
  H_{s,j}(m)=B(E^s F^j(y_rho^m q),y_rho^m v)/m!
is a polynomial of degree<=S+j when s-j=e. Indeed
  F^j(y_rho^m q)=sum_{u=0}^j binom(j,u)(m)_under_u
                    y_rho^{m-u} partial_{w_rho}^u F^{j-u}q.
Pair a fixed monomial of the second factor with y_rho-degree m+beta. Its
contribution after division by m! contains
  (m)_under_u (m+beta)!/m!,
a polynomial of degree u+beta<=j+S. This argument initially uses m>=j,
which suffices to identify the polynomial; the same falling-factorial formula
holds for all m>=0, with terms u>m zero. At the top degree S+j one must have
u=j and beta=S. Every y-coordinate except y_rho then vanishes, giving
  [m^{S+j}]H_{e+j,j}(m)=B(w_rho^{e+j}partial_{w_rho}^j h,g).

The coefficient outside H after (TI3.3) is
  binom(A,o)*(z+1)_e/A_under_e * (-1)^j/[j!^2 binom(A-e,j)]
   = (-1)^j (z+1)_e (A-e-j)_under_{o-e-j}/(o! j!).
Its degree in n is o-e-j. This proves the bound S+o-e; symmetry of the Fock
form and self-adjointness of E^jF^j prove the bound with max(e,e'). The leading
coefficient is the common scalar (z+1)_e/(o!S!product d_i!) times
  B(w_rho^e sum_j (-w_rho)^j partial_{w_rho}^j h/j!,g).
The finite Taylor sum is h|_{w_rho=0}. Its product with w_rho^e is in_e f,
and Fock orthogonality replaces g by in_e g. This proves (TI3.2).

TI4. LOGARITHMIC INTEGRALITY VIA FINITE DIFFERENCES

For integral f,g, Gamma has degree<=d=S+o. Let S>0 and put
  L0=floor(log_p(S+o+r)), L3=floor(log_p(3S)).
Then p^K Gamma(T) has integral binomial coefficients whenever the integer K
satisfies
  K >= [2(r-1)+3]L0+(N+2)(r-1)L3.                           (TI4.1)
If S=0, Gamma is an integral constant. In particular the source exponent
  K=10(N+3)r^2(floor(log_p(S+o+r))+1)
works. This proves GamInt with its published constant 10, through an alternative
shorter argument. The sharper GamIntC with its exact smaller exponent is not
being claimed as a literal theorem here. The parameter-dependent form below
is sufficient for the one-tie mean used in this component.

Proof. For a composition a_1+...+a_r=R, Legendre's floor formula gives
  0<=v(R!/product a_c!)<=(r-1)floor(log_p(max(1,R))).           (TI4.2)
The same argument with two parts bounds v(binom(R,j)) by floor(log_p R).
All monomials in fr_n(f) have integral coefficients and the prescribed row
degrees. The divided contraction is
  F^j/j! = sum_{alpha_1+...+alpha_r=j}
                (product alpha_c!) product_c binom(partial_{w_c},alpha_c)
                                             binom(partial_{y_c},alpha_c),
where binom(partial_x,a) means partial_x^a/a!. These divided derivatives
preserve integral coefficients. Thus every coefficient of
  H_j=F^j fr_n(f)/j!
has valuation>=v(j!)-(r-1)L0, and likewise for g.

Evaluate only at n=S,S+1,...,S+d; all these n are at most 3S. The rows in H_j
have degrees d_i for i!=N, o-j, n-j. By (TI4.2), each factorial monomial
weight in their Fock pairing has valuation at least the sum of these row
factorial valuations minus (N+2)(r-1)L3. Self-adjointness gives
  Gamma(n)=sum_{j=0}^o (-1)^j binom(A-j,o-j)/binom(o,j)
             *B(H_j(f),H_j(g))/[(n-S)!S!product_i d_i!].       (TI4.3)
The potentially large linear factorial valuations cancel exactly:
  2v(j!)+v((o-j)!)+v((n-j)!)-v(o!)-v((n-S)!)-v(S!)
                       -v(binom(o,j))
    =v(binom(n-j,S-j))-2v(binom(o,j))-v(binom(S,j)).
The first term is nonnegative; the two remaining binomial losses total<=3L0.
This proves p^K Gamma(n) integral at these d+1 consecutive integers. Newton's
formula writes it as sum_{j=0}^d Delta^j(p^K Gamma)(S) binom(T-S,j), with
integral coefficients. The identity
  binom(T-S,j)=sum_{i=0}^j binom(-S,j-i)binom(T,i)
has integral translation coefficients, so its ordinary binomial coefficients
are integral too. Finally L3<=L0+2<=3(L0+1); the source constant 10 dominates
the right side of (TI4.1) for every N>=0,r>=1.

Useful form with the source's scale. If S<=2a and ell=log_p(8a+r)+1, then
L0,L3<=ell. Hence
  K >= ((N+4)(r-1)+3)ell                                  (TI4.4)
suffices. For r>=2 this is at most (N+3)r^2 ell. Thus the actual normalized
matrix p^{ceil(kappa)}Gamma is integral in the binomial basis whenever
kappa>=(N+3)r^2 ell. No further scalar multiplication is needed for this
one-tie estimate.

TI5. A COMPLETE p-ADIC POLYNOMIAL-MATRIX AVERAGING LEMMA

Let A(T) be an n by n matrix with entries sum_{k=0}^d c_k binom(T,k), c_k in
the valuation ring of a nonarchimedean normed field extending Q_p. Suppose
det A is nonzero, with degree delta, leading coefficient lambda0 and a
factorization with roots zeta (multiplicity included) in a normed extension.
Assume integral unimodular left/right diagonalizations at the sample points
(in the actual Q_p setting these are the usual Smith forms).
Let lambda=v(lambda0), E>0 and h be a nonnegative integer, with
1<=Y<=p^h and d<=p^h. Fix t_j=t0+u*j, 0<=j<Y, with integer t0,u and p not
dividing u. Let beta(t) count the Smith factors of A(t) with valuation>=E,
including zero factors with infinite valuation. If
  lambda+delta/(p-1)<=n*L,
then
  (1/Y)sum_j beta(t_j)<=n[d/Y+(L+d*h/Y)/E].                    (TI5.1)
This is the source's WindowFullAP, with all norms normalized by |p|=p^{-1}.

Proof of integral discs. Every coefficient of binom(t+p^h X,k) is integral
for k<=p^h. In a product term choose l of the k factors to supply p^h X.
At each level p^i, 1<=i<=h, the remaining factors from the consecutive block
have at least floor(k/p^i)-l divisible factors; together with the l copies
of p^h this contributes at least floor(k/p^i). Summing i gives v(k!).
The assertion includes k=p^h; factorials have no contribution from i>h.

Pointwise Smith argument. Use integral unimodular left and right matrices to
make A(t) diagonal. Their inverses are integral by the adjugate formula, so
the entire transformed polynomial A(t+p^h X) has integral coefficients.
If beta diagonal entries have valuation>=E, its determinant coefficient of
X^k for k<beta has valuation>=E(beta-k): a degree-k term uses a positive
degree entry in at most k rows, leaving at least beta-k small constant rows.
For k>=beta all determinant coefficients are simply integral.

The Gauss norm of the determinant polynomial is
  G_t=|lambda0| product_zeta max(p^{-h},|t-zeta|).
This uses multiplicativity of the nonarchimedean polynomial Gauss norm: divide
each factor by a coefficient of largest norm and reduce modulo the maximal
ideal; a product of nonzero residue polynomials is nonzero. In that reduction
the largest degree is
  m_t=#{zeta:v(t-zeta)>=h},
because outside-disc factors reduce to nonzero constants, and inside-disc
factors have nonzero linear coefficients. Thus the coefficient at degree m_t
attains G_t. The preceding Smith estimate yields, also when m_t>=beta,
  E beta(t)<=lambda+sum_zeta min(v(t-zeta),h)+E m_t.            (TI5.2)
For m_t>=beta use G_t<=1, so the non-root-count term is nonnegative. Roots
equal to t are allowed; their truncated valuation is h.

Root counting. For a fixed zeta the integers j with v(t_j-zeta)>i-1 lie in
at most one residue class modulo p^i. Indeed any two have integer difference
of valuation>i-1 and hence at least i, and u is a p-adic unit. There are at
most Y/p^i+1 such j. The inequality
  min(v,h)<=sum_{i=1}^h 1_{v>i-1}
is valid also for negative real valuations. Therefore the sum over j of
min(v(t_j-zeta),h) is at most Y/(p-1)+h. As Y<=p^h, the closed radius-p^{-h}
discs about these progression points are disjoint, so sum_j m_{t_j}<=delta.
Sum (TI5.2), use delta<=nd, and divide by YE. This proves (TI5.1).

TI6. THE ACTUAL ONE-TIE GRAM AVERAGE, WITH ITS REMAINING INPUT EXPLICIT

Let f_1,...,f_n be an integral basis adapted to the filtration by powers of
w_rho in the source's MMwZ (highest-weight/prefix) lattice; e_i are their
orders. Suppose every equal-order initial Gram block
  G_e=(B(in_e f_i,in_e f_j)/product_l d_l!)_{e_i=e_j=e}
has v(det G_e)<=kappa4 times its size. This is exactly the relevant
GramDeterminantGradedHypothesis, not a consequence of polynomial integrality.
Choose M=ceil(kappa), kappa>=(N+3)r^2 ell, r>=2, a>=0, S<=2a, z<=2a and
ell=log_p(8a+r)+1. Then p^M Gamma has integral binomial coefficients by TI4.

The determinant degree is delta=sum_i(S+o-e_i), and its nonzero leading
coefficient is
  product_e [p^{M |I_e|}
      (e! binom(z+e,e)/(o!S!))^{|I_e|} det G_e].             (TI6.1)
For a determinant term indexed by a permutation pi, the degree bound is
  n(S+o)-sum_i max(e_i,e_{pi(i)})<=n(S+o)-sum_i e_i.
Equality forces e_i=e_{pi(i)} for all i (sum of nonnegative differences is
zero). Therefore precisely the equal-order blocks survive in the top
coefficient; TI3 gives (TI6.1). This proves both degree and nonvanishing.

Legendre's formula v(t!)=(t-s_p(t))/(p-1) shows, for each e<=o<=S,
  v(e! binom(z+e,e)/(o!S!))+(S+o-e)/(p-1)
    = [s_p(o)+s_p(S)-s_p(e)]/(p-1)+v(binom(z+e,e))
    <=3 ell.                                               (TI6.2)
The digit sums of o and S contribute at most ell each; the binomial contributes
at most ell because z+e<=4a. Hence lambda+delta/(p-1)<=n(M+kappa4+3ell).

On an arithmetic progression n_j=s+u*j with integers s>=S, u>=1, Y>=1,
p not dividing u and
n_{Y-1}<=2a, take h=ceil(log_p(8a+r)). Then d=2S and Y are at most p^h,
and h<=ell. TI5 gives
  average_j #{Smith factors of p^M Gamma(n_j) with v>=E}
   <= n[2S/Y+(M+kappa4+3ell+2S*ell/Y)/E].                   (TI6.3)
This is a source-normalized one-tie exceptional-fraction estimate. The source
uses a strict bad threshold v>E; the same displayed upper bound applies.
If kappa4<=kappa, the numerator M+kappa4+3ell is <=2kappa+1+3ell.
The present statement does not suppress the +1 from rounding M=ceil(kappa).

The source's sharpened MeanGamX/MeanGamC arranges slightly different rounding
and logarithmic constants. We have proved the actual matrices' averaging
mechanism, with the explicit harmless displayed constants, rather than claiming
literal equality to every sharpened archival budget. Converting (TI6.3) into
the full segment badShare average also requires the partner and dimension

TI7. THE FINAL DIMENSION-WEIGHTED TRANSFER IS ELEMENTARY

This section records a complete conditional argument, so that the eventual
missing construction is not obscured by another averaging wrapper. Put
  D_m(a)=binom(a+m,m),
  H_m(a,b)=D_m(a)D_m(b)-D_m(a-1)D_m(b-1),
with D_m(-1)=0. These are the polynomial and harmonic dimensions; the
dimension formulas follow from the rational E/F decomposition used above.
For N>=1 sites, m>=1 and nonnegative functions f,g, take nonempty finite
Cartesian boxes B0 and B+. Suppose on the enlarged box B+
  sum_{j in {0,...,J}^N} f(A-j,B-j)
     product_s H_m(A_s-j_s,B_s-j_s)/[D_m(A_s)D_m(B_s)] <=g(A,B). (TI7.1)
Only terms with nonnegative arguments are used. This is the source KeyIneq
after canceling the common projective-space factor using RestDims.

Let B0 be a smaller Cartesian box such that every translate (a+j,b+j) is in
B+. Suppose for every scalar pair (a,b) in its coordinate intervals,
  sum_{j=0}^J H_m(a,b)/[D_m(a+j)D_m(b+j)]>=delta,
and |B+|<=2 delta^N |B0|. Sum (TI7.1) over B+. Keep only triples obtained
from (a,b) in B0 by simultaneous translations (a+j,b+j); the map to
(A,B,j) is injective. Its j-sum factors across sites, giving
  delta^N sum_{B0}f<=sum_{B+}g.
Dividing by volumes proves mean_{B0} f<=2 mean_{B+}g. This is avg_abs/avg_of.
