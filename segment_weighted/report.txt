Scope
=====
This is a mathematical reconstruction from the pinned source. It does not
record a Lean build or kernel replay. It proves the actual level-lattice,
harmonic comparison, terminal-window and finite-sum mechanisms. It is not
a proof of RH, GRH or the whole Type-I theorem. In particular, the upper
and lower coarse rank-on-digits estimates and the simultaneous choice of
all numerical parameters are separate. Every use of a numerical bound
below is explicit. The other new components GG, TP and RL have their own
claims and ledgers.

Notation
========
R=Z_p, K=Q_p, p odd. B is the bilinear Fock pairing:
  B(x^alpha,x^beta) = 0 if alpha!=beta, and alpha! otherwise.
There are N=2s+1 signed chain rows, r coordinates per row, t disjoint
adjacent vector--covector ties, and a terminal vector row paired with an
extra row xi. Each tie also has scalar variables Z_i,S_i. Put
  Etilde_i=Z_i S_i + sum_c x_(a_i,c)x_(a_i+1,c)
and analogously Etilde_+ using the terminal row and xi.

Y(d) is the actual rational multidegree space with all prefix first-weight
cutoffs imposed; Y_R(d)=Y(d) intersect R[x]. A cutoff is the largest
subrepresentation all of whose monomials have first coordinate weight at
most the specified integer. It is not just a restriction on a chosen
set of highest vectors. The proof uses this precise definition.

SW1. Direct levels, saturation, and base change
=============================================
For a legal level j=(j_1,...,j_t,j_+), let
  L_j=Y_R(d(j)) tensor_R R[xi]_(B-j_+)
inside the polynomial ring in the chain rows and xi. Multiplication in
disjoint variables identifies this tensor product with its indicated
image. In particular its rank is
  dim_K Y(d(j)) * binom(B-j_+ + r-1,r-1).

The raw level map is multiplication by
  prod_i Etilde_i^j_i * Etilde_+^j_+ * prod_i Z_i^z_i * Z_+^z_+.
Order the S-multidegrees lexicographically. The leading S-degree of a
nonzero image is j, with coefficient Z^(z+j) f(x,xi). This is a nonzero
polynomial because multiplication by a monomial is injective. A finite
relation has a largest level j, so its leading coefficient cannot be
cancelled by any other level. This proves independence of levels.

If a rational sum of raw levels has integral coefficients, extracting
the largest S coefficient shows that the corresponding f is integral.
Expand f in xi monomials. Each chain coefficient lies rationally in Y
and is integral, hence belongs to Y_R. Thus f belongs to L_j. Subtract
the integral raw level and descend. This proves SS_R is exactly the
intersection of its rational span with the coefficient lattice. It also
proves that all finite-degree lattices used here are finite and free.

For completeness, Y commutes with scalar extension. Fix a finite-degree
polynomial space V. For EACH prefix constraint ell separately, let
A_(ell,i) be that prefix's action generators and P_ell its forbidden-weight
coefficient projection. Its largest stable subspace in the allowed weights is
  intersection ker(P_ell A_(ell,i_1)...A_(ell,i_m)),  m>=0.
Use words only in that same prefix's generators, not mixed-prefix words.
Each decreasing kernel sequence stabilizes in finite-dimensional V; then
intersect the finitely many resulting prefix subspaces. Thus a finite
collection of linear equations with rational coefficients defines Y.
Kernels of finite matrices commute with field extension.
This proves the base-change assertion, instead of merely asserting that
linear independence survives a field embedding. The block rank over
the ramified field is the same as the sum of the ranks above.

Original source route: SSDirect.ss_direct, ss_sat; LvlDimCondition;
RestDecompProved; RestDimBCProved. The maximal-S-degree argument is the
short form of the source's leading-monomial extraction.

SW2. Harmonic level comparison with a logarithmic integral cost
==============================================================
For a single disjoint pair w,e of r coordinate rows, write
  E=sum w_c e_c, F=sum partial_w_c partial_e_c,
  Et=ZS+E, Ft=partial_Z partial_S+F.
For f independent of Z,S of bidegree (o,n), define
  P_(o,n,z) f =
    sum_l (-1)^l Et^l Z^z F^l f /
                  [l!^2 binom(o+n+r+z-1,l)].
The range is 0<=l<=min(o,n). It is the harmonic projection of Z^z f
in r+1 paired coordinates. This follows directly from
  [Ft,Et]=r+1+Euler
and cancellation of successive coefficients in Ft(P f).

The inverse expansion is
  Z^z f = sum_l Et^l P_(o-l,n-l,z)
              ( F^l f/[l!^2 binom(o+n+r+z-l,l)] ).

Here is an explicit verification of its coefficient cancellation.
Set H=o+n+r+z. After substituting P and collecting Et^j Z^z F^j f,
the coefficient is
  sum_(l=0)^j (-1)^(j-l) /
   [(j-l)!^2 binom(H-2l-1,j-l) l!^2 binom(H-l,l)].
For j=0 it is 1. For 1<=j<=min(o,n), H>=2j+1, and its l-th summand is
  a_l=(-1)^(j-l)(H-2l)(H-j-l-1)! /
                   [(j-l)! l! (H-l)!].
For 0<=l<j put
  b_l=(-1)^(j-l)(H-j-l-1)! /
                   [(j-l-1)! l! (H-l-1)!],
with b_-1=b_j=0. Direct cancellation of factorials gives
  a_l=(b_l-b_(l-1))/j,
including the two endpoints. The sum telescopes to zero.

The divided contraction has logarithmically bounded denominators:
  F^l/l!^2 =
    sum_(|nu|=l) binom(l;nu)^(-1)
        prod_c partial_w_c^[nu_c] partial_e_c^[nu_c].
The divided coordinate derivatives are integral. Legendre's formula
bounds v_p binom(l;nu) by (r-1)floor(log_p l), so the source's slightly
weaker r floor(log_p(2A)) is sufficient when l<=2A. Every residual
vector is still in the correct rational Y space, as checked below;
coefficient integrality then puts it in its actual Y_R lattice.

For prefix preservation, collect all coordinate derivatives in a vector
row. Their span is stable under the prefix GL action, by the derivative
commutator; their first coordinate weight either decreases by one or
stays unchanged. Hence they preserve each first-weight upper cutoff.
A complete vector--covector contraction commutes with that action and
preserves its weight. If a prefix ends between the two rows, the
covector derivative acts outside the prefix, and the vector derivative
inside it can only decrease weight. If both rows are outside, the
operator commutes and has zero prefix weight. These cases prove HypJoin
for every specified prefix. The terminal derivative proves HypU.
This argument also applies to every iterate needed above.

In either expansion the remaining inverse binomial has valuation at
most floor(log_p(6A+r)), when o,n,z<=2A. Thus multiplying by p^u, where
  u=r floor(log_p(2A))+floor(log_p(6A+r)),
moves the integral generators in either direction. The change of
levels does not multiply this loss by the degree: the entire finite
expansion is paid for once. Replacing u by ceil((r+1)(log_p(8A+r)+1))
and treating each of the t+1 disjoint pairs in turn gives
  p^U PS_R subset SS_R, p^U SS_R subset PS_R,
  U=(t+1)ceil((r+1)(log_p(8A+r)+1)).

The Fock identities used for the harmonic family are equally explicit.
If h is harmonic of total degree d, then
  Ft Et^j h=j(d+r+j)Et^(j-1)h.
Move the adjoint Ft across j times. This gives
  B(Et^j h,Et^j h')=j!^2 binom(d+r+j,j)B(h,h').
Different levels at fixed full row degree are orthogonal: move the
smaller number of powers across and the remaining positive contraction
annihilates the harmonic member. The harmonic projection is self-adjoint
and fixes its image. Thus B(P f,P g)=B(Z^z f,P g).
Since the left input has S-degree zero, only the S-degree-zero term of
P g pairs with it, and the Z pairing contributes z!. This proves
  B(P f,P g)=z! B(f,Phi_(o,n,z)g).
The product of these disjoint-pair identities is exactly SW2.4.

SW3. Actual block integral bound
===============================
For each legal level j choose good dual families in L_j for the
normalized form B(Phi_all .,. )/d_j!, with pairing p^D. Harmonic
transport multiplies this by c_j d_j!. Put M=max_j v_p(c_j d_j!).
Multiplying both families by p^U and one family at level j by
p^M/(c_j d_j!) gives integral raw-block families paired by
  p^(D+2U+M) delta_ab.
Orthogonality across distinct levels is essential here.

Factorial estimate. The full extended row degrees do not depend on j.
Every scalar Z is assigned to the vector row it extends, every scalar S
to the covector row; these assignments are disjoint. For one pair, its
two full degrees are (o+z+j,n+j). The integer
  (o+z+j)!(n+j)!/[j!^2 z! o! n!]
is a product of multinomials. Therefore the factorial portion of
c_j d_j! divides the full row-factorial product. The extra binomial
binom(o+n+r+z+j,j) costs at most floor(log_p(8A+r)).
For any monomial mu of those full degrees, the row-factorial product
divided by mu! is a product of multinomials. There are
  d=(N+1)r+2(t+1)
variables, each assigned to exactly one row. Its valuation is at most
d floor(log_p(8A+r)). Adding the t+1 extra binomials proves SW3.1.
This verifies the bound for every monomial in every SS_R polynomial,
not just for one chosen highest-weight vector.

With A=(3s+2)a, N=2s+1, 2s+2<=r, p>=(r+1)^2, r>=4 and
Lambda=log_p(8a+r)+1, one has
  floor(log_p(8A+r))<=Lambda.
This follows from 8A+r <= (3s+2)(8a+r) and 3s+2<=p.
Also
  U <= (t+1)[(r+1)(Lambda+1)+1].
For t<=s and Lambda>=1,
  [(N+1)r+3(t+1)]Lambda+2U
   <=(s+1)[(4r+5)Lambda+2r+4]
   <=10(s+1)(r+1)Lambda.
Adding any nonnegative kappa0 gives the literal levelPassageLoss.

Now pass to the actual ramified lattice, w^2=p, v_p(w)=1/2. Write sb
for specConst and e=D+2U+M. If
  e<=v_p(mu!)+D+loss  for all supported mu,
put F0=floor(D+loss), q=F0-e-v_p(sb), and scale both families by w^q.
Their coefficients satisfy exactly
  v_p(coefficient)+[v_p(mu!)+v_p(sb)]/2>=0.
Their block pairing sb B(.,.) has diagonal valuation F0.
The first-family span is free. In its saturation, a primitive vector
has at least one coefficient alpha with v_w(alpha)<=0 in that family:
otherwise it would be divisible by w inside the saturated lattice.
Pairing with the corresponding partner has valuation <=F0. Hence the
saturation is a GoodSub at depth D+loss. Its rank loss is at most the
sum of level bad counts. This proves BlockBoundUniformiser.

SW4. Normalized tensor pairs and orthogonal bases
================================================
Let terminal degree be nxi and dxi=binom(nxi+r-1,r-1).
For a good pair x_i,y_i in the chain Y lattice, use
  x_i xi^alpha, [nxi!/alpha!] y_i xi^alpha, |alpha|=nxi.
The bracket is an integer. The terminal normalized Fock pairing equals
1 on the corresponding monomials and 0 on distinct ones. The internal
ties only differentiate chain variables, so act trivially on xi.
This constructs goodCount(Y)*dxi actual dual pairs in L_j. Subtracting
from its rank, as computed in SW1, proves SW4.1.

The intermediate form is symmetric since each E^l F^l is Fock
self-adjoint and the different ties have disjoint supports and commute.
For a finite free R lattice and any symmetric K-valued form, choose a
nonzero matrix entry of smallest valuation. If some diagonal entry has
that valuation, use its basis vector. If none does, for the minimal
off-diagonal entry B(e_i,e_j), the norm of e_i+e_j is
B(e_i,e_i)+2B(e_i,e_j)+B(e_j,e_j), whose valuation is the minimum,
since p is odd. The vector is primitive. For every lattice vector v,
B(e,v)/B(e,e) is integral, so subtracting this multiple of e splits
off an orthogonal free rank-one summand. Repeat until the remaining
form is zero. This proves orthogonal diagonalization also when the
form is initially nonintegral or degenerate. Smith normal form then
identifies the good rank as the number of diagonals with valuation<=D.

SW5. Terminal-window polynomial, leading determinant and averaging
=================================================================
For an orthogonal good family f_i of the intermediate form, write d_i
for the nonzero diagonal values and assume v_p(d_i)<=D. Let J=min(o,nxi)
and q=o+nxi+r-1. Define
  m_l(u,v)=B(E^l F^l Phi_ties u,v)/(l!^2 binom(J,l) d_j!).
The identity binom(q+z,J)/binom(q+z,l)
=binom(q+z-l,J-l)/binom(J,l) gives the exact polynomial matrix
  p^x0 binom(q+z,J) B_z(f_i,f_k)
   =sum_(l=0)^J (-1)^l binom(q+z-l,J-l) p^x0 m_l(f_i,f_k).

Internal tie operators have coefficient denominator at most (r+1)L
each. The divided terminal operator E^l F^l/[l!^2 binom(J,l)] costs
at most (r+1)L as well, by the divided-contraction expansion from SW2
and the inverse-binomial bound. For a supported monomial of L_j,
the quotient d_j!/mu! has valuation <=[(N+1)r+2(t+1)]L by the
disjoint row assignment. Pair coefficient by coefficient to conclude
that
  x0=[(t+1)(r+1)+(N+1)r+2(t+1)]L
makes every m_l integral. Vandermonde expresses the displayed translates
as integral combinations of binom(z,h). The leading coefficient is
  p^x0 diag(d_i)/J!;
all l>0 terms have smaller degree. Thus determinant degree is Jn,
including J=0, and it is nonzero. Its leading valuation lambda satisfies
  lambda+Jn/(p-1)
   <=n[x0+D+J/(p-1)-v_p(J!)]
   <=n(x0+D+L).

For z>=0 the multiplier p^x0 binom(q+z,J) is a nonzero integer. It
cannot decrease any Smith valuation. The number of bad factors of B_z
at integer threshold k is therefore at most the number of factors of
the integral matrix with valuation>=k+1. TI5 gives SW5.3 on every
consecutive window of length W, provided k>=0 and W,J+1<=p^h.

The specialization uses only the explicit inequalities listed in the
main text, plus the degree bounds for A=(3s+2)a. In particular oPlus<=a
ensures z=aPlus-oPlus>=0. Since r+1>=5,
  J/W <=(5/2)b'(r+1)Y/a <5(r+1)Y/(2p)<=1/(2Y).
Taking h=ceil(log_p(a+1)) gives h<=Lambda<=kappa. The coefficient
inequality
  2[(t+1)(r+1)+(2s+2)r+2(t+1)+1]
     <=3(s+2)r^2
for r>=4,t<=s, together with L<=2Lambda, gives x0+L<=kappa.
For D=floor(D1), k=floor(YD1), D1>=kappa>0,
  J/W+(x0+D+L+(J/W)h)/(k+1)
    <=1/(2Y)+(2D1+D1/(2Y))/(YD1)
    <=3/Y.
The source uses 5/Y, leaving slack. Extending from the good orthogonal
span back to L_j loses at most rank(L_j)-n directions. Combining with
SW4 yields the exact RestPlus estimate in the main text.

SW6. Fixed digit classes and bounded endpoint dimension change
=============================================================
The root text includes every inequality used. Fix digits and write
P_u C_u=a_u-d_u where d_u is the sum of the covector digit entries.
Compatibility implies the divisibility and d_u<(r+1)P_u<=b'. Since
a_u>=a>25b' and the other coarse degree A_u is at most b_u/P_u,
with b_u<=5b'/2, one has C_u>=A_u in the whole window.
Consequently both the kept late orders and the legal tie levels have
their C_u bound automatically. Early sg_u orders obey
P_u sg_u<=a/2 while P_u C_u>a-b'>a/2. All other kept conditions depend
only on the fixed block labels. This proves constancy of the actual
block-level index set, not merely of its cardinality.

Taking one coordinate to its largest compatible value preserves both
the degree window and the fixed residue. Its coarse degree changes
x to y with xP>=3a/4, y>=x, and
  r(y-x)P <=r[a/((r+1)Y)+xa]<=9a/8<=3xP.
Thus x>=1 and the only changing ambient-dimension factor has ratio
  binom(y+r,r)/binom(x+r,r)
   =prod_(h=1)^r (y+h)/(x+h)
   <=(y/x)^r<=exp(r(y-x)/x)<=e^3.

SW7. Multiple-tie arithmetic specialization and its exact premises
=================================================================
This claim consumes TP11, the exact reconstructed theoremMV7. It does
not reprove the representation-theoretic mean by counting bad events.
The source mean weights the i-th exceptional dimension at the endpoint
in coordinate i. Its epsilon is exactly SW7.1 with E=Y*kappa.
For the actual coarse progressions the sufficient inequalities are
SW7.2 and the additional initial-point and degree bounds in the main
text. The weighted-rank theorem remains valid with these as supplied
data; the universal derivation of the whole data package from HI is
not silently assumed to have been reconstructed.

For the numerical estimate, multiply the S_i bound by 2(r+1)Y^2.
The b' term is <=(5/2)a and the other term is <=4a, since 2s+1<=r+1
and p>=(r+1)^2Y^2. Dividing by Y_iP_i>=a/[2(r+1)Y] gives
S_i/Y_i<=6.5/Y<=7/Y. Then
  epsilon_i<=14/Y+2/Y+14 Lambda/(Y^2 kappa)
             <=16/Y+7/(12Y^2)<=17/Y.
For initial-point legality, the source gives
  n0_i P_i >=a-(r+1)P_i-(5/2)b',
  S_i P_i <=(5/2)b'+(4s+2)a/p.
The first is >=a-(7/2)b'. Since b'<a/p and p>=4s+8,
the second is <=(4s+4.5)a/p<=a-3.5a/p<=a-3.5b'.
Equivalently their gap is >=a-6b'-(4s+2)a/p>=0.
These estimates include the natural-number subtraction and floors in
the actual initial point; no continuous interval replaces it.

SW8. Weighted finite-sum theorem
===============================
This is a general elementary statement specialized to the exact
source functions. Assume the inequalities SW8.1--SW8.2 and fixed index
set I, and assume each endpoint map u_i sends A to itself with
m(u_i(a))<=K m(a). With eps1=(30+CM)/Y, eps2=5/Y, K=e^3,
  sum_(a,w,z) b(a,w,z)
   <= |W|[sum_(a,z)q(a,z)d(z)+eps2 sum_(a,z)y(a,z)d(z)]
   <= |W|[eps1 sum_(i,a,z)y(u_i(a),z)d(z)+eps2 sum_a m(a)]
   <= |W|[eps1 sum_(i,a)m(u_i(a))+eps2 sum_a m(a)]
   <= (eps1 t K+eps2)|W|sum_a m(a).
No property of the fibers of u_i is used, so its noninjectivity is
irrelevant. This is an exact proof of the weighted transfer, including
all endpoint multiplicities.

The remaining input SW8.1 is concrete:
  sum_(kept blocks of fixed digits) rank(blockLat)<=mCl.
By SW1 it is exactly the level-dimension sum, and the formal source
obtains it from the upper-rank clause of flipOneThree.adm11. This
companion does not replace that clause by a reference to admissibility.
It is a named open obligation in the human reconstruction. The
lower-rank clause (at least half of mCl), needed later in the full
Type-I density theorem, is also open here.

SW9. Depth arithmetic
====================
Let N1>=1,s<=N1,t<=s,Y>=1,CM>=1,Lambda>=1.
Because s+2<=3N1 and r^2<=(r+1)^2,
  kappa=3(s+2)r^2 Lambda<=9N1(r+1)^2 Lambda.
Thus
  YD1=tY^2 kappa+CM*tY kappa+CM*Y kappa
      <=[9+18CM]Y^2 N1^2(r+1)^2 Lambda.
Also 10(s+1)(r+1)Lambda<=20N1(r+1)^2 Lambda.
The advertised larger constant C>=18(1+CM)+20 therefore bounds their
sum. The kappa0 term is carried unchanged on the two sides. Floors can
only reduce the depth used in SW3.
For the additional mass conversion the sufficient constant is
  C>=2[(30+CM)e^3+5]e^3.
Taking the maximum of these two constants, plus 1, fixes C before any
particular field, digit class or polynomial degree. TP11 permits CM=1.
This proves the numerical implication; it is not by itself proof of
all global parameter-selection inequalities.

SW10. Exact coarse-degree alternative under the literal Std conditions
=====================================================================
This claim is used in RL's actual raw column construction. Write
k=r+1, lambda=La, epsilon=N1*lambda/k, delta=de, beta=be. The Std
conditions imply k>=5, epsilon>=1/k>0,
  40epsilon<=delta<1/6,
  0<=beta<=delta/2-18epsilon,
  E0>=0, E0+Q<=epsilon*a,
  b'>=k^3 Q, Q>=1.
Thus kQ<=b'/k<=epsilon*b'.

For one segment let d_i be its odd, strictly increasing interior
distances, G its length, Qj=p^G<=Q and P_i=p^(G-d_i).
The split index sigma is characterized by i<sigma iff p^d_i<a/b'.
For compatible digits with k entries each <P, the coarse degree
A=(g-sum digits)/P satisfies
  g-kP<AP<=g,
with b'(1-beta)<=g<=b'(1+beta).
The coarse initial degree is A0=floor((a-E0)/Qj), so
  a-E0-Qj<A0Qj<=a-E0<=a.
The excluded-band clause of Std says that no odd p^d lies in
[(a/b')(1-2delta),(a/b')(1+2delta)]. It therefore strengthens the
two cases of the sigma test to strict inequalities on either side.

The two scalar inequalities needed are
  (1-2delta)(1+beta)(1+delta)<=1-epsilon,
  (1-beta-epsilon)(1-delta)(1+2delta)>=1.
For the first replace beta by delta/2 and expand:
  (1-2delta)(1+delta/2)(1+delta)
   =1-delta/2-(5/2)delta^2-delta^3<=1-epsilon.
Here delta>=40epsilon. For the second beta+epsilon<=delta/2,
and
  (1-delta/2)(1-delta)(1+2delta)
   =1+delta(1/2-(5/2)delta+delta^2)>=1
because 0<=delta<1/6.

Early case. Since pd=p^d<(a/b')(1-2delta),
  A(1+delta)Qj
   <=b'(1+beta)pd(1+delta)
   <=a(1-2delta)(1+beta)(1+delta)
   <=a(1-epsilon)
   <=a-E0-Qj<A0Qj.
Thus A(1+delta)<A0.

Late case. Since kP<=epsilon*b', AP>b'(1-beta-epsilon).
The factor (1-beta-epsilon)(1-delta) is positive. With
pd*b'>a(1+2delta), we obtain
  A(1-delta)Qj
   >b'pd(1-beta-epsilon)(1-delta)
   >a(1+2delta)(1-beta-epsilon)(1-delta)
   >=a>=A0Qj.
Thus A0<A(1-delta).

This proves the precise coarse_degree_alternative of the pinned source
under literal Std, H>=1. The identity defining sigma and parity of the
interior distances are properties of the actual alternating segment
indices. It does not prove that parameters satisfying the excluded-band
condition exist in the arithmetic application.
