1. ARITHMETIC OF THE SCALARS — QD1–QD3

The proof correctly separates algebraicity, the distinguished complex
norm, all complex conjugate norms, and the norms under all q-adic
embeddings. A positive-power identity transfers the latter assertions
only after algebraicity is established. No analytic continuity is ascribed
to an arbitrary field embedding.

For QD2 I checked the actual finite-idelic class relation and the role of
the supplied splitting-character restrictions. A continuous character of
the profinite integral-unit group has open kernel by the no-small-subgroups
property of the circle, and hence finite image. A class-group power and a
second finite-order power remove the integral unit. Squaring the infinity
formula produces the CM ratios conjugate(tau(b))/tau(b). Their conjugate
complex norms are one. If the original finite idele is one above q, the
same class relation makes b and b^{-1} integral over Z_(q); this is the
actual premise that gives all q-adic norms one. The proof tests ResEta
against the already supplied actual quadratic character and does not
assert universal eta uniqueness.

QD3 uses both literal lattice comparisons. Their prime-to-q integer
denominators give integral multiples of U and U^{-1}, so determinant norms
are bounded in both directions. Rational span or equal rank alone is not
used as a replacement. Local-to-integer denominator clearing is valid on
finite generators and does not require a globally free integral lattice.

2. FINITE GAUSS PHASE AND THE DETERMINANT SIGN — QD4–QD7

I independently checked QD4's finite Fourier matrix identity. For
b(x,y)=q(x+y)/(q(x)q(y)), direct multiplication gives
FTF=(S/sqrt(N)) T^{-1} F^{-1} T^{-1}, hence (FT)^3=(S/sqrt(N))I.
The bicharacter is symmetric, so F^2 is reflection and F^4=I. The
determinants of F and T have finite order; the determinant identity proves
finite order of S/sqrt(N). The radical reduction includes the case of a
nontrivial character on the radical, where the sum vanishes. This works
in residue characteristic two and does not assume q(-x)=q(x).

QD5 uses the same compatible semicharacters and the positive Iop, whose
nonzero isometry assertion was proved in VI5. On a common sufficiently
small even lattice, the three lattice lines are rank-one eigenspaces in
one finite Heisenberg representation. The trace of the three projectors
has the phase of the intertwiner-composition scalar, with a positive
factor from the nonzero pairwise overlaps. Heisenberg trace orthogonality
leaves the kernel x+y+z=0. The displayed product of actual semicharacters
and actual cocycles is quadratic there, with finite-order values. QD4
therefore proves exactly finite-order phase. This does not assert the
source's stronger exact eighth-power bound and does not invoke the
both-ranks-even signed/positive comparison in the present general ranks.

QD6 was checked against ThetaPullbackConstantSqEqDet, PhDetMulIntegral,
IntegralGaussianUnitary, IntegralHeisKerSumDiag, and the finite Gaussian
quotient source. The map is the one whose determinant is det(1-G*).
The Gaussian has positive real quadratic part when one is not an
eigenvalue. Completing the square gives the factor 1/det(1-u), with
positive Haar scale, and the phase alpha(x) exp(-pi i Im H(Bx,x)).
B+B*=1 gives the displayed polarization identity. The source's finite
quotient by (1-u)Lambda' makes this phase a finite quadratic function.
On the theta-line side the sole surviving translate coset contributes
positive volume. Thus c det(1-G*) has finite-order phase. The identity
conjugate(d)=(-1)^r det(G)d yields c^2=zeta det(G), with the stated sign.
The common product mean square proves |c|=1 in this basis-change step;
it does not normalize the unrelated global c0. Inserting a rational
norm-one scalar avoids the finitely many eigenvalues, and QD5 closes the
composition without a fixed-vector exception.

QD7 preserves the exact product-theta representation of e0 and cancels
its unchanged prefactor. Pullback commutes with the finite sums and their
positive square-root indices. The determinant is the actual restriction-
of-scalars determinant, and the four lattice premises needed for QD3 are
carried explicitly. The scalar is c times the specified composition
phases and their inverses; it is not an arbitrarily chosen algebraic
scalar on an abstract isomorphic line.

3. ACTUAL ACTION, SPLIT SUM AND DECOMPOSITION — QD8–QD16

QD8 classifies the continuous character of the actual compact vacuum
line. Restriction to a maximal torus gives integer exponents, permutation
conjugacy makes them equal within each unitary factor, and diagonalization
gives a determinant power on the whole compact group. This avoids an
unproved uniqueness assertion for the auxiliary splitting character.
The rational finite/real comparison keeps the source/target convention,
including conjugation on a chi edge.

QD9 uses a compact open lattice stabilizer and the continuous line
character, so a positive power has value one. QD10 separately computes
the distinguished norm: the same nonzero c0 multiplies the two mean-square
identities, and normalized finite coset counts and coordinate Jacobians
cancel. No claim that the period-normalized e0 or c0 has unit norm is
made. Equality of all jets is passed to the entire function before using
its Gaussian mean square.

I checked QD11's primitive split calculation against
OmVacuumEqSplitFacSmulSum.form_sum and om_vacuum_eq_splitFac_smul_sum,
and against SplitPlacePairMovesLatticeVacuumReading.moves_reading.
The two half-representative sets give the finite double Heisenberg sum.
The invariant coinvariant functional kills every nonzero first-half
coset and evaluates the sum to |R'| times its original nonzero value.
The splitFacO convention is literally chi_T(x0)^{-1} chi_S(y0).
The residual multiple is positive at this stage. Approximation modulo
twice the common lattice preserves the lattice-vacuum sign, and the
nonzero new vacuum identifies the remaining coefficient with its actual
period semicharacter. Projection to the two halves proves the quotient
indexing. Thus the same actual split action reads as the same positive
intertwiner; QD5 accounts for composition phase. QD12 uses QD10 only to
remove the remaining positive factor. Propagation is through the algebraic
subgroup generated by split-supported elements, not a topological limit
of algebraic values.

QD13's approximation is the explicit local Hilbert–90 ratio, followed by
field weak approximation. At a conjugation-fixed prime its valuation is
zero, also in the ramified case. Each quasireflection has only finitely
many projector-denominator primes; sufficiently close approximation to
one makes it and its inverse integral at the unwanted nonsplit primes.
QD14 applies this to a finite quasireflection decomposition on the actual
definite vertex. Cayley approximation produces the required rational
initial element. Cutting the finitely many remaining bad split components
then gives the literal rational/split/stabilizer factorization. This is
not claimed for a new arbitrary indefinite unitary space or as an
analytic prime-existence theorem.

QD15 keeps all four local lattice hypotheses when applying the rational
comparison. QD16 then proves uniqueness from one nonzero Taylor
coefficient of the same nonzero entire intertwiner image. Comparing the
empty protected set with each singleton gives one scalar satisfying all
good-prime unit conditions simultaneously. There is no hidden interchange
of infinitely many choices and no substitution of unrelated lattices.

4. LITERAL DICTIONARY AND SHARP FINITE BOUNDS — QD17–QD22

QD17's source/target lattices are the actual rational intersections in
the fixed frames. Local duality and intersection give self-duality, and
rank-one Hom generators give the required orbit inclusion. Actual Weil
covariance, fixed real Cartans and exact Haar norm give IsVacO on those
lattices. The chi case interchanges the correct local comparisons.

QD18 computes the exact half-quotient order at split primes over F.
Local freeness over each DVR is sufficient; global free bases are not
assumed. The disjointness of selected conjugate supports counts each
base prime once. Multiplying local ideal norms gives n dividing
k^([F:Q]ab), with ramification multiplicities included. Trivial rank,
empty support and k=1 give n=1.

QD19 is the finite Fourier expansion of the supplied normalized coset
datum, using its actual d.z=1 and d.P=locPl hypotheses. Its coefficients
are zeta_i/n with zeta_i^(2k^2)=1. Since 2 symplOH=sympl and both scaled
vectors are integral, that exponent follows directly. The denominator
and all-conjugate l1 assertions then follow without a guessed residue
depth. The purely typographical comma in the exponent of QD.24 was
reported, removed by the author, and the current corrected QD19 bytes
are the ones bound in this review.

I checked QD20 directly against HeisenbergRootSmulFiniteRationalLatticeDecomp
and the finite/rational/real jet sources. If w'=M_f+u with u integral,
the actual sign epsilon in {1,-1} and the half-symplectic character give
zeta=epsilon psi(-symplOH(M_f,u)) with zeta^(2k)=1. Rational
quasi-periodicity sends the finite rational vector to the negative real
component. The stated X=-fr_T^{-1}M_infinity fr_S and s=delta X therefore
have the correct sign; the host xi_R and period delta/sqrt(2 pi xi_R)
are retained in the Gaussian formula. The outer opp on a chi edge is
not dropped.

QD21 defines r_i=opp_Qbar(c_i) zeta_i', which is exactly the conjugation
needed for the two outer opp occurrences to cancel in the linear jet
formula. It supplies every literal DictAt clause: the same self-dual
lattices, the same all-conjugate scalar, k-torsion period shifts,
k^([F:Q]ab)-integrality, all-conjugate coefficient l1 bound, every good
prime lattice/unit conclusion, and the exact mon.mapDomain(fvar) and
period-normalized jet identity. The uncollected coefficients lie in
Q(mu_(2k^2)), since the additional root order 2k divides 2k^2. This is
only the coefficient field, and the text does not extend it to kappa.

QD22 correctly states a conditional source endpoint. The original
