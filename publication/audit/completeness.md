## What the detailed edition includes

Edition 5 assembled 65 texts into eight chapters. They comprise the overview, 42 mathematical notes, one guide to the formal source, 13 research modules, three TeX texts from an earlier round and five texts prepared for publication. The repository at that revision contained 313 Markdown notes under `manuscript/notes/` and 252 files named `main.tex` under `research/modules/`. These counts compare distinct source paths in the book’s contents with files in those two directories. Some notes revisit the same calculation with different hypotheses or notation. Historical checkpoints and source indexes also serve purposes other than exposition. The number of files left out therefore gives little indication of how many mathematical arguments still need to be written. [Edition 5 contents at the published revision](https://github.com/bmatschke/grh-alpoge-claude/blob/c2fc76a890d33ecb9f6357cdefc0406acaf14a97/publication/full/contents.json)

In the prime-selection argument, QW11 of edition 5 deduced the existence of sufficiently many norm primes from a weighted count in the principal ray class. The modulus and the length of the interval both vary, so a prime number theorem for each fixed field and modulus would leave the needed uniformity unresolved. The research module on ray primes gives the intervening argument. It constructs the ray-class characters, bounds the loss at a possible exceptional real zero, and proves the short-interval inequality with constants chosen before the field and interval. Those pages now follow the reduction. [Prime-count reduction, QW11](../../research/modules/arithmetic/quantitative_windows/main.tex#L398-L440) · [Ray-prime derivation, QR1–25](../../research/modules/arithmetic/ray_prime_estimates/main.tex)

Earlier in the construction, the rational primes at which either of two lattice indices is nonzero must be fixed before the twisting characters are chosen. Finiteness of the bad primes for one lattice does not by itself give a finite set for all the later choices. A separate research note explains how to choose a bounded collection of initial data and how the two indices behave when the coefficient field is enlarged. Its argument uses the original integral theta image and its Hermitian dual. A reader following this point in detail should consult that note alongside the two-pass construction. [Choosing the auxiliary primes before retwisting](../../manuscript/notes/arithmetic/UNTWISTED_AUXILIARY_PRIMES.md) · [Construction on the common window](../../manuscript/notes/arithmetic/COMMON_DESIGN_WINDOW_TRANSFER.md)

The cohomological window also uses an argument that still has to be read across several texts. For each twist and each odd prime, complex conjugation splits the strict Selmer group into two parts. One must bound both parts on the same CM field and on the same interval as the contribution at 2. The final bound has to hold for every finite set of primes, with thresholds fixed before choosing the field. The chapter on simultaneous windows proves the estimate at 2 and at the reference layer. The odd-prime character estimates and their passage to a single bound on the original field are worked out in separate research modules. [End of the simultaneous-window argument](../../research/modules/galois/simultaneous_cohomological_windows/main.tex#L942-L960) · [Odd-minus estimate, OB8](../../research/modules/construction/odd_minus_bounds/main.tex#L756-L827) · [Odd-plus comparison, HV10](../../research/modules/construction/point_hv_assembly/main.tex#L792-L886) · [Combining the three contributions, VW1–4](../../research/modules/arithmetic/analytic_chain_closure/main.tex)

## Published results whose scope can be checked directly

* **Galois representations on the cohomological window.** Theorem 2.1.1 of Barnet-Lamb, Gee, Geraghty and Taylor applies to the polarized cuspidal representations in B9.02 in every rank. The detailed edition identifies its Hodge–Tate numbers with B9.04, computes the Weil polynomial for every spherical place and compatible period comparison, and uses Chebotarev and semisimplicity to obtain B9.38 for any weak attachment. At ramified places the toral and full tame comparisons use the same global representation. The separate account of the Roadmap's construction in even rank derives the subtraction of two families of auxiliary characters under its odd-rank and stable-range hypotheses. [Published Theorem 2.1.1](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v179-n2-p03-p.pdf#page=38) · [Spherical attachment](../../manuscript/notes/galois/SPHERICAL_GALOIS_ATTACHMENT.md) · [Ramified comparisons](../../manuscript/notes/galois/RAMIFIED_LOCAL_COMPARISON.md) · [Even-rank construction](../full/even-rank-attachment.md) · [Galois claim records](../../research/modules/galois/polarized_galois_attachment/claims.json)
* **A nonzero morphism of hermitian vector bundles.** Bost and Chen's Proposition 2.10(3) bounds the minimum slope of the source by the maximum slope of the target plus the height of the morphism. The manuscript applies the proposition to the map on a filtration step composed with projection onto a quotient, and accounts for its squared-norm convention. This is a result that can be cited from the journal. Jean-Benoît Bost and Huayi Chen, *Concerning the semistability of tensor products in Arakelov geometry*, J. Math. Pures Appl. 99 (2013), 436–488. [Journal article](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.matpur.2012.09.009) · [Application to the filtration](../../manuscript/overview.md#why-a-family-of-period-maps-controls-the-mean-slope)
* **A lower bound for arithmetic volume.** Moriwaki's Corollary 6.4 gives the inequality between volume and arithmetic self-intersection for a smooth hermitian line bundle on a projective, generically smooth arithmetic variety when the line is nef on geometric fibres and has semipositive curvature. The comparison passage to the cuspidal flag lattice belongs to the reconstruction. It involves smoothing the metrics, boundary vanishing, two changes of integral coordinates and one trace. Atsushi Moriwaki, *Continuity of volumes on arithmetic varieties*, J. Algebraic Geom. 18 (2009), 407–457. [Journal article](https://doi.org/10.1090/S1056-3911-08-00500-6) · [Corollary 6.4 in the author's text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0612269#page=42) · [Application on the flag cover](../../manuscript/notes/periods/PERIOD_VOLUME_BEFORE_SELECTION.md)
* **The equivalence for crystalline lattices.** Theorem 1.2 (Theorem 5.6) of Bhatt and Scholze identifies prismatic $F$-crystals on the integers of a $p$-adic field with the appropriate category of integral crystalline representations. The local application in this proof also uses precise gauge, Frobenius and divided-cyclotomic identities on its chosen integral lattice. The categorical equivalence is a published input. Those identities require the integral comparison and local computations described below. Bhargav Bhatt and Peter Scholze, *Prismatic F-crystals and crystalline Galois representations*, Cambridge J. Math. 11 (2023), 507–562. ## Three passages still requiring a derivation

**1. Integral models on the theta-variety cover.** The volume chapter proves what the comparison of section counts gives once the tower of integral models, flag frames and comparison metrics exists. B8.26u constructs precisely such a tower over the selected CM host and deep cover. A complete exposition needs an internal proof that the prescribed tower satisfies each of the clauses of B8.26r, including simultaneous choices at the bad primes and control of the metric near the boundary. Its construction and estimates have a detailed external account available for reconstruction. [Statement and use of the good root towers](https://github.com/rh-lean-roadmap/rh-lean-roadmap/blob/c7755c1086f05d0918ee8496d95912e4f35e22f9/docs/src/book/main.tex#L20030-L20049) · [Longer source proof and Lean references](https://github.com/rh-lean-roadmap/rh-lean-roadmap/blob/c7755c1086f05d0918ee8496d95912e4f35e22f9/docs/src/book/main.tex#L49923-L50035)

**2. The spectral estimate before summing the flag steps.** The mixed-period calculation gives the scalar coefficient one, the recursion bound, the effect of the two level primes, and the scope of the resulting overlap estimate. It invokes positive-kernel inequalities for the successive flag blocks and compatibility of the cutoff limits with the normalized initial energy. The remaining comparison is to follow each such estimate on the growing family of flags to a proof in the detailed edition or to a published theorem whose hypotheses cover that family. Fixed-rank decay with an unspecified rank-dependent constant does not give the required uniform bound. [Calculation of the mixed-period estimate](../../manuscript/notes/theta/MIXED_PERIODS_AT_THE_CONSTRUCTED_RANKS.md) · [Interpolation at a differing bit](https://github.com/rh-lean-roadmap/rh-lean-roadmap/blob/c7755c1086f05d0918ee8496d95912e4f35e22f9/docs/src/book/main.tex#L46244-L46295) · [Energy estimates through cutoff removal](https://github.com/rh-lean-roadmap/rh-lean-roadmap/blob/c7755c1086f05d0918ee8496d95912e4f35e22f9/docs/src/book/main.tex#L46595-L46661)

**3. Integral Galois comparison before the character obstruction.** The preceding arithmetic chapters derive the reduction to the model lattice, compute the residual Sen multiplicities, and prove B8.42bobh. On its character line the displacement from the standard lattice is an integer at each embedding. Iterating the Frobenius height inequality bounds that integer by the cyclic carry, and the position conditions exclude the resulting Sen weight. This supplies the implication that forbids a character subquotient. Upstream, the level-one argument uses the prescribed Wach inclusions on a chosen prism and the integral comparison with the crystalline filtered module at the primitive divisor. The publication-standard audit still needs a theorem-by-theorem account of how these precise lattice properties follow from published results and from the local arithmetic of the specified field and integral representation.
