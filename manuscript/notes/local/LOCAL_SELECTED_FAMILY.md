# What one choice of automorphic forms gives the whole family

The prime-sum argument needs families of automorphic forms on arbitrarily large windows. Two questions about **a member of such a family** have shorter answers than the previous obligation list indicated. Do the local Hecke lattices account for the whole space? Can the next theta lift use the very columns, coefficient field and real realization selected at the first step? Both answers are yes. The common construction chooses an automorphic bundle once. Its local specialization and the data for the next lift can be read from that choice. Producing enough bundles to obtain a family on the required windows is a further mathematical assertion.

The class here is the one used by the global argument. The source's class selection takes the intersection of the prescribed good-point condition with the further conditions needed by the different rows, chooses their common bundle, and separately invokes the existence theorem for the design. The original linkage is `IsLinked(p)`, with the parameter chosen for those rows. The argument below also works with any further condition imposed on the chosen bundle and any subclass of the resulting class. ## Retaining the point behind a choice

Write $F$ for a family, $x$ for its scale and $a$ for a member of its finite index set at $x$. For $x>F.x_1$ its family membership gives a single set of family parameters and a bundle $b$ at the actual size constant $F.C_{sz}$. Use the bundle chosen by the common selector. If that selector has already been specified with the source's selection property, use its $b$ throughout. Two identifications come with it. The construction identifies $(D,O,b)$ with the data of a good point. The link then identifies that triple with the data of a weighted point $q$ and its compatible bundle $b_q$. Together they give

$$
   (D,O,b)=(q.w.D(q.Z),q.O,b_q).
$$

Here $D=F.D(x,a)$, and $O$ includes the lattices, maps and chosen family object. The equality is in the space of triples in which the type of each later coordinate depends on the earlier ones. We can therefore prove the assertions for $q,b_q$ and substitute once. This works even if several different weighted points could give the same forms. ### The local lattices account for the whole space

The normalized local construction of the point $q$ contains its character frame, twist and local specialization together. More precisely, the original parameter reserves a component for the normalized choice. Its first coordinate is the core; the core's arithmetic datum includes the realization `nt`. This is an `NTrue` for **this** $q.w.D(q.Z)$, and forgetting the character-tuple equality gives an `NBuilt` for exactly the same datum. Keeping `nt` also keeps the equality of the full tuple of data, character frame, choice and twist. Thus the normalized integral Hecke lattices, their coefficient places and residual representations all refer to the one chosen point. Transport along the displayed equality gives the required certificate on $F.D(x,a)$. This accounts for the literal certificate in the original `Prm` construction. [Original parameter and projection][core] · [Arithmetic datum inside the core][nat] · [Core and extension][ncore] · [Contents of both certificates][nb]

To see the numerical meaning, fix a level $1\le j\le m$ and a place $w$ with a nontrivial tuple twist. Let $V$ be the twisted Hecke space, and let $H$ be the commutative algebra generated by its normalized integral Hecke operators. The commuting operators split into simultaneous generalized eigenspaces after extending scalars as specified in the construction. Each characteristic-zero eigensystem reduces to a character of $H$ modulo the coefficient maximal ideal. Group the eigenspaces according to this residual character. Every eigenspace is counted exactly once. The local indices at $(j,w)$ enumerate the supported maximal ideals without repetitions. The lattice attached to each index spans exactly the corresponding group of eigenspaces over the fraction field. Additivity of dimension gives

$$
  \sum_{i\in \mathrm{locs}(j,w)}
       \operatorname{rank}_{O_i} M_i
     =\dim_{L} M_{\mu_{\mathrm{tw}}(w),j}.
$$

The coefficient extensions used to compare the spaces preserve dimension. The correspondence between an actual lattice and its summand is what licenses counting ranks here. It is proved on the original localizations in LTD3–5. The same identity also follows directly from the selected record's Hecke action and normalized local comparison, using the proved split and integral Hecke theorem. [Rank comparison][rank] · [LTD3–5](../../../research/modules/theta/local_theta_dictionary/main.tex#L142-L366) · [Integral Hecke theorem](LOCAL_HECKE_SEMISIMPLICITY.md) · [Direct rank argument][direct]

### The next lift uses these columns and this coefficient field

Assign to $(F,x,a)$ the record $R=b.R$ of its already selected bundle. This defines the original threshold selector on the class, with threshold $F.x_1$ and the source's additional strict inequality $x>F.x_1$. Its columns are exactly the columns of the linked point $q$: the comparison in the link identifies these columns and identifies the pair consisting of the theta realization and its provider. The real component of $R$ therefore supplies a real witness for its own theta maps. The base datum of $q$ is by definition the true-column datum $q.w.I.\mathrm{rec7}(q.w.l,q.Z)$. In particular the coefficient field in this assertion is the field of $R$'s columns. [Projection of the common selector][bundle] · [Compatibility of columns and theta][pins] · [Linked bundle][bw] · [Definition of the true-column datum][rec7]

Why does the choice of field matter? At each of the two type primes $g_k$, the true-column datum has a nontrivial additive character of a residue field of characteristic $g_k$ with values in this field. A nonidentity value has order exactly $g_k$. Consequently the field contains every $g_k$th root of unity under its prescribed embedding. For **each word in the selected columns** the level-zero character is a product of inverses of additive character values at these primes. Its values belong to the same embedded field and their orders divide $g_0g_1$. This proves the finite-character assertion for $R$ itself simultaneously for all words. [RSL4–6](../../../research/modules/construction/second_layer_global/main.tex#L167-L320) · [Finite characters for the supplied form][finite]

The seven family conditions required along with the selector follow from membership of the same class. Two concern the common parameter lists and their windows. The small-modulus bound is a component of the point's twisting condition. The two lists used to state tame distinctness agree term by term. The wall bound follows by expanding the admissible weight sum, whose terms are the same after the cases in the definition are separated. The last two conditions are the stored use of tuples and the uniform admissibility of twists. This also explains the one threshold adjustment for the wall bound. [Seven conditions from class membership][duty7] · [Comparison with the required conditions][duty] · [Weight sum][wall] · [Comparison in the global consumer][frozen]

For both conclusions one may take $x_0=F.x_1+1$ whenever the consumer wants $x\ge x_0$. This precedes every index and every word. Every subclass used in forming the target, choosing the number of columns, and imposing the design conditions retains membership in the original class. The outer uniform assertion called `Closed` follows because the result holds for every member: take $\epsilon_1=1$ and the zero transported bound at every allowed $\epsilon$. No largeness estimate is used in this implication. [Design subclass][d3] · [Uniform quantifiers][closed]

## What follows for the reconstruction

On the original selected class this supplies the two whole memberwise obligations `LOCAL_NBUILT_COMMON` and `SECOND_LAYER_COMPATIBLE_SELECTION`. In particular the local rank identity `GLOBAL_LOCAL_COVERAGE` follows. The original historical register also tracks the geometry needed to produce a linked bundle. Membership supplies the base square and rational tests at every chosen index, and one can prove the retained-dimension estimate on the selected family. Constructing a family of such points on sufficiently large windows remains essential to the prime-sum argument. The other independent inputs concern period heights and spectral estimates. There are two presentations of the arithmetic data in the pinned source. The original argument above uses `Prm`, `NTrue` and `NBuilt`. In the later presentation the corresponding projection has type `NormChoiceConstructed`; its cuspidal parameter comparison is restricted to representations satisfying the stated local condition. Calling that projection by its source name `toNBuilt` would obscure the difference. For rank coverage in the later presentation one may take the local Hecke lattice family, its normalized comparison and the tied Hecke action from the same point. The direct rank argument above applies to those spaces without converting either Langlands parameterization to the other.
