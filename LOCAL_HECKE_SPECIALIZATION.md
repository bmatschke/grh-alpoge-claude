# One integral Hecke space for congruent automorphic forms

Two characteristic-zero eigenforms can have different Hecke eigenvalues and still have the same eigenvalues modulo a prime. The proof needs to keep such forms together. It must also remember which integral vectors came from the original space of algebraic modular forms. A dimension count over the complex numbers loses both pieces of information. The local specialization theorem constructs an integral module for each congruence class and puts the Galois characters used to twist the forms over the very same coefficient ring.

**Theorem (local specialization of a given compatible family).** Take the character frame, the rational data on both sides, the realized spaces of definite forms and the compatible choice of twists in the pinned theorem. Assume its level, arithmetic and coefficient hypotheses. These include product levels with the indicated local types, rationality and integrality on the given generators, splitting at the distinguished primes, the stated integral coefficient image, and coefficient-field representatives of the unramified twist values. Keep the embeddings and generators fixed throughout. Prescribe any finite set of complex numbers at each coefficient place. There exist local coefficient rings, local Hecke spaces and twist characters satisfying the full local specification on this family, including all the prescribed values. In the source these are $X,T$ and `NdSpec`. This gives the human proof of `ndSpec_exists` with its original hypotheses. Item 15 of Alpöge’s remark describes joining the integral structures across Hecke characters. Items 17–18 explain that common divisors and indices affect the cost of the period argument. The construction here gives the local integral space on which those comparisons can be made. The proofs below also give the split-prime density hypothesis for every host. ## Choosing coefficients once

Fix the index $j$ of a space of forms and a place $w$ of its coefficient number field $L$, of residue characteristic $\ell$. The comparison $\iota_w:\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell\simeq\mathbb C$ and the original complex embedding $\epsilon$ fix the embedding $e=\iota_w^{-1}\epsilon$ of $L$. We take the compositum of four finite subfields of this particular $\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell$.

1. **The eigenvalues.** The rational Hecke operators on the finite-dimensional space $U$ span a finite-dimensional subspace of $\operatorname{End}_L(U)$. Choose a basis from these commuting operators. After adjoining the roots of their characteristic polynomials over the embedded coefficient field, the basis operators admit a common triangular form. Every other operator lies in their span and its eigenvalues occur on the diagonal over the same field. This gives $F_1(j,w)$, including for the full range of unramified generators. [Finite splitting argument, MR23](automorphic_realization/main.tex#L841-L891)
2. **The values already prescribed.** Adjoin $e(L)$ and the inverse images under $\iota_w$ of the finitely many requested numbers. Each inverse image belongs to the algebraic closure of $\mathbb Q_\ell$, so the extension $F_2(w)/\mathbb Q_\ell$ is finite. This works even for a requested complex number transcendental over $\mathbb Q$. The comparison with $\mathbb C$ here is an abstract field isomorphism. [Finite-value argument, HLP23](local_hecke_projection/main.tex#L689-L721)
3. **The host field at $\ell$.** Let $E$ be the host CM field and $N$ its specified cyclotomic conductor. One may take $F_3(w)=\mathbb Q_\ell(\mu_{\operatorname{lcm}(\ell,N)})$. Every embedding of $E$ lands in the field generated by the $N$th roots of unity. The displayed local field is closed and normal, so it contains all conjugates of every completion $E_u$, $u\mid\ell$. Realizing the prescribed valuation over $u$ identifies its global decomposition group with the local absolute Galois group. [Proof for the prescribed prolongations and scalar maps](LOCAL_VALUATION_COMPARISON.md)
4. **The twist coefficients.** The finite integral coefficient ring of the original twist must be embedded compatibly with $e$ on the whole of $L$. Realize its larger comparison ring inside $\mathbb C_\ell$. Its embedding of $L$ and $e$ induce the same place, so a single element of $\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell/\mathbb Q_\ell)$ identifies them. Transport the finite twist field by the inverse element to obtain $F_4(w)$. The larger comparison ring can stay in $\mathbb C_\ell$. [Proof of the two simultaneous comparison identities](LOCAL_COEFFICIENT_DESCENT.md)

All four conclusions persist on passing to a larger finite subfield with the fixed embedding. Thus $K=F_1F_2F_3F_4$ works for this pair $(j,w)$. Its ring of integers $O$ is the single ring used below. Only $F_1$ depends on $j$.

## Keeping exactly one congruence class

Extend $U$ to $V=K\otimes_{L,e}U$. At an eligible split prime the level is hyperspecial and the level character is trivial on the local maximal compact. Indeed the type primes and the primes supporting the tuple character have been excluded: the conductor is a union of fibers over the real field, and the tuple uses only the stated type, conductor and distinguished primes. The two places of $E$ over a split prime have the same completion as the real field there, so the prescribed conjugate split place and a uniformizer exist. The rationality clause for the given realization supplies the operator on $U$. The complex spherical Hecke algebra supplies commutation, integral eigenvalues at primes away from $\ell$, and semisimplicity. An injective field extension preserves the minimal polynomial, so semisimplicity passes to $V$. Consequently $V$ is a direct sum of simultaneous eigenspaces for the eligible generators. [Complex Hecke theorem](LOCAL_HECKE_SEMISIMPLICITY.md) · [Eligibility for the given local level](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/IwasawaDecomposition/HeckeGeneratorPlacesOutsideLevelOfStable.lean#L9-L52)

Write $A$ for the $O$-algebra generated by those operators. Reduction of the eigenvalue systems divides them into classes. Every maximal ideal of $A$ arises in this way: the algebra embeds by evaluation in a finite product of copies of $O$, which is integral over its image, so lying over supplies a coordinate. The local indices in the theorem are precisely the triples $(j,w,\mathfrak m)$ with $1\le j\le m$, $w$ twisted, and $\mathfrak m$ one of these maximal ideals. A zero space contributes no index. For a selected class $\mathfrak m$, let $p_{\mathfrak m}$ be the projection onto the sum of its characteristic-zero eigenspaces. If $L_0$ is the $O$-span of the specified lattice generators, use the image
$$
N_{\mathfrak m}=p_{\mathfrak m}(L_0).
$$
It is finite torsion-free, hence free over the discrete valuation ring $O$, and spans exactly that sum of eigenspaces. The integral-action hypothesis on the given generators and the commutation of $p_{\mathfrak m}$ with $A$ give an action on $N_{\mathfrak m}$. Its image is the algebra of eigenvalue functions on the class. Faithfulness follows by testing a function on a nonzero vector of each eigenspace after inverting the uniformizer. This identifies the lattice, action and multiplicities while preserving the original comparison map and relations. [Residual classes and projection, HLP9–15](local_hecke_projection/main.tex#L284-L483) · [Action and relation module, HLP17–22](local_hecke_projection/main.tex#L525-L686)

For a small example, take a two-dimensional space and an operator with eigenvalues $0$ and a uniformizer $\varpi$. Over $K$ they give distinct lines. Evaluation identifies $O[T]/(T(T-\varpi))$ with the pairs $(a,b)\in O^2$ satisfying $a\equiv b\pmod{\varpi}$. Indeed a linear polynomial $a+cT$ has values $(a,a+c\varpi)$, and every allowed pair has this form. The pair $(1,0)$, projecting to a single line, becomes available after inverting $\varpi$. Integrally the two lines belong to one congruence class. This is why the construction projects the given lattice onto the entire class.

## The character on this lattice

For a twisted $w$, the compatible choice supplies a finite-order Hecke character, a character of the chosen Artin quotient, agreement through the original coefficient field of their values at good Frobenius elements, and the required local admissibility. Global reciprocity first gives the Galois character attached to the idèlic character. Finite Chebotarev then propagates the given comparison to every element of the Galois group. In particular the attached character and the separately selected Artin-quotient character have the same kernel. The source sends a uniformizer to inverse arithmetic Frobenius. Local units detect ramification, while triviality on the full multiplicative group at $u\mid\ell$ gives triviality on the entire decomposition group. The integral character transported to $O^\times$ has the same kernel. Its order is an $\ell$-power, the kernel is open, and complex conjugation sends its values to their inverses. Its ramification set is exactly $Q(w)$. At a prime in $Q(w)$ the pre-existing base twist is unramified, so multiplying the two preserves a nontrivial inertia value. Continuity of the base twist and openness of the new character's kernel give continuity of the product. [Character comparison and the role of the prescribed choice](LOCAL_RECIPROCITY.md) · [Integral transport and product, HLP24–28](local_hecke_projection/main.tex#L723-L872)

Admissibility puts $Q(w)$ at split primes away from $\ell$, the type primes and the relevant distinguished prime. In the untwisted case $Q(w)$ is empty. The exceptional set is likewise split: the two distinguished primes have this property by hypothesis. At the type primes the residue field has prime cardinality by the host data, so a stabilizing complex conjugation would act trivially on it and lie in inertia, contradicting unramifiedness of $E/E^+$. This gives the disjointness and splitting required in the specification. The hypothesis on the image of the original integral coefficient ring identifies it with the algebraic integers of its stated subfield. The original relation quotient therefore base changes to a free module over $O$ as in HLP22. Together with the prescribed finite values, the two twist comparisons and the local Galois comparisons, these are precisely the ingredients of the construction in HLP31. In particular, the proof uses the selected character, the selected tame generators and the image of the selected lattice. ## Why split primes suffice to test a Galois character

Let $F=E^+$ and remove any finite set $S$ of primes of $E$. The lifts of arithmetic Frobenius at primes $u\notin S$ above split primes of $F$ are dense in $G_E$. Indeed, a basic open set prescribes an element $g$ in a finite Galois quotient $\operatorname{Gal}(P/E)$. Take a finite Galois closure $P'/F$ of $P$ and lift $g$ to $h\in\operatorname{Gal}(P'/E)$. Chebotarev over $F$ supplies an unramified prime $v$ outside the primes below $S$ with Frobenius conjugate to $h$. Choose the prime of $P'$ above $v$ to make that Frobenius equal to $h$. Since $h$ fixes $E$, $v$ splits in $E/F$. Its residue-field cardinality equals that of the induced prime $u$ of $E$. At a prolongation to the algebraic closure the decomposition group surjects onto the absolute Galois group of the residue field. Any lift of the residue-field arithmetic Frobenius thus restricts to $g$ on the unramified extension at $u$. This meets the specified open set, with the exact arithmetic Frobenius convention of the proof. Chebotarev here is the unconditional density theorem. ## What is still required on the selected family

The preceding theorem starts with compatible rational realizations, levels, lattices, integral Hecke action and twist choices. The original obligation asks for these objects together with the automorphic and Galois specializations on one eventual family of the original design. Its closed-class condition must hold on that family. The companion proof now constructs the original automorphic specialization and the local Hodge comparison on these same data. To complete the local obligation one must still obtain the base-change blocks, semisimple residual Galois representations and their compatibility with the chosen types and spaces of forms, as well as the common family itself. Nine whole obligations are open in the reconstruction.
