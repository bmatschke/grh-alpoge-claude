==========================

This increment closes a shared dependency chain all the way from the
actual finite local-field interfaces and integral arithmetic-group
homology to the uniform global cohomology estimate LemmaK, and then to
the simultaneous two-primary/reference-layer window theorem ThmBab.
It also closes the entire exceptional odd-plus supplier CountZ.

input. CZ proves its quadratic class-number estimate directly. The
classical K-theory, local-duality and Iwasawa inputs are recorded below;

  ThmBab + PointHV + ThmBcFree
      -> JointWin + ThmBc -> literal VWinAt -> fixed-pair windows.

This is a narrowed cohomological frontier, not completion of RH or
Dirichlet GRH. The original-family local denominator tables, compatible

requirements. The cohomological window by itself does not supply them.

3. Exact finite local-K closure
===============================

LK works on every original finite intermediate field

  Q_l subset K subset PadicAlgCl(l),

with its actual absolute Galois group and cyclotomic character cycK.
PadicAlgCl denotes the given algebraic closure, not its metric
completion. LK1 identifies the Krull topology, the continuous
inhomogeneous cochains and their differentials, and the roots-of-unity
representation. The integer ring is a rank-[K:Q_l] Z_l lattice.

The coefficient ring is R=Z/l^r with r>0, and the finite R-module M
may be nonfree. LK2 supplies finiteness in degrees 0, 1, 2 and vanishing
above 2. LK3 identifies the actual cyclotomic dual, fixes the H2
invariant before the module, and compares the source's 0–2, 1–1,
2–0 and flipped 1–1 cup maps. The source 1–1 cup has the dual
coefficient first. Finite biduality proves the flipped map without
dividing by two; the argument includes l=2.

LK4 proves the exact Euler identity

  |H1(K,M)| = |H0(K,M)| |H2(K,M)| |M|^[K:Q_l].

The exponent comes from the integer lattice of K. It does not rely on
M being free, and it does not impose prime-to-residue-characteristic
coefficients. The accepted leaves are classical finite local Tate
duality/finiteness and the local Euler formula, with their precise
specializations written in LK. Primary references are Milne,
Arithmetic Duality Theorems, second edition, I Theorem 2.1,
Corollary 2.3 and Theorem 2.8, and Algebraic Number Theory,
Theorem 7.38. The registry is local_k_duality/classical_inputs.json.

LK is deliberately scoped to these three native K interfaces. It
does not automatically certify every differently named LocalFiniteness,
LocalPublishedTheorems, GreenbergWiles or normalized Poitou–Tate
package occurring in the later automorphy/counting branches. Those
packages may be classically derivable, but their precise objects and
compatibilities still have to be matched where used.

4. Actual torsion homology and KBridge to LemmaK
================================================

TB constructs the ordered perfect-form permutation complex for the
actual GL_N(O_L), using the original totally complex number field.
The Hermitian inequalities form a locally finite polyhedron in the
positive cone. Its normal-cone cover is locally finite. A finite-cycle
reduction to a compact convex set and a finite open thickening prove
the nerve acyclic. A support-preserving chain homotopy handles ordered
tuples, including repetitions. No equivariance of that auxiliary
homotopy is required; the original exact complex itself is equivariant.

Common minimal vectors span L^N and give a bounded-index integral
matrix. The left-orbit count uses its free row module, without a
principal-ideal assumption on O_L. The inverse Hermitian matrix bounds
coordinates of all nearby minimal vectors; reduction modulo an integer
then gives a finite bound by the algebraic norm. Finite stabilizers
inject modulo 3 through a torsion-free congruence kernel. In particular
the construction does not discard small-prime torsion.

The double-complex spectral sequence has stabilizer homology on its
first page. The bottom-row edge in total degree m has no incoming
differential after the first page, so its limiting term is a subgroup,
and its torsion remains bounded by the integral column-norm estimate.
The other filtration pieces are finite and bounded by their stabilizer
groups. This gives TorsBound(49), and the explicit numerical conversion
gives TorsBoundPow(22). The group is the source's actual
HGL L (4e+4) (2e), with its full torsion subgroup.

KB next constructs the existential KBridge using the canonical finite
Quillen group K_(2e)(O_L) tensor Z_l. KB1 compares the actual compact
continuous cochains with their finite reductions; the finite-system
limit argument does not assume integral H2 finite in order to prove
it finite. KB2 applies finite-coefficient algebraic-to-etale K comparison
and the low-cohomological-dimension spectral sequence, then passes
through explicitly controlled inverse limits to the original H2.
The prime 2 and the degree 2 case are included with the required
fourth roots of unity and cofinal coefficient exponents.

KB3 transports the canonical Hurewicz map through integral homology
stability to HGL at the actual finite matrix size. The squarefree
Arlettaz factors imply that its kernel is killed by the source's
specified l-power exponent. KB4 therefore produces both maps and the
literal KBridge. It does not claim a universal theorem for arbitrary
source Plus or KPublished models; selecting the canonical model is
sufficient for the existential interface being closed.

The finite duality reduction uses LD3 and LK2/LK3 on the original local
places D.A. KB6 writes the henselization/completion transport into
native finite K subset PadicAlgCl(l), preserving roots of unity,
cochains, cups and restriction maps. The mod-l injection and finite
Poitou–Tate bound reduce the generator count to a strict unramified
class-group character count and local invariants. The ensuing finite
module estimate combines KBridge, the actual class-number bound and
TB10. KB8 supplies literal LemmaK with the numerical constant 31,
chosen before l,L,Omega,D,e.

The foundational citation leaves are Quillen finite generation and
the canonical model, Borel even-degree rank zero, finite-coefficient
Dwyer–Friedlander comparison, finite etale/Galois comparison,
van der Kallen integral stability, Arlettaz Hurewicz kernel control,
finite class-field theory, Minkowski's ideal representative bound,
and the henselization/completion finite-extension comparison.
k_theory_bridge/classical_inputs.json records the exact forms and
primary theorem references. The chapter supplies the transfers,
limits, exponent comparison and numerical estimates; these are not
hidden inside a generic invocation of K-theory.

6. CountZ is a full additional leaf closure
==========================================

CZ works with the original O tensor_(Z_l)(Q_l/Z_l), negative
cyclotomic action, and local restriction kernels. A continuous cocycle
has finite image, so its cohomology class is l-primary torsion. Using
divisibility, every l-torsion class has a representative with values
in M[l]. This reduction does not assume finiteness of the Selmer
group being shown zero.

If l−1 divides e, the residual action is trivial. Its representative
is an additive character unramified outside l. Finite rational class
field theory places its field in a cyclotomic layer. Surjectivity of
the actual cyclotomic character on the original l-adic inertia lets
one local coboundary witness work globally.

In the second case, l−1 divides 2e but not e, and e is even. Then
l=1 mod 4 and the residual action is quadratic. CZ constructs the
quadratic field in the original Omega by an evaluated finite Gauss
sum, proves its discriminant is l, and proves h_K<l by a
two-dimensional convex-body argument and ideal counting. Normalize
c(delta)=0 at an inertia element with nonsquare cyclotomic reduction.
At every original l-adic place, the invertibility of alpha(t)−1
forces l m_A=0 for its local coboundary witness. The restricted
cocycle is consequently an everywhere-unramified additive character
of G_K, killed by the class-number bound. The other index-two coset
then vanishes as well. This local witness step precedes class-field
descent and cannot be omitted.

Both cases give the literal PropZ. In CaseZ, the source excluded-prime
set contains 3 and the count already excludes 2; character parity
forces a positive even twist. The level-lN group is a subgroup of the
vanishing level-l group. Thus Vlen=Vn=0 on the original data, and
the actual VLe inequality holds with C=0 before every host, cutoff,
field, prime and coefficient choice. The accepted leaf is finite
ray/Hilbert class-field theory with the explicit finite-extension
transfer; neither PropZ, a general Selmer-control theorem, nor a
quadratic class-number estimate is retained as an input.

7. The precise remaining count frontier
=======================================

CountI is not an elementary count. The focused inspected source route
is InpZ plus AutomorphyUnramifiedCoefficients to OneChar, then
PropGK, CorGK and literal CountI. OneChar bounds the original Selmer
length by valuations of trace differences for compatible automorphic
points. PropGK creates one nonzero integer uniformly controlling the
character orbit. CorGK sums its prime valuations, and the CountI
argument chooses its auxiliary quadratic field, two primes and exponent
before the varying host and finite prime set. The actual KatoTwo/RigII,
adjoint vanishing, modularity, rank-three congruence-number and
automorphy inputs remain. The separate older CorG has a different
fixed-exponent shape; it must not be interchanged with CorGK merely
because the names are similar.

CountII and CountIII share one useful full work package. The exact
entry sp4Main_alt takes SymplecticFourImageDense, CohomologyHyp,
LemmaM/Mprime/P, NumberSp4Alt, RigiditySymplecticFour, CorX and
SoV/SoFin for every original Omega and l. It builds and estimates
one Raw object for the original host case, selects a nonvanishing
trace, and finally extends coefficients to obtain the needed roots
of unity. The return to the original O must preserve the character
order, kernel, conductor, parity, and normalized Vlen. Reconstructing
only the auxiliary-coefficient inequality would leave the interface
unclosed. The short wrapper using HypothesesH' hides a substantial
automorphic package and is not a single automatically accepted
classical theorem.

The named premises of source soFin and soBar are now available from
KB8, LD3 and LK2/LK3, but their actual descent arguments remain human
work. In particular an arbitrary compact subset of PadicAlgCl need
not lie in a finite extension. The finite-extension capture for a
continuous cocycle uses the cocycle equation and a Baire argument;
the coefficient lattice and the passage from finite strict-Selmer
length to vector vanishing must then be written. CorX additionally
needs the actual generalized Bernoulli unit and residual unramified
cocycle theorem, including inverse characters and the tame-index
UnrEv condition. CZ's exceptional vanishing does not imply CorX.

CountV uses a rank-three host-frame deformation chain. Its exact
entry countV_of_finiteness_duality_euler_host retains ThmS6_s,
LemmaM/Mprime, native local finiteness/duality/Euler, global
finiteness/Euler, normalized Poitou–Tate, and
HostVanE/HostXpp/HostRigid_s/HostNumber_s. The same frame, marked
point, universal deformation ring and automorphic point predicate
must be retained through all applications. HostNumber_s supplies
one trace-separating element and a valuation sum with its exponent
chosen before these data. LK's native K statements alone do not
fill the differently packaged local/global premises here.

Even after all four counts are supplied, their PointHV assembly is
a separate remaining chain: CountVs and CountIV, sumIn and sumOut,
and point must retain common constants, auxiliary primes, every
fixed reference-layer function j, and the original host and local
data. The formal source implication identifies this task; this audit
does not label an unwritten reconstruction of that implication proved.

The new DA25-HV target records give exact pinned entries for these
chains and their actual mathematical premises. count_frontier_sources
adds the precise definitions behind the short source wrappers.
