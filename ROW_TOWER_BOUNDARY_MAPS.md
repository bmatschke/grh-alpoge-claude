# The boundary maps at an arbitrary removal of a hyperbolic plane

Let $a,n$ be the big and small source ranks, $d=a+n$, $e=(a-n)/2$ and $h=p+q$. Work in the row range $n,e>2h+1$ with the given splitting characters and $\kappa_w=m_{\alpha,w}-m_{\beta,w}>d$ at every real place. Write $\nu=\alpha/\beta$. Fix an upper triple, an adapted removal and a lower triple with the **same** characters. On each triple fix real quotient maps and finite maps normalized by the spherical and marked zeta vectors. Assume the local Weil and Howe model identifications of the reconstruction and that the lower triangle has a nonzero matched-test comparison and both individual lifts have nonzero image. These hypotheses suffice to compare the indicated **line coefficients** of the upper lifts. The argument applies when both sources of the lower triple are noncompact. The [tower induction](ROW_TOWER_INDUCTION.md) supplies the lower hypothesis.

| Upper defect | Plane removed from | Coefficient compared | Relation with lower scalar $c_1$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| $q>0$ | Small source and target | $\beta(l)\lvert l\rvert_E^{a/2}$ | $c=c_1/(C_\beta D^\beta L^{S_0}(e+1,\nu))$ |
| $q=0,\ p>0$ | Big source and target | $\alpha(l)\lvert l\rvert_E^{n/2}$ | $c=C_\alpha D^\alpha L^{S_0}(e,\nu)c_1$ |

The convention is $I_b=cI_s$. Each $C$ is nonzero and fixed across twists on the same fitted real and rational data with the same Haar measures. In the first line the finite factor has ideal signs $(-1,-1)$ and epsilon argument $-e$. In the second the signs are $(+1,+1)$ and the argument is $1-e$. The local epsilon sign is $+1$ in both. ## Unfolding over a noncompact lower source

For the constant term at a rational target line, averaging over the center of its Heisenberg radical singles out null rational source vectors. The zero vector gives ordinary evaluation into the target with one plane removed. The nonzero vectors form one source orbit by Witt extension. The horizontal unipotent average kills every nonzero Fourier index in the lower target by nondegeneracy of its form. For the big-source orbit this leaves the scalar-line evaluation $Q_A$ in the direction $H.x\otimes H'.y$. For the small-source orbit first apply Poisson summation on each rational line in the dual polarization and invert the conjugate scalar. The resulting nonzero terms give $Q_B$ in the direction $H.y\otimes H'.x$. The $B(0)$ summand vanishes at the primed slot. The omitted $A(0)$ terms and the original zero term carry the alpha law. The source-line parabolic, oscillator modulus and the two changes of scalar variable therefore give the same powers and characters as in the explicit [alpha unfolding](ROW_FIRST_ALPHA_STEP.md#rank-one-convergence-and-the-two-orbits) and [beta unfolding](ROW_FIRST_BETA_STEP.md#both-beta-coefficients). Only a single plane is removed. The lower target can have arbitrary Witt index. [Zero and big-orbit identity][ubig] · [Projective-line correction and split into the two laws][usmall]

Here are the convergence details when the lower source is indefinite. At a fixed lower target point, integrate the absolute lower theta function of each scalar slice before integrating over the scalar. The [chamber estimates](ROW_TOWER_INTEGRABILITY.md) bound that first integral by a continuous seminorm of its real test, for each fixed finite part. For the beta slice full rank survives at the prescribed split place. In the two eliminated planes a relation among the surviving lower rows, together with the removed source null vector, would give two upper row functionals vanishing on the lower target and the first target null vector. Both then have at most one remaining target coordinate, producing an upper row relation. The [mixed-evaluation support argument](ROW_FIRST_MIXED_SQUARE.md#extracting-beta-on-the-two-sides) uses Fourier orthogonality on this fiber and works for every size of the surviving block. For the big alpha cone the lower big source is in the unrestricted convergence range. For ordinary big zero evaluation the source rank is unchanged and the target rank decreases by two, which improves its source convergence inequality. For ordinary small evaluation with $q>0$ use the [primed replacement](ROW_BETA_PRIMED_REPLACEMENT.md) before evaluating. The local sieve needs $n-1<(d-2)/2$, which follows from $e>0$. Its translated differences have convergent upper integrals, its evaluated test has lower full rank, and on this representative the projective-line zero corrections vanish. [Precise support and correction implication][repl]

In the adapted mixed polarization, restriction to the scalar direction and all surviving variables gives a joint Schwartz slice, after the fixed partial Fourier transform. Every required lower real seminorm of that slice is bounded by $C_N(1+\|l_\infty\|)^{-N}$ for arbitrarily large $N$, also on compact translates of the input. At a finite exceptional place the scalar lies in a fractional lattice. Its compact domain has a finite partition on which the lower Schwartz–Bruhat section is constant, by local constancy and common compact support in the joint variables. Almost everywhere the scalar is restricted to the integral lattice and the section is the lower vacuum. With volume-one units, absolute integration in the scalar costs at most the product of $(1-Nw^{-z})^{-1}$ outside the exceptional set, where $z=e+1$ or $e$. At exceptional places the positive exponent sums the geometric tail towards zero. At infinity $z>0$ controls the origin in every complex coordinate and Schwartz decay controls infinity. Thus the iterated absolute integral is finite by $z>1$. The compact source average has finite-dimensional span on polynomial and smooth finite inputs. This proves the Fubini step over the lower source and gives the identity with the integrated lower theta function of $Q_A$ or $Q_B$. The joint Schwartz argument also permits integration in the lower Schwartz topology. On polynomials the surviving degree is bounded since the two adapted planes are orthogonal to the surviving block in the positive oscillator metric. ## Detecting the two boundary operators without upper nonvanishing

The [source and target estimates](ROW_TOWER_INTEGRABILITY.md) put each upper polynomial lift in target $L^2$. Freeze all factors except one. The resulting target-equivariant image kills the infinitesimal or smooth source-translation relations. By local Howe duality the full theta module has finite length and a unique irreducible semisimple quotient. Such a slice in the unitary target realization is semisimple. The orthogonal complement of an invariant submodule splits its closure. Admissibility makes each compact type finite dimensional, so on smooth finite vectors or Harish-Chandra vectors this Hilbert splitting recovers the algebraic one. Equivalently, the projections onto its irreducible summands have the same local theta kernel. Thus the slice kills the prescribed quotient kernel even if the entire upper image is zero. When varying the designated primed place, a second split place supplies a full-rank representative of its vacuum class. Changes by source translations there preserve integrals, with the first primed place ensuring convergence until the replacement has been made. The argument then permits the full local test space at the first slot. The lower lifts factor in the same way. Their **known** nonzero images make the induced maps on the algebraic restricted tensor injective by simplicity. In particular one can freeze any nonzero local images when testing a lower vector by its theta function on the whole target. [Smooth and real quotient descent](ROW_BETA_SQUARE_INTEGRABILITY.md) · [Restricted-tensor detection](ROW_STAGE_STANDARD_TESTS.md#injectivity-and-a-polynomial-shared-by-the-twists)

The ordinary real evaluation onto lower polynomials is surjective by extension with a value-one Gaussian. For the real cone, take a lower polynomial and a compatible finite factor giving a nonzero integrated lower value at some target point. Freeze the finite factor and that point. The lower seminorm estimate extends this evaluation to a nonzero continuous functional $\ell$ on real Schwartz vectors, invariant under the entire lower real source by right invariance of quotient Haar measure. It kills the lower real quotient kernel on polynomials. This constructs the functional even on a noncompact lower source. The [beta Fourier localization](ROW_BETA_LOCAL_COMPARISON.md#nonvanishing-on-the-two-actual-oscillator-spaces) and the [alpha localization](ROW_FIRST_ALPHA_STEP.md#comparison-of-the-alpha-boundary-maps) use exactly this invariance. On the fiber over a nonzero scalar direction only the **upper compact** group is integrated, the angular splitting phases cancel, and the density has constant phase with strictly positive modulus at a detecting point. The condition $\kappa_w>d$ ensures nonzero scalar-line Fourier transform off zero in the beta calculation. Thus $\ell Q_\infty$ is nonzero on some polynomial by density. This proves nonvanishing of both projected real operators. [Construction from lower nonvanishing on primed data][bloc] · [Real localization and polynomial density](ROW_BETA_LOCAL_COMPARISON.md)

At finite places freeze projected lower vectors with nonzero image. The spherical vacua and the two marked zeta vectors have nonzero projected evaluations. At the designated split place the beta cone on the vacuum can be used for detection after replacing it by a full-rank representative. The projected local cone is invariant under upper source translations, by writing the compact and scalar average against a lower source-invariant functional as an integral on the stabilizer quotient of a nonzero vector. The compactly supported scalar section is integrable locally even at zero. Consequently the replacement has the same projection as the vacuum. Now a kernel vector in one upper slot gives zero upper integrated function by individual descent, hence zero for its selected line coefficient. The nonzero frozen lower vectors and lower injectivity force its projected boundary value to vanish. Translating first by a line-parabolic element gives the same argument for covariance of these descended maps, with the prescribed Levi character and trivial radical action. The upper comparisons are still to be deduced. [Stabilizer and the designated place][baug] · [One-slot descent](ROW_BETA_LOCAL_COMPARISON.md#finite-quotient-descent-for-the-beta-coefficient)

## Why the real parabolic map is unique at every signature

At each real place put $P=a-p$, $Q=p$, $R=d-h$, $S=h$, $s=(m_\beta+P-Q)/2$ and $t=(m_\beta-P+Q)/2$. In the polynomial Fock realization the generating compact highest vector for $U(R)\times U(S)$ has weights
$$
 \bigl((s+u)^{[P]},s^{[R-P]}\bigr),\quad
 \bigl((t+v)^{[Q]},t^{[S-Q]}\bigr),\qquad
 u=(\kappa-R+S)/2,\quad v=(\kappa+R-S)/2 .
$$
We use the usual polynomial local theta identification in its generating-type form. The joint-harmonic vector generates the quotient under the complex target Lie algebra, and the compact highest vectors of this particular weight form one line in the quotient. These properties hold on the actual product of the real models as well. In the pinned computation the source determinant shift selects the positive rectangular degrees $u,v$. The compact Casimir separates the generating highest weight from the other highest types in the polynomial reduction, and the local lines tensor over the real places. The explicit weight and the one-line property are the local inputs here. [Weight and realization of the actual quotient][weights] · [Harmonic weights and Casimir separation][harm] · [Highest line on the product quotient][hw]

For beta the lower list is obtained by deleting the **last** entry in each block. These two weights sum to $s+t=m_\beta$, since $q>0$. For alpha delete the **first** entry in each block. They sum to $s+t+u+v=m_\alpha$, since $p>0$. The differences $P-Q$ and $R-S$ are unchanged by the respective removals, so all retained weights agree with the lower list. These are coordinates of the compact groups. The rational columns of the adapted hyperbolic pair are prescribed separately. [Compact embeddings for both removals][emb]

Let $D$ be a descended parabolic map and $v_0$ the upper compact highest vector. Intertwining the retained compact matrices forces $D v_0$ to have the retained highest weight and to be killed by the retained compact raising operators. Thus it belongs to the lower highest line. To prove determination by this value, restrict first to the compact type generated by $v_0$. Give the two deleted coordinates grade $-1$ for beta and $+1$ for alpha and give every other coordinate grade zero. The deleted-coordinate diagonal sum acts through its scalar under $D$. In each compact block every negative root operator involving a deleted coordinate strictly lowers the grade, since the two deleted entries are at the last or the first positions respectively. Lowering operators on the retained coordinates have grade zero. Compact highest-weight theory therefore says that the grade of $v_0$ is maximal in that type, and its top graded subspace is generated by retained lowering operators. All lower grades are killed by $D$ by the scalar law. If $D v_0=0$, intertwining the retained operators kills the top subspace too. Finally $\mathfrak g_{\mathbf C}=\mathfrak p_{\mathbf C}+\mathfrak k_{\mathbf C}$ by parabolic Iwasawa decomposition. Ordering the enveloping algebra with parabolic factors on the left shows that a parabolic map vanishing on the generating compact type vanishes everywhere. The argument works for the tensor product of places, using the sum of the grades. Hence the two nonzero maps found above are proportional. This calculation avoids a compactness assumption on the lower target. [Parabolic equations and highest-vector determination][pline] · [Passage from covariance to these Lie algebra equations][real]

For a pair of twists, fix the actual real quotient maps, embeddings and Haar measures at both levels. The real splitting exponents agree across the pair, so the real Weil operators, evaluations and scalar-line integrals agree. Evaluating a matched pair with nonzero projected lower image shows that the proportionality scalar is shared by both twists. At unmarked finite places spherical generation, Iwasawa and the action on the vacuum determine the map. At the marked places the unchanged rectangular model, parabolic multiplicity one and evaluation on the prescribed zeta vector give exactly the width ideals and Gauss factors in the table. These finite arguments use ranks, characters and ordered columns, all of which are fixed in each removal. They use no real signature assumption. Tensoring with the real comparison gives lower matching on finite sums of polynomial tensors, with the indicated scalar. Applying the lower comparison now proves the line identity in the table. Ordinary evaluation lifts a nonzero lower vector on its side, hence detects a nonzero upper coefficient. A matched partner exists by local surjectivity, with a full-rank representative whenever required.
