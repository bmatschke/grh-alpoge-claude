# Finite cells of the mixed row period

Fix a row-test choice in the large-scale Hida range and an allowed tuple $t$. Write $W(t)=W_0\cup Q(t)$ for its marked split halves and $I=\{1,\ldots,m-2\}$ for the internal row nodes. For $j\in I$, let $R_{j,w}$ be the split row subgroup and $J_{j,w}$ the prescribed right level. A label $\beta$ specifies a double coset in $R_{j,w}\backslash\mathrm{GL}_{r_j}(E_w)/J_{j,w}$ at every $(w,j)$. For the finite datum $\Phi$ define $S_t(\Phi)$ by requiring that at every $w\in W(t)$ the local flat transform of the moved flipped tensor for $\beta_w$ is nonzero as a function of its column and row arguments. This is the literal support `ch.S.supp`, independent of the archimedean polynomial, input vector and quotient measures of the mixed iteration. The finite transform uses its prescribed unit-volume measure. [Local definition][cs-data] · [Global support][cs-supp] · [Range of the row assertion][cs-range]

## The labels and the reference tuple

The set $S_t(\Phi)$ is finite. At a marked half, if the flat transform is nonzero, one value of its integral is nonzero, so there is a choice of the free variables for which the tensor is nonzero. Its integral-lattice support bounds the inverse of the next matrix applied to the prescribed column frame once the preceding matrix and its inverse are bounded. The companion bound comes from translating a free variable in the Fourier integral. In flag-triangular coordinates $b$, the tensor is unchanged for all shifts in a fixed small ball, and the Fourier factor changes by $\psi_w(b_{ii}u)$ on the $i$th tail diagonal. A nonzero integral forces this character to be one throughout the ball. Its annihilator is bounded. Removing the allowed flag-unipotent and core blocks yields $g_{j+1}=r w'$ with $r\in R_{j+1,w}$ and both $w'$ and its inverse bounded in terms of the preceding bound. The set of such $w'$ is compact, by projecting the closed set of pairs of mutually inverse matrices in two compact boxes. Its image in the double quotient is finite because the right level is open. Starting at the fixed initial representative gives finite possibilities at every node by induction. Take the product over the finitely many marked halves. This argument needs only local Fourier integrals of compactly supported, locally constant functions. [Matrix and character bounds](../../../research/modules/theta/theta_test_inputs/main.tex#L243-L324) · [Induction through the actual levels][cs-fin] · [Application to the flipped grid tensor][cs-application]

For $w\in W_0$ the right level is $\mathrm{GL}_{r_j}(\mathcal O_w)$ for all internal nodes, `keepAt` is false, and the depth $e_w$ is the positive staircase depth. The same support and translation equations now preserve the *specified staircase*. Starting at the staircase in node zero, a nonzero flat transform forces the next matrix to lie in $R_{j,w}\,D_{j,w}\,\mathrm{GL}_{r_j}(\mathcal O_w)$, where in the given frame $D_{j,w}$ has diagonal $1$ on the core and $\varpi_w^{e_w},\varpi_w^{2e_w},\ldots$ on the tail. The chosen representative through the staircase sends this class back to $D_{j,w}$, so the induction continues with the exact preceding matrix. Thus $\beta$ can vary only at $Q(t)\setminus W_0$. At the reference tuple, $Q(t_0)=\varnothing$ since its twist character is trivial. For every catalogue datum, $S_{t_0}(\Phi)$ is empty or consists of the same staircase label. In fact the [ramification calculation on the untwisted restriction](ROW_GRID_PRODUCT.md#cancellation-and-the-restricted-levels) gives $Q(t)\subseteq W_0$ for every tuple present there. All these tuples thus have the same set of marked halves. At each of them the staircase is forced by the base depth and the fixed integral level, independently of $t,\Phi$. Across the entire first-pass catalogue every retained label is this one staircase. Its term can still depend on the tuple and finite datum. When $m=2$, there are no internal coordinates and there is likewise at most one label for any tuple. Both assertions allow the corresponding period to vanish. ## Which cells contain the actual integrand?

For the mixed integrand $q(h)$ fix the finite expansion of the flipped test, both real polynomials, the input, and the admissible measures. Set $h_j=1$ outside $I$. The support statement is
$$
 q(h)\ne0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \text{there is a unique }\beta\in S_t(\Phi)\text{ such that }h_j=r_jp_j b_j(\beta)k_j\quad(j\in I),
$$
where $r_j$ belongs to the adelic period subgroup, $p_j$ is a real point, $b_j(\beta)$ is the chosen finite representative and $k_j$ belongs to the finite lattice stabilizer. This implication follows from the row's lattice-vacuum support and local split contraction identities, as follows. [Precise representation and support assertions][cs-def]

First $q$ is a finite sum of definite periods of contracted kernels. If $q(h)\ne0$, some summand has nonzero period. In this summand every even paired contraction is nonzero. Indeed a zero contraction makes the evaluation of the product of the kernels zero at every argument. The definite period preserves such linear relations. It is obtained by applying linear Weil and Fock operations to the factors, summing theta and integrating on the definite quotients, where continuous automorphic kernels are integrable. [Passage of product relations through the definite quotient][cs-tensor] · [Selection of the nonzero summand][cs-choose]

Away from the finite places under $W(t)$, transport the original lattice vacua by the vacuum base point $b^0$. The resulting lattices $N_j=b^0_j\Lambda_j$ there are self-dual, their Witt flags and last vectors have the prescribed primitive position, and the splitting characters are unramified. Define the condition at $j$ to mean that $h_j(b_j^0)^{-1}$ belongs, off the marked set, to the product of the period subgroup and the stabilizer of $N_j$. The initial condition follows from $h_0=1$ and the marked support of $b_0^0$. In a nonzero even paired contraction neither of the two flat factors can vanish. The Bessel-to-Jacobi and Jacobi-to-Bessel flat support rules therefore propagate the condition from $2k$ to $2k+1$ and then $2k+2$ whenever $2k+4\le m$. To apply each rule, the right stabilizer fixes the transported vacuum. The left period subgroup acts by an invertible core or Heisenberg operator and a character, so preserves vanishing. In Witt coordinates the point-support equations bound the inverse tail diagonal and the shift-character equations give its forward integrality. In the Bessel case one splits off the last unit-norm line in the transported self-dual lattice and uses the single integral orbit on the complementary core. Almost everywhere the point is already integral, giving adelic factors. Evenness of $m$ makes this induction cover all $j\in I$. Undoing the vacuum transport leaves the fixed rational flag tilt $\delta_j$, a variable $x_j$ supported at the marked halves and their conjugates, a real factor, and the original finite lattice stabilizer. [Shape of the transported row lattices][cs-shape] · [The two support rules](../theta/ROW_PERIOD_ASCENT.md#testing-the-relative-coset-at-the-prescribed-finite-level) · [Actual two-edge induction and final tilt][cs-off]

Let $g_{j,w}$ be the local matrix of $x_j$. For the sign convention of the local split model, take the diagonal sign matrix $s_{j,w}$ in the frame, and assign the class of $s_{j,w}^{-1}g_{j,w}$. To see this is a cell for *any* admissible choice of representatives, compare the chosen matrix with $s_{j,w}^{-1}g_{j,w}$ by left row and right level factors. The inverse left factors lift simultaneously to an adelic element which, after conjugation by the common tilt, belongs to the period subgroup. The residual marked matrices are the inverse right level factors. Together with the identity off the marked places they lie in the original lattice stabilizer. Real and finite factors commute. This gives the asserted factorization with a candidate label. Conversely two representatives in the same cell agree modulo left row and right level after projection at every $(w,j)$. The real factor projects to one, the tilt is unique, and its conjugation with the sign identifies the period projection with the local row subgroup. Their local double cosets are therefore equal at every coordinate. The sign appears in the representative, after the unsigned staircase class has been selected. [Signed representative][cs-repdef] · [Cover from the marked coordinates][cs-cover] · [Existence and uniqueness of its label][cs-rep]

Finally translate to $p\,b(\beta)$ using the left period and right lattice invariance of the mixed integrand. The former is equivariance and a change of variable in the definite contractions, while the latter uses invariance of the *full flipped tensor* at the prescribed level. If $\beta\notin S_t(\Phi)$, some local flat datum is identically zero. Thus the zero finite expansion represents the product of the local flat data, using the same polarizing idempotent as for the input expansion. Apply the local comparison of the contracted tensor with an expansion of that product, with this zero output. The resulting full sum of contracted kernel evaluations vanishes for every choice of remaining arguments. The definite-period linear relation now gives $q(p\,b(\beta))=0$, contrary to the choice of $h$. This argument allows cancellation among summands of the input expansion. [Invariance at the prescribed level][cs-inv] · [Zero expansion and vanishing under the definite period][cs-zero]

The local comparison used in this last paragraph has a precise scope. For a pure input, the split Fourier formula for adjacent arrows gives the product of the local flat factors. At the final pair the second map extends by zero at the positive line and uses the last-row Fourier variable. Both formulas respect finite linear relations in their matrix test functions. Expand the flipped input into finitely many products of residue-class tests and take the products over the marked halves. Each half contributes one scalar for the fixed tuple and label, common to these expansions. The difference of two expansions of the resulting function is zero on all half-space arguments. Its image under the Heisenberg combinations of the common base vacuum is therefore zero, since these combinations factor linearly through the product function. This is the zero-output case of `LocAt`, with the actual sign $-1$. Its proof uses the split local double-step and endpoint Fourier identities and the core test-function relations. No value of the scalar, nonvanishing of a retained period, common real line, or absolute convergence of the outer mixed iteration enters. Those are separate parts of the period formula. The support result is uniform in the sense of the Hida thresholds, with a finite set for each actual $t,\Phi$. It gives no estimate for the growth of its cardinality with the scale. ## The common staircase volume and the finite arithmetic class sum

Fix the first-pass choice and let $K$ denote its coefficient field inside $\mathbf C$. The [ramification calculation](ROW_GRID_PRODUCT.md#cancellation-and-the-restricted-levels) gives $W_0\cup Q_t=W_0$. The ranks, depths and interior lattice levels are common to all these tuples. Use $\beta_\star$ for the staircase in the naturally identified double-coset sets. [Local places, depths and ranks][js-grid] · [Interior levels and the staircase][js-one]

There is an exact formula for the coefficient of this label. Put $c_j=m-1-j$, $b_j=\operatorname{topRank}(j)$, $q_w=\mathrm Nw$, and
$$
 M=\sum_{j=1}^{m-2}\left[\binom{c_j+1}{3}+b_j\left\lfloor\frac{c_j^2}{4}\right\rfloor\right].
$$
At $w$ the coefficient is $q_w^{-e_w M}$. In fact the representatives chosen through $D_{j,w}$ take that exact matrix at the staircase label. In the frame with core size $b=b_j$ and tail size $c=c_j$, its diagonal entries are $1$ on the core and $\varpi_w^{e_w i}$ on tail positions $1\le i\le c$. Write $h_r=0$ on the core and $h_i=i$ on the tail. The symmetric unipotent group has free entries $(r,s)$ precisely where $L_r<L_s$, with $L_{\rm core}=c+1$ and $L_i=2i$. Product additive measure on these free entries is Haar. Order the coordinates by increasing level difference. Left translation then gives a triangular coordinate change with all diagonal entries equal to $1$. Normalize the integral box to have volume $1$. Conjugating the hyperspecial by $D_{j,w}$ scales each free entry by $\varpi_w^{e_w(h_r-h_s)}$. If $A$ is the integral box in the unipotent group and $B$ the conjugated box there, the coefficient uses $[A:A\cap B]/[B:A\cap B]$. This equals $\operatorname{vol}(A)/\operatorname{vol}(B)$, the reciprocal of the volume of the conjugated box. The exponent of $q_w$ in it is therefore $e_w\sum_{L_r<L_s}(h_r-h_s)$. The tail-to-tail terms sum to $-\binom{c+1}{3}$. For each core entry the remaining terms sum to $\sum_{2i<c+1}i-\sum_{2i>c+1}i=-\lfloor c^2/4\rfloor$. Multiplication over $j$ gives the formula, including the empty product for $m=2$. [Staircase matrix and representatives][js-rep] · [Product of inverse volumes][js-volume] · [Exact root coordinates][js-unip] · [Orientation of the index quotient][js-index]

Define $J_{t,a}(x)$ to be zero for empty support and otherwise the class sum at $\beta_\star$. The finite answer then has the **same nonzero prefactor for all tuples and tests**,
$$
 v_K(t,x,a)=\left(s_{AA}\prod_{w\in W_0}q_w^{-e_w M}\right)J_{t,a}(x).
$$
Here $s_{AA}=\prod_{w\in P_A}(\sqrt{q_w})^{n_w}\in K^\times$ by the conductor and square-root clauses. Before multiplying by $s_{AA}$ the volume factor has the exact positive integer denominator $\prod_w q_w^{e_w M}$. The entire prefactor cancels from ratios of nonzero values and can be absorbed into the common scale for the composite-row tests. [Auxiliary exponent and positivity][js-aa] · [Actual class sum and expansion independence][js-sum]

For the field and remaining denominators one can compute $J_{t,a}(x)$ using the prescribed depth expansion. Its coefficients are products of actual local values in $K$. For a nonzero local datum the phase condition puts the additive character on the nonzero integrand in $K$, and compact-open additive boxes have rational measure. The jet condition puts the two chains' matrix entries on the actual class representatives in $K$. The rational input and the specified level characters give $K$-coordinates for the source evaluation. Rational frame substitution and the normalized rational branching pairing give a value $B_{c,d}\in K$ for each pair of a class $c$ and a pure term $d$. Thus $J_{t,a}(x)=\sum_{c,d}\tau(d) B_{c,d}/g_c\in K$, where $g_c$ is the product of the finite stabilizer orders at the top and row vertices. The class pairing itself is independent of the admissible expansion. For any finite valuation $v$ of $K$ this gives $v(J_{t,a}(x))\ge\min_{c,d}\{v(\tau(d))+v(B_{c,d})-v(g_c)\}$, with $v(0)=+\infty$ and the empty minimum $+\infty$. Bounding these remaining denominators uniformly requires estimates for the normalized jets, input lattices and class weights. Field membership alone gives no such bound.
