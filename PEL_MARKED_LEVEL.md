# Why marking half the torsion gives a fine unitary moduli scheme

The moduli problem remembers the points of an abelian variety belonging to one of two conjugate factors of its torsion. The polarization pairs the two factors perfectly. This explains both why marking one factor removes all automorphisms and why the moduli scheme at the initial level can be defined over the imaginary quadratic field without adjoining roots of unity of that level.

**Level comparison.** Fix the project's positive-rank PEL lattice $(L,B)$ over $\mathcal O=\mathcal O_E$, with $B$ integral, alternating and unimodular, and with conjugation adjoint to the action of $\mathcal O$. Let $X/S$ be a principally polarized abelian scheme with this endomorphism action and the prescribed signature over the characteristic-zero base. For the original levels $(M,\mathfrak b,A)$, the two source markings, including their geometric compatibility condition, are equivalent to an integral PEL level for the subgroup $H$ below, with the prescribed pairing root at $A$. This comparison is functorial on arbitrary base schemes. [Original lattice, levels, markings and morphisms][source] · [Lan on integral levels, §§1.3.6–1.3.7][lanbook]

## Which half is marked?

Write $\bar{\mathfrak b}$ for the conjugate ideal. The equations $\mathfrak b\bar{\mathfrak b}=M\mathcal O$ and $\mathfrak b+\bar{\mathfrak b}=\mathcal O$ give an idempotent $e$ in $\mathcal O/M$ which is $1$ modulo $\mathfrak b$ and $0$ modulo $\bar{\mathfrak b}$. Its conjugate is $1-e$. On $L/ML$ put

$$
 C=e(L/ML)=\bar{\mathfrak b}L/ML,\qquad
 C'=(1-e)(L/ML)=\mathfrak b L/ML .
$$

The form $B$ modulo $M$ vanishes on each summand because $B(ex,y)=B(x,(1-e)y)$. Its restriction to $C\times C'$ is perfect by unimodularity. The source's map $\eta$ kills $\mathfrak b L$ and agrees on $\bar{\mathfrak b}L$ with a choice of full torsion coordinates. It therefore records a labelled copy of $C$ in $X[M]$. The second map $\eta_A$ records full coordinates on $X[A]$. Both must come from one compatible choice of coordinates. [Original geometric condition][source] · [Earlier calculation for isometries](principal_uniformization_v48/main.tex#L255-L294)

Let $G(\widehat{\mathbf Z})$ be the group of $\mathcal O$-linear automorphisms of $L\otimes\widehat{\mathbf Z}$ scaling $B$ by a unit $\nu(g)\in\widehat{\mathbf Z}^{\times}$. Set

$$
 H=\{g\in G(\widehat{\mathbf Z}): g|_C=1,\quad
                    g=1\text{ on }L/AL\}.
$$

This group contains the principal congruence subgroup at $N=\operatorname{lcm}(M,A)$. If $g$ fixes $C$, duality forces $g|_{C'}=\nu(g)\,\mathrm{id}$ modulo $M$. Indeed $B(x,gy)=\nu(g)B(x,y)$ for every $x\in C$ and $y\in C'$. Thus a similitude can act through the multiplier on the complementary half. A polarization-preserving automorphism has multiplier $1$ and so acts identically on both halves.

## From Tate coordinates to families

At a geometric point, the source's compatible maps and primitive roots specify a full integral Tate isomorphism $v$, including the identification of its rank-one module of pairing values with the Tate module of roots of unity. In degree $n$ these are the $\mathcal O$-linear isomorphism $v_n:L/nL\simeq X[n]$ and the generator $\xi_n$ with pairing $\xi_n^{B(a,b)}$ on $v_n(a),v_n(b)$. Conversely such a $v$ gives all the source maps by reduction. In the source the pairing is the commutator for the theta group of $\mathcal L^{\otimes n}$. A lift there is $s_x:t_x^*\mathcal L^{\otimes n}\simeq\mathcal L^{\otimes n}$. Using $s_x^{-1}$ as the lift in the convention of van der Geer–Moonen, the quotient of the two products is $s_x(z)s_y(z+x)/(s_y(z)s_x(z+y))$, exactly the source scalar. Their identification of the theta commutator with the polarized Weil pairing applies to $n\lambda=\phi_{\mathcal L^{\otimes n}}$. This fixes the meaning of the root below even when the pairing is written in inverse conventions. [Source commutator][comm] · [Theta group multiplication, §8.1][theta] · [Polarized Weil pairing, Proposition 11.20 and §11.22][weil] · [Complex sign in the original convention](principal_uniformization_v48/main.tex#L1031-L1077)

The two markings extracted from $v$ are $a\mapsto v_M(ea)$ and $a\mapsto v_A(a)$. Two full coordinates differ by an element of $G(\widehat{\mathbf Z})$, and these maps agree precisely when that element belongs to $H$. In particular the construction keeps the compatibility on common divisors of $A$ and $M$. Since $B$ is unimodular of positive rank, there are $u,v'\in L$ with $B(u,v')=1$. The value of the pairing at $(\eta_A(u),\eta_A(v'))$ is then $\xi_A$. It is independent of the choice of coordinates. Equality to the prescribed $\zeta_A$ cuts out an open and closed subscheme after base change to $K_A(l)$, since primitive $A$-th roots form a finite étale scheme. The condition is automatic for $A=1$. [Source field and root][field] · [Lan's finite orbits, Definitions 1.3.7.3 and 1.3.7.6 in the author's revised thesis][lanbook]

Here is the descent from geometric points. At each $n$ the paired $\mathcal O$-linear isomorphisms $(L/nL)_S\simeq X[n]$, together with the pairing-value identification, form a finite étale scheme of frames. On an object admitting a full Tate isomorphism on every geometric fiber, the frames lifting to full coordinates form an open and closed subscheme at level $n$. To see this uniformly, consider the finite groups of paired similitudes of the model lattice modulo multiples $m$ of $n$. Their images modulo $n$ stabilize along a cofinal divisibility sequence. The stabilized image is the image of $G(\widehat{\mathbf Z})$ by compactness of the inverse limit of finite groups. Choose one $m$ where stabilization holds. Using any full coordinates on each geometric fiber, the liftable frames are precisely the image of the level-$m$ frame scheme in the level-$n$ frame scheme. A map of finite étale schemes has open and closed image. This argument works over nonreduced and non-Noetherian bases as well.

Apply this at $N$. The liftable frames realizing given markings form a finite étale cover of $S$, since agreement of maps of finite étale groups is an open and closed condition and every geometric fiber has a witness. Its fibers are single orbits of the image of $H$ modulo $N$. Over that cover, all higher compatible finite lifts exist after finite étale cover by the same construction. This gives precisely Lan's integral level. Conversely the expressions $v_M(ea)$ and $v_A(a)$ are invariant on such an orbit, so they descend to sections giving the source's two maps. These operations preserve isomorphisms and commute with base change. [Lan, Lemmas 1.3.6.5 and 1.3.7.5 and Definition 1.3.7.6][lanbook]

## Automorphisms and the field at the initial level

A geometric polarized automorphism fixing the markings is the identity on $X[M]$ by the perfect cross-pairing above. Its order is finite because the automorphism group preserving a polarization is finite. Choose an odd prime $\ell\mid M$, which exists since $M$ is odd and at least $3$. The kernel of reduction modulo $\ell$ on $\mathrm{GL}(T_\ell X)$ is torsion free. Indeed, if $1+D$ first differs from the identity modulo $\ell^{s+1}$ with $s\ge1$, raising to a power prime to $\ell$ preserves $s$, and raising to the $\ell$-th power increases $s$ by exactly one by the binomial expansion. Therefore the automorphism fixes $T_\ell X$. The Tate action on endomorphisms is faithful, since an endomorphism killing $X[\ell^r]$ factors through $[\ell^r]$ and $\operatorname{End}(X)$ is a finitely generated free abelian group. This proves triviality of geometric inertia. Thus the PEL problem with this integral marking is represented by a smooth quasi-projective scheme by Lan's Theorem 1.2.4, taking the set of non-inverted primes to be empty. The theorem explicitly permits this automorphism criterion. [Lan, Definition 1.2.2 and Theorem 1.2.4][lan]

The reflex field is contained in $K=\mathbf Q(\sqrt{-d})$. The determinant polynomial has multiplicity $p$ at each embedding restricting to the distinguished embedding of $K$ and multiplicity $q$ at its conjugate. Every Galois automorphism fixing $K$ preserves these multiplicities. If $p=q$ the polynomial is rational. If $p\ne q$, evaluating on $\sqrt{-d}$ distinguishes its two roots with unequal multiplicities, giving reflex field $K$. At the canonical level $M=g_0g_1$, the two chosen primes over $g_0,g_1$ specify $\mathfrak b$ by extension and multiplication, and $A=1$. Thus base change to $K$ gives exactly the requested base field for the marked PEL scheme. At higher levels use $K_A(l)=K(\zeta_A)$ and the fiber specified above. The comparison here concerns the markings of a polarized abelian scheme. Recovering the source's rigidified line with its prescribed $w_0$ identity is the additional polarization comparison in the construction of the full moduli object.
