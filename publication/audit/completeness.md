## What the detailed edition includes

Edition 5 assembled 65 texts into eight chapters. They comprise the overview, 42 mathematical notes, one guide to the formal source, 13 research modules, three TeX texts from an earlier round and five texts prepared for publication. The repository at that revision contained 313 Markdown notes under `manuscript/notes/` and 252 files named `main.tex` under `research/modules/`. These counts compare distinct source paths in the book’s contents with files in those two directories. Some notes revisit the same calculation with different hypotheses or notation. Historical checkpoints and source indexes also serve purposes other than exposition. The number of files left out therefore gives little indication of how many mathematical arguments still need to be written. In the prime-selection argument, QW11 of edition 5 deduced the existence of sufficiently many norm primes from a weighted count in the principal ray class. The modulus and the length of the interval both vary, so a prime number theorem for each fixed field and modulus would leave the needed uniformity unresolved. The research module on ray primes gives the intervening argument. It constructs the ray-class characters, bounds the loss at a possible exceptional real zero, and proves the short-interval inequality with constants chosen before the field and interval. Those pages now follow the reduction. Earlier in the construction, the rational primes at which either of two lattice indices is nonzero must be fixed before the twisting characters are chosen. Finiteness of the bad primes for one lattice does not by itself give a finite set for all the later choices. Its argument uses the original integral theta image and its Hermitian dual. A reader following this point in detail should consult that note alongside the two-pass construction. The final bound has to hold for every finite set of primes, with thresholds fixed before choosing the field. The chapter on simultaneous windows proves the estimate at 2 and at the reference layer. The odd-prime character estimates and their passage to a single bound on the original field are worked out in separate research modules.
