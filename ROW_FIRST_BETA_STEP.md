# The first beta step above a definite triangle

Suppose $m\ge 2$, fix $i$, and take $j=m-2$. Write $a=\dim_E V$ and $n=\dim_E V'$ for the large and small source ranks of this square. In this component of the Witt tower the signature defects are $p=0, q=1$. An adapted pair of rational hyperbolic planes $H\subset V'$, $H'\subset W$ gives $V'_1=H^\perp, W_1=(H')^\perp$, and the lower triangle is definite. At each real place the upper source signatures are $(a,0),(1,n-1)$ and the upper target signature is $(a+n-1,1)$, with the chosen convention for the skew-Hermitian form. The plane $H'$ supplies a rational isotropic line, and the real signature bounds its maximal isotropic dimension by one. The rank increment across the grid is $a-n=D.R.t+D.R.r+6$. Thus $e=(a-n)/2\ge1$ is independent of both $i$ and the twisting tuple $t$. Put $z=e+1$. Fix the planes, additive character, mixed-model evaluation maps, Haar measures and real quotient maps before varying $t$. Write $I_{b,t},I_{s,t}$ for the big and small theta integrals. A subscript 1 denotes the corresponding lower integral. [Grid signatures][sig] · [Rank formula][rank] · [Parity and increment][inc]

Fix common upper and lower irreducible matching quotients for these Weil splittings. Normalize the finite maps by the spherical vacuum off the marked set and by the prescribed split zeta integrals on that set. At infinity use the polynomial Fock models. On these data we compare the two integrated theta functions for **every matched Schwartz test** with the required finite full-rank condition on the small side. The proof uses the mixed-model covariance formulas, local Howe duality, the lattice estimates, and the definite comparison. Writing $c_{1,t}$ for the lower definite scalar, the result is
$$
 I_{b,t}(\Phi)=c_t I_{s,t}(\Phi'),\qquad
 c_t=\frac{c_{1,t}}{C D_t L_t(e+1)} .
$$
Here $D_t$ is the explicit product of ideal characters and local epsilon factors below, and $C\ne0$ is fixed before the twist. Ordinary evaluation kills the alpha component. The beta component determines the displayed scalar. Cuspidal orthogonality then proves the equality of the full functions.

## Both beta coefficients

Write $P'=M'N'$ for the stabilizer of the line $EH'.x$ in the target and take $N'(F)\backslash N'(\mathbb A_F)$ of mass one. The two laws of the constant term under $m(l,1)\in M'$ are $A_t(l)=\alpha_t(l)|l|_E^{n/2}$ and $B_t(l)=\beta_t(l)|l|_E^{a/2}$. They distinguish the beta coefficient $B_f$ uniquely. If $D=\operatorname{CT}f=D_\alpha+B_f$, choose $|l|_E\ne1$ and solve the two dilation equations to obtain
$$
 B_f(h)=\frac{D(m(l,1)h)-A_t(l)D(h)}{B_t(l)-A_t(l)}.
$$
The denominator is nonzero since both splitting characters are unitary and $a>n$. In particular this coefficient is linear and commutes with every right translation. [Normalized constant term and the two laws][ct]

On the big side the extra variable in the mixed Schrödinger model is $x\in V(\mathbb A_F)$. In the rational theta sum the center of $N'$ acts in the $x$-summand by $u\mapsto\psi(cu\langle x,x\rangle)$ with $c\in F^\times$ for a compatible parametrization of the center. A nonzero rational $x$ has nonzero norm by definiteness, so its central average vanishes. At $x=0$ the entire unipotent acts trivially and the theta kernel is that for $(V,W_1)$ evaluated at $E_0\Phi=\Phi(0,\cdot)$ in mixed coordinates. The remaining dilation is $B_t(l)$. Compactness of the two quotients being integrated and rapid decay of the Schwartz theta series justify termwise averaging. Thus the whole constant term is its beta coefficient and
$$
 B_{I_{b,t}(\Phi)}(m(1,h_1)h)
 =C_0 I_{b,1,t}\big(E_0\omega_t(1,h)\Phi\big)(h_1).
$$
Here $C_0\ne0$ accounts for the fixed Heisenberg evaluation normalization and the Haar quotient of the two measures on the same $U(V)$; both are determined before the twisting character is chosen. For the conventional compatible coordinates and probability measure on the unipotent, this is Wu's unitary tower formula for the cuspidal constant representation on the anisotropic source. [Wu, Proposition 3.1, equations 3.2–3.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1409.0770#page=9) · [Central averaging][center] · [Source measure and zero orbit][big]

On the small side central averaging leaves both zero and the nonzero rational null vectors in $V'$. Choose the adapted vectors $H.x,H.y,H'.x,H'.y$ with unit cross pairings. The unitary group is transitive on nonzero null vectors by Witt extension. To compute the rest of the unipotent average for $x=H.x$, expand in the $W_1(E)$ variable dual to the horizontal part of $N'$. Its term indexed by $w$ changes by the additive character pairing $w$ with the horizontal translation. For $w\ne0$ nondegeneracy on $W_1$ supplies a translation with character different from one, so invariance of the integral forces this term to vanish. At $w=0$ the remaining displacements lie in the directions killed by mixed evaluation and the phase is one by isotropy of $H.x$. This leaves the lower theta sum at the translated test. The sum over $w$ at fixed outer variables is absolutely convergent by Schwartz decay, with summand norms unchanged under these compact-quotient unipotent translations. [Horizontal unipotent averaging][horiz]

For a fixed rational null line, denote by $A(\lambda)$ and $B(\lambda)$ the lower theta sums obtained by translating in the directions $H.x\otimes H'.y$ and $H.y\otimes H'.x$ in the two mixed polarizations. Transport the line to the chosen one by a rational unitary matrix before making this definition. These directions pair nondegenerately. Partial Fourier transformation in the resulting adelic $E$-coordinate and Poisson summation on $E\subset\mathbb A_E$ give $\sum_{\lambda\in E}A(\lambda)=c_\psi\sum_{\lambda\in E}B(\lambda)$. The fixed $c_\psi\ne0$ compares the two evaluations and self-dual measures. The reindexing on nonzero multipliers in the second polarization is $\lambda\mapsto\bar\lambda^{-1}$. For a primed test the $B(0)$ term vanishes: evaluation here pairs with a rank-deficient local vector at the distinguished split place, where the test in matrix coordinates is supported on full rank. More intrinsically the evaluation is invariant under the Heisenberg translations in one nonzero source direction, whose annihilator is supported on matrices with a nonzero left kernel. This statement also applies to group translates by covariance of the primed space. Consequently the two **nonzero** sums differ by $-A(0)$ after incorporating $c_\psi$. The negative zero term for each projective line, together with the original zero orbit, has the alpha law from the mixed-model Levi action. The regrouping is justified initially wherever the two nonzero-vector sums are absolutely summable. This holds for the first polarization by the original Schwartz theta expansion and for the second almost everywhere in the source integral by the absolutely unfolded integral below. The projective-line correction is their difference there. [Poisson comparison and line corrections][small] · [Vanishing for primed tests][zero] · [Mixed-model Levi action][law]

The remaining nonzero-vector sum in the $B$ polarization unfolds over the stabilizer of a vector. Iwasawa for the stabilizer of its line leaves $U(V'_1)$, the compact $K\subset U(V')(\mathbb A_F)$, and an idele $l\in\mathbb A_E^\times$. Passing from the vector stabilizer to this line parabolic contributes inverse modulus $|l|_E^{1-n}$: the $n-2$ horizontal $E$-coordinates scale by $l$ and the central $F$-coordinate by its norm. The oscillator contributes $|l|_E^{(a+n)/2}$. Their product is $|l|_E^{z}$; the character is $\nu_t=\alpha_t/\beta_t$. With $E_B$ the second mixed-model evaluation and $\rho_\psi$ the Heisenberg action, define
$$
 (Q_t\Phi')(y)=\int_K\int_{\mathbb A_E^\times}
 E_B\big(\rho_\psi(\bar l\,H.y\otimes H'.x)\,\omega'_t(k,1)\Phi'\big)(y)
 \nu_t(l)|l|_E^{z}\,d^\times l\,dk .
$$
The lower quotient of $U(V'_1)$ is compact. The translated and evaluated slices are Schwartz in the scalar-line and lower variables in the compatible mixed polarization. For factorizable data the absolute scalar integral at almost all finite primes is bounded, with volume-one units, by $(1-N\mathfrak p^{-z})^{-1}$. Their product converges since $z\ge2$. At a fixed exceptional prime the scalar variable stays in a fractional lattice and the series tending to zero is geometric; at infinity rapid decrease controls infinity, while $z>0$ controls zero. Choosing a Schwartz bound with sufficiently many powers also sums over the lower rational lattice uniformly on compact sets of lower representatives. Compact $K$-finite averaging preserves these estimates. The unipotent average of the original small integral can be interchanged using the primed estimate with source-height exponent zero and the finite source volume. This proves
$$
 B_{I_{s,t}(\Phi')}(m(1,h_1)h)=C_1 I_{s,1,t}\big(Q_t\omega'_t(1,h)\Phi'\big)(h_1).
$$
The target dilation is $\alpha_t/\nu_t=\beta_t$ with norm exponent $a/2$, as follows by $l\mapsto l\bar t'^{-1}$ for the target idele $t'$. The constant $C_1\ne0$ is the product of the kernel normalization, $c_\psi$ and the orbit-integration measure. None depends on $t$. This uses the two elementary mixed-model identities, namely Heisenberg covariance and the partial Fourier change of polarization; the complete small-source identity also relies on the earlier primed convergence estimate. Wu's cited proposition assumes a cuspidal source. The constant representation on the upper small source has a proper rational parabolic, so it is the nonzero-vector unfolding that gives this second formula. ## Scalar for this step

Let $c_{1,t}\ne0$ be the proportionality constant on the lower definite triangle for its specified real and finite maps. For matched upper polynomial and finite data, the local comparison to be used on the lower definite quotient is equality of the tensor images of the following pair:
$$
 \big(C_2 D_tL_t(z)E_0\Phi,\ Q_t\Phi'\big),\qquad
 D_t=\nu_t(\mathfrak b)^{-1}\nu_t(\mathfrak c)^{-1}
       \prod_{v\in T_t}\epsilon_{v,t}(-e).
$$
Here $\mathfrak b=H.\mathfrak b, \mathfrak c=H'.\mathfrak b$, and $\epsilon_{v,t}(-e)$ means the source's local factor for the character, parameter $-e$ and sign $s=+1$, including its Gauss sum and Haar normalization. The signs follow from the conjugate beta shift: the scalar-line ideal is $\overline{\bar{\mathfrak b}^{-1}\mathfrak c}=\mathfrak b^{-1}\bar{\mathfrak c}$; since $\nu_t$ is trivial on the base ideles, its value there is $\nu_t(\mathfrak b)^{-1}\nu_t(\mathfrak c)^{-1}$. The adapted ideals are prime to the controlled ramification. The standard local computation uses sign $+1$ for beta. The pinned construction likewise yields ideal signs $(+1,+1)$ and the local sign $+1$ for alpha. All these signs are choices of witnesses in the pinned construction. [Ideal witnesses][ideal] · [Selection for the two shifts][signs] · [Beta standard vector][std] · [Alpha standard vector][stdA]

For the actual grid $L_t(z)$ is exactly the continued **finite** Hecke value of the primitive character denoted by $\mathrm{nuH}(G,t,i,j)$. The square character agrees with it adelically. Every prime of $E$ above the omitted base set $S_0$ is ramified for it: at the base place the compulsory character ramification survives the unramified factors, and at the other omitted places its deeper ramification survives the tame factors. The primitive Euler factor is therefore already one at each omitted prime. Since $z\ge2$, the resulting Euler product is absolutely convergent and nonzero and agrees with its continuation by its defining identity. This argument is specific to the actual grid characters and does not insert any archimedean gamma factor. [Ramification and equality on actual squares][hecke]

The [real scalar-line argument and the finite local calculations](ROW_BETA_LOCAL_COMPARISON.md) give the individual factors. On the real side the scalar is nonzero and common to all tuples for these fixed maps: the infinite exponents, the real Weil actions in the common models, and the measures are identical. At an unmarked place the local geometric series starts at the valuation of $\mathfrak b^{-1}\overline{\mathfrak c}$. Its norm contributes $N(\mathfrak b^{-1}\overline{\mathfrak c})^{-z}$ to the fixed scalar. At a marked place the two standard tests have the same volume factor and their ratio is the indicated Gauss sum. The [finite descent proof](ROW_BETA_LOCAL_COMPARISON.md#finite-quotient-descent-for-the-beta-coefficient) gives the parabolic law on the common upper quotient. The unmarked calculation then extends by Iwasawa and the marked one by ramified multiplicity one. Products of the descended maps preserve equality of finite sums of pure tensors. On the lower compact quotients the [definite comparison](ROW_DEFINITE_THETA_COMPARISON.md) holds for all polynomial and finite data with equal quotient images. Its application here therefore permits the evaluated small test without a restriction on the support of that test. The ordinary evaluation used for the alpha term below is a different operator. [Exact tensor identity in the pin][local]

Multiplying the lower matched-pair identity by the preceding constants gives, on the Levi,
$$
 C D_t L_t(z) B_{I_{b,t}(\Phi)}
 =c_{1,t} B_{I_{s,t}(\Phi')},\qquad C=C_2 C_1/C_0\ne0 .
$$
This determines the only possible upper scalar $c_t$ on a single pair. Choose a lower big polynomial test whose definite theta integral is nonzero at some $h_1$, using definite nonvanishing. Evaluation $E_0$ maps the upper polynomial data onto the lower polynomial data. In compatible product coordinates the added variables can be filled with a vacuum of nonzero value at the origin, with its scalar adjusted to one. Lift the lower test and choose any upper small primed partner by the matching surjectivity. At $h=1$, $B_{I_b}\ne0$; its comparison to $B_{I_s}$ forces the latter nonzero also. Therefore
$$
 c_t C D_t L_t(e+1)=c_{1,t},\qquad
 \frac{c_t}{c_{t_0}}=
 \frac{c_{1,t}}{c_{1,t_0}}\frac{D_{t_0}}{D_t}
 \frac{L_{t_0}(e+1)}{L_t(e+1)} .
$$
This is the recurrence for the tower constants on the fixed compatible models. Identifying them with constants obtained with different square tests or different real maps requires the corresponding change-of-data comparison. ## The alpha coefficient and the cuspidal difference

Take $c_t=c_{1,t}/(CD_tL_t(z))$. The quotient identity gives zero beta coefficient on the Levi for the difference $F=I_{b,t}(\Phi)-c_t I_{s,t}(\Phi')$. It applies again after any simultaneous compact target translation: these translations preserve polynomials, matching and the primed condition. Unipotent invariance and the beta character law now give zero throughout the target by parabolic Iwasawa decomposition. The other coefficient uses ordinary evaluation on the small side. In the formal reduction one first moves a primed test, without changing its integral, to $\Psi$ with the ordinary small evaluation at zero primed for the pair $(V',W_1)$. The projective-line corrections then vanish by the primed evaluation property in the new model. Its alpha coefficient on the Levi is a fixed scalar times $I_{V',W_1}(E'_0\Psi)$. In this formula the small source is still indefinite; only the target has been lowered. At a real place its source has a positive and a negative row and the target is definite of dimension $d_1=a+n-2>0$. After inserting the splitting character of exponent $k$, invariance forces every infinitesimal diagonal generator to have eigenvalue $-k/2$ on a monomial detected by the theta functional. In the Fock convention of the source, the positive row has weights $N+d_1/2$, the negative row has weights $-N'-d_1/2$, $N,N'\ge0$. No common value exists, so every such monomial is killed. For fixed finite components the theta functional is continuous by the primed estimate; Haar invariance under the real source justifies differentiating, and the monomials span the polynomial data. Applying the argument after each compact right translate, and using Iwasawa and unipotent invariance, kills the entire alpha coefficient. The [full-rank replacement](ROW_BETA_PRIMED_REPLACEMENT.md) constructs this representative by explicit source row operations, preserving the entire upper integral. [Local replacement][move] · [Vanishing after evaluation][alpha] · [Diagonal Fock weights][fock] · [Differentiating the invariant functional][real]

At rational Witt index one, all proper rational parabolics are stabilizers of isotropic lines and are rationally conjugate by Witt extension. With both coefficients zero at every right translate, the difference is cuspidal. We now pair the difference with each of its two theta summands. Orthogonality to both gives it zero norm and continuity then gives pointwise equality. [Norm argument in the pin][end]

## Orthogonality in the first beta step

Keep the compatible upper and lower Weil splittings fixed. Write $W=W_1\oplus H'$, $V'=V'_1\oplus H$, with $V,V'_1,W_1$ definite. Let $F$ be a cuspidal difference of an upper big polynomial theta integral and an upper primed small polynomial theta integral, allowing arbitrary scalar multiples. The proof of orthogonality to the big image works for every target cusp form. For the small image one only needs the local matching properties at places over one good rational prime.

**The big image.** Put $Y=V$, $X=W_1$, and write $\theta^X_{Y,\psi}$ for Wu's lift from $U(Y)$ to $U(X)$. The constant representation on $U(Y)$ is irreducible cuspidal because $Y$ is anisotropic. The lower nonvanishing test shows that $\pi=\theta^X_{Y,\psi}(\mathbf1)$ is nonzero. It is cuspidal because $X$ is anisotropic and irreducible by Wu's Theorem 5.5. Write $\chi_1,\chi_2$ for Wu's splitting characters acting by determinant on $U(X),U(Y)$ respectively. Apply his Theorem 5.4(1) with the additive character inverted:
$$
 \theta^Y_{X,\psi^{-1}}(\chi_1^{-1}\pi)=\chi_2\mathbf1 .
$$
Now $\chi_1^{-1}\pi$ is irreducible cuspidal, and this return lift is nonzero cuspidal on $U(Y)$. Apply Theorem 5.1(2), going **one hyperbolic plane up** from $X$. It makes the whole space $\theta^{W}_{Y,\psi}(\chi_2^{-1}\chi_2\mathbf1)$ orthogonal to cusp forms on $U(W)$. This is exactly the upper big lift of $\mathbf1$. The determinant factors cancel even for nonquadratic splitting characters. The Witt tower keeps the splitting characters unchanged when a hyperbolic plane is added, and equivalent mixed models transport the test space. The nonzero lower test is the only lower nonvanishing used in this argument. [Wu, §§2.2, 3 and Theorems 5.1, 5.4, 5.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1409.0770#page=15) · [Definite nonvanishing](ROW_MATCHED_SQUARE_TESTS.md) · [Orthogonality required in the pin][cu-orth]

**The small image.** Take a primed Schwartz test $\Psi$ and put
$$
 T_\Psi(g)=\int_{[U(W)]} F(h)\overline{\theta_{V',W}(\Psi;g,h)}\,dh.
$$
Cuspidal rapidity for the polynomial difference $F$ is the usual Siegel-set estimate applied to a smooth finite-level form of uniform moderate growth, as verified for these theta integrals in the pin. It justifies convergence of this integral and rational left invariance in $g$. [Rapidity for these differences][cu-rapid] · [Miller–Schmid on rapid decay on Siegel sets](https://arxiv.org/abs/1106.2149) · [Convergence and covariance][cu-pet]

Choose a rational prime $\ell$ completely split in $E$ and outside the finite exceptional places, including the place supporting the primed condition. For each $v\mid\ell$ the exact local hypothesis needed is that $F$ admits a finite-sum realization by varying the big split Schwartz test through equivariant maps with rapidly decreasing values. Here is how the good-place matching data give this realization. At $v$ the two surjective source-coinvariant maps have the same irreducible $U(W)(F_v)$-quotient $P_v$. Their kernels on local tests are generated by source translates minus their originals, as proved in the [split matrix calculation below](#the-good-place-kernel-on-both-rectangular-blocks). Source invariance of the absolutely convergent integrals therefore makes each local variation of either incoming tensor factor through $P_v$; at $v\ne v_1$ this variation preserves the primed support at $v_1$. Finite tensor expansions give a $U(W)(F_v)$-map $S:P_v^{\oplus m}\to$ automorphic functions with $F$ in the image. The preimage of the right-translation-stable subspace of rapidly decreasing functions is a submodule. A finite direct sum of simple modules is semisimple, so this preimage admits an equivariant projection. Compose $S$ with that projection and then with the big test-space surjection onto each summand. The resulting maps have rapidly decreasing values and recover $F$. This uses the good local quotient and the kernel assertion; upper global proportionality is absent from its hypotheses. [Local decomposition][cu-decomp] · [Semisimple projection][cu-proj]

Pair each such realization with the small test at $v$. The two split matrix ranks $a,n$ sum to the target rank $d$. Stacking the two matrices gives the right-equivariant distribution on square matrices from the good-place calculation. Its determinant character has nonreal phase at the uniformizer: the ratio $\nu$ is unitary and its uniformizer value has infinite order by the CM weight and valuation argument. The rank-stratum argument therefore gives the corresponding left transformation law, and the left multiplier on the small rows cancels the oscillator multiplier. This proves $T_\Psi(gu_v)=T_\Psi(g)$ for every $u_v\in U(V')(F_v)$ and every $v\mid\ell$. This local calculation uses the two complementary split matrix spaces and convergent pairings. It holds at these finite places independently of real signature. [Split distribution and its transformation law](ROW_DEFINITE_REVERSE_LIFT.md) · [Application to rapidly decreasing local realizations][cu-loc] · [Good character][cu-good]

Use the rational split torus $a(u)$ which acts by $u,u^{-1}$ on the two adapted vectors in $H$ and as the identity on $V'_1$, for $u\in F^\times$. At the finite places away from $\ell$, the sequence $b_k=(a(\ell^k)_v)_{v\nmid\ell}$ lies in a compact subgroup, since it is the image of the local unit ideles away from $\ell$. The finite Weil vector is smooth, so $b_k$ fixes $\Psi$ for $k$ in a positive arithmetic progression of multiples. Covariance of the theta kernel in its source variable removes this factor. Right invariance at every $v\mid\ell$ removes the $\ell$-part. Since $a(\ell^k)$ is rational, on that progression
$$
 T_\Psi(1)=T_\Psi(a(\ell^k))=T_\Psi(a(\ell^k)_\infty).
$$
At each real place the remaining component goes out the adapted rational cusp, with sole logarithmic Witt coordinate $k\log\ell$. In the full-rank lattice estimate for fixed $h$, the nonzero minor in the expanding row gains $e^{k\log\ell}$. Taking more Schwartz powers than needed for the unrestricted lattice count gives $\theta(\Psi;a(\ell^k)_\infty,h)\to0$. For dominated convergence use the exponent-zero estimate uniform in the source, $|\theta(\Psi;g,h)|\le C\,\operatorname{ht}(h)^A$. Rational invariance permits minimizing the $h$-height, and rapidity of $F$ then gives a bounded majorant on the finite-volume target quotient. Hence $T_\Psi(1)=0$. For arbitrary $g$, apply the same argument to $\omega'(g,1)\Psi$, which is again primed; this yields $T_\Psi(g)=0$. The designated local rank condition is needed only for the tests whose orthogonality is asked for. [Full-rank lattice count](ROW_SQUARE_REGULARIZATION.md) · [Uniform majorant][cu-est] · [Comparison with the source's global-constancy argument][cu-van]

Finally the exponent-zero estimate and rapidity bound the absolute double integral over the two finite-volume quotients. Fubini identifies $\langle I_{s,t}(\Psi),F\rangle$ with the integral of $\overline{T_\Psi(g)}$, hence with zero. Together with big orthogonality this gives $\|F\|_2^2=0$, and continuity gives $F=0$. This proves upper proportionality on matched polynomial tests. It uses the lower big nonvanishing, Wu's tower theorems with the compatible splittings, and the good split quotient and distribution calculation. The reverse integral on the small side only had to be tested on functions with the prescribed full-rank support. [Pairing-to-orthogonality][cu-fub]


### The good-place kernel on both rectangular blocks

Let $v$ lie over the completely split rational prime chosen above. Put $k=F_v$, $Q=\#\mathbb F_v$, let $\varpi$ be a uniformizer, and take $r=a$ or $r=n$ with the same $d=a+n$. In barred split coordinates put $S=C_c^\infty(M_{r,d}(k))$. If $\eta,\chi$ are the left and right unitary determinant characters, the two actions are
$$
 L_Af(Y)=\eta(\det A)|\det A|^{d/2}f({}^t A\,Y),
 \qquad R_h f(Y)=\chi(\det h)|\det h|^{r/2}f(Yh).
$$
Here $\eta$ is the split ratio of the two splitting characters or its inverse. In the common orientation it is $\beta/\alpha$ on the big side and $\alpha/\beta$ on the small side. On both sides $\eta(\varpi)$ has infinite order at the chosen place. Let $D$ be the linear span of the differences $L_A f-f$. The good-place kernel assertion is $\ker(q|_S)=D$ for either of the prescribed quotient maps $q$. [Actions in the barred model][gk-act] · [Both orientations and the kernels][gk-both] · [Infinite-order uniformizer value](ROW_DEFINITE_REVERSE_LIFT.md#the-good-local-character)

First, every class in $S/D$ has a full-rank representative. Write a local test as a finite linear combination of ball indicators. For each ball, Smith row and column operations and row dilations put the first $r_0$ rows in prescribed integral residue classes with independent reductions and the remaining rows in $\mathcal O_k^d$. Starting with those $r_0$ independent rows, sieve out the next row if its residue lies in the span of the first $i$. There are $Q^i$ possible linear combinations. Each subtraction is a shear followed by division of that row by $\varpi$, and all the row operators have determinant $\varpi^{-1}$. The defining relation in $S/D$ therefore multiplies the class by
$$
 1-\eta(\varpi)Q^{\,i-d/2}.
$$
The small-source indices satisfy $i\le n-1<d/2$. On the big side $i$ can reach or exceed $d/2$. For $i\ne d/2$, the two terms have different absolute value. At the possible middle index the factor is $1-\eta(\varpi)\ne0$. Thus division at each step is valid for **both** rectangles, through the last index $i=r-1$. The final representative is compactly supported on the open full-row-rank locus $U_r$. This sieve takes place in the quotient by actual translates, before applying the given $q$. [Construction on arbitrary balls](ROW_DEFINITE_LOCAL_QUOTIENTS.md) · [General local surjectivity][gk-surj]

Second, the source coinvariants of $C_c^\infty(U_r)$ are an irreducible smooth $\mathrm{GL}_d(k)$-representation. The row-space map onto $\mathrm{Gr}_r(k^d)$ is a $\mathrm{GL}_r(k)$-torsor; each pivot chart has a section given by a frame with the pivot minor equal to the identity. Put $Y_0=[I_r\ 0]$. Weighted Haar integration on each fiber gives
$$
 Jf(g)=\chi(\det g)|\det g|^{r/2}
 \int_{\mathrm{GL}_r(k)}f(uY_0g)\,\eta(\det u)|\det u|^{d/2}\,d^\times u .
$$
The integral kills every $L_Af-f$. For $p=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}A&0\\ C&B\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ in the lower parabolic of type $(r,d-r)$, changing variable $u\mapsto uA$ yields
$$
 Jf(pg)=\delta_P(p)^{1/2}(\chi\eta^{-1})(\det A)\chi(\det B)\,Jf(g),
 \qquad
 \delta_P(p)=|\det B|^r|\det A|^{-(d-r)} .
$$
Thus the target is normalized induction of the displayed two unitary determinant characters. On a compact-open pivot chart choose a locally constant bump of weighted integral one in the fiber. This lifts every local section. Compactness of the Grassmannian gives a finite partition for a global section. In the same charts a compactly supported locally constant function is a finite sum of products of base and fiber functions. Refining the base partition reduces a zero fiber integral to a finite combination of fiber functions of weighted integral zero. Multiplication by the weight changes that condition to ordinary integral zero on $\mathrm{GL}_r(k)$. Such a function is a sum of left-translation differences: express it as a finite sum of indicators of left translates of one sufficiently small compact open subgroup, whose Haar measures are equal and whose coefficients sum to zero. Undo the weight to get the $L_A$ differences in the original coordinates. Hence $J$ induces the asserted isomorphism on full-rank coinvariants. Bernstein's irreducibility theorem applies since both determinant characters are smooth, irreducible and unitary on the two Levi blocks. [Bernstein, §0.2, Corollary and §8.2](https://www.math.tau.ac.il/~bernstei/Publication_list/publication_texts/Bernstein-P-invar-SLN.pdf) · [Pinned full-rank induced model][gk-ind] · [Passage to the matrix model][gk-transfer]

The sieve gives a surjection from this irreducible module onto $S/D$. The prescribed $q$ annihilates $D$, is onto a nonzero space, and its kernel is stable under the target action by matching equivariance. Consequently both quotients of the full-rank module are nonzero, and both quotient maps are isomorphisms. This proves $\ker(q|_S)=D$. In the upper cusp comparison, the good local matching maps themselves are still required. Once they have been supplied, the kernel equality adds just their barred split realization, the good character property, and Bernstein's theorem. For the compactness assertion used in the torus argument, write $B_H$ for the rational change of basis from the two Witt vectors and a basis of $V'_1$. ## Schwartz tests, uniqueness and the scope of the recurrence

The equality established above for polynomial vectors passes to the Schwartz tests in the definition of matching. Fix their finite expansion and the approximating archimedean vectors in that definition. On the small side every sum in the approximating sequence has full-rank support at the same split place. To use the seminorm estimate term by term when individual finite components in the given expansion have no support restriction, take a basis of their finite-dimensional span adapted to its subspace of functions with full-rank support there. The coefficients of the complementary basis vectors vanish for every approximant, by linear independence after restriction away from full rank. Taking the finite linear combinations of Schwartz limits gives the same conclusion for the limit. Each surviving finite component is now primed and fixed. The [pointwise estimate and its Schwartz seminorm](ROW_BETA_SQUARE_INTEGRABILITY.md) give convergence of its integrated theta function at every fixed target point. The big integral has the same continuity. This proves $I_{b,t}=c_t I_{s,t}$ on every matched pair. The detecting polynomial pair above has both integrals nonzero, so $c_t$ is nonzero and unique. This conclusion concerns an adapted pair of triangles with the specified matching maps. The comparison constant $C$ can depend on those planes, the measures, and the fixed real realizations. The [grid ratio calculation](ROW_FIRST_BETA_GRID_RATIOS.md) transfers these ratios across triangles, markings, row tests and measure changes. ## From the first beta model to the grid square

Fix $j=m-2$ and a tuple. Suppose the upper polynomial identity holds on the **realized** square model, or on a model identified with it by intertwiners carrying the two Weil actions, primed test space, measures and matching relation to the specified ones. Write $c_{\rm mod}$ for its constant. The comparison of the Darboux readings with the square's common quotient contributes the nonzero scalar $s$ of [the change of presentation](ROW_DEFINITE_GRID_TRANSFER.md#the-scalar-on-the-two-presentations-of-the-model). Given equal global images $p_AF=p_BF'$, take a polynomial representative $F_0$ of $F'$ whose small reading is primed at the designated place. The difference $F'-F_0$ is a finite sum of source-translation differences on polynomial data. Choose one fresh split place outside their supports, with vacuum there for **all** these finitely many tests, and apply its same nonzero-degree regularizer $w$ throughout. The resulting kernels of the auxiliary tests are absolutely integrable, $w$ commutes with those source translations, and their paired terms consequently integrate to the same number. Thus $I_s(w;F')=I_s(w;F_0)=d(w)I_s(1;F_0)$, the last equality using integrability of the primed representative. The uniqueness of the fresh-place regularized value gives $J_s(F')=I_s(1;F_0)$. Matching $F$ with $sF_0$ yields $I_A(F)=s\,c_{\rm mod}J_s(F')$. This argument never requires the original small kernel to be integrable.
