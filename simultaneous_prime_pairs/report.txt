The component obtains PrimesRel, twistK, meanSqK, the continuum bad-share

NAMED CLASSICAL INPUTS, NOT RECONSTRUCTED HERE

CN: the quadratic analytic class-number formula, including the real
regulator convention. It gives L(1,chi)>=c/sqrt(f) for real primitive
nonprincipal chi of conductor f. The elementary derivative calculation in
QP1 then gives the effective gap >=c/(m^2 log m) when f<=m.

BD: Bombieri's log-free density estimate and its exceptional-zero factor.
For the family of primitive conductors and heights <=R, its zero count in
Re(s)>=sigma is <=C R^(A(1-sigma)). If beta=1-delta is the exceptional
zero at scale R, deleting that one zero gives <=C delta log(R)
R^(A(1-sigma)). The constants are absolute. Reference: Bombieri, Le grand
crible dans la theorie analytique des nombres, Asterisque 18, section 6,
Theorem 14; first edition 1974, second edition 1987.

HD: Heath-Brown's hybrid density theorem, Canadian Journal of Mathematics
31 (1979), 231–240, Theorem 1. For fixed h>0 the primitive sum is
O_h((Q^2 T)^(2(1-sigma)+h)), uniformly for sigma>=11/14. Only the fixed
choice sigma=399/400, T=Q, h=1/100 is needed. The exponent is 9/200,
which is strictly less than the source endpoint's 1/4.

These inputs are established classical theorems but their full proofs have
not been expanded in this manuscript. Their precise hypotheses and roles
are retained in the ledgers. Neither a citation nor an interface wrapper is
counted as a newly reconstructed proof of them. The source's particular
large numerical density constants are not inferred from existential C.

1. RELATIVE PROGRESSION ESTIMATES

QP1 proves the effective exceptional-zero gap using CN, periodic character
sums and a uniform derivative bound near one. QP2 gives the across-modulus
Page uniqueness argument from the nonnegative logarithmic derivative of
zeta L(chi1) L(chi2) L(chi1 chi2); equality of primitive characters is
distinguished from merely having the same character values at one level.

QP3 applies BD at the cutoff R=m(T+3). If the fixed-modulus exceptional
zero is still exceptional there, its delta log R factor is retained.
Otherwise that factor is bounded below and the ordinary estimate suffices.
An exact integration of the density function proves the weighted zero-sum
bound. This is a relative estimate: discarding the exceptional factor would
lose control when the positive main term is small.

QP4 takes T=m^8 exp(sqrt(log u)). The right-half explicit formula is
obtained from CC by combining near-zero terms with the pole-at-zero
constant before bounding them. The signed local identity is
b0=sum_(rho in Z_chi(1)) m_rho/rho+O(log(N+2)); the minus sign in the
explicit-formula zero sum therefore gives -m_rho(u^rho-1)/rho.
The right-half constant terms have |rho|>=1/2 and are harmless.
The small denominator is replaced by
(u^rho-1)/rho, whose integral representation is harmless. Character
orthogonality then yields a relative error for
  phi(m) psi(u;m,a), main = u-theta u^beta/beta.
The main term is >=u min(1,(1-beta)log u)/4; BD supplies that same small
factor in the zero error, while QP1 absorbs the remaining contour error.

QP5 removes prime powers and applies partial summation on [sqrt(u),u].
It checks the exact main term
  Li(u)-theta Li(u^beta), Li(u)=integral_2^u dt/log(t).
There is no 1/beta in this expression. All constants precede the modulus,
the residue class and the exceptional character. This proves PrimesRel at
u>=m^C under CN and BD. The stronger-looking source wrapper PNTAPRelW has
a fixed 1/(8 log m) definition of exception; this manuscript does not
identify that literal wrapper. The prime-correlation proof uses PrimesRel
with its sufficiently small existential c1 directly.

2. FROM PROGRESSIONS TO MEAN SQUARES

QP6 derives the actual U^(9/10) lower count in every admitted progression.
It remains valid when an exceptional zero suppresses the main term because
the effective gap is polynomial in the modulus and log(m)=o(log U).

QP7 sums relative progression errors over CRT fibers. Orthogonality kills
the exceptional contribution unless its character restricts to the
specified Jacobi character on the fiber. Page uniqueness allows at most
one squarefree index to be both aligned and very exceptional. Outside
that index the normalized correlation is O(Y^-B) for any fixed B. Relative
errors are summed against positive main terms, avoiding a spurious
phi(s) loss. This is the actual twistK conclusion.

QP8 proves coefficient estimates from Chebyshev's elementary bound. For
c_y(n)=Lambda(n)min(1,y/n), prime-power blocks have size O(log y), their
squared sum is O(y log y), and the square or modulus-supported block is
O(sqrt(y)+w log y). It also derives the Mertens upper bound with leading
coefficient exactly one, by the factorial identity.

QP9 partitions every prime power into its square/modulus-supported block
or its odd prime component. The square expansion then has only two
possible exceptional shapes: one prime, or a product of two distinct
primes. Their contributions are bounded explicitly by 2BR+2B^2. This works
for every bounded coefficient sequence; multiplicativity is not required.
QP10 applies it on [Y,Y^K] with cutoff (Y^K)^400, proving meanSqK with
one absolute constant and a threshold depending on epsilon,K.

QP11 addresses the real continuum rather than a discretized substitute.
The exact right derivative D satisfies
  y D(y)=S(y, kappa times indicator(n>y)).
The all-bounded-sequences mean square therefore controls D as well.
The fundamental theorem of calculus and Cauchy–Schwarz allow each prime
to choose its own witness y in a dyadic interval. QP12 then sums Markov
bounds over dyadic intervals, obtaining a bad proportion at most
32C/[tau(1-2^-tau)] Y^-tau. Finite base-character families are handled
by a union bound with the threshold chosen before the family varies.

3. A SHORTER PROOF OF TRUNCATION AND THE EXACT DERIVATIVE CONSTANT

QP13 extends CC's contour argument to heights T>u while retaining the
left-edge term that CC had absorbed under T<=u. The Perron nearest-term
error remains O(log u), and the good contour heights lie below Q. A box
free of zeros with real part >399/400 also excludes reflected zeros with
real part <1/400 at those heights. Thus reciprocal zero weights are
uniformly controlled.

QP14 splits the tail of sum Lambda(n)chi(n)/n at
  V=exp(B (log Q)^2).
Below V, the finite-height box and explicit formula give the required
power saving. Above V, the real-zero exclusion from the same box permits
CC's uniform global estimate. Choosing fixed B sufficiently large makes
that last integral polynomially small in Q. The result, for 3<=Z<=V, is
  |tail(Z)| <= C [Z^(-1/400)(log Q)^2 + (log Q)^6/Q
                     + Z^-1 log^2(QZ)].
For an imprimitive nonprincipal character, the primitive inducer has the
same box in Re(s)>0. The difference of the tails at its original level is
at most 2 log(N)/Z, obtained by summing the geometric tails at the deleted
primes. This is already absorbed by the displayed estimate. Consequently
the bound holds for literal GoodC, without a primitivity assumption.
At Z=(Y^K)^400 this proves tailK. The exact centered identity includes
the term -y tail(Z); it is not an absolute-convergence assertion.

QP15 instead takes Z=(log Q)^1000. The tail is bounded, so the leading
coefficient-one Mertens estimate gives
  |L'/L(1,chi)| <= 1000 log log Q + C.
The original height window and epsilon<1/52 imply the exact 4 log Y
bound, since 200epsilon<50/13<4. This leaves a fixed margin 2/13 to
absorb log log Y and constants. An unspecified coefficient C log Y
would not suffice for PairGood; the exact four is checked.

4. CHOOSE BOTH PRIMES AND TRANSFER ALL QUANTIFIERS

QP16 uses HD to bound bad primitive boxes by O(Q^(1/4)). The primitive
principal character can be counted separately, so the source's totalized
pole convention cannot add an uncounted exception. A prime q>F assigned
conductor f q with f<=F is determined by that conductor. This injection
controls the number of bad primes even when a different witness character
is selected for each prime.

QP17 proves primitivity of coprime-conductor quadratic twists and the
uniqueness of every primitive inducer allowed by the source Ind relation
at a positive ambient modulus, as required by the literal ind_unique.
QP18 combines the bad-box count with the continuum bad-share estimate;
the former costs O(U^(3/8)) while the progression contains at least
U^(9/10) primes. The truncation formula supplies the centered square-root
bound for all y in the polynomial interval.

QP19 chooses q=3 mod 8 in its first exponential window, simultaneously
over all characters of the fixed base modulus. QP20 chooses d=7 mod 16
and d=-1 modulo q times the odd primes <=Y^(epsilon/5). The modulus has
logarithm <=4Y^(epsilon/5). Applying the generic stage with exponent
6epsilon/5 preserves every admitted inequality and accommodates q as the
one modulus prime larger than the truncation. Both stages have positive
good proportions and thresholds uniform in the previously chosen prime.

QP21 chooses an actual pair from these nonempty subsets. QP22 applies
the result with base modulus 8M and checks every PairGood field: exact
windows and congruences, coprimality, all primitive twisted-character
quantifiers, centered bounds throughout [Y,Y^A], derivative coefficient
four, and the prescribed j-dependent heights of the zero boxes.

QP23 splits each real primitive host character into its base factor and
its q,d quadratic components. Odd conductor exponents are <=1 and the
two-adic exponent is <=3. This supplies RealBox and the exact conductor
bound on the already specified compatible host. QP24 uses it to discharge
the real-zero-exclusion premise of CC's ThinH for that same host.

QP25 checks the exact rescaling in winPairs, including its 401-to-400
cutoff and [Z,Z^(3/2)] interval. The geometric VWinAt(psi,401) premise
remains genuine. It is not supplied by a mean square or by PairEx.
