# The finite positive-defect energy at a nonsingular corank-one point

On the local frame and mass comparisons in B8.10d, the squared amplitude for positive saturation defect satisfies
$$
 \sum_d w_d |a_N(d)|^2
       \le H^{20(r+1)^2}\,q^{-(k-1)} n.                          \tag{1}
$$
Here $k\ge4r+7$, $r\ge1$, $q$ is the residue cardinality, $H$ the local point height and $n$ the unsigned reference mass. The original bound uses $H^{39(r+1)^2}q^{-(k-2r-5)}$. Grouping by the defect order before applying Cauchy–Schwarz uses the stronger exponents. The argument also gives a bound for the global finite product which retains the size of each defect prime.

## Local inputs and the normalization of the move

Use the actual lattice presentations $d,\Lambda$, their inverse-stabilizer weights, frame lattice $M$, saturation quotient $D$, positive defect $\beta=|D|=q^i$, and image cardinality $q^l$ in $D$, $0\le l\le i$. The defect exponent satisfies $i\le a$ with $q^a\le H$. At an active place $H\ge q$. Put $D_k=k-1$, $s=r+1$ and
$$
 A_0=H^{9s^2+12s},\qquad A_1=4H^{4s}.
$$
The two unsigned local counting inputs are the following. At fixed $d$, presentations of image size $q^l$ have mass at most $A_0q^{lD_k}$. Summed over $d$ with weights $w_d$, presentations of defect order $\beta$ have mass at most $A_1\beta^{-D_k}n$. These are counts of integral frames and their overlattices on the stated local model. On the first-type part of a positive defect one has $l<i$. The second-type part partitions by the largest occupied flag level $J$ and the number of classes at level $J$. Neither $J$ nor this latter cardinality is fixed when applying (1). [Mass at fixed image cardinality][im] · [Distribution of the saturation index][mass] · [First-type and second-type partition][partition]

In a second-type family the symmetric elementary move gives
$$
 |a_{\rm family}(d)|\le 7q^{-(k-2r-4)}m_{\rm family}(d),           \tag{2}
$$
where $m$ denotes the unsigned mass at this $d$. Here are the normalizations needed for this assertion. The [last-layer character](SPECTRAL_MOVE_CHARACTER_CONDUCTOR.md) is nontrivial, the exact-level pair share is $\beta_J/\beta$, and the affine trace-zero scalar fibre has measure one whenever the norm congruence holds. The [twisted norm-congruence calculation](SPECTRAL_TWISTED_NORM_CONGRUENCE.md) bounds the unsigned normalized row error and the signed row by $(24/7)q^{1-\rho}$, where $\rho=k-2r-3$. The transported witnesses preserve the [original datum and its phase ratio](FLAG_BLOCK_ENERGY_AND_CUTOFF.md#claim:FBE71). The common-prefix quotient argument in the character calculation then shows that each primitive move stays in the family. We spell out why forward and backward reference measures have the same scale.

In the notation of the character calculation put $A=\Lambda\cap Y$, $U=\Lambda\cap Y^\perp$. The sum map identifies the lattice of prefix pairs $b+b'\in\theta\Lambda$ with $\theta A$, since the two prefixes are independent. Furthermore
$$
            A/(M\cap Y)\ \simeq\ (\Lambda\cap B)/M .
$$
Surjectivity is the tail subtraction in the proof of the character formula. By independence of the two full frames, $M\cap Y$ is exactly the lattice generated by the two prefix frames with the same $\theta$-scaled differences. Its volume is independent of the integral witness: in fixed flag coordinates the two prefix transformations are unipotent triangular matrices of determinant one; the difference scaling gives a fixed determinant. Consequently the Haar volume of $A$ is $\beta$ times a fixed constant. The self-dual lattice $\Lambda$ maps onto the integral dual of $A$ under Hermitian pairing, by saturation and extension of linear forms. Its kernel is $U$. In the fixed ambient exact sequence $0\to Y^\perp\to V\to Y^{\vee}\to0$, the volume of $\Lambda$ is the same for all self-dual $\Lambda$ and the volume of the dual of $A$ is a fixed constant divided by the volume of $A$. It follows that the volume of $U$ is proportional to the volume of $A$. These arguments allow a degenerate restriction of the form to $Y$. Hence the volume of the move reference lattice (prefix pairs, perpendicular vectors, trace-zero scalar) is a fixed constant times $\beta^2$. In particular it agrees at the two ends of every retained move of fixed $\beta,J$.

The move with isotropic $b$, $u\perp b$, and $c+\bar c=h(u,u)$ is $z\mapsto z+h(z,u)b-h(z,b)(u+cb)$. Expansion of the Hermitian pairing shows it is an isometry and substitution gives the inverse with $(u,c)$ replaced by $(-u,\bar c)$. In trace-zero coordinates $t=c-h(u,u)/2$ reversal sends $(u,t)$ to $(-u,-t)$ and fixes the prefix pair. This preserves Haar measure in the fixed parameter space. With the original reverse admissibility of the move in the same family, the equality of reference scales just proved gives symmetry of the normalized kernel. For a fixed starting lattice the possible parameters are compact: the prefix pair and $u$ lie in compact lattices and $c$ in $\pi^aO_E$. The target lattice depends locally constantly on these parameters since its stabilizer is open. Only finitely many targets occur. For the invariant kernel, unfolding against inverse stabilizer volumes interchanges start and finish. Apply the signed row bound on one side and replace the unsigned row sum by one on the other at a second cost of $(24/7)q^{1-\rho}$. This gives (2), since $48/7<7$. For the reverse admission, the inverse congruence in FBE71 puts $-u$ in the moved lattice. Both prefix frames remain fixed. Thus the pair keeps its level, and $(x+x')/\theta$ remains integral there. Conjugation preserves the scalar ideal and the trace. These are the move conditions at the reverse endpoint whenever it belongs to this same family. ## Keeping the defect order fixed through the squared estimate

At fixed $i>0$ let $P_i(d),F_i(d)$ denote the two unsigned masses of the first and second types. The sum of the absolute bounds (2) over the second-type subfamilies costs at most $\epsilon F_i(d)$ with $\epsilon=7q^{-(k-2r-4)}$, since their masses are disjoint. For the signed subtotal $a_i(d)$ of *all* terms of defect $q^i$, positivity and Cauchy–Schwarz give
$$
 |a_i(d)|^2\le [P_i(d)+\epsilon F_i(d)]^2
 \le [P_i(d)+F_i(d)]\,[P_i(d)+\epsilon^2 F_i(d)] .
$$
The bound at fixed image size and its geometric progression give
$$
 P_i(d)\le\frac{A_0(\beta/q)^{D_k}}{1-q^{-D_k}},
 \qquad F_i(d)\le\frac{A_0\beta^{D_k}}{1-q^{-D_k}} .
$$
Use these in the *second* bracket. Sum the first bracket over $d$ using the actual mass law for this $\beta$. The power $\beta^{D_k}$ cancels and
$$
 \|a_i\|_{L^2(w)}^2\le
 \frac{A_0A_1}{1-q^{-D_k}}\big[q^{-D_k}+49q^{-2(k-2r-4)}\big]\,n .
$$
The rank condition gives $2(k-2r-4)\ge k-1$, including equality at the lowest permitted rank. There are at most $a$ positive exponents $i$. Adding their norms before squaring gives an overall factor $a^2$. Thus a more precise version of (1) retains $a^2 A_0 A_1$ and the bracket displayed above. For (1), use $1/(1-q^{-D_k})\le2$, $a\le H$, $400\le H^9$ when any positive defect occurs, and $9s^2+16s+11\le20s^2$ for $s\ge2$. When positive defects do not occur, the left side is zero. For the generic subtotal $a_Z$, every contributing image has cardinality one. Hence at every place
$$
                 \|a_Z\|_{L^2(w)}^2\le A_0 n
$$
by the fixed-image bound and $|a_Z(d)|\le m_Z(d)$. This also works when $H=1$. [Two individual family bounds][first] · [Signed family and its unsigned normalization][second]

## Global finite product and corank-one energy

Fix a represented nonsingular point and set $\Omega$ to the places where positive defect or a mass comparison can occur. At fixed $\gamma$, for each defect subset $J$ write $V_J$ for its actual class-amplitude vector with the original squared-mass weights (including the normal or multiplicity variable). The class-to-local comparison of B8.08n and Claims 4–5 in B8.10d bounds $\|V_J\|^2$ by $V_0 J_2(\gamma)$ times the product of the squared local norms with $a_N$ on $J$ and $a_Z$ elsewhere. Here $J_2$ is the common squared real-amplitude factor of that comparison and $V_0$ the class mass normalization. This is a separate squared bound for each subset, sufficient even when the attained tuples or phases differ between subsets. At $J_2=0$ all the vectors vanish. At $J_2>0$, applying the triangle inequality *in the original class Hilbert space* gives
$$
 \frac{\|\sum_{J\ne\varnothing} V_J\|}{\sqrt{J_2(\gamma)}}\le
 \sqrt{V_0}\,\prod_v H_v^{10s^2}\sqrt{n_v}\,
       \left[\prod_{v\in\Omega}(1+q_v^{-(k-1)/2})-1\right].        \tag{3}
$$
Here and below a convergent reference product is understood in the original mass normalization. More generally the part having at least $h\ge1$ positive places has the same bound with the bracket replaced by $e^S S^h/h!$, where $S=\sum_{v\in\Omega}q_v^{-(k-1)/2}$. This follows by bounding the elementary symmetric sums by $S^j/j!$. [Subset expansion and orbit factorization][assembly]

In the selected range let $z=(k-1)/2$. At most $f=[F:\mathbb Q]$ prime ideals have any prescribed residue cardinality $n\ge2$: $n$ is a power of one rational prime, with at most $f$ places above it. Thus
$$
 S\le f\,2^{-z}\left(1+\frac{2}{z-1}\right)
$$
by integral comparison. Since $k\ge11$ and $f\le k$ on the prescribed family, this is at most one (the upper bound with $f=k$ decreases for $k\ge11$). Hence $e^S-1\le2S$ and the square of the bracket in (3) is at most $9f^2\,2^{-(k-1)}$. If all possible positive defect primes have norm at least $Q\ge2$, use $f Q^{-z}(1+Q/(z-1))$ in the exponential instead, restricting the expansion to the places actually admitting $a_N\ne0$.

With the project's comparison $n_v\le8H_v^{9s^2+2m}n_v^{\rm gen}$ on $\Omega$, $n_v=n_v^{\rm gen}$ elsewhere, and $m=2s$, this gives the finite squared class factor
$$
           9 f^2\,2^{-(k-1)} H(\gamma)^{29s^2+2m+3}\,
                                   n_{\rm gen}(\gamma).             \tag{4}
$$
Indeed $8^{|\Omega|}\le H(\gamma)^3$ and $n_{\rm gen}=V_0\prod_v n_v^{\rm gen}$. Substitution of the [normalized label multiplicity](SPECTRAL_CORANK_NORMAL_LABELS.md) in the weighted class Cauchy–Schwarz step then uses only $C^f\widehat N_G$ from counting the labels. With the specified generic mass, reciprocal point weight, archimedean integral and measure comparisons of B8.10db–dc and B8.10h, the remaining factors are bounded exactly as in B8.10d: $H,\widehat N_G$ are at least one on represented support, $\widehat N_G\le N_G$, and the new height exponent is below the original one. The extra $9f^2 C^f$ is absorbed in $\exp(C'm^5 f\log b)$. The resulting conditional estimate is
$$
 P(U_{\rm ng})\le 2^{-(k-1)}\exp(C'm^5f\log b)\,\Delta_{u,v}.
$$
This is the nongeneric **corank-one** energy contribution. The generic scalar, higher corank, and signed first-moment estimates require their own arguments. The frame counting and real-energy inputs are explicitly retained in the deduction.
