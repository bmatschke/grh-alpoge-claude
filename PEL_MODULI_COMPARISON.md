# The fine moduli space of the marked unitary abelian varieties

The interior family in the construction is a classical PEL moduli space. Its points classify principally polarized abelian varieties with an action of a CM order, prescribed multiplicities for that action on tangent spaces, and a finite marking. The abelian varieties themselves can vary in positive dimension. At each real place this freedom is the choice of a positive complex plane in a Hermitian space. The theta line on the universal abelian scheme is determined by the polarization and the element of trace one in the order.

**Theorem.** For every host, lattice datum and level of the original construction with $p+q>0$, there is a smooth quasi-projective fine moduli scheme over $K_A(\ell)=\mathbf Q(\rho,\zeta_A)$ of relative dimension $[E:\mathbf Q]pq/2$. Its universal family, with its canonical cubical line and both torsion markings, satisfies the original definition of `Moduli`, including its assertion about dual numbers. This proves the interior existence assertion `N1ab`. We use Kai-Wen Lan's integral PEL moduli theorem in characteristic zero. Here “integral” refers to the lattice and its Tate modules at *every* finite prime. [Original assertion][n1ab] · [Definition of the required space][target] · [Lan, Definition 1.2.2 and Theorem 1.2.4][lan]

## The signature determines the field

Write $L$ for the given projective $\mathcal O_E$-lattice, $B$ for its unimodular alternating form, and $K=\mathbf Q(r)\subset E$, where $r^2=-d$. Identify $K$ with its image in $\mathbf C$ by the embedding $\rho$ taking $r$ to the distinguished square root. Set $f=[E:K]$. The conjugation on $E$ is adjoint for $B$. Choose the complex structure $J$ supplied by the lattice datum. With Lan's Tate lattice $\mathbf Z(1)$ one can use the form $(-2\pi i)B$ and $h_0(i)=J$. Indeed $-i\langle x,Jx\rangle=2\pi B(Jx,x)>0$ for $x\ne0$. In writing finite pairing exponents relative to $B$, the generator of this copy of the Tate lattice is $-2\pi i$. The generator at level $A$ is specified intrinsically by the finite pairing in the marking comparison below. The adjoint identity for $h_0$ follows from $J^2=-1$ and invariance of $B$ under $J$. These are precisely Lan's PEL lattice axioms. [Original lattice datum][datum] · [Lan, §1.1 and Lemma 2.1.2][linear]

For $a\in E$, put $T(a)=\operatorname{Tr}_{E/K}(a)$. The complex trace of $a$ on $(L_{\mathbf R},J)$ is
$$
p\,\rho(T(a))+q\,\overline{\rho(T(a))}.
$$
In fact the source specifies the real traces of $a$ and $Ja$ as twice the real part and minus twice the imaginary part of this expression. Together they determine the complex trace. The embeddings $E\to\mathbf C$ are linearly independent, so the resulting complex multiplicity is $p$ at embeddings extending $\rho$ and $q$ at the other embeddings. Lan's description of the reflex field as the field generated by these traces gives $F_0=K$ if $p\ne q$ and $F_0=\mathbf Q$ if $p=q$. For the first statement evaluate at $r$, whose trace is $f(p-q)\rho(r)$. Thus $F_0$ embeds into the required $K_A(\ell)$ at every level, including $A=1$. [Original field][target] · [Lan, Corollary 1.2.5.6][reflex]

This also identifies Lan's determinant condition over an arbitrary $K_A(\ell)$-scheme $S$. After a finite étale extension splitting $E$, the idempotents in $E\otimes_{\mathbf Q}\mathcal O_S$ split the Lie bundle into locally free summands $V_\tau$. On a geometric fiber the source's dual-number tangent vectors of character $\tau$ are exactly the vectors in $V_\tau$. Its predicate that they admit a basis of length $p$ or $q$ fixes the rank of each summand. Consequently the characteristic polynomial of $a$ acting on the Lie bundle is
$$
\prod_{\tau|_K=\rho}(X-\tau(a))^{p}
\prod_{\tau|_K=\bar\rho}(X-\tau(a))^{q}.
$$
The same computation applies with a universal variable element $a$ and gives Lan's determinant polynomial law. Conversely a primitive element of $E/\mathbf Q$ has distinct conjugates and its characteristic polynomial recovers every rank after splitting. Ranks of locally free summands are detected on fibers even on a nonreduced scheme. This proves the equivalence on dual numbers as well as on the reduced bases in the universal property. [Source tangent spaces and signature][lie] · [Lan, §§1.2.5 and 1.3.4][reflex]

## The family over the specified field

Use Lan's moduli problem with the set of non-inverted primes empty. It classifies actual abelian schemes and actual isomorphisms, with the given endomorphism action and an integral level orbit on the paired Tate modules. The integral pairing on $L$ is perfect. In this moduli problem a paired Tate isomorphism therefore forces the polarization to induce an isomorphism on every Tate module. In characteristic zero its finite kernel is trivial, so the polarization is principal. The proper semiabelian groups in the source are abelian schemes. [Lan, Definition 1.2.2 and Remark 1.2.3][lan] · [Abelian schemes in the original construction][abelian]

Two identifications give the universal object its precise meaning in the source.

- The two marked sections form a single orbit under the subgroup fixing the prescribed half of $L/ML$ and all of $L/AL$. This comparison includes the existence of full compatible Tate coordinates at every geometric point and the root occurring in their Weil pairing. The geometric automorphism group of a marked object is trivial. The full proof works in families, including at levels where $A$ and $M$ have common divisors. [The marked-level comparison](PEL_MARKED_LEVEL.md)
- The principal polarization $\lambda$ determines the line by pulling the Poincaré biextension $B_\lambda$ back along $x\mapsto(x,w_0x)$. The identity $w_0+\bar w_0=1$ makes its polarization $\lambda$. Conversely the source's graph identity recovers this line up to unique rigidified isomorphism. The comparison respects the normalized cube and all cartesian morphisms. [The theta-line comparison](PEL_THETA_LINE.md)

Lan's Theorem 1.2.4 now gives a smooth quasi-projective scheme. Base change it to $K_A(\ell)$ and take the locus on which the primitive pairing value at $A$ equals the prescribed $\zeta_A$. This is open and closed because the value is a section of a finite étale scheme. Retain the entire locus. In particular, the source asks only for the geometric paired Tate lattice to be isomorphic to $L\otimes\widehat{\mathbf Z}$ and for the specified tangent signature. A rational homology type, or an identification with the integral lattice $L$ over $\mathbf Z$, is additional information. Lan explicitly distinguishes the analytic Shimura variety for a fixed rational type from the full PEL moduli space, identifying the former with an open and closed subspace over $\mathbf C$. Using the full moduli problem here avoids any assumption that a local genus has a single rational or integral class. Its open and closed locus is still quasi-projective. The resulting immersion in projective space is quasi-compact since the ground field is noetherian and the scheme has finite type. [Lan, Theorem 1.2.4][lan] · [Lan, Definition 2.5.7][components] · [Original quasi-projectivity][qproj]

At a complex point the polarized Hodge filtration splits into conjugate pairs indexed by the $f$ places of $E^+$. In each pair one chooses a $p$-plane in dimension $p+q$. Its annihilator in the conjugate space is forced by the polarization. Positivity is an open condition. The tangent space to the Grassmannian at a plane $U$ is $\operatorname{Hom}(U,\mathbf C^{p+q}/U)$, of dimension $pq$. Thus each component has complex dimension $fpq$. This computation also covers $p=0$ or $q=0$, when the moduli scheme is étale. The finite markings have no infinitesimal variation. Smoothness and relative dimension can be checked after extension from $K_A(\ell)$ to $\mathbf C$, giving the exact dimension in the theorem. [Lan, Lemma 2.1.2 and §2.4][period] · [Dimension in the source][target]

## Why the tangent assertion follows

Let $X$ be this scheme. Give its universal abelian scheme the canonical theta line and markings just described. For an object over a reduced $K_A(\ell)$-scheme $S$, its resulting PEL structure determines a unique classifying map $S\to X$. The isomorphism to the pullback of the universal PEL family preserves the line, its cube, the CM action and the two markings by the two comparisons. It is therefore a source `ObjMap`. Conversely every such cartesian map gives an isomorphism of the PEL families, which forces the classifying map to be the same. This proves the stated universal property. [Original objects and maps][obj] · [Universal property][target]

Finally let $D_k=\operatorname{Spec} k[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2)$ and $x:D_k\to X$. The map $x_0^{\mathrm{const}}$ reduces $x$ to $\operatorname{Spec} k$ and extends it constantly back to $D_k$. Both maps have the same composite with $\operatorname{Spec} K_A(\ell)$, since a number field is formally étale over $\mathbf Q$. If one source object admits cartesian maps to the universal family over $x$ and $x_0^{\mathrm{const}}$, then it gives isomorphic marked PEL families over $D_k$ for the two maps. Lan's scheme represents the functor on *all* schemes over the field, so $x=x_0^{\mathrm{const}}$. This is exactly `Moduli.unram`. When the relative dimension is positive, nonconstant infinitesimal deformations change the isomorphism class of the marked family. [Dual-number statement][target] · [Lan's category of base schemes][lan]

The original formal construction reaches the same interior existence assertion by obtaining a fine space at a deeper prime-power level and descending to the requested level. In the human proof Lan's theorem and the two comparisons above supply that entire interior step. The next geometric work is the identification of the particular analytic charts and their universal families, and the compactification and boundary data needed later. Those require their own comparison with the original chart and model definitions. [Original descent proof][formalproof] · [Subsequent model structure][model]
