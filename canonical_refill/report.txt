2. RF1: the canonical selector and the finite group facts
--------------------------------------------------------
For h,t in A, the set of natural v for which ofNat(v)+i*h=t for some
i>=0 is nonempty: v=t.val works with i=0. Use its least element.
The possible v are identical for t and t+h. Given an exponent i for t,
i+1 works for t+h. Given an exponent i for t+h, i+ord(h)-1 works for t
because ord(h)*h=0 and ord(h)>0. Thus the selector is constant on every
h-orbit and actually belongs to it. This proves bsH_orb and bsH_step.

The later odd-order argument uses the actual Frame structure. It has a
cyclic subgroup A0, a homomorphism h:A -> a finite 2-group, injective on
A0, and o=[A:A0]. If h(t)=0, then o*t belongs to A0 by the order of the
quotient, and its image is zero, so o*t=0. If t has odd order, the order
of its image divides both that odd order and a power of two. Hence its
image is zero and ord(t) divides o. This proof is the content used from
FrameFacts; it is not an assumption that every arbitrary large order is
even. The actual size datum bounds o<=x^(epsilon/100).

3. RF2: offsets, positions and the defining instance
----------------------------------------------------
The position map sends (false,t) to tau1(t) and (true,t) to tau2(t).
Its injectivity follows from the injectivity of iota and the different
first signs of tau1,tau2. Consequently the labels of the actual top and
thick places are exactly:
  top:   false and t in TT;
  thick: true and t in image(s -> dig(s)+u0, T2).
They are disjoint. Thin means neither.

For j in J subtract b_T(t) from the actual weight at a top, and subtract
zero at all other positions. The source win_top, win_thick, win_thin give
the three closed intervals in RF.5. For j outside J choose their lower
endpoints. This proves every Inst.off bound, including all natural j.
Adding back b_T at a top recovers w_j exactly for j in J. Since the
position bijection is the supplied one, this is the exact source
inst_fill identity for any tau,z with eTau(tau)=plGG(z).

The partner map is
  partner(p,t,e)=( IsSg(p) AND Odd(e), t+e*h_p ).
In particular the sign changes at odd steps only for a signal prime.
No parity or orbit representative is replaced. Setting wPart to fill at
that partner proves the literal partner identity for every exponent.

If w''>=1, top fill>=b_T+b'/x>=b'/x=n*w''>=1, thick fill>=b>=1, and
thin fill>=w''>=1. This proves the all-index lower bound wPart_ge.

Set ep(p)=ceil_+(Ce*f*k^2*log_p(a)). Set kapI to the infimum of the
actual universal kapISet, which includes the nonnegativity condition and
quantifies over the full unramified-quadratic local coefficient settings.
The universally trivial loss kappa=1 gives a rank-zero certificate, so
this set is nonempty and bounded below by zero. Its infimum is attained
because the required output rank is an integer: for any fixed local
problem, approximate the infimum closely enough that the required real
rank still lies above the integer immediately below its ceiling, and use
the integer certificate supplied by that approximating loss. This is the
GJ1 argument already retained in the note, not a new estimate on kapI.

EpDef only identifies the displayed ceiling. EpTie says that the same
integer equals the e in the actual local model's Mna-to-M sandwich. RF2
proves EpDef; LedOK supplies EpTie at RF7. These assertions must not be
interchanged, and the e term is retained in tauP.

4. RF3: exact exceptional support
---------------------------------
Use the finite set S_tw of nontrivial twists and the finite word set U.
Assume DGam_u>0 for every u. The integer B_S of RF.7 dominates X'', both
auxiliary primes, d, every T_u and every DGam_u. All primes p>B_S are
therefore different from those named primes, and cannot divide DGam_u.

The set of w over p is finite: p is nonzero in the characteristic-zero
number ring, so the prime ideal of any w over p is a divisor of (p).
The factorization of the nonzero ideal (p) has finite support. This also
handles the source finite_over statement at p=0: no finite place has
residue characteristic zero. Taking the finite union for 0<=p<=B_S,
then adding S_tw, gives S. No bound on the *number* of places is needed.

This is the source's Big predicate, with all five parts retained. Its
twist condition is used to ensure every nontrivial twist lies in support;
the forcing and error cutoffs themselves depend on the residue prime and
the named exceptional primes. The support may be larger than needed;
minimal support or a canonical smallest support is not claimed.

5c. Forcing values.
For a length d>=1 and real vector D_s indexed by Z/d, define
  lpF(p,d,D)=p/(p^2-1)*sum_s D_s - sup lpFeasible(p,d,D).
The feasible real numbers are sum_{s,i<d}x_{s,i}, with x_{s,i}>=0,
x_{s,i}=0 unless i is odd and i<d, and every row satisfies
  sum_{i<d}p^i*x_{s,i} + sum_{i<d}x_{s-i,i} <= D_s.
This is exactly the source finite linear-program definition, with its
given supremum convention. RF does not prove a new optimizer estimate.

Let pos_p((delta,t),e) be (delta XOR [IsSg(p) and Odd(e)],t+e*h_p).
It has period n_p. For z in {false,true}*A set

                s -> (k-1)*w_j(tau(pos_p(z,s.val)))).
Then
  FInert_{jp}=sum_tau (k-1)*w_j(tau)/(p^2-1)
       if p is inert and h_p=0;
  FInert_{jp}=sum_z FOrb_{jp}(z)/n_p otherwise.
Changing the starting point shifts the cyclic variables in the finite
linear program and preserves both its feasible objective set and its
linear sum. Thus FOrb is constant on the orbit. Dividing the all-position
sum by n_p counts each actual orbit once. These identities match the
source FOrb_step and sum_orbits, and they explain the normalization.

fockDeg_j=sum_tau(sum_i |kaBig(j,tau,i)|+sum_i |kaSmall(j,tau,i)|),

  FRamD_j= -(k-1)/2 * sum_t min(w_j(false,t),w_j(true,t)).

Complex conjugation preserves the residue rational prime: for an ideal

over p. Equivalently, if two different primes p,q had positive valuation
at the same conjugate place, Bezout would force positive valuation of 1.
Thus Fval is conjugation invariant because it factors through p_w.

7. RF6: the exact NoGain exclusion
---------------------------------
NoGain(p) is the disjunction of:
  (a) p<X'*(1+Cr*rhoB), p inert, and at least one of
      ord(h_p)<x^(epsilon/4);
      p>=p0 and some i>=1 has |p^i-X'|<=rhoB*X';
      Cand(p) and Sig_p>x^(-epsilon);
  (b) p>X'';
  (c) p is neither inert nor signal;
  (d) IsCs(p), meaning inert with nonzero odd-order h_p.
If VV is nonempty, its defining if-condition yields Cand and the loss
bound. This directly contradicts (a)'s first and third alternatives.
For the second, Cand already gives p<X', so the absolute-value condition
gives precisely both InBand power inequalities; Cand excludes that band.
Candidate p<X'<=X'' excludes (b), and its class excludes (c).
The finite-group argument in section 2 and o<=x^(epsilon/100) exclude
(d). Thus no property of the sign or size of Cr or rhoB is needed for
this exclusion: their first guard is simply irrelevant once every
internal alternative is impossible. This is literal not_noGain.

9. RF8: the final output is an actual lattice setter
--------------------------------------------------
Let g_kept(j,w)=0 on drop and gain(j,w) otherwise. Let
  s(u,w)=sum_{j in J}(g_kept(j,w)+F(j,w)-E(u,j,w)).
Define I_u as the finite product of p_w^(-floor(e_w*s(u,w))) over S.
Each factor is an invertible nonzero fractional ideal, and exponents
can be negative or positive. Thus I_u is an actual fractional ideal.
The source nPrime is the O_K-span of products a*y with a in I_u and
y in N_u. It is precisely the module product I_u*N_u. Support terms
with s(u,w)=0 contribute exponent zero, so extra places in S have no
effect on this product.

setDiv replaces only each displayed source lattice and its height and
the enclosing HH bound. It keeps the underlying vector spaces and
Hermitian forms. Its exact height expression is
  hExpl6_u = SZbP_u + sum_{j in J}kernelPlaceMeanBound_{uj}
                    -sum_{j in J}L6lo_j
                    -mean_embeddings(log kapR).
These are the source's previously defined numerical height terms; no
new bound or metric identity is inferred from setting this value.
The common HH is max(1,sFrame(N',h)+muMax(RR)), which supplies its
required lower-bound field by the maximum inequality.

The precise compatibility issue is that auxiliary fields can mention D.
For VV, gainP, tauP, ioP, TTP and NP, inspection of their definitions
shows that the replacement fields do not occur. Their values are equal.
For the row-side structure, downTo is a recursively composed map with
unchanged inputs, hence is equal by induction; iota, its image subspace,
and iota composed with its adjoint are equal. The kapSharp identity and
rational-root set characterization therefore transport. Local quadratic
twisting uses unchanged Bloch–Kato and Selmer-length expressions, so its
same witnesses transport too. This explains the source extraTr and
twAt_tr rather than treating type conversion as a new arithmetic proof.

The resulting Pt carries actual NprimeDef and h_eq identities, plus the
NuDef and all compatibility fields proved above or explicitly supplied

19. RF16: the actual exponential word count is below 2^(k-3)
----------------------------------------------------------
The definition GamL is exp(L.CGam*m^5*f*x^epsilon). Under Val, L.CGam
is exactly C.CGam; Std gives G=C.CGam>=1 and F=C.P.CF>=1. The same
Val record supplies

  card Word=ceil_+(3*GamL^2*x^F).

This proof preserves that exponential expression. In particular it
does not infer an upper word count from the lower ChCode condition.
Let H=m^5*f*x^epsilon. The exact windows give

  m^5<=32*x^(5/2-5epsilon/16),
  f=2n<=4*x^(2+8epsilon),
  H<=128*x^(9/2+139epsilon/16),
  k>=x^(9/2+160epsilon/16).

The strict exponent saving is 21epsilon/16. Choose x0 so that x>=x0
implies k>=6 and

  x^(21epsilon/16)>=2048G,
  F log x+log 6 <= (1/8)x^(9/2+160epsilon/16).

The latter is a direct instance of RF11, with positive right exponent;
the former is a single positive-power threshold depending only on G
and epsilon. Then 2GH<=k/8 and F log x+log 6<=k/8.

Set A=3exp(2GH)x^F. Since x>1, F>=1 and G,H>=0, A>=3. The natural
ceiling satisfies ceil_+(A)<A+1<=2A. It is positive, so taking its
logarithm is legitimate and gives

  log(card Word)<=log 6+2GH+F log x<=k/4.

For k>=6, k-3>=k/2, and log 2>1/2. Thus
k/4<=(k-3)log 2. Exponentiation yields the real inequality
card Word<=2^(k-3). Since k>=6, the exponent k-3 is exactly the
source natural subtraction; both sides are natural numbers, and the
cast is order-reflecting. This proves the literal szRow_card_words_le.

No cD or ch choice is used, so their lower thresholds may be 0. The
single x0 is chosen from epsilon and the fixed C before D,L. This is
the exact SzRow quantifier order. This closure removes parameter
family 20 from next_targets.json, leaving 14 residual families. Full
ParameterChoice' and SizesAt remain the two packages required by the
conditional NumAt join, and no earlier RF1–RF15 proof is altered.
