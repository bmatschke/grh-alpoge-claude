1. PURPOSE AND PRESENT RESULT

This audit accepts the stipulated correctness of the checked Alpoge-Claude
Lean proof. Its subject is the unfinished human reconstruction. A missing
identification or proof below concerns that reconstruction, not the formal
proof. “GRH” in this document means the statement for Dirichlet L-functions.

The useful change in version 0.30 is a connected route from the final
analytic theorem to the actual arithmetic producers. It also closes five
groups of readable arguments: metric transport (MT), host-window
installation (HX), Case-I aggregation and target specialization (KA), the
tied numerical window deduction (ZW), and the weaker analytic endpoint
(AQ). These are completed implications with their hypotheses retained;
they do not constitute a completed human proof of RH or Dirichlet GRH.

The selected route has two major unresolved parts. One constructs the
original cohomological host windows for a fixed target. The other
constructs one compatible arithmetic family and proves its local,
divisibility, rank, geometric and spectral properties. The final numerical
deduction and analytic contradiction are now written, so those deductions
need no additional independent producer.

For ordinary RH, the counting side is smaller than the unrestricted
Dirichlet-GRH side: three counting interfaces suffice, whereas four are
retained for an unrestricted target. This is a proved specialization of
the original source quantifiers, not a guess that all difficult
automorphy disappears for the principal character.

“Minimal” means a sufficient frontier relative to this selected source
route, after removing proved implications, repeated producers and
stronger optional interfaces. It does not assert mathematical uniqueness
or optimality, and it is not a transitive listing of every Lean
declaration. The next investigation should follow the obligations shared
by several necessary branches, rather than automatically resume the last
chapter written.


2. CLASSIFICATION AND COMPLETION ARE DIFFERENT

Class | Meaning
A     | A direct application of a published theorem, with the exact
      | consumed hypotheses, objects, normalization and range checked.
B     | An elementary or moderately difficult independent argument,
      | including finite algebra, numerical estimates and exact assembly.
C     | A substantial arithmetic, automorphic, geometric or spectral
      | argument whose actual producer needs human reconstruction.
D     | An unresolved source identification, object equality, normalization,
      | theorem match or quantifier transport in our understanding.

A can close an application of a very deep theorem. B does not mean
already completed: a finite installation remains open until written on
the original objects. A D task may resolve to a short identification or
may expose a substantial C proof. No package is A merely because its name
includes “Literature” or resembles a familiar theorem.

Most importantly, a proof of P implies Q is not a proof of Q while P
remains an unsupplied producer. Separately constructed objects also cannot
be substituted for a common witness without the needed identification.
This matters for the common arithmetic family and for the common Hecke
ideal in the counting branch.

The machine-readable map distinguishes logical dependency edges from
work-sharing and proposed-reuse edges. A shared dictionary may help prove
several obligations without being sufficient for any whole obligation.
An optional rank-four-to-rank-three bridge is not mandatory if the
rank-three theorem is reconstructed directly.


3. WHAT THE FINAL THEOREM ACTUALLY NEEDS

Fix a primitive target character chi before choosing auxiliary fields,
primes, coefficient rings or windows. Write Lambda for the von Mangoldt
function and gamma for Euler's constant. The original centered ramps are

  G(x) = sum_{n <= x} Lambda(n)(x/n - 1)
         - x log x + (1 + gamma)x,

  G_xi(x) = sum_{n <= x} Lambda(n)xi(n)(x/n - 1)
            + x L'(1,xi)/L(1,xi)

for a nonprincipal primitive xi; G_1 means G. A principal character of
larger modulus is first replaced by its primitive inducer, with missing
Euler factors handled explicitly. The plus sign in the nonprincipal
centering term is part of the normalization.

If chi has order o, let C_chi be the distinct primitive characters
inducing its powers. The fixed real target sum is

  H_chi(x) = G(x) + (1/(3o+1))
                    sum_{xi in C_chi, xi != 1} Re G_xi(x).

The character weights are positive; this does not say H_chi itself is
nonnegative. The target chi occurs among the constituents. For chi = 1,
the family consists only of the principal character and H_chi = G.

The selected pointwise arithmetic contract supplies, for every sufficiently
small epsilon > 0 and every starting threshold, an arbitrarily late
window [Y,Y^(3/2)] on which

  H_chi(x) <= x^(1/2 + (e_D + e_L + 7)epsilon).

The constants e_D and e_L are chosen before epsilon. In the selected
shapeWin caller they are 2 and 0, giving 1/2 + 9 epsilon. A zero to the
right of 1/2 is excluded by taking epsilon sufficiently small and then
using the arbitrarily late windows. Applying this at chi = 1 gives RH.
Applying it for every primitive target and then using the functional
equation and imprimitive Euler factors gives Dirichlet GRH. The principal
pole at 1 is treated separately throughout. [AN1–AN3, GH6, ZW4; S1–S4]

The connected source route is:

  Original count suppliers imply VWinAt(chi,401).
  HX4 installs HostExW(3/2,(GChi chi).req) on the same target.
  This host input feeds the compatible-design construction.
  That design and nine closed predicates on the same family give
  FamilyClassConnected.
  ZW4, the existing window bridge and the analytic argument give
  the required zero exclusion.

The host window is not itself the compatible geometric family. Conversely,
the common-family assembly cannot omit the cohomological HypV supplied
through that host route.

For a fixed unrestricted target chi, the selected sufficient counting
frontier is

  CorGK at K_chi, PMExAlt, CountIII_chi, CountV_chi,
  where K_chi = Kstar(pK(chi)).

PropGK for that same K_chi is a sufficient stronger input for CorGK.

The source's host character psi, which is distinct from the fixed target
chi, has order dividing 2^(s+2) times the order of chi. Case III requires
the order of this full host character to be odd and psi to be nontrivial.
If chi has power-of-two order, the host character also has power-of-two
order, so those Case-III conditions are incompatible. KA5 proves this
directly; KA6 checks that every consumer through VWinAt preserves the
same target.

The sufficient cut therefore becomes

  CorGK at K_chi, PMExAlt, CountV_chi

for every power-of-two-order target, including chi = 1. The separate
Case-III seed and promodular construction are absent from this selected
ordinary-RH route. They remain necessary producers on the selected
unrestricted Dirichlet route. Nothing here proves a universal CountIII
theorem from one empty case.

For the principal target, K_chi is isomorphic over Q to Q(sqrt(-7)),
whose class number is 1, and the exceptional prime set is {3,7}. This
does not make Count V a finite calculation: dyadic conductors are still
unbounded at those primes. KA also checks nonempty examples in Cases I,
II and V, including characteristic 3. Accordingly, a support theorem
restricted to coefficient characteristic greater than 3 cannot close the
entire principal-target branch.


6. COMPLETED ARGUMENTS AND THEIR PRECISE BOUNDARIES

6.1 Metric transport: MT1–MT7

The family already stores the actual ideal relation N_u = I_u M_u, with

  I_u = product_w p_w^(-floor(e_w s_(u,w))).

The infinite-place comparison metric is unchanged by its definition. With
the source slope normalization and the local logarithmic weights ell_w,

  mu(N_u) - mu(M_u) - 2 sum_w ell_w e_w s_(u,w)
    = 2 sum_w ell_w(floor(e_w s_(u,w)) - e_w s_(u,w)).

The finite rounding error is bounded by the weighted support. MT accounts
for the small primes, the actual T_u and distinguished exceptional
primes, and every prime divisor of D_Gamma,u; it does not assume the last
quantity is prime. The place normalization
sum_{w above p} e_w ell_w = log p needs no Galois assumption.

The elementary prime-product bound and the source size bounds yield one
constant 2(log 4 + 5 + 10|C_Delta|), on the original X'' scale, before all
later x, indices and rows. This is the literal LinkMu required by the

6.5 Weaker analytic contract: AQ1–AQ5

For a fixed target and its fixed positive character family, it is enough
that for each 1/2 < theta < 1 in a sequence decreasing to 1/2, one can
choose a fixed delta > 0 such that

  for every eta > 0 and every Y_0 there is Y >= max(2,Y_0) with

  integral_Y^(Y^(1+delta)) max(H_chi(x),0) x^(-theta-1) dx <= Y^eta.

The family and delta are fixed before eta and the chosen window. Any
fixed positive stretch is allowed. A pointwise bound H_chi(x) <= C x^theta
implies this integral bound, since C delta log Y is eventually at most
Y^eta. The integral condition allows short positive spikes. A signed
integral is insufficient because cancellation can hide them.

AQ1 identifies this exactly with the source's nonnegative-envelope
contract. AQ2 gives explicit gamma localization for every delta > 0.
Writing g(u) = H_chi(e^u), the localization controls the tails outside
[U,(1+min(delta,1))U]. AQ3 uses the already reconstructed Mellin
continuation and a nearest-pole argument, choosing eta after the
putative pole and before invoking the window hypothesis. Positive
character weights prevent cancellation between coincident zero poles,
and possible real poles have positive residues. AQ4 supplies the
fixed-target and Dirichlet-GRH consequences.

AQ5 gives another useful reduction. Between consecutive integers all
nonprincipal ramp terms are affine, whereas the principal centering
gives H_chi''(x) = -1/x. If L_n is the affine chord on [n,n+1], then

  0 <= H_chi(x) - L_n(x) <= 1/(8n).

Bounds at the integers in [Y,Y^lambda] therefore give real bounds on a
slightly shorter fixed-stretch interval [Z,Z^mu], with Z = ceil(Y) and
1 < mu < lambda. Both floor(x) and floor(x)+1 then remain among the
supplied integer endpoints.

These are complete analytic implications. No new arithmetic supplier of
the integral or integer-window contract is claimed. It would be useful
to investigate whether either contract reduces arithmetic uniformity,
but that saving has not been proved. [AQ1–AQ5; S4]

9. PUBLISHED THEOREMS: MATCHED APPLICATIONS AND SCOPE LIMITS

Seven applications were freshly checked against primary sources in the
literature review; HX1 adds the least-prime application. These are A in
the scopes below. This list does not assert that the full surrounding
arithmetic constructions are A.

9.1 Quadratic analytic class-number formula

For a primitive real nonprincipal character, the associated field is
quadratic with fundamental discriminant D and conductor |D|. Factoring
its zeta function as zeta(s)L(s,chi_D) gives the required normalization.
For D < 0, the regulator is 1, the class number is at least 1 and the
number of roots of unity is at most 6. For D > 0, there are two roots
of unity and the regulator is at least log((1+sqrt(5))/2). Thus the
consumed lower bound L(1,chi_D) >> |D|^(-1/2) has an absolute positive
constant. No effective uniform Siegel bound is inferred. [QP1/CN]

Primary reference: A. V. Sutherland, MIT 18.785, Lecture 19 (2021),
analytic class-number formula, page 1.
https://math.mit.edu/classes/18.785/2021fa/LectureNotes19.pdf

9.2 Bombieri's exceptional-factor zero-density theorem

Theorem 14 uses one parameter T for primitive-character moduli and zero
height. Set T = R and its real-part parameter equal to sigma. Its single
exceptional Landau–Page zero is real and simple; after removing it the
bound carries the required factor (1-beta_1) log R. Adding that one zero
back costs at most one in the unconditional count, absorbed in the
absolute constant. The exponent is an existential absolute constant;
the audit does not claim a particular large explicit numerical value.
[QP/BD]

Primary reference: E. Bombieri, Le grand crible dans la theorie analytique
des nombres, Asterisque 18, section 6, Theorem 14, pages 38–39.
https://www.numdam.org/item/AST_1987__18__1_0.pdf

9.3 Heath-Brown's hybrid density specialization

Theorem 1 applies for Q,T >= 1 and 11/14 <= sigma <= 1, to primitive
characters, counting both signs of zero height and multiplicities.
Its exponent on Q^2 T is 2(1-sigma)+h for fixed h > 0. Setting
T = Q >= 2, sigma = 399/400 and h = 1/100 gives Q^(9/200), which is
bounded by Q^(1/4). Each bad primitive character contributes a zero;
any principal-character convention costs at most one. These are the
actual substitutions required by QP16/HD.

Primary reference: D. R. Heath-Brown, The Density of Zeros of Dirichlet's
L-Functions, Canadian Journal of Mathematics 31 (1979), Theorem 1,
page 231.
https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/7BEA17255131BE21F20DED8223306040/S0008414X00014942a.pdf/div-class-title-the-density-of-zeros-of-dirichlet-s-span-class-italic-l-span-functions-div.pdf

9.4 Thorne's polarized adjoint vanishing

The matched QX1 application uses a CM field, dimension at least 2, an
actual regular algebraic cuspidal polarized pair, a fixed isomorphism
Qbar_ell with C, and irreducibility on the full cyclotomic extension.
There is no residual-adequacy premise or lower bound on ell. The written
transport identifies the absolute Galois subgroup, the polarized adjoint
including minus transpose at conjugation, and the Bloch–Kato local
conditions. This gives the scoped adjoint vanishing once the actual
automorphic representation is present. It creates neither an automorphic
point nor characteristic-ell support. A different rank-three application
needs its own match.

Primary reference: J. A. Thorne, On the vanishing of adjoint Bloch–Kato
Selmer groups of irreducible automorphic Galois representations,
Theorem 6.1, page 24, and section 6.3, page 25.
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2207.04925

9.5 Caraiani's local compatibility and temperedness

Theorems 1.1–1.2 apply to the actual conjugate-self-dual cuspidal
representation over a CM field, cohomological for an irreducible
algebraic coefficient. QX9 matches those conditions. They give
Frobenius-semisimplified Weil–Deligne compatibility away from ell and
temperedness at every finite place. The away-from-ell restriction belongs
to the compatibility conclusion, not the temperedness conclusion. A
generic polarized version or compatibility at ell requires its own
recorded theorem and normalization.

Primary reference: A. Caraiani, Local-global compatibility and the action
of monodromy on nearby cycles, Duke Mathematical Journal 161 (2012),
Theorems 1.1–1.2, page 2312.
https://www.ma.imperial.ac.uk/~acaraian/papers/lgc1.pdf

9.6 Hida's rank-two ordinary control

The matched SX3 module is the definite quaternionic system over a real
quadratic field, ramified at its two real places and at no finite place,
with the specified fixed central character and level system. The module
axioms A0–A4 and the control hypotheses are checked for this system.
Hida's characteristic-zero ordinary line/quotient statement does not need
the residual absolute irreducibility added for the optional integral
realization. This application supports the rank-two Case-II seed. It
does not construct the rank-four unitary module or the six-field
HidaPropsAlt ideal.

Primary reference: H. Hida, Control of nearly ordinary Hecke algebras,
Luminy lecture (2007): Theorem 1.2, Lemmas 2.1–2.2, Theorem 2.4,
Corollary 2.5, Theorem 3.1 and Remark 3.1.
https://www.math.ucla.edu/~hida/Lum07.pdf

9.7 Complete-local connectedness

The matched UX10 application uses a complete Noetherian local ring A,
an actual proper ideal I and a closed bad set Z in V(I). The strict
dimension inequality is

  dim Z < min(c,d-1) - cd(A,I),

with c the connectedness dimension of Spec A and d its least component
dimension. The regular power-series presentation and the relation count
bound the needed quantities; b generators give cd(A,I) <= b. The citation
converts those bounds into connectedness. It does not supply the actual
ring identification, properness, component dimensions or bad-locus
dimension estimates.

Primary reference: Stacks Project, Lemma 52.14.1, tag 0ECR.
https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0ECR

9.8 Xylouris's least prime in an arithmetic progression

Theorem 1.1 gives P(a,q) <= C q^5.18 for positive coprime a,q with one
absolute constant. HX1 uses a = 1 and q = N >= 2, so coprimality is
automatic. Taking

  A = max(1, 5.18 + log(max(C,1))/log 2)

absorbs the constant and gives a prime p congruent to 1 modulo N with
p <= N^A. This is exactly SmallOne, uniformly in N.

Primary reference: T. Xylouris, On the least prime in an arithmetic
progression and estimates for the zeros of Dirichlet L-functions,
Acta Arithmetica 150 (2011), Theorem 1.1, page 66.
https://www.impan.pl/shop/en/publication/transaction/download/product/83078

9.9 Why a candidate support theorem is not yet an A closure

Allen–Newton–Thorne, Theorem 4.1 together with section 4.2, is a candidate
for the remaining support assertion. Its residual-Schur condition
requires appropriately polarized individual constituents and their
pairwise distinctness; polarization of the total representation alone
can instead pair different constituents. The theorem also has
genericity, precise auxiliary local conditions, primitivity, cyclotomic
Schur and no-quotient-of-order-ell hypotheses, together with ell > 3 and
ell not dividing the dimension.

The whole list has not been matched to both actual QX Links. The actual
rank-four Hecke/support construction remains C, and this proposed
replacement is D. The surviving principal-target characteristic-3 case
also prevents discarding the theorem's coefficient-prime restriction.
These are limitations of the proposed human shortcut.

Primary reference: P. B. Allen, J. Newton and J. A. Thorne, Automorphy
lifting for residually reducible l-adic Galois representations, II,
Theorem 4.1 and section 4.2.
https://arxiv.org/pdf/1912.11269

11. WHICH BRANCH SHOULD COME NEXT?

The highest-value next investigation is the actual shared
local/theta/operator installation. The shared real-place second-layer,
mass-ratio and genus normalization should be investigated alongside it
where independent work is possible.

These are common inputs to design existence, denominator comparisons,
period/height control, local gains and spectral estimates. Resolving the
source dictionary is a bounded D task that tells us exactly which C
proofs remain and prevents duplicating the same comparison under several
consumer names. The deliverable should contain:

  (a) the literal original local, theta, toric and operator objects;
  (b) the same coefficient embeddings, row maps, lattices and metrics;
  (c) the exact hypotheses and quantifier order of each supplier;
  (d) the exact downstream consumer satisfied by each identification;
  (e) a separation of already written comparisons from unsupplied C work.

Completion means installing a required original instance or proving its
actual producer. Another conditional theorem assuming that same instance
does not retire the obligation.

Bounded follow-ups with useful payoff include locating the original
level-projection surjectivity z_L, completing the Count-V frame/length
and vanishing installations, and writing the OneChar plus rank-three
number-to-PropGK join. Their purpose is to remove a whole interface or
expose its precise deep input, rather than merely add lemma labels.

The rank-four ordinary support/Hecke-ideal construction remains a large,
valuable C task for both RH and Dirichlet GRH. Its priority should be
assessed against those shared global producers, not assigned merely
because version 0.29 ended near it. The separate Case-III seed/promodular
branch remains important for the unrestricted Dirichlet theorem, but
its absence from the ordinary-RH cut changes its comparative priority.

The positive-part integral interface is worth keeping as an alternative
research target. It earns a change of route only if an arithmetic
supplier is proved with fewer or weaker prerequisites. Renaming the
unresolved work as an “integral envelope” or a “direct gap” is not itself
such a reduction.
