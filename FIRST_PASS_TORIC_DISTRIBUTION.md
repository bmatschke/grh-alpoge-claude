# The toric determinant on the common auxiliary primes

For the built first-pass CM host, fix a nonzero index, its residue characteristic $\ell$, and a toric label admitting the critical integer $s\ge1$. Its coefficient ring is $O$. Write $N_0$ for the [uniform denominator exponent](FIRST_PASS_ORDER_CLASS_BOUND.md) and $\Theta_Q(\psi)=\sum_\delta\theta_{Q,\lambda,\delta}\psi(\delta)$ for the original evaluation. We obtain the full abelian-label polynomial hypothesis in the [coprime-power specialization](FIRST_PASS_COPRIME_SPECIALIZATION.md), with a bound of two for each residual exponent. It applies to every stipulated family of paired primes meeting the one open condition below. A proof using the two halves of the auxiliary family also appears in the independent Roadmap book, Proposition B8.07q. [Roadmap argument][road] · [Pinned toric proposition][prop]

**Removing a split pair.** Suppose $Q$ adds $P,\bar P$ to $Q'$ above a split prime of norm $q$, away from the base conductor and $\ell$. Inflate a character of the smaller ray quotient to the larger. With $a$ the product of the label and testing character readings at arithmetic Frobenius at $P$, we have
$$
 \Theta_Q(\psi)=E_q(a,q^{-s})\Theta_{Q'}(\psi),\qquad
 E_q(a,t)=(1-at)(1-a^{-1}t)+(q-1)t^2 .
$$
At this prime the maximal order is the product of two local integer rings and the order of conductor exponent one imposes equality of the residues. Compute in the half-plane of absolute convergence with $t=q^{-z}$. The unrestricted local series for the unramified twist is $[(1-at)(1-a^{-1}t)]^{-1}$. At the new order the two valuations may both be zero, yielding one unit orbit, or both be positive, in which case each pair of valuations admits $q-1$ residue unit ratios. Thus its series is $1+(q-1)t^2/[(1-at)(1-a^{-1}t)]$, giving the claimed multiplier.

For the passage to the global readings, write an integral finite idèle as the chosen representative of its order ideal class times a principal idèle times an order unit. The ambiguity of the principal multiplier is precisely the global order units. This reindexes the original lattice sums over the order classes into the integral idèle sum modulo completed order units. The global principal character relation supplies the angular lattice powers. Absolute convergence follows by comparison with the ideal zeta series, since forgetting the order-unit level has finite fibres for a fixed conductor. Local factors can therefore be grouped before continuation. At a split base-conductor prime of exponent $m>0$ the finite character has exact conductor $m$. At equal valuations $0<j<m$, the unit ratios congruent to one modulo the $(m-j)$-th power give a vanishing sum of this primitive character. At two valuations at least $m$ the entire unit-ratio character sums to zero. Unequal valuations with minimum less than $m$ are excluded. Only the unit term remains. The same computation applies to a ramified twist of conductor exponent one at an auxiliary pair. Hence the character-weighted sum over order classes is the Hecke $L$-value at $s$ multiplied by the factors $E_q$ at its unramified auxiliary pairs and the fixed scalar of the label. The identity follows by continuation from $\Re z>1$, also at $s=1$. The coefficient at $\delta$ uses the fibre of the *inverse* order class. Its evaluation weights the class by the inverse Picard twist. Global reciprocity sends its uniformizer class to inverse arithmetic Frobenius. Comparing both levels under the same complex reading gives the displayed formula in the coefficient field, using $E_q(a,t)=E_q(a^{-1},t)$. [Idèle Euler series][Euler] · [Class reindexing and continuation][class] · [Order fibres and reciprocity][rec]

At empty auxiliary conductor the sum is a fixed nonzero scalar times the Hecke $L$-value of the label. For $s>1$ use its absolutely convergent Euler product. At $s=1$ use the classical nonvanishing theorem on $\Re z=1$ for unitary Hecke characters. The actual angular exponent $\kappa_\tau+s>0$ excludes a norm power. Thus
$$
 b_\lambda=v_O\!\left(\ell^{N_0}\sum_\delta\theta_{\varnothing,\lambda,\delta}\right)
$$
is finite and independent of the auxiliary level. [Host character](FIRST_PASS_TORIC_COEFFICIENTS.md) · [Endpoint and grid nonvanishing](ROW_CRITICAL_HECKE_RATIOS.md) · [Empty-conductor comparison][empty]

**Polynomial factors in the two halves.** At level $M\ge1$ put $n=\ell^M$. The designated tame element $z_0$ has nontrivial image under the residual label character $\bar\lambda$. Indeed its distinguished character component is residually nontrivial, of order prime to the residue characteristic of the designated place. The discrepancy between the label and this component or its inverse is killed by a power of that residue characteristic. On the chosen inertia element the discrepancy also has order prime to it. It is therefore trivial there. Take $H_\lambda=\ker\bar\lambda$, open and normal. A first-prime Frobenius conjugate to $z_0$ modulo $H_\lambda$ has $\bar a\ne1$ for the trivial test. Since $q\equiv1\pmod{2n}$, its $E_q(a,q^{-s})$ is a unit, with residue $(1-\bar a)(1-\bar a^{-1})$. [Residual character and inertia][inertia]

Lift $\ell^{N_0}\Theta_Q$ to a polynomial $f$ over $O$ by sending a group element of ray coordinates $(d_j)$ to $\prod_j(1+Z_j)^{d_j}$ using representatives in $[0,n-1]$. Use the same construction to obtain $g$ on the first half alone. The class sums are finite with integral scaled coefficients. Successive removal at the trivial test gives $v_O(g(0))=b_\lambda$. Set the second-half variables to zero. The defining reciprocity coordinates read the $a$-th second Frobenius as $1+X_a$ on the first half. Repeated removal gives $f=Hg$ modulo these zero variables and the first-half relations $(1+X_a)^n-1$. Here $H$ is the product of the second-pair factors with the inverse of $1+X_a$ represented by $(1+X_a)^{n-1}$. Equality at all $n$-th root tuples implies the congruence. Divide successively by the monic coordinate relations and test the resulting polynomial of separate degrees $<n$ over the characteristic-zero splitting field. This division requires no factor $1/n$. [Polynomial lifts][lift] · [Coordinatewise interpolation and factorization][prop]

Modulo the uniformizer and $X_a^n$, each factor has the form
$$
 (1-\alpha(1+X_a))(1-\alpha^{-1}(1+X_a)^{-1}).
$$
For $\alpha\ne1$ it has a unit constant term. For $\alpha=1$ it equals $-X_a^2/(1+X_a)$. Thus $H$ is a unit at the origin times $\prod_a X_a^{e_a}$ in this residue quotient with $e_a\in\{0,2\}$, including in residue characteristic two. The bound on the constant term and the exponent bound are independent of $M$. Intersect $H_\lambda$ over the finitely many labels before invoking the Galois selection. This proves the toric factor assertion separately from the construction of the Galois primes. [Source exponent calculation][order] · [Intersection used in selection][joint]
