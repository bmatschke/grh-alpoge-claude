# The constant term along an isotropic plane

Let $E/F$ be the CM extension on the row, $G=U(W)$, $d=\dim_E W=a+n$, $a-n=2e>0$. Consider a finite linear combination of the big and small theta integrals with the prescribed polynomial tests. On the small side retain the prescribed support condition at the distinguished finite place. Denote the combination by $D$. Suppose its inputs give continuous, left $G(F)$-invariant theta integrals of moderate growth, with source invariance also for Schwartz–Bruhat substitutions at a fresh split place. Suppose the constant term of $D$ along one rational isotropic line vanishes at **every** right argument. Then the constant term of $D$ along every rational isotropic plane vanishes if $e>1$. Here the big and small integrals use the row's reciprocal unitary characters and the same target Weil and Whittaker conventions. At $(p,q)=(1,1)$ the actual inequalities give $e>5$, $n>5$ and the real signature of $W$ bounds its rational Witt index by two. Thus the plane is the last additional maximal-parabolic test whenever the rational Witt index is two. The lemma assumes the line identity. It does not compute a comparison scalar. [Row hypotheses][pc-geom] · [Rank-two implication in the pinned proof][pc-assem]

## From the plane to one Fourier coefficient

Fix the plane $U$, its dual isotropic plane, and let $M(g)$ be the factor of the Levi acting by $g$ on $U$, by conjugate inverse transpose on its dual, and trivially on the middle space. For $x\in G(\mathbb A_F)$ write
$$
 H_x(g)=D_{P_U}(M(g)x),\qquad
 w(x)=\int_{E\backslash\mathbb A_E}\psi(-s)H_x\!\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&s\\0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)ds.
$$
Use probability unipotent measures and a standard global additive character. Witt extension carries the assumed line identity to every rational line. Average $H_x$ along the upper unitriangular group. The preimage of that group in $P_U$ with the plane radical is the radical for the flag $L\subset U$, where $L$ is the first coordinate line. Integrating in the other order starts with the radical of the parabolic for $L$. Compactness of rational unipotent quotients and invariance of their probability measures give zero. Thus the additive mean vanishes for every right argument. All other characters of $E\backslash\mathbb A_E$ are $s\mapsto\psi(bs)$, $b\in E^\times$. Rational diagonal conjugation changes these coefficients to $w$ at right translates. If $w$ vanishes identically, Fourier uniqueness on this compact additive quotient and continuity give $D_{P_U}=0$. [Whittaker equivariance, transitivity and recovery][pc-wh]

## The two-column calculation at a split place

Fix $x_0$. Choose a finite place $v$ split in $E$, outside the ramification of both characters, both sets of exceptional vacuum and test factors, the distinguished small-source support place, and the denominators of a rational basis completing the two columns of $U$. Require $x_{0,v}$ and its inverse to be integral in the chosen splitting. These conditions omit finitely many places. Pick one $w\mid v$, set $k=E_w=F_v$, and write $\pi,\mathcal O,Q$ for its uniformizer, integers and residue cardinality. Let $b_m\in\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb A_E)$ equal $\operatorname{diag}(\pi^m,\pi^{-m})$ at $w$ and the identity everywhere else, including at the conjugate place. In the split target projection, completing the basis makes $M(b_m)$ act by that diagonal on the first two matrix columns, fixing the others. The basis change and its inverse are integral. On a neighborhood of $x_0$, the split projection of $x$ and its inverse remain integral as well. Hence these additional right factors preserve the lattice indicator. [Vacuum in the split model][pc-vac] · [Frame, isolated torus and integrality][pc-torus] · [Simultaneous choice of place][pc-place]

Freeze the other factors of one integral and compose the resulting local map with the plane Whittaker functional, incorporating the frame change and fixed right argument as above. This gives a complex linear functional $L$ on $\mathcal S(M_{h,d}(k))$, with $h=a$ or $n$. Write $Y=(y_0,y_1,\ldots)$ by columns. For $\gamma\in\mathrm{GL}_h(k)$ and any row $\rho$ with $\rho_0=0$,
$$
 L(\phi(\gamma Y))=\xi(\det\gamma)L(\phi),\qquad
 L(\phi(Y+y_0\rho))=\psi_w(\rho_1)L(\phi).
$$
The first formula follows by source translation in the theta integral. Its determinant factor is the inverse of the split Weil scalar, with the transpose of the acting row matrix ranging over the whole group. For the second, the column transformation fixes the first basis vector, sends the second to itself plus $\rho_1$ times the first, and acts trivially modulo $U$ on $U^\perp$. Its lift at this split place is consequently a plane-radical element times that upper unitriangular element on $U$. The plane average removes the radical, and translation of the Fourier character gives the displayed factor. The split target determinant is one. Substituting at $v$ preserves the original small-source support condition at its other place. The oscillator identities and source integration hold on the whole local Schwartz–Bruhat space, while the **original** factor here is a scalar multiple of $\mathbf1_{M_{h,d}(\mathcal O)}$. [Split actions][pc-action] · [Derivation of both laws][pc-laws] · [Original polynomial and support-restricted tests][pc-tests]

Put $c=\operatorname{cond}(\psi_w)$, with $\psi_w$ trivial on $\pi^c\mathcal O$ and nontrivial on $\pi^{c-1}\mathcal O$. The two laws force $L$ to be supported where $y_0,y_1$ are dependent. Here support means that it kills every Schwartz–Bruhat function vanishing on that closed set. To see this, near any point $Y$ where the columns are independent, invert a fixed nonzero two-row minor to choose a continuously varying row $\lambda_Y$ satisfying $\lambda_Yy_0=0$, $\lambda_Yy_1=1$. Take $t$ with $\psi_w(t)\ne1$. Combine the column shear with row $t\lambda_Y Y$ and the left transvection $I-t y_0\lambda_Y$. For this frozen $Y$, the composite fixes $Y$, has functional character $\psi_w(t)$ and preserves arbitrarily small lattice cosets centered at $Y$. More precisely, the two increments on the ambient matrix space are commuting square-zero operators $A,B$. Starting at a center $Y_*$ with any standard lattice $\Lambda_0$, the lattice $\Lambda=\Lambda_0\cap A^{-1}\Lambda_0\cap B^{-1}\Lambda_0\cap(AB)^{-1}\Lambda_0$ is stable under both increments at $Y_*$. On a neighborhood their continuous variations still preserve this same lattice, by compactness and openness. Each composite at a center $Y$ in that neighborhood preserves $Y+\pi^j\Lambda$ for all $j$. The characteristic function of each such ball has zero $L$-value. Finite partition of a test's compact support proves the assertion. [Independent-column support][pc-support] · [Adapted lattices][pc-lattice]

Let $B(p,\mu)$ be $L$ on the indicator of the box with $y_0\in\pi^p\mathcal O^h$, $y_1\in\pi^\mu\mathcal O^h$, all remaining columns integral. Set $S(p,\mu)=B(p,\mu)-B(p+1,\mu)$ and $r_h=\xi(\pi)Q^{h-d}$. The three relations needed are
$$
\begin{aligned}
 B(p,\mu)&=0 &&(\mu-p<c),\\
 S(p,\mu)&=r_h S(p+1,\mu) &&(\mu-p\ge c+1),\\
 S(p,\mu)&=Q S(p,\mu+1) &&(\mu-p\ge c).
\end{aligned}
$$
For the first, $y_1\mapsto y_1+t y_0$ preserves the box for $t\in\pi^{\mu-p}\mathcal O$, which contains an element where $\psi_w(t)\ne1$. For the last two, partition the shell by the first row attaining valuation $p$ in column zero. Subtract integral multiples of that row from all later rows, sorting the later entries modulo $\pi^{p+1}$. The resulting summands are weighted values of pivot sets with just one row of valuation $p$ and all the other rows in that column of valuation at least $p+1$. Each residue multiplicity is a power of $Q$ independent of $p,\mu$. Multiplying the pivot row by $\pi$ moves the pivot to $p+1$ and gives the factor $\xi(\pi)$. Restoring the unique-pivot condition sorts $h-1$ row residues. Restoring the original bounds on the other entries of the pivot row sorts $d-1$ column residues in the opposite direction. Row shears have determinant one, and the column shear seen by $\psi_w$ is in $\pi^{\mu-p-1}\mathcal O$. Thus the net factor is $\xi(\pi)Q^{h-1}/Q^{d-1}=r_h$. For the last relation, dependence gives $y_1=s y_0$ on the pivot set, so if the pivot entry of $y_1$ lies in $\pi^\mu\mathcal O$, all its nonpivot entries already lie in $\pi^{\mu+1}\mathcal O$. Sorting the pivot entry modulo $\pi^{\mu+1}$ gives $Q$ equal contributions by the column shear with parameters in $\pi^{\mu-p}\mathcal O$. These arguments compare indicators on the support of $L$ and allow arbitrary singular contributions there. [Row and column residue counts][pc-count] · [Pivot recurrences and exact cutoff][pc-rec]

The boundary relation terminates the sum in $p$. Writing $A(\mu)=B(\mu-c,\mu)$, the second relation gives $S(\mu-c-j,\mu)=r_h^j A(\mu)$ for $j\ge0$. The third gives $A(\mu)=Qr_h A(\mu+1)$. Hence for $m\ge0$ the value on the translated original lattice is
$$
 B(-m,m)=A(0)Y_m(r_h),\qquad
 Y_m(r)=(Qr)^{-m}\sum_{i=0}^{2m-c}r^i,
$$
with an empty sum equal to zero. The identity includes the boundary $y_0=0$ without imposing any support condition there. [Finite telescoping and original lattice][pc-solve]

The characters in the two row laws are $\xi_b=((\beta/\alpha)_w|\cdot|^{d/2})^{-1}$ and $\xi_s=((\alpha/\beta)_w|\cdot|^{d/2})^{-1}$. Since $d=a+n$, their parameters are $r=(\alpha/\beta)_w(\pi)Q^e$ and $r^{-1}$ respectively. Reversing the geometric sum gives $Y_m(r^{-1})=r^cY_m(r)$ even when the sum is empty. The determinant of $b_m$ is one, so no $m$-dependent target character remains. By linearity, at **every** $x$ in the neighborhood fixed above there is a scalar $A_x$ independent of $m$ such that
$$
 w(M(b_m)x)=A_xY_m(r).
$$
No nonvanishing assumption on this scalar or on $Y_0(r)$ is needed. The unitarity of $\alpha,\beta$ gives $|r|=Q^e>Q$. Therefore $|Y_m(r)|\sim |r^{1-c}/(r-1)|(|r|/Q)^m\to\infty$. [Both parameters, shared multiplier and neighborhood][pc-pair]

## Why moderate growth is sufficient

Restrict $H_{x_0}$ to $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb A_E)$. It is continuous, invariant under $\mathrm{SL}_2(E)$ on the left, and its upper unipotent mean is zero. On a compact set times the usual rank-one cusp parameter, in the order $a_\infty^j\kappa$ with $a_\infty=\operatorname{diag}(2,1/2)$ at infinity and identity at finite places, it satisfies a bound $C_K 2^{jN}$ for $\kappa\in K$. Indeed $D$ has polynomial growth in height, the unipotent quotient has compact representatives, and height is submultiplicative. [Growth of the original integrals][pc-growth] · [Growth after averaging and restriction][pc-height]

Here is the smoothing argument that allows this limited regularity. Right-convolve on $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb A_E)$ by a nonnegative smooth, compactly supported bump, locally constant at finite places, of mass one, in an arbitrarily small identity neighborhood. For $H=H_{x_0}$ the unipotent mean of the convolution stays zero. On subtracting this zero mean, the convolution at $a_\infty^j\kappa$ is an average of differences of translates of the bump against $H$. After a rational additive translation the unipotent variable ranges over a fixed compact set with finite components in an arbitrarily small prescribed subgroup. This follows from additive strong approximation and a bounded fundamental domain at infinity. Conjugation by $a_\infty^{-j}$ contracts its archimedean coordinates by $4^{-j}$. The finite components can be made to fix the bump uniformly for $\kappa$ in the chosen compact set. The two bump translates thus differ by $O_K(2^{-j})$ on a common compact region, by smoothness at infinity. Their integrals against $H(a_\infty^j\,\cdot)$ are $O_K(2^{j(N-1)})$ if $N\ge1$. Repeating $N$ times gives a continuous convolution bounded on every such cusp region, hence globally bounded by adelic $\mathrm{SL}_2$ reduction. The total support of the repeated convolution can be taken in an arbitrarily small neighborhood of the identity. [Rank-one reduction and contraction][pc-reduct] · [One step and induction][pc-smooth]

Let $w_H(g)=w(M(g)x_0)$. In that neighborhood the common multiplier implies $Y_0(r)w_H(b_mz)=Y_m(r)w_H(z)$, without division. Integrate this identity against the composite bump in $z$. Fubini identifies the resulting Fourier coefficients with those of the convolution of $H$. Its coefficients along $b_m$ are bounded by its global supremum, since the additive quotient has mass one. Letting $m$ tend to infinity forces its coefficient at $1$ to vanish. Shrink the total support to $1$ and use continuity to obtain $w(x_0)=0$. Since $x_0$ was arbitrary, the plane constant term vanishes. The argument uses local constancy only for the fresh nonarchimedean Weil tests and the chosen smoothing kernels. It needs no prior boundedness of the adelic plane constant term, no control of all derivatives of $D$, and no cuspidal-spectrum assertion for either individual summand. [Bounded-coefficient and continuity argument][pc-kill]
