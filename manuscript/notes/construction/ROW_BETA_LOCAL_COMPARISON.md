# The local operators in the first beta comparison

For a compatible upper and lower pair of models at signatures $(0,1)$ and $(0,0)$, the two local boundary operators are ordinary evaluation on the big side and the scalar-line integral on the small side. This note computes their ratio in the prescribed quotient normalizations. The [global constant term](ROW_FIRST_BETA_STEP.md) assembles the factors. At finite places the proof first descends each operator to the upper quotient and derives its parabolic transformation law. The spherical and ramified calculations then determine it on all inputs.

## The real maps at the first beta step

Put $n=a'$, $d=a+n$, $e=(a-n)/2$ and $\kappa_w=m_{\alpha,w}-m_{\beta,w}$. In the $(p,q)=(0,1)$ triangle write $E_{0,\infty}$ for the mixed big evaluation and $Q_\infty$ for the real compact average and scalar-line integral of the small test, at exponent $e+1$. Fix the upper and lower real matching maps $p_b,p_s$ and $p_{1,b},p_{1,s}$, the two mixed evaluations, and the measures. The identity on real polynomial tests is
$$
 p_{1,s}(Q_\infty f')=c_\infty p_{1,b}(E_{0,\infty}f)
 \qquad(p_s f'=p_b f).
$$
We prove nonvanishing on each oscillator space, descent to the upper quotient, and uniqueness after descent. In this first step the lower target is compact, which simplifies the first and third points. [Real component of the exact matching law][br-src]

### Nonvanishing on the two actual oscillator spaces

The big map is onto the lower polynomial space before projection. In mixed coordinates extend a lower test by a Gaussian in the added variables with value one at the origin. Equivalently use the surjectivity of the mixed evaluation on polynomial data. Since $p_{1,b}$ is onto a nonzero module, $p_{1,b}E_{0,\infty}$ is nonzero. [Polynomial lifting at this step][br-ep]

On the small side choose a nonzero linear functional on the finite-dimensional lower compact Howe module. It gives a nonzero continuous functional $\ell$ on the lower real Schwartz space invariant under the lower source $U(n-2)$ at each real place. To construct it, average over that compact source with the actual splitting. Its central circle selects a single total polynomial degree at each place because the lower source is negative definite and the lower target positive definite. In the Fock expansion all other degrees vanish. The surviving subspace is finite-dimensional; by compact Howe it maps isomorphically to the trivial-source quotient. This extends the chosen functional from polynomials. In particular, proving $\ell Q_\infty\ne0$ proves $p_{1,s}Q_\infty\ne0$. The following proof works for *every* nonzero continuous invariant $\ell$. [Oscillator action and angular exponents][br-weight] · [Precise invariant-functional statement][br-bump]

Let $I$ index the real places, $x=H.x$, $y=H.y$, $x'=H'.x$, $y'=H'.y$, and $N(u)=\prod_{w\in I}|u_w|^2$. In the primed beta polarization define the scalar slice
$$
 A_f(k,u)=\ell\left(E_{B,\infty}\rho(\bar u\,y\otimes x')\omega'_\infty(k,1)f\right),
 \quad
 W(u)=\prod_w(u_w/|u_w|)^{\kappa_w}|u_w|^{2e}.
$$
Here $k$ is in the compact real source subgroup. Changing multiplicative Haar measure on $(\mathbb C^\times)^I$ to additive measure cancels one power of $N(u)$ in the original exponent $e+1$. Thus $\ell Q_\infty f$ is a fixed nonzero multiple of $\int_K\int A_f(k,u)W(u)\,du\,dk$. The slices are continuous in $k,u$, rapidly decreasing in $u$ uniformly in $k$. In compatible coordinates this is restriction of a Schwartz function in the line and lower variables followed by a continuous functional. Compact oscillator translates give a bounded set of Schwartz seminorms. [Change of measures and conjugation][br-haar] · [Slice and integral][br-slice]

Suppose this integral vanishes for every $f$. Mixed evaluation is onto on Schwartz functions, by tensoring a prescribed Schwartz test with a function equal to one at zero in the evaluated coordinates. Therefore choose $f_0$ with $A_{f_0}(1,0)\ne0$. Fourier injectivity gives $\widehat A_{f_0}(1,t_0)\ne0$ for some $t_0$. The Heisenberg direction $x\otimes y'$ is dual to $y\otimes x'$ with unit pairing. Its translate modulates the slice and shifts the frequency, so arrange $H=\widehat A_f(1,1)\ne0$. [Choice of a nonzero frequency and its shift][br-freq]

For $\xi(k)=k^{-1}x$, define $\pi(k,t)=(2t_w\xi(k)_w)_w$ and $b=\pi(1,1)$. Translating $f$ in the Heisenberg directions $v\otimes x'$ translates the scalar slice by a linear function $u\mapsto u+\beta(k,v)$, whose dual pairing with $t$ is $\langle v,\pi(k,t)\rangle$. This follows by projecting $kv$ onto the $y$ direction; the $x$ and lower-source directions disappear under $E_{B,\infty}$. Consequently our vanishing hypothesis implies that the pushforward under $\pi$ of
$$
 \widehat A_f(k,t)W(t)^{-1}N(t)^{-1}\,dk\,dt
$$
vanishes near $b$. To justify the Fourier step, test against a smooth bump near $b$. Fourier inversion of that bump converts all the translates into multiplication by its pullback along $\pi$. In the trace-pairing Fourier convention the scalar-line identity converts $W$ into a nonzero scalar multiple of $W(t)^{-1}N(t)^{-1}$ away from the coordinate hyperplanes. A direct proof compares the two integrals on the test $\prod_w\bar u_w^{\kappa_w}e^{-2\pi |u_w|^2}$ using Fourier inversion and dilations. The original radial integrals have exponents $\kappa_w+2e$, and the reciprocal ones have radial density proportional to $r^{\kappa_w-2e-1}e^{-2\pi r^2}\,dr$; they are finite and nonzero since $\kappa_w>d>2e$. Their phases are constant. Fubini in the localized identity is legitimate since the slices decay rapidly and the preimage of a sufficiently small compact ball around $b$ is compact and avoids all $t_w=0$. This last fact follows from compactness of $K$ and uniform upper and strictly positive lower bounds for $\|k^{-1}x_w\|$. [Gaussian computation and local Fourier identity][br-ft] · [Localized Fourier argument][br-loc] · [Translations producing all dual directions][br-tr]

The vanishing is impossible. On the fibre $\pi(k,t)=b$, one has $kx=tx$. The compact group's action in this line stabilizer and lower-source invariance give
$$
 A_f(k,u)=\nu_\infty(t)N(t)^{r_X}A_f(1,tu),
$$
where $r_X=d/2$ is the source modulus exponent in the beta mixed Levi law. This is the only place that the small source stabilizer enters. The upper splitting character on this line is precisely $\nu=\alpha/\beta$. Fourier scaling then gives on the whole fibre
$$
 H^{-1}\widehat A_f(k,t)W(t)^{-1}N(t)^{-1}
 =N(t)^{r_X-e-2}=N(t)^{n-2}>0.
$$
By continuity and compactness the real part remains positive throughout the inverse image of some neighborhood of $b$. A nonnegative smooth bump supported there and positive at $b$ has strictly positive pairing with the rotated density. Indeed the inverse image of its positive locus contains an open neighborhood of $(1,1)$ of positive Haar measure. This contradicts the localized vanishing. Thus $\ell Q_\infty$ is nonzero. Real Fock polynomials are dense in Schwartz topology (Hermite expansion), $Q_\infty$ is continuous, and it maps polynomials to polynomials, so a polynomial detects this very same operator. [Fibre computation with signs][br-fibre] · [Local hypotheses and $\kappa$ range for the beta step][br-bump] · [Passage back to polynomial vectors][br-pt]

### Descent and the compact branching calculation

The primed cone operator has a local invariant integral which gives the descent directly. Write $A=U(V'_\infty)$ and $S=A_x$ for the stabilizer of the vector $x$. For a Schwartz test $f'$ put
$$
 D(f')=p_{1,s}E_{B,\infty}\rho(y\otimes x')f'.
$$
On Schwartz vectors interpret $p_{1,s}$ by its continuous compact projection constructed above. The formula agrees with its prescribed values on polynomials. Write $S=N_X\rtimes U(V'_1)$ in the frame $x,V'_1,y$. Elements of $S$ take $y$ to $y+v_1+c x$. The extra translations $(v_1+cx)\otimes x'$ are killed by mixed evaluation; their symplectic pairing with $y\otimes x'$ is zero since $x'$ is isotropic. The mixed Levi formula sends the $U(V'_1)$ action to the lower source action, killed by the compact projection. Hence $D$ is $S$-invariant. The stabilizer is unimodular since $U(V'_1)$ acts with absolute determinant one on its Heisenberg radical. With compatible measures a nonzero fixed multiple of
$$
 \int_{S\backslash A} D(\omega'(g,1)f')\,d\dot g
$$
equals $p_{1,s}Q_\infty f'$. Indeed write $g=m(l)k$ modulo $S$. In moving $m(l)$ across the Heisenberg translate, $m(l)^{-1}y=\bar l y$. The evaluation contributes $\nu(l)N(l)^{d/2}$ and the quotient measure contributes $N(l)^{1-n}d^\times l\,dk$. This recovers $\nu(l)N(l)^{e+1}$ in the defining cone integral. The integral converges absolutely by the scalar-line Schwartz estimates above. Right invariance consequently proves invariance under the entire upper source. [Mixed source and target actions][br-mix] · [Directions killed by evaluation][br-freq] · [Iwasawa and the line modulus][br-iwa]

For the target line parabolic $P$, its radical fixes $x'$ and acts trivially after mixed evaluation. Its Levi $m(t,h_1)$ moves the scalar-line argument from $l$ to $l/\bar t$ and acts on the evaluated test by $\alpha(t)N(t)^{(n-2)/2}\omega'_1(1,h_1)$. Changing $l$ to $l'\bar t$ adds the factor $\nu(t)^{-1}N(t)^{e+1}$. The product gives $\beta(t)N(t)^{a/2}\Pi_1(h_1)$. Thus $p_{1,s}Q_\infty$ defines a $(\mathfrak p,K\cap P)$ map on the **full** upper primed polynomial oscillator coinvariants. The big zero evaluation gives the same law directly on the big coinvariants. Since the big upper source $U(a)$ is compact, its trivial-source subspace in the polynomial oscillator has an invariant positive definite form. It is a finite-length admissible Harish-Chandra module by real Howe theory. A unitary Harish-Chandra module is semisimple by taking orthogonal complements on finite-dimensional compact isotypic components. Real Howe duality makes its maximal semisimple quotient the unique irreducible $\Pi$ with multiplicity one. Thus the big zero evaluation already gives a nonzero map $B:\Pi\to\Pi_1$ with the indicated parabolic law. [Exact beta exponents][br-exponents] · [Howe, real algebraic correspondence](https://www.math.utah.edu/~ptrapa/math-library/howe/howe-transcending-1989.pdf)

At a single place write $s=(m_\beta+a)/2$, $k=(\kappa-d)/2$. The unprimed compact source realizes in $\Pi$ a nonzero $K=U(d-1)\times U(1)$ type
$$
 T=\left(\det^{s}\,\mathbb S_{((k+1)^a)}\mathbb C^{d-1}\right)
       \boxtimes u^{(m_\beta-a)/2}.
$$
In the positive target columns the $a$-row determinant to power $k+1$ has source trace weight $(d-2)/2+k+1=\kappa/2$. It survives the compact source projection. The lower definite module $\Pi_1$ is $\det^s\mathbb S_{((k+1)^a)}\mathbb C^{d-2}$, since lowering replaces $d$ by $d-2$. Both weights include the target splitting. [Fock highest weights][br-weights] · [Actual character shifts][br-weight]

The generation fact needed on the primed side concerns the *full* coinvariants, since descent to $\Pi$ is under proof. In the Fock model these coinvariants are generated by the image of a compact highest-weight vector of type $T$. Here one can use the pinned algebraic generation theorem: filter by homogeneous degree, reduce quadratic source relations in the coinvariant to their annihilator terms of lower degree, and use target quadratic operators on the remaining joint harmonic part. The compact harmonic source-character space at its generating weight is generated by its compact highest-weight line. The positive-side rectangular weight and its negative-side complementary weight coincide after the relative $\kappa/2$ trace twist. More explicitly the two possible polynomial degrees in the weight formula are $(\kappa-d+2)/2$ and $(\kappa+d-2)/2$, and the resulting positive block has the former added precisely in its first $a$ positions. The cyclic highest vector is nonzero since the prescribed upper small quotient is nonzero. Its compact orbit spans one copy of the irreducible $K$ type $T$ by finite-dimensional highest-weight theory. [Full Fock quotient and highest vector][br-gen] · [Generation on the full source coinvariants][br-cyc] · [Two weights and their twist][br-two]

The intersection $K\cap P$ is $U(d-2)\times U(1)_{\rm diag}$ in a Cartan-adapted hyperbolic frame. The ordinary interlacing restriction from $U(d-1)$ to $U(d-2)$ contains the lower weight exactly once; its extra positive-coordinate weight is $s$. Including the negative circle gives $s+(m_\beta-a)/2=m_\beta$, precisely $\beta$ on $U(1)_{\rm diag}$. Thus the space of $K\cap P$ maps from $T$ to the required output has dimension one. Finally $\mathfrak g=\mathfrak p+\mathfrak k$ by Iwasawa. PBW ordering puts all $\mathfrak p_\mathbb C$ operators to the left of $\mathfrak k_\mathbb C$ operators. Since $T$ generates the full primed coinvariant module, a parabolic-equivariant map on that module is determined by its values on $T$. The same argument for products uses the exterior tensor product of the types. On the full primed module compare $p_{1,s}Q_\infty$ with the pullback $B\circ p_s$. Both have the required parabolic law and their restrictions to $T$ belong to the same one-dimensional space. They are therefore proportional on the full module. This proves descent through the prescribed upper quotient, including on all upper kernel vectors. Both nonvanishing results above force their scalar to lie in $\mathbb C^\times$. For fixed real projections, polarizations and measures the two original real operators depend only on the fixed infinite exponents and Weil splitting there. The scalar is therefore common to the twisting tuples. [Source's parabolic multiplicity and use of nonvanishing][br-mult] · [Uniqueness across the choices][br-src]

The proof uses the two local mixed-model covariance formulas with their stated splittings, the Fock generation theorem and the real scalar-line calculation. The numerical value of the scalar depends on the chosen mixed evaluations and real quotient identifications.

## Finite quotient descent for the beta coefficient

Fix the upper triple $(p,q)=(0,1)$ and its definite lower triple, with the prescribed irreducible quotient maps on each. Write $p_v,p_{1,v}$ for the two projections on the side under consideration, $A_v$ for big zero evaluation or the small cone $\Lambda_v$, and $J,J_1$ for the corresponding integrated theta functions. Put $d=a+n$, $z=(a-n)/2+1$, and let $P=N\rtimes M$ be the target parabolic stabilizing $EH'.x$. For each finite $v$ there is a unique map $b_v:\Pi_v\to\Pi_{1,v}$ satisfying, for **every** local Schwartz–Bruhat test $x$,
$$
 b_v(p_v x)=p_{1,v}(A_v x),\qquad
 b_v\Pi_v(u m(t,h_1))=\beta_v(t)|t|_{E_v}^{a/2}\Pi_{1,v}(h_1)b_v.
$$
Here $u\in N(F_v)$ and $t\in(E\otimes_F F_v)^\times$. The proof uses the individual lifts and the two unfolded boundary identities at this step. [Local assertion on the selected triples][desc-end]

**Detection by the lower lift.** The [individual square-integrability argument](ROW_BETA_SQUARE_INTEGRABILITY.md) shows that $p_vx=0$ kills the upper integrated theta function with any fixed admissible remaining factors. Source invariance gives a map from the full local theta lift of the trivial source. Its image inherits a positive definite invariant product from $L^2$ and is semisimple by admissibility and finite length. The multiplicity-one cosocle identifies every nonzero such image with the quotient selected by $p_v$. At the prescribed primed place $v_1$, one may freeze a second split place with a full-rank representative of its vacuum class before allowing arbitrary tests in the active slot. Right source invariance preserves the original integrals on their domain during this replacement. On the lower definite triple both lifts factor through their local maps by the same reasoning. Each lower lift has a nonzero pure polynomial test, by the definite doubling nonvanishing input and finite expansion. Starting from such a test, vary one local factor. The kernel on its projected module is target-stable, and the old test shows that the kernel is proper. Irreducibility then makes it zero. Change in this way each of the finitely many non-vacuum components and the real polynomial component. Thus any admissible lower pure tensor with all components nonzero in their respective quotients has nonzero theta integral **as a function on the lower target**. [Definite seed][desc-seed] · [Finite and real propagation][desc-detect]

**The distinguished split place.** The small cone must respect the vacuum class even if the vacuum itself lacks full-rank support. Let $D$ be a scalar functional on lower tests that kills lower-source translation differences. Before compact averaging, set
$$
 F_x(g)=\int_{E_{v_1}^{\times}} D\!\left(E_B\rho(\bar l H.y\otimes H'.x)\,\omega'(g,1)x\right)\nu(l)|l|_{E_{v_1}}^z\,d^\times l,
$$
where $E_{v_1}=E\otimes_F F_{v_1}$. Mixed-model covariance for the **source** parabolic $P_X$ scales the evaluated integrand by $\nu(t)|t|_{E_{v_1}}^{d/2}$ and changes its scalar-line argument from $l$ to $tl$. In fact the extra shifts along $H.x$ and $V'_1$ are killed by evaluation, and $D$ removes the residual lower-source action. Changing variable to $tl$ cancels the character and leaves $|t|_{E_{v_1}}^{d/2-z}=|t|_{E_{v_1}}^{n-1}=\delta_{P_X}(m(t,g_1))$. The scalar integral converges by the same finite-place Schwartz and shell estimate as the cone. It takes the functional through a finite-dimensional family of evaluated tests. The Iwasawa quotient integration formula now makes $\int_K F_x(kg)\,dk$ independent of $g$. Take $D=\lambda\circ p'_{1,v_1}$ for every algebraic linear functional $\lambda$. These functionals separate points, so $p'_{1,v_1}\Lambda_{v_1}$ kills upper-source translation differences on the **entire** local Schwartz space. [Transformation of the evaluated slice][desc-slice] · [Scalar integration and compact averaging][desc-average] · [Iwasawa modulus][desc-iwa]

At this place the primed upper kernel is exactly the span of those differences. In the barred split chart the row sieve produces full-rank representatives modulo differences since $i\le n-1<d/2$ at each stage. On a pivot chart the weighted row-orbit integral identifies full-rank coinvariants with normalized parabolic induction from the two unitary block characters. This induced representation is irreducible, and its surjection to the nonzero upper quotient is an isomorphism. In particular the chosen full-rank representative of the upper vacuum has the same projected cone as the vacuum. If one also needs a fresh split place in a frozen vacuum slot, its class is nonzero concretely: the primitive-frame indicator at $Y_0=[I_n\ 0]$ has fiber integral over $g\in\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathcal O_v)$ equal to one, with the unramified character and unit-volume subgroup. Its class is the vacuum class multiplied by $\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}(1-\eta(\varpi_v)(\# k_v)^{i-d/2})\ne0$ in the standard barred coordinates. One may choose this auxiliary place outside all nonstandard chart and lattice data. [Full-rank coinvariants and orbit integral](ROW_FIRST_BETA_STEP.md#the-good-place-kernel-on-both-rectangular-blocks) · [Passage through the prescribed primed kernel][desc-aug]

**Fixing the other factors.** On the big side choose a real upper polynomial whose evaluation has nonzero lower projection, by surjectivity. On the small side take the polynomial produced by the [real cone nonvanishing calculation](ROW_BETA_LOCAL_COMPARISON.md#nonvanishing-on-the-two-actual-oscillator-spaces). At an unmarked finite place the big vacuum evaluates to the lower vacuum, and the small vacuum gives $u_w\Omega'_{1,w}$ with $u_w\ne0$. At a marked place the explicit standard tests give a nonzero lower class on each side. The standard primed value differs from the big value by its nonzero Gauss factor. At $v_1$ use the preceding full-rank representative. We may now freeze all factors except $v$. By factorization of the two boundary formulas their lower terms are
$$
 c\,J_1(f_1\otimes\psi^{v}\otimes A_vx),\qquad c\ne0,
$$
with $c$, the real test $f_1$ and the finite tests away from $v$ independent of $x$. More precisely, take a finite set containing the active place, all marked places and all nonstandard test factors. Outside it use the lower vacua and absorb their unmarked Euler factors in $c$. The absolute Euler product at $z\ge2$ is nonzero, as are the finitely many width and Haar factors. The projected lower factors in every frozen slot are nonzero. [Choice of test on each side][desc-pt] · [Factorization with one variable place][desc-fac]

If $p_vx=0$, the upper theta function vanishes. Its beta coefficient vanishes, so the displayed lower theta function vanishes. Lower detection forces $p_{1,v}A_vx=0$. Since $p_v$ is onto, this defines $b_v$ uniquely. Initially the primed argument uses full-rank representatives at $v_1$. The source-invariance calculation there identifies the projected cone of every test with that of such a representative. Finally apply the unfolded beta transformation law to the upper test translated by $u m(t,h_1)$. It equates the two lower functions obtained by placing $A_v\omega_v(1,u m(t,h_1))x$ and $\beta_v(t)|t|_{E_v}^{a/2}\omega_{1,v}(1,h_1)A_vx$ in the variable slot. Subtract and apply the same detection to their difference. This proves the asserted covariance on all quotient classes. The matching maps intertwine target actions, so the two resulting $b_v$ have the same source, target and parabolic law. Their proportionality factor is then determined by the unmarked vacuum or the marked multiplicity and standard-test calculation. ## The cone scalar at every unmarked finite place

At the first beta step put $z=(a-n)/2+1$, where $n$ is the upper small-source rank. Write $\mathfrak b,\mathfrak c$ for the adapted ideals of $H\subset V'$ and $H'\subset W$. Fix $v\notin T_t$, and let $\nu=\nu_t$. For each $w\mid v$ set
$$
 \mathfrak a=\mathfrak b^{-1}\overline{\mathfrak c},\qquad
 m_w=\operatorname{ord}_w(\mathfrak a),\qquad
 t_w=\nu_w(\varpi_w)(Nw)^{-z}.
$$
The value of $\nu_w(\varpi_w)$ is independent of the choice of uniformizer because $\nu_w$ is unramified. With the lattice stabilizer and the multiplicative units both of mass one, the primed local cone operator satisfies
$$
 \Lambda_v\Omega'_v=u_v\,\Omega'_{1,v},\qquad
 u_v=\prod_{w\mid v}\frac{t_w^{m_w}}{1-t_w}\ne0.
$$
This includes both places above a split $v$ and the single place with its own residue norm above an inert $v$. [Haar normalizations][uv-meas] · [Local factor over all places above $v$][uv-prod]

Indeed, the line of $H.y$ in the Hermitian lattice has scalar ideal $\overline{\mathfrak b}^{-1}$ by self-duality and the unit cross pairing. The line of $H'.x$ in the skew-Hermitian lattice has ideal $\mathfrak c$ by adaptation. The direct lattice decompositions on the two planes imply that the intersection of the tensor lattice with $E(H.y\otimes H'.x)$ has scalar ideal $\overline{\mathfrak b}^{-1}\mathfrak c$. The beta shift uses $\bar l$ in this direction, so the condition on $l$ is $\operatorname{ord}_w(l_w)\ge m_w$ for each $w\mid v$. Inside the lattice the isotropic Heisenberg translation fixes the vacuum and evaluation gives precisely the lower vacuum. Outside the lattice choose a dual lattice translation in the complementary direction whose commutator character is nontrivial. That translation fixes the vacuum and leaves the evaluation unchanged; commuting it across forces the evaluated vector to vanish. The stabilizer average preserves the vacuum off $T_t$. The remaining integral is therefore over the indicated valuation shells. The shell $\varpi_w^k\mathcal O_w^\times$ has multiplicative measure one and contributes $t_w^k$. Since $|t_w|=(Nw)^{-z}<1$, summing over $k\ge m_w$ gives the formula even for negative widths. [Adapted line ideals and the conjugated shift][uv-ideal] · [Evaluation inside and outside the lattice][uv-eval] · [Application to the actual averaged vacuum][uv-vac] · [Shell integral][uv-shell]

The big zero-orbit evaluation sends $\Omega_v$ to $\Omega_{1,v}$. The matching quotients identify the upper vacuum images and identify the two lower vacuum images, the latter being nonzero. Hence, on the common upper vacuum class, the small cone followed by lower projection equals $u_v$ times big evaluation followed by lower projection. This also determines the comparison on every upper class once the two operators descend to the prescribed beta-equivariant maps to the lower quotient. In fact, write $g=pk$ by Iwasawa decomposition for the target parabolic stabilizing $EH'.x$. The compact factor $k$ preserves the upper vacuum line. The two maps have the same parabolic transformation law under $p$, so their difference after scaling by $u_v$ vanishes on every $g$-translate of the vacuum class. Those translates span the irreducible upper quotient since that class is nonzero. At the distinguished good place the source first descends the full-rank representatives through finite coinvariants. [Parabolic argument on all translates][uv-iwa] · [Descent at the distinguished place and application][uv-all]

Finally $\prod_w t_w^{m_w}=\nu(\mathfrak a)N(\mathfrak a)^{-z}$. The adapted ideals have valuation zero at the controlled places. Since $\nu$ is trivial on the base ideles, $\nu(\mathfrak a)=\nu(\mathfrak b)^{-1}\nu(\mathfrak c)^{-1}$. Thus $N(\mathfrak a)^{-z}$ is the explicit width contribution to the tuple-independent cone normalization. The denominators supply the unmarked Euler factors; the marked local comparison and the real constant enter separately. [Character of the beta ideal][uv-char] · [Norm separated in the global product][uv-norm]

## The marked split beta factor on all quotient classes

Keep the first beta step and fix $v\in T_t$. Put $k=F_v$, $Q=\#(\mathcal O_k/\pi)$ and $\chi=\mathrm{chrW}(\nu_t,\tau_v)$ on the chosen split factor. The conjugate factor has character $\chi^{-1}$. Here $\pi=\mathrm{unifAt}(v)$, $f=\mathrm{condExp}(\chi)\ge1$ is the exact conductor, $\psi_v$ has conductor $\mathcal O_k$, and $z=(a-n)/2+1=e+1$. Write $p,p'$ for the paired projections onto the upper local quotient and $p_1,p'_1$ for the corresponding lower ones. Once big evaluation $E_v$ and the primed cone $\Lambda_v$ descend to maps on the common upper quotient with the beta parabolic law, their comparison for *all* matched Schwartz tests is
$$
p'_1(\Lambda_v\phi')=\epsilon_v(\chi,-e)\,p_1(E_v\phi)
\qquad (p\phi=p'\phi'),
$$
with the following scalar in the source normalization:
$$
 \epsilon_v(\chi,-e)=
 Q^{fe}\,\chi(\pi)^f
 \sum_{u\in(\mathcal O_k/\pi^f)^\times}
            \chi(\widetilde u)^{-1}\psi_v(\widetilde u/\pi^f).
$$
The lifts $\widetilde u\in\mathcal O_k^\times$ may be chosen arbitrarily. Both characters in each summand are constant on lifts. The local sign parameter in the pinned beta construction is $+1$. The extra factor $\chi(+1)$ is one. [Conductor and conjugate character][mv-cond] · [Original Gauss sum, epsilon and sign convention][mv-eps] · [Local sign in the step factor][mv-sign]

**Evaluation on one pair.** In split matrix coordinates choose the first $a$ designated columns on the big side and the last $n$ on the small side. With designated columns $D$, take the test supported where $Y_D\in\mathrm{GL}_{|D|}(\mathcal O_k)$ and every entry outside $D$ belongs to $\pi^f\mathcal O_k$, of value $\chi(\det Y_D)^{-1}$ there. The normalized split zeta maps identify their images. The analogous pair at the lower ranks also has equal, nonzero images. These tests are fixed under the respective local source lattice stabilizers. [Formula for the tests][mv-test] · [Matched images and invariance on the actual triple][mv-data]

Give additive Haar measure $dx$ the normalization $\mathrm{vol}(\mathcal O_k)=1$. The big evaluation integrates $a$ width-$\pi^f$ coordinates and gives $Q^{-af}$ times the lower big test. In the primed cone, the same $a$ unconstrained narrow coordinates give $Q^{-af}$. The remaining translated and modulated matrix entries in the adapted hyperbolic frames give the function $D_\chi(x)\widehat D_\chi(y)$ on the two split torus variables, with $D_\chi=1_{\mathcal O_k^\times}\chi^{-1}$ and $\widehat D_\chi(y)=\int D_\chi(r)\psi_v(yr)\,dr$. For $\operatorname{ord}(y)<-f$, integrating on additive cosets modulo $\pi^f$ gives zero. If $\operatorname{ord}(y)>-f$, multiplication by a unit on which $\chi$ is nontrivial but the additive character is unchanged likewise gives zero, using primitivity. On $y=u\pi^{-f}$ the transform is $\chi(u)\widehat D_\chi(\pi^{-f})$. Integrating against $\chi(x)\chi(y)^{-1}|xy|^z$ with unit multiplicative mass gives
$$
 \chi(\pi)^f Q^{fz}\widehat D_\chi(\pi^{-f}),\qquad
 \widehat D_\chi(\pi^{-f})=Q^{-f}\!\sum_u\chi(\widetilde u)^{-1}\psi_v(\widetilde u/\pi^f).
$$
In particular the scalar is nonzero, since the Fourier transform of the nonzero $D_\chi$ is supported on this one shell and determined throughout it by the displayed value. The matched lower standard pair cancels the identical $Q^{-af}$ on the two sides. [Big-side evaluation and cancellation][mv-cal] · [Primed matrix integration and split torus integral][mv-cone] · [Identification with the stated epsilon][mv-gauss]

**Passage to all inputs.** Let $L,L'$ be the two descended maps to the irreducible lower quotient. For the stabilizer of the target isotropic line they kill the unipotent differences and have the same Levi law $\beta_t(l)|l|_E^{a/2}$ times the lower target action. Pull them back through the big-source realization with its character $\beta_t/\alpha_t$. At this split place the latter is ramified. Set $d_1=a+n-2\ge a$ and write an $a$-by-$(d_1+2)$ matrix as its middle block $Z$ and two exterior columns $x,y$. The split-model reduction says that a map with these invariances is determined by the tests $\Psi(Z)1_{\mathcal O_k^a}(x)1_{\mathcal O_k^a}(y)$ for $\Psi$ supported on full-row-rank middle blocks. On the full-rank charts the right shear transformations and left changes of basis reduce arbitrary exterior-column cosets to this family. The boundary reduction uses a unit on which the ramified character is nontrivial, together with the source coinvariant relations. [Parabolic transformation law][mv-law] · [Matrix reduction, including the full-rank and boundary cases][mv-red]

On that family the two maps intertwine the middle $\mathrm{GL}_{d_1}(k)$ action. The full-rank functions modulo the twisted left $\mathrm{GL}_a(k)$ relations have irreducible quotient in the sense used here: any stable subspace containing the relations either contains every full-rank test or meets the full-rank tests only in the relations. This follows by realizing the full-rank orbit as an induced representation and using irreducibility of the normalized product of the two unitary determinant characters, distinct on units here. Consequently the kernel of the nonzero map $L$ on this family is contained in the kernel of $L'$. Its image is the whole irreducible lower quotient. Thus $L'$ induces an endomorphism there intertwining the middle group. The lower quotient is countable-dimensional, being a quotient of local Schwartz–Bruhat functions, so Schur's lemma over $\mathbf C$ makes this endomorphism scalar. The matrix reduction applied to $L'-cL$ extends the scalar to every input. The displayed standard pair has nonzero big evaluation and fixes that scalar as $\epsilon_v(\chi,-e)$. This proves the marked-place comparison after descent. The [finite quotient descent](#finite-quotient-descent-for-the-beta-coefficient) supplies the two equivariant maps used in this last passage.
