MATHEMATICAL CHECKS

QR15. The finite Blaschke product divides out all zeros in the closed
R-disk with their actual multiplicities. There is no zero at zero.
On the unit circle it has modulus one, so the maximum principle gives
the required bound on the quotient. The product of |a_j| gives the
zero count N*log(1/R)<=L. The logarithm exists on a slightly larger
zero-free disk around the closed R-disk, which justifies the Poisson
derivative estimate and its limiting-radius formulation. The logarithmic
derivative of (z-a)/(1-conj(a)z) has PLUS conj(a)/(1-conj(a)z);
the displayed remainder uses this sign correctly. Its bound combines
with the zero count and yields precisely the local-source conclusion.

QR16. The rescaling sends radii 1/2 and 3/5 to 5/7 and 6/7 in the
radius-7/10 disk. Its leftmost real coordinate is 11/20, inside the
analytic half-plane and at the stated growth boundary. At 5/4+it,
the lower L-bound is 1/25; the principal factor has modulus >=1/4,
giving 1/100. The upper bound follows from |s|+1<=|t|+3.
All zero terms have nonnegative real parts on sigma>1. Dropping
unselected terms gives the correct UPPER bound for G, because they
appear with a minus sign. The error is uniform over the family.

QR17. Checked both numerical cases independently. For a real
character with 0<|gamma|<30epsilon, the two conjugate zeros give
120/(4621epsilon), while the sole principal pole contributes
1/(60epsilon). The difference is positive and implies the stated
weaker 300C estimate. The two zeros fit inside the local disk.
In the other case the selected zero contributes at least
4/(7epsilon); the main pole is 1/(2epsilon), and all shifted
principal terms together are bounded by 5/(156epsilon).
The residual gap is positive and 9 divided by it is less than
300. For nonreal characters the shifted pole terms are absent.
The chosen epsilon obeys all smallness assumptions and is too
small for either resulting inequality. The conclusion allows
exactly real zeros of real characters.

QR18. The positivity inequalities count zero OCCURRENCES, including
multiplicity. A repeated zero of one character is therefore covered.
For two distinct nonprincipal real characters, 1,chi,psi,chi*psi
are distinct and there is exactly one principal pole. A principal/
nonprincipal pair uses the two-term inequality. In all cases the
two zero terms exceed the unique pole plus at most four errors
for the chosen c_un. Thus uniqueness is across the entire family
and the surviving zero is simple, not merely a bound on the
number of exceptional characters.

QR19. The smaller strip is indeed inside the zero-free strip.
The local radius-3/5 zeros have ell(im rho)<=4ell(t)/3, giving the
stated c0/(4ell(t)) lower real separation for nonexceptions.
The exceptional zero cancels with coefficient exactly one.
If it is outside the local disk, its distance from a point in
the radius-1/2 disk is >=1/10. The range sigma>=7/4 is controlled
directly by absolute convergence and the pole/exception distances.
The conclusion also establishes F(s)!=0 away from listed zeros,
as required for the quotient, and matches literal ExcZ.

QR20. Truncated Perron retains the full floor endpoint. The infinite
kernel is 1_{y>1} away from y=1; tail integration by parts and the
uniform sine-integral bound give the stated min estimate. At y=1,
K_T(1)=atan(T/a)/pi, so the full endpoint costs at most |b(u)|.
The at most two integers nearest u absorb arbitrarily close
nonintegral endpoints without a distance-to-integers assumption.
The remaining near terms cost u log^2(u)/T and the far series is
bounded by the integral test. Uniform a<=3 and u^a=e*u are valid
under 2<=T<=u. Exact source quantifiers were checked.

QR21. One of the two left edges is at least tau from the unique
possible exceptional zero. Both lie in the half-width zero-free
strip for every |t|<=T. Horizontal edges are also separated from
the real pole and exception. There is no pole from 1/s because
sigma>=1/2. The logarithmic derivative has residues +1 at the
principal pole and -1 at a simple exceptional zero, giving the
displayed signs and u^beta/beta term. If the exception lies
outside the rectangle on its left, inserting it costs the
stated 2u^(1-2tau). The conservative constants 18 and 4 cover
the horizontal and vertical integrals, respectively. The
residue of L itself is correctly absent from the main term.

QR22. In the large range eta W>1, T=e^(eta W)>2 and T<=u.
ell(T)<=3W, M<=K W^4, and u^(1-2tau)<=u/T^2 hold with the
specified constants. The resulting sum of Perron and contour
errors is bounded by (2P+22K+2)W^4u/T. The exponential-series
inequality correctly absorbs W^4. In the complementary range,
the coarse absolute Chebyshev bound supplies O(u), while
exp(-eta W/2)>=1/2. The displayed C7 covers that range without
the source's sharper numerical Chebyshev constant. The
existential uniform explicit-formula endpoint is unchanged.

QR23. The chosen c0 is common to all characters and lies within
both zero-free and uniqueness widths. QR14 supplies the gap
c9*B^-46 for the sole possible exception. The residue difference
is the integral of t^(beta-1); X>=e makes X^(beta-1)<=exp(-Delta).
Convexity on [0,1] gives exp(-Delta)<=1-Delta/2. The argument
deducts the loss ONCE across the family, rather than once per
character, which is essential for the positive margin.

QR24. Character orthogonality at the trivial class has no
character-sign ambiguity. Summed endpoint errors are at most
3 B C7 X exp(-c7 W). Dividing by hX Delta/4 gives
(12C7/c9) B^47 h^-1 exp(-c7W), bounded by the displayed
exponential with coefficient (46+2)c-c7. The two conditions
in the minimum defining c make the ratio <=1. The exception
leaves hX Delta/2, the error uses at most hX Delta/4, and division
by |G|<=B gives c9 hX/(4B^47). Thus exponent 47 and the
uniform-before-family constant order are correct.

QR25. The retained QW11 prime-power removal is applied with

prime count. The conductor and short-interval restrictions
are preserved. The literal ShortNorm, levelPrimeN and
