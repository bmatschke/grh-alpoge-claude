# The reference theta kernel after extension of coefficients

The reference tuple is the choice with trivial twist. At this tuple the arithmetic coefficient field can grow while the oscillator representation still acts on the original space of complex-valued functions. A splitting character specifies how the unitary group acts in the metaplectic cover. A choice of lattice and vacuum specifies the finite test function. A polynomial in the Fock model specifies the normalization of an archimedean intertwiner.

**Theorem.** Fix the original analytic realization on a CM extension $E/F$ and an enlargement of its coefficient field retaining the underlying arithmetic data, character frame and marked prime indices. Assume the enlarged realization has the level prescribed by its column. At the reference tuple both splitting characters agree with the old ones, as do the marked residue characters for the row and the set of twist primes. Suppose also that the old realization satisfies the original existence conditions for its chosen analytic maps (`ExAt`), and use the [comparison of the models and reference maps](THETA_COEFFICIENT_EXTENSION.md). At every matched row the enlarged Weil action has the prescribed splitting, the transported test function is the required lattice-and-vacuum vector, and the transported polynomial map satisfies both equivariance identities and the specified value on unit vectors. This is the reference-tuple assertion `WeilAt`. [Character hypotheses and conclusion][level] · [Weil hypotheses and conclusion][weil]

## Which characters enter the oscillator representation?

Number rows by $i=0,\ldots,m-1$ and put $j=i+1$. Write $\mu_j^0$ for the row's untwisted idèle character. At a tuple $t$ the row character is $\mu_j(t)=\mu_j^0\xi_t$. The reference Hecke character for $\xi_t$ is trivial. Its idèle realization is therefore trivial as well. Indeed, the ratio of two continuous idèle class characters realizing the same Hecke character equals one on the copy of $E^\times$ supported at each finite place $P$. Density in $E_P^\times$ and continuity give equality on that whole local group. Products of elements supported at finitely many places are dense in the finite idèles, so the ratio is one there. It is also one on principal idèles. Weak approximation at the infinite places makes their archimedean components dense in $E_\infty^\times$, proving equality on all idèles. This proves uniqueness of the realization, including its values on the infinite components. [Meaning of realization][chardef] · [Uniqueness in the source][unique]

The two stages use the same frame Hecke characters for $\mu_j^0$ and $\eta$. Applying uniqueness first to $\xi_t$ and then to these two characters identifies the ordered pair in the row lift as

$$
   (\chi_V,\chi_W)=(\mu_j^0,\mu_j^0\eta^{-1})
$$

on both sides. At a marked prime $P$, the specified residue character $c_{j,P}$ is fixed by $c_{j,P}(u)^{-1}=\mu^0_{j,P}(a)$ for an integral lift $a$ of $u\in k_P^\times$. Such a lift exists and is a unit at $P$. Evaluating both stage prescriptions on it gives exactly the same residue character with the same inverse convention. Finally the ramification set of $\xi_t$ is $Q_t\cup\overline{Q_t}$. Triviality forces $Q_t=\varnothing$ at the reference tuple on both sides. This proves the entire level-character comparison. [Row character convention][ordered] · [Marked inverse character and twist support][leveldef] · [Comparison across fields][level]

The use of two characters is classical. In Kudla's splitting of a unitary dual pair, $\chi_V$ restricts to the quadratic character of $E/F$ raised to $\dim V$ and specifies the splitting on $U(W)$. Likewise $\chi_W$ restricts with exponent $\dim W$ and specifies the splitting on $U(V)$. At nonsplit nonarchimedean places, Harris–Kudla–Sweet give the character conditions and the lifting homomorphism explicitly. Their ordered pair $(\chi_1,\chi_2)$ in (0.2) is indexed by the two *groups acted on*, so the source's dimension-indexed characters occur there as $(\chi_W,\chi_V)$. The source fixes its convention at split places explicitly as well. For determinant coordinates $x$ on the $V$ side and $y$ on the $W$ side its phase is $\chi_W(x)^{-1}\chi_V(y)$. Substitution of the displayed pair reproduces this very phase after enlargement. [Kudla, *Splitting metaplectic covers of dual reductive pairs*](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02773003) · [Harris–Kudla–Sweet, (0.2) and Appendix A, (a.2)–(a.6)](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0894-0347-96-00198-1) · [Source's determinant phase and parity conditions][phase]

The original normalized action already satisfies the Heisenberg intertwining law, rational invariance, continuity and unitarity. These identities use the same unitary groups, additive character and action on the same functions after the [coefficient enlargement](THETA_COEFFICIENT_EXTENSION.md). The old split-place identity therefore holds with its original positive modulus factor as well. The spherical-vacuum identity at unramified odd nonsplit places and the real differential formula use the original lattices, frames, signatures and infinitesimal character exponents. All those inputs agree. This gives the full Weil action required at this tuple. [Local identities for the full action][full] · [Transport of the action][action]

## The finite test and the polynomial normalization

At a fixed row put $V=V_i$ and $W=W_i$. Start with the old unit vacuum $f_0$, invariant under the lattice at the finite places and satisfying the Fock vacuum equations at the real places. The reference test function is obtained in the split local model by applying it to a particular function of finite matrices. In adjoint frame coordinates, its support is on the conjugate half of the marked split factors and requires integrality. At each marked prime its reduction has identity top square block of size $\dim W$ and zero entries below that block in the first $\dim W$ columns. The associated factor $\omega_K$ is the indicator of these conditions. The remaining determinant factor $\omega_Q$ is an empty product at the reference tuple and thus equals one, whatever values were assigned to twist characters away from their support. The two stages have the same marked idempotents, lattices, frames and symplectic form. The defining translation, modulation and vacuum identities therefore specify the same set of local-model maps. The old realization supplies such a map and gives $f$ on this test for every such map. Both existence and the value for every choice carry over. This proves the required test-vector assertion. [Matrix cutoff and empty product][cutoff] · [Adjoint frame and conjugate half][test] · [Vacuum normalization and local-model definition][vacuum]

For the last assertion, let $U_\tau$ be the lowest compact type in the framed row discrete series at the real place $\tau$, and let $C_i$ be the complex definite coefficient space. The old polynomial map is a bilinear map
$$
 I_i:\left(\bigotimes_\tau U_\tau\right)\times C_i
          \longrightarrow \mathcal{P}_i .
$$
Here $\mathcal{P}_i$ is the polynomial Fock space in the matrix variables at all real places. Its realization on the global theta functions uses the test function $f$ above. The two required transformation laws intertwine the action of the archimedean group of $V$ on $C_i$ and the action of each framed maximal compact subgroup of $U(W_\tau)$ on $U_\tau$. The same framed representations, coefficient action, and polynomial-to-function map occur on both sides. Substitution in each transformation law gives the enlarged equivariance. [Both transformation laws and the normalization][poly]

The target polynomial $\Delta_i$ is explicit. At each real embedding it is a product of powers of minors of the matrix of variables. The positive weights use upper-left minors. The negative part uses $k$ by $k$ minors whose rows start just below the positive block and whose columns are the last $k$ columns. The exponents are the successive differences of the prescribed partitions, with a final zero appended. Multiplication over the embeddings gives $\Delta_i$. All indices and integer exponents depend only on the host signatures, the row and its weights. Consequently the polynomial in the new realization is coefficientwise the original one. [Polynomial and minors][minors] · [Row signatures and weights][delta]

The old existence condition turns the weak specification of $I_i$ into its actual normalized identity. In particular it supplies $u_\tau\in U_\tau$ and $w\in C_i$ of norm one with $I_i(\bigotimes_\tau u_\tau,w)=\Delta_i$. Take these same vectors. Their norms agree under the identified Hermitian structures, and the copied bilinear map has exactly the same value. This establishes the required existential statement with scalar one. Together with the two preceding assertions it proves the theorem at every row. [Passage from the weak to the realized map][exact] · [Rowwise assembly][assembly]

This comparison takes the original normalized realization as input. At nontrivial twists the determinant factor and its ramification set reappear. The [reference integral comparison](THETA_REFERENCE_LIFT.md) proves the additional identities for the theta lift and its good projection. [Integral and projected lift definitions][integral]
