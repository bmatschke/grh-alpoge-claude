1. EXECUTIVE PLAN

The terminal analytic deduction is already written. The central task is
to supply its remaining arithmetic, geometric and quantitative inputs
on the same objects and with the required order of choices, then join
them into one proof. The final manuscript also needs consolidation so
that a reader can follow the mathematical mechanism without following
the chronological claim ledger. [S1–S5; CMP1–CMP6]

The most useful immediate split is four concurrent producer streams:

A. Common arithmetic end data: construct the actual GdEnd supplier and
   develop the separate CountV host/support/catalogue lane.

B. Principal geometry and test rings: consume the now-complete finite
   holomorphic FamilyUnif/N1c, produce the principal framed charts and
   test-ring dictionary, then the remaining spanning and common readings.

C. Analytic nonvanishing and comparison: prove the exact BesselNVGap,
   then the original orbital/fiber and Siegel–Weil identifications.

D. Global comparison and joins: prove independent denominator, retained
   defect, period/height and spectral inputs while maintaining the exact
   common-family and selection interfaces. Integration/review can be
   owned here, with independent review assignments rotated.

These are scheduling recommendations, not newly asserted dependency
edges. Several streams meet at common forms, fields, frames, measures
and family choices. Parallel work should produce explicit interface
theorems so that those choices can later be identified literally.
There is no justified calendar estimate or unique shortest critical
path at present; some of the remaining entries are large theorems.

3. THE FINAL MATHEMATICAL TARGET

The existing conditional main proof organizes the conclusion around a
fixed primitive target character. Compatible data give a centered,
smoothed prime-sum identity. Arithmetic divisibility, archimedean size
and overlap estimates give the required square-root-scale bound on
arbitrarily late intervals [Y,Y^(3/2)]. A zero to the right of 1/2
contradicts that bound on every sufficiently late such interval.
The functional equation and primitive/imprimitive comparison then give
the full intended conclusion. For ordinary RH, the target is psi=1.
[S5; CMP1–CMP5]

The remaining reconstruction should therefore be evaluated by two tests:

  1. Does it supply one of the actual input theorems used by this route,
     with all its objects, hypotheses and choice order?
  2. If it replaces that theorem by a simpler sufficient statement, has
     the implication from the replacement to the final consumer been
     written and independently reviewed?

CMP6 records an alternative averaged sufficient input. It is available
if it genuinely removes a remaining producer and all its measurability
and uniformity hypotheses can be supplied. It is an optional route,
not an additional task that must be completed alongside the main route.

  can be assigned as soon as their inputs are fixed.

  Analytic worker: write the full BesselNVGap contract and reconstruct
  the source nonvanishing argument, or match an exact sufficient
  published theorem with every hypothesis checked. The packet and
  norm estimates already completed are the downstream consumers.

  Global/integration worker: choose one complete remaining LinkN,
  LinkIRR, LinkDIVt or LinkS producer after checking the original
  current dependencies; develop independent estimates while recording
  the exact common-family join requirements. Rotate review work so
  every new proof is reviewed by someone other than its author.

After a bounded task closes, allocate the freed worker to the highest-
impact remaining producer, including the CountV or additional GRH lane.
Do not let the most recently edited chapter determine all future work.
Do not postpone all quantitative analysis until geometry is finished:
it may reveal the exact strengths and normalizations geometry must
supply. Conversely, do not count a conditional quantitative consumer
as construction of its arithmetic/geometric inputs.

For each handoff, record:
  • The exact original statement and scope.
  • The given objects and the order of universal/existential choices.
  • The written proof and the completed claims it consumes.
  • Named external foundations with hypotheses checked.
  • The exact remaining input, if the result is conditional.
  • The next consumer and the equality/transport that connects them.


15. CRITERIA FOR ACCEPTING PROGRESS AND CLOSING AN OBLIGATION

A useful result can be a complete internal theorem without closing a
whole selected obligation. Report both levels explicitly.

Before marking a whole obligation closed:

  1. Compare the written statement to the original contract in [S1]
     and its pinned source predicates.
  2. Cover every permitted coefficient ring, prime, embedding, level,
     index and eventual guard used by that contract.
  3. Preserve its actual objects and normalizations, especially the
     common field, marked element, lattice, ideal, theta map, metric,
     measure and kernel.
  4. Preserve choices made before later universally quantified data.
     Separate existence witnesses may need a further compatibility
     proof before they can be joined.
  5. State and discharge the actual remaining producer premises, or
     retain them when they are part of the original conditional
     contract. Do not close an unconditional producer by adding one.
  6. Obtain an independent mathematical and interface review; bind it
     to the exact proof text and source ranges.
  7. Update the selected register and claim dependencies while keeping
     predecessor proof bodies and provenance intact.

Simplification is welcome when it reduces this work. WRS's direct
built-membership route is an example: it proved the original endpoint
without completing a stronger historical construction. For any similar
replacement, record exactly which dependency was bypassed, why the
original consumer still follows, and where the stronger construction
may remain necessary for a different obligation.


16. FINISHING THE MATHEMATICS AND THE HUMAN EXPOSITION

Ordinary RH assembly
  Once every required shared input is written and the common-family
  interfaces are joined, instantiate CMP5 at the principal target.
  Give a standalone ordinary-RH theorem with an explicit map from
  each remaining named input to its completed proof. The present
  roadmap does not assert that this stage has been reached.

Dirichlet-GRH assembly
  Complete the seven additional obligations and install the Case-III
  conclusions. Instantiate the conditional main argument for every
  original primitive target and retain the full primitive/imprimitive
  passage. A result restricted to selected characters or conductors
  would not meet this goal.

Final dependency check
  Starting at each final theorem, follow the human mathematical
  dependencies. Every premise must be supplied by a written proof or
  a precisely stated published theorem with its hypotheses checked.
  No undefined producer, unproved same-object identification, hidden
  nonemptiness assumption, or new unrecorded choice-order requirement
  may remain. The claim inventory itself does not establish this.

Expository consolidation
  Reorganize the cumulative chronological reconstruction into a
  natural mathematical reading order, for example:
    1. The prime-sum detector and conditional main theorem.
    2. Common arithmetic geometry, principal charts and theta maps.
    3. Deformation/Selmer, automorphy and representation counts.
    4. Real/adelic kernels, Bessel periods and measure comparisons.
    5. The simultaneous local and archimedean quantitative comparison.
    6. Ordinary RH, followed by the additional Dirichlet-GRH cases.

  Keep exact definitions near first use, standardize notation, explain
  the role of each major theorem, and replace repetitive installation
  fragments by readable combined arguments where their scopes agree.
  Historical proofs, corrections and provenance can remain in separate
  appendices and the continuation bundle. Preserve an index from the
  final theorem numbering back to the reviewed claim records.

Completion means both mathematical closure and a proof a suitably
trained reader can verify without consulting Lean. No Lean/kernel
replay is part of this reconstruction task. A PDF can be rendered when
useful; the complete TeX and continuation bundle remain the essential
deliverables.
