# One ray specialization controls every determinantal ideal of a projected theta basis

On one first core with its original Hecke action, integral averaged theta columns and reciprocal ray coordinates, Kaiwen Jin's specialization uses a single character to control the full determinant on every projected row. The deletion of an auxiliary prime is a **matrix** equality, which also controls the sums of the smallest elementary-divisor valuations at every order. The result below uses the same prescribed conductors to avoid, sides, unit powers, and first-core hypotheses as Jin's proposition. [Jin's statement and hypotheses][jin-input] · [Our common-root construction](FIRST_PASS_COPRIME_SPECIALIZATION.md)

Let $O$ be the fixed coefficient DVR at a single active index, with absolute valuation $v_\ell(\ell)=1$. At one of its pairs $(j,b)$ write $B$ for the full integral ambient lattice in the projected rational span and $L\subset B\otimes\operatorname{Frac}(O)$ for the starting projected lattice. Choose a basis represented by the prescribed averaged series. Let $L'(a)$ be the span of the specialized projected series in the same rational space after coefficient extension at the side power $(2b-1)a$. Here $a$ ranges over **all** integers prime to $\ell$. Relative to $B$, write $s_h(L)$ for the normalized valuation of the fractional ideal generated by the order-$h$ minors of a basis matrix. It equals the sum of the smallest $h$ Smith valuations. The same definition for a spanning list uses all column minors. Scalar extension of $B$ preserves this normalization.

For any $\epsilon>0$ the conductor and character in the specialization can be chosen so that, simultaneously on all those pairs, all unit powers, and all $1\le h\le\operatorname{rank}B$,
$$
                  s_h(L'(a))\le s_h(L)+\epsilon .                \tag{1}
$$
The original strict Selmer, toric and tame bounds hold on this choice with their given tolerances. A smaller tolerance per label makes the sum of the errors less than any prescribed positive budget. Orders for rank-zero rows are empty. One may enlarge the forbidden conductor set by the fixed bad primes of the [lower marking models](SECOND_PASS_EXACT_MARKING_DESCENT.md) before this choice. The argument is on a supplied first core with Jin's integral matrix comparison and specialization polynomials.

## Extracting a minor from the deletion of primes

Fix a row of rank $n$ and use the single nonzero clearance $c=x A_*$ of the projected theta vectors in Jin's basis of $B$. Write $M_0$ for the $n$ cleared original columns. Let $v_h$ be the minimum of the integral uniformizer valuations of its order-$h$ minors. Thus $0\le v_h\le v_n=v(\det M_0)$ and $v_h/e=s_h(L)+h v_\ell(c)$, where $e=v(\ell)$. At any admissible auxiliary depth $M$, take the same selected $n$ columns of the cleared matrix $M_Q(X,Y)$. First-group coordinates are $X$ and second-group coordinates are $Y$, with the sign already fixed by the actual side. Integral representatives of matrix entries in the finite group ring can be used throughout. When $Y=0$, the iterated deletion of the second group gives
$$
 M_Q(X,0)=H(X)M_1(X)
 \quad\bmod\big((1+X_j)^{\ell^M}-1\big)_j.
$$
Here $H$ is the product of the corresponding integral Hecke matrices, and $M_1$ is the matrix at the first group alone. Deleting the first group at zero left-multiplies $M_0$ by a unimodular matrix, since every first-group Hecke factor then has unit determinant. Hence the minimum valuation of an order-$h$ minor of $M_1(0)$ is exactly $v_h$. Choose row and column subsets $I,J$ realizing that minimum, for each row, depth and $h$. One may pass to a common subsequence on which these choices are constant, since only finitely many subsets occur for each of the finitely many rows. [Integral cleared basis][jin-basis] · [Actual deletion matrices and their reductions][jin-delete]

Let $H^{[h]}$ denote the matrix of $\bigwedge^h H$ in the induced ordered bases. Let $C_h(H)$ be the matrix of signed complementary $(n-h)$-minors, with indices transposed so that
$$
                         C_h(H)H^{[h]}=(\det H)I .              \tag{2}
$$
This identity follows by applying $H$ to both factors of the perfect exterior pairing $\bigwedge^h B\times\bigwedge^{n-h} B\to\det B$. It holds over every commutative ring, including the integral group ring. At $h=n$, $C_n(H)=(1)$. Form the scalar polynomials
$$
 F_h(X,Y)=\big(C_h(H(X))M_Q(X,Y)^{[h]}\big)_{I,J},
 \qquad G_h(X)=\big(M_1(X)^{[h]}\big)_{I,J}.
$$
Equations (2) and the deletion identity give $F_h(X,0)=(\det H(X))G_h(X)$ modulo the first-group period relations. Also $v(G_h(0))=v_h$. Jin proves that the reduction of $\det H(X)$ modulo the coordinate powers is a monomial of bounded order in each coordinate times a polynomial with unit constant term. The factor here is exactly that same determinant at every order $h$. This avoids a larger degree bound for the new scalar tests. One applies the polynomial argument to $F_h$, an integral linear combination of minors, so it also covers matrices whose minimum minor changes its row position at a deletion. [Residual determinant factor][jin-delete]

## One choice for all the inequalities

Adjoin these finitely many $F_h$ to the existing Hecke, toric and Selmer tests before choosing the root order. In the common one-variable substitution, every new test has the same monomial-order bound for its deletion factor and $v_h\le v_n$ for the constant valuation. The bounded-witness argument therefore provides a coefficient of valuation at most $v_h$ at an order bounded independently of depth. In detail, if the reduction of the substituted deletion factor has first unit coefficient at $t$, vanishing of every coefficient of the product through $(v_h+1)t$ modulo $\pi^{v_h+1}$ would force $G_h(0)\equiv0\bmod\pi^{v_h+1}$ by consecutive coefficient comparison. The vanished $Y$-coordinates and the first-group period relations are divisible by $\pi^{v_h+1}$ up to this order at the prescribed large depth. [Substitution and coefficient comparison][jin-witness]

Each test with such a bounded witness excludes only finitely many root orders at which two terms tie for least valuation, with a bound independent of the later depth. Increase the interval length by the sum of the bounds for the new tests, then choose the auxiliary depth beyond that interval. At a remaining root order $k$ the minimum is unique. For $\ell\nmid a$, every primitive $\ell^k$-th root raised to $a$ or to $-a$ has the same valuation after subtracting one. Thus at the character selected by this interval the normalized valuation of $F_h$ is at most $v_h/e+\epsilon$ for every prescribed side power, while all the earlier tests satisfy their bounds. The construction may change $Q$ and $k$ when more tests are imposed. [Common interval and signs][jin-root]

The evaluated entries of $C_h(H)$ are integral. Consequently the minimum valuation of the order-$h$ minors of the evaluated $M_Q$ is at most the valuation of $F_h$. Including the other specialized series in the image can only enlarge the determinantal ideal. Removing $c$ from each column subtracts exactly $h v_\ell(c)$ from both sides. This proves (1), with the same normalization even if the coefficient extension ramifies. For example, if the starting and specialized projected columns are integral and the starting lattice has at least $h$ unit Smith directions, the sum of the first $h$ specialized valuations is at most $\epsilon$. The proof gives the inequalities for these ordered partial sums. The ramification of the new field may grow, so an arbitrarily small normalized error can still represent a positive integral length. The later terminal Hermitian pairing requires its own comparison. The uniform existence of the first cores and the required comparisons on their actual geometry remain separate.
