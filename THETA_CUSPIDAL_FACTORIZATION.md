# Why the cuspidal theta integral factors through the chosen real intertwiner

Fix an inner step $k+1<m$, a twist $t$, and an automorphic multiplicity map $F$ at the input node. The desired map sends a vector at the output node to an automorphic function there. The question is whether the integral $B_F(P,y)$ depends only on $I(P,y)$, where $I$ is the **particular** real intertwiner stored at $k$. Two polynomials and vectors can have the same image under $I$ despite giving quite different integrands.

**Factorization theorem.** Suppose $I$ satisfies the nonvanishing, boundedness and covariance conditions of `IsItW` for this twist. Use the stated Fock and vacuum identifications. Suppose the theta integrals and their compact averages converge absolutely, give smooth cuspidal functions of uniform moderate growth with the prescribed finite level character, and satisfy the compact and differentiated covariance laws of I77A3–5. Use the rapid-decay and smooth-globalization results there. Then there is a unique multiplicity map $U_F$ on the algebraic tensor of the smooth output representations such that
$$
                         B_F(P,y)=U_F(I(P,y))
$$
for every polynomial $P$ and smooth input tensor $y$. The assignment is linear in $F$. This gives the implication needed for the inner multiplicity arrow $R.\mathrm{ex\_ioY}$. In particular its proof can cite algebraic Howe duality for real reductive dual pairs in place of a separate downward image theorem for arbitrary auxiliary modules. ## Convergence and the growth bound on the prescribed integral

These hypotheses on the integral can be checked on a fixed original row. Write $G$ for its output group and $H$ for the integration group. In the rational Schrödinger polarization the theta kernel is
$$
 K_\phi(g,h)=\sum_{a\in L(\mathbb Q)}(\omega(g,h)\phi)(a).
$$
Here $L$ denotes the polarized space after restriction of scalars to $\mathbb Q$, and $D_0=\dim_{\mathbb Q}L$. This function is the evaluation at zero in the [rational-lattice model](WEIL_AUTOMORPHIC_MODEL.md). Keep the finite test and its level fixed. For the fixed groups there exist an integer $A$ and an archimedean Schwartz seminorm $S$ such that
$$
 |K_\phi(g,h)|\le C\,S(\phi)\, H_G(g)^A H_H(h)^A .
$$
The heights use the matrix and its inverse. The exponent can be chosen **before any differentiation**. Constants in this assertion are for the given arithmetic stage. [Original height and seminorm estimate][kgrowth]

Here is one way to see the estimate. Choose rational symplectic coordinates and a finite translation and modulation lattice fixing $\phi$. Shrink the lattice so the Heisenberg half-pairing is integral on it. Write $u=\omega(g,h)\phi$ in Schrödinger coordinates. There is a positive integer $b$, bounded by a fixed power of $H_G(g)H_H(h)$ times a level constant, such that $u$ is fixed by all pure translations and all pure modulations from $b\widehat{\mathbb Z}^{D_0}$ in these coordinates. Indeed conjugate a Heisenberg displacement by $(g,h)^{-1}$. Clear denominators in the resulting linear matrix by taking, for each rational prime, the largest denominator of its entries in the fixed lattice coordinates. The product of these prime powers is bounded by the product of the finite matrix heights of the two factors to a fixed power, since the action on the tensor space is given by products of entries of the matrices and their inverses. Fixed changes of rational coordinates, polarizations and levels cost a fixed multiplicative constant. The transferred unitary action identifies the Heisenberg stabilizers exactly. [Finite denominator and level comparison][levelbound]

Normalize the finite dual coordinates for the additive character, absorbing its fixed conductor into the initial lattices. Modulation invariance restricts the finite support of $u$ to $b^{-1}\widehat{\mathbb Z}^{D_0}$. Translation invariance makes it constant on cosets of $b\widehat{\mathbb Z}^{D_0}$. Evaluation on one such coset costs at most its inverse square-root volume, $b^{D_0/2}$ for unit volume on the original lattice. At infinity, weighted Sobolev embedding bounds the supremum of $(1+|x|)^{D_0+1}|u(x,x_f)|$ by this factor times an $L^2$ seminorm involving finitely many products of multiplications by coordinates and partial derivatives. For instance apply ordinary Sobolev embedding of order $>D_0/2$ to polynomially weighted functions, and then use the finite-coset evaluation bound. Only the rational points $a\in b^{-1}\mathbb Z^{D_0}$ can contribute. Their sum of weights $(1+|a|)^{-D_0-1}$ is $O((1+b)^{D_0})$, by counting points in unit cubes. Finally commute each of the finitely many real Heisenberg generators through $\omega(g,h)$. Each becomes a linear combination of generators with coefficients bounded by a fixed power of the archimedean matrix heights. Unitarity removes $\omega(g,h)$ in their $L^2$ norms. This proves the asserted bound. In particular changing $\phi$ by an archimedean differentiated test changes only $S(\phi)$ in the estimate. [Heisenberg derivative bounds][realbound] · [Evaluation bound in the original lattice coordinates][latticebound]

For $F(y)$ in the given input multiplicity space all right derivatives are rapidly decreasing in the quotient height. One can use Borel–Labesse–Schwermer as in R89D2. Both the kernel and $F(y)$ are left rationally invariant. In the $h$ variable choose a representative of height at most twice the infimum on its rational coset. The bound above and input decay then give an integrable majorant on the finite-volume quotient, locally uniform in $g$. The same works on the specified Haar fundamental domain, for compact translates of the test and input, and with any fixed number of derivatives. Compact right translates of a fixed input are uniformly rapidly decreasing by the height inequality. [Cusp decay and arithmetic components](continuation_v89/decay.tex#L48-L102) · [Dominated integral on the specified domain][integraldom]

Absolute convergence now allows right translations and differentiation inside the integral. A derivative in $G$ replaces $\phi$ by $d\omega(X,0)\phi$. This is another Schwartz function of the same finite level. Applying the **same** estimate gives the same growth exponent for every derivative word. Simultaneous right translation in $H$ is a change of integration variable on the quotient. Differentiating it gives the sum of the oscillator derivative and the derivative of $F(y)$, with sum zero. The compact average used below is absolutely integrable by the same majorant and compactness. Rational invariance gives automorphy, and the level law $\omega(\kappa,1)\phi=\chi(\kappa)\phi$ gives exactly the prescribed right character. For polynomial tests this law is inherited from the finite vacuum, since its finite translations commute with the real raising operators. [Covariance, integrability and differentiations][laws] · [Finite level on an interior step][levellaw]

The [vanishing of all proper constant terms](THETA_INTERIOR_CUSPIDALITY.md) now puts these smooth outputs and their derivatives in the cuspidal space. Rapid decay applies to a smooth, uniformly moderately growing function with vanishing constant terms even before determining its infinitesimal character. Thus projecting the outputs in cuspidal $L^2$ below uses no prior identification of the archimedean theta partner. After factorization, equivariance transfers the scalar action of the center from the output discrete series to the image. This supplies the central-finiteness condition on the final multiplicity map. [Borel–Labesse–Schwermer, §§4.4–4.5](https://www.numdam.org/item/CM_1996__102_1_1_0.pdf#page=13) · [Growth and central finiteness in the final domain][zfinite]

## The local pairing to which Howe's theorem applies

At a real place $v$, write $G_v$ for the output group and $H_v$ for the integration group. Let $V_v,Y_v$ be the respective irreducible Harish–Chandra modules, and $\mathcal P_v$ the polynomial Fock module in the integral. For any irreducible output module $S_v$, consider a bilinear map
$$
              b:\mathcal P_v\times Y_v\longrightarrow S_v
$$
that intertwines the output action and is invariant under the simultaneous input action. These are identities for the two complexified Lie algebras and their maximal compact groups. Use the local models of I77A1–2 on both sides of each identity. Pulling back to a common cover and moving the two vacuum characters to the corresponding representation factors preserves these Hom spaces. Indeed tensoring a map on its domain and range by the same character is an equivalence, and pulling back along a surjection gives exactly the same intertwiners. The source frame change is the same in $b$ and $I$.

To apply Howe's theorem, associate to $P$ the linear map $y\mapsto b(P,y)$. Let $E_P$ be the span of the orbit of $P$ under the input maximal compact group $K_H$. It is finite-dimensional since this action preserves degree. Invariance shows that $b(P,\cdot)$ vanishes on every $K_H$-type whose dual is absent from $E_P$. Indeed $b$ restricted to $E_P\otimes Y_v[\tau]$ is invariant in that group, so factors through the trivial type. Each of the finitely many remaining isotypic spaces $Y_v[\tau]$ is finite-dimensional by admissibility. The map $b(P,\cdot)$ therefore belongs to the **algebraic** tensor product $S_v\otimes Y_v^\vee$, where $Y_v^\vee$ is the compact-finite contragredient. The differentiated input identity says exactly that the resulting map on $P$ intertwines the contragredient action. Conversely evaluation on $y$ recovers $b$. In the covering Hilbert coordinates of I77A1 the input identification is antilinear and the contraction is conjugate-linear in that coordinate. Their composite in the actual input $y$ is bilinear. This explains the single contragredient in the formula.

Howe's Theorem 2.1 says that the multiplicity module of a prescribed irreducible input $Y_v^\vee$ in the polynomial model has a unique irreducible quotient, with unique maximal proper submodule. Consequently there is at most one output isomorphism class $S_v$ with such a nonzero $b$, and its space of pairings is one-dimensional by Schur's lemma. This is a statement on the polynomial model itself. A nonzero pairing with output $V_v$ identifies the partner. [Howe, *Transcending classical invariant theory*, Theorem 2.1 and its proof, pp. 538, 547](https://www.math.utah.edu/~ptrapa/math-library/howe/howe-transcending-1989.pdf)

## Projecting the integral to the cuspidal spectrum

Write $V=\bigotimes_{v\mid\infty}V_v$ and $Y=\bigotimes_{v\mid\infty}Y_v$. Factor the polynomial variables by place and restrict $I$ and $B_F$ to $Y$. Their outputs are compact-finite for the output group because its compact action on the polynomial has finite-dimensional orbit. Simultaneous compact averaging, as in I77A3, shows that $I$ is still nonzero on this restriction. This is important for an intertwiner obtained by choosing a witness.

Put the values of $B_F$ in the Hilbert space of cuspidal $L^2$ functions of the specified finite level and character. The rapid decay of the functions and their derivatives legitimizes the differentiated right action there. The cuspidal spectrum is a Hilbert direct sum of irreducible unitary representations. Restricting to a finite level character amounts to taking its isotypic subspace in the finite component of each summand. Choosing orthonormal bases in these multiplicity spaces thus gives orthogonal projections to individual copies of irreducible archimedean representations. They commute with right translation at infinity, hence with its derivatives on smooth vectors. Denote a projection by $p_a$ and the compact-finite part of its copy by $S_a=\bigotimes_v S_{av}$. These projections separate vectors, including when several automorphic representations have the indicated compact types. [Arthur, *An Introduction to the Trace Formula*, Theorem 12.1 and §24](https://www.claymath.org/library/cw/arthur/pdf/61.pdf)

At each place obtain a nonzero local pairing $q_v:\mathcal P_v\otimes Y_v\to V_v$ from $I$. Take a nonzero value on a pure tensor, hold the other inputs fixed, and apply a linear functional to the other output factors that leaves the value nonzero. The covariance identities give the local laws above. Its image is a nonzero submodule of $V_v$, hence all of $V_v$. Every local slice of $I$ is therefore a scalar multiple of $q_v$. By the same argument, every local slice of $p_a B_F$ is zero if $S_{av}\not\simeq V_v$, and is a multiple of $q_v$ after a fixed isomorphism when $S_{av}\simeq V_v$. Functionals on the other output factors separate algebraic tensors.

Consequently $I$ kills the slot relations $\ker q_v$, and so does each $p_a B_F$. Quotienting by all the slot relations gives the tensor of the local quotients, namely $V$. Thus $I=C\circ(\bigotimes_v q_v)$ where $C$ is a nonzero endomorphism of the irreducible external tensor product $V$. By Schur's lemma $C$ is a nonzero scalar. In particular $I$ is onto $V$ on compact-finite vectors, and its kernel is precisely the sum of those slot relations. Every $p_a B_F$ kills this kernel, so $B_F$ does as well in cuspidal $L^2$. The same holds for the actual functions. A continuous function on the adelic quotient vanishing almost everywhere vanishes everywhere, since quotient Haar measure gives positive mass to every nonempty open set. This proves the desired factorization on compact-finite vectors, including the case where the entire global lift is zero.

## Returning to the original smooth functions

Define $T_F(I(P,y))=B_F(P,y)$ on compact-finite vectors by the result just proved. Its values inherit the finite level character, cuspidality, smoothness and uniform moderate growth. Its compact and Lie equivariance follow on the generators from the two covariance laws. Apply the smooth extension for such a map into cuspidal functions used in I77A5 and W90G1–2 and restrict to the algebraic tensor of the local smooth spaces. The extension has the prescribed equivariance and continuous point evaluations in the local smooth-vector Sobolev topology. It is unique by density. [Smooth globalization and seminorms](continuation_v90/globalization.tex) · [Application in I77A5](continuation_v77/interior.tex#L296-L342)

For an arbitrary smooth pure input, the compact averaging identity of I77A3 writes the **pair** $(I(P,y),B_F(P,y))$ as a finite sum of pairs with compact-finite input. One averages the input maximal compact at each place, expanding its polynomial orbit in finitely many matrix coefficients. The coefficient bound for $I$ and joint absolute integrability for the theta kernel justify the two averages. The graph of the smooth extension is linear, so it contains the original pair. Linearization gives the identity on the prescribed smooth tensor domain. Uniqueness on compact-finite vectors uses the surjectivity proved above. Uniqueness of smooth extension then makes $F\mapsto U_F$ linear by linearity of the integral.

**Conclusion for the original interior arrows.** Take an original weak realization with its prescribed framed models, step vacua, levels and Haar domains, a chain satisfying the parameter condition, and the input and output multiplicity spaces identified with the corresponding cusp-form spaces. The [real occurrence theorem](THETA_INTERIOR_DISCRETE_SERIES.md) supplies one nonzero real intertwiner across all twists at each interior index. The weak choice therefore certifies its stored intertwiner. The kernel estimates above and the [constant-term argument](THETA_INTERIOR_CUSPIDALITY.md) give the global hypotheses for each twist. Applying the factorization theorem proves both interior existence assertions on this realization. In the conditional equivalence E77A6, these two assertions can now be supplied. The other assumptions there concern the preliminary weak realization, its levels and models, and the analytic inputs for the definite end and rows.
