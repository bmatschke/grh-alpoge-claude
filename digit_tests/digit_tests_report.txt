1. A FINITE PRODUCT PROOF OF THE DIGIT-FACTORIAL APPROXIMATION [DT1–DT4]

This is a derived simplification of the source's DigAbs/GaAt ingredient.
It gives a stronger degree bound than the source interface requires. None
of the later constants are reduced on that account in this version.

Theorem. Let p be any prime. For finitely many indices v, let g_v>=0,
P_v=p^{g_v}, and 0<=rho_v<P_v. For b=(b_v) in N^V put

  Gamma(b)=prod_v (rho_v+P_v b_v)! /
                    (rho_v! b_v! (P_v!)^{b_v}).

Gamma(b) is a p-adic unit. For each integer c>=1 and epsilon in {1,-1},
there is a finite rational polynomial Q(b), written in the multivariate
binomial basis with coefficients in Z_(p), such that

  total degree Q <= 2(c-1),
  v_p(Gamma(b)^epsilon-Q(b)) >= c for every b in N^V.

For c=0, Q=0 suffices. The source asks for degree <=4c in DigAbs and
<=6c in GaAt, under p>=5. The theorem implies those statements, and
continues to hold at p=2,3. This stronger scope is a human deduction,
not an assertion about what the released Lean theorem exports.

1.1. Integer-valued ordered sums [DT1].

For positive integers t_1,...,t_m define

  S_t(b)=sum_{0<=u_1<...<u_m<b} u_1^{t_1}...u_m^{t_m}.

Write T=sum t_i. S_t is a rational polynomial in b of degree at most
T+m, integer-valued on N. Consequently its expansion in binom(b,j)
has integer coefficients. To prove this without a summation formula,
use S_empty=1 and

  S_(t_1,...,t_m)(b)
     =sum_{u=0}^{b-1} u^{t_m} S_(t_1,...,t_(m-1))(u).

Products of integer-valued polynomials remain integer-valued. Every
rational polynomial R of degree d that takes integer values on N has
Newton expansion R(b)=sum_{j=0}^d Delta^j R(0) binom(b,j), and the
coefficients are integers because they are finite integer combinations
of R(0),...,R(j). Finally

  sum_{u=0}^{b-1} binom(u,j)=binom(b,j+1).

These statements prove the induction and the degree bound T+m. As
every t_i>=1, m<=T, so degree S_t<=2T. The same argument works with
Z_(p) instead of Z. No denominator arising from Faulhaber polynomials
is ever estimated separately; the integral binomial basis is decisive.

1.2. A filtered product lemma [DT2].

Suppose F(u)=1+sum_{t>=1} a_t u^t, where a_t lies in p^t Z_(p).
For an infinite series this statement is interpreted at finite p-adic
precision: modulo p^c, only t<c is retained. For each b in N consider

  C_F(b)=prod_{u=0}^{b-1} F(u).

Expanding by the occupied positions gives, at total selected degree T,
the finite sum over positive compositions (t_1,...,t_m) of T of

  (a_(t_1)...a_(t_m)) S_(t_1,...,t_m)(b).

Each coefficient product is in p^T Z_(p), and each ordered-sum polynomial
has degree <=2T with integral binomial coefficients. Discarding all
T>=c therefore gives a universal polynomial approximation modulo p^c
of degree <=2(c-1), with binomial coefficients in Z_(p).

There is no uniform-in-b convergence issue. For each fixed b the product
contains b factors, and every discarded summand is divisible by p^c.
The finitely many retained ordered sums produce one polynomial valid
for all b. This distinguishes the pointwise finite expansion from its
universal polynomial expression.

The construction respects a useful filtration: its contribution of
grade T has degree <=2T and binomial coefficients in p^T Z_(p).
Products of finitely many such filtered expressions have the same
property, keeping only total grade <c. This holds also for independent
variables: multiply their binomial polynomials and add their grades
and total degrees.

If F is a finite product of factors (1+q u) with q in p Z_(p), its
inverse has a finite-precision expansion with coefficient of u^t in
p^t Z_(p), by the geometric series. Hence the same argument applies
to C_F(b)^{-1}. No appeal to an infinite-product analytic theorem is
needed: work throughout in Z_(p)/p^c Z_(p).

1.3. Exact factorial factorization [DT3].

Let U(n)=prod_{1<=i<=n,p does not divide i} i. Repeatedly remove
multiples of p from (p^g b)!. This gives the exact identity

  (p^g b)! = p^{b(p^g-1)/(p-1)} b! prod_(l=1)^g U(p^l b).

Apply the same identity at b=1 and divide. With P=p^g,

  Gamma_(P,rho)(b)
    = binom(Pb+rho,rho) prod_(l=1)^g U(p^l b)/U(p^l)^b.

Every displayed quotient has a product expression in p-adic units:

  U(p^l b)/U(p^l)^b
    =prod_(u=0)^(b-1) prod_(1<=a<=p^l,p does not divide a)
          (1+p^l u/a),

  binom(Pb+rho,rho)=prod_(i=1)^rho (1+Pb/i).

Here v_p(p^l/a)=l>=1. Also i<P implies v_p(P/i)>=1.
This proves unitness, indeed Gamma_(P,rho)(b)=1 modulo p.
When g=0, P=1 and rho=0, and Gamma_(1,0)(b)=1 exactly.

1.4. Complete approximation proof [DT4].

For each l, the inner product in u has coefficient at u^t in
p^{lt} Z_(p), hence in p^t Z_(p). Apply the filtered product lemma
to the quotient U(p^l b)/U(p^l)^b or its inverse. The extra factor
binom(Pb+rho,rho), or its inverse, has coefficient at b^t in
p^t Z_(p), and thus has the same filtration with the better degree
bound t. Multiply the finitely many expressions and discard total
grade >=c. Grade T has degree <=2T and p^T-integral binomial
coefficients. The final degree is <=2(c-1).

Repeat for the finite index set V, multiply, and truncate by the sum
of the grades. This proves the full multivariate theorem with total
degree independent of |V|, all g_v and all rho_v. It is essential to
truncate by TOTAL grade, not to retain degree 2(c-1) separately in
every variable and then multiply without truncating.

2. SOURCE INTERFACE AND OPERATOR INTERPRETATION [DT5]

The source's gamR is exactly Gamma_(P,rho); gamPl and gamNo are its
products over the plane and normal variables. Its cstOf is

  C_rho=prod_v rho_v! prod_x (P_x!)^(dPl_x+dNo_x).

On a fixed digit class alpha_v=rho_v+P_v beta_v, the coefficient
Fock factorial factors exactly as

  alpha! = C_rho beta_Pl! beta_No!
               Gamma_Pl(beta_Pl) Gamma_No(beta_No).

Therefore the original Fock pairing becomes the source's twisted
plane/normal pairing after digit splitting. This is a coefficientwise
identity; disjoint digit classes do not pair, because the coefficient
Fock form is diagonal in monomials.

Every rational coefficient in Z_(p) maps to O: denominators prime to
p are units in the DVR. Thus the approximation above supplies exactly
the coefficient functions required by DigAbs and GaAt. In particular
the source unit function ga may be chosen to be Gamma_Pl itself.

For a multiindex m, divided differentiation satisfies

  X^m partial^[m] X^alpha = prod_v binom(alpha_v,m_v) X^alpha.

Here partial^[m]=prod_v partial_v^(m_v)/(m_v!). Hence a binomial
polynomial in the exponent vector is the diagonal action of a finite
sum of operators X^m partial^[m]. The total number of derivatives is
|m|. The same operator uses |m| multiplications, and preserves every
monomial exponent after the two operations are combined.

This proves, directly, the source's diagK_binomCombF identity. It
explains why polynomial degree controls the representation-weight
budget of a digit correction. The representation estimate converting
that operator degree to a highest-weight shift is a separate lemma;
no integral spectral-projector estimate is inferred just from this
identity.

3. THE HIGHEST-WEIGHT COST OF A DIAGONAL CORRECTION [DT6]

For a subset S of vector/covector letters, write lambda_1,S(F) for
the largest first coordinate of a highest weight in the rational
GL_k-submodule generated by F. This is the source's lam1S, expressed
through its characteristic-zero isotypic interpretation. The value
for F=0 is -infinity. We use the standard characteristic-zero weight
theory: a constituent of dominant highest weight lambda has every
coordinate of every weight <=lambda_1. This is a statement about
finite-dimensional rational modules over the fraction field.

If Q(alpha) is a binomial polynomial of total degree d, then

  lambda_1,S(diag(Q)F) <= lambda_1,S(F)+d.

Proof. Let E_v=X_v partial_v. The operator
X^m partial^[m]=prod_v binom(E_v,m_v) is a polynomial of total degree
|m| in the E_v. Under conjugation by GL_k on the indicated letters,
an E_v belonging to a vector or covector letter lies in a copy of
End(K^k) or End((K^k)^*). All its torus-weight coordinates are in
{-1,0,1}. An E_v on a letter outside S is invariant. Thus the GL_k
span of a product of at most d such operators has every weight
coordinate <=d. Apply this operator module to the module generated
by F. Every weight coordinate in the resulting tensor product is
at most lambda_1,S(F)+d; hence the first coordinate of each of its
highest weights is at most that value. Taking the sum of the
operators proves the assertion. Division by m! occurs only in this
fraction-field weight argument; coefficient integrality of the
operator is already proved by its binomial eigenvalues.

This proof works for signed letters and arbitrary subsets S. It does
not assert integral decomposition into isotypic summands. Together
with DT4 it gives a shift <=2(c-1) for the factorial correction at
precision c>=1. In applying the source interfaces we retain their
larger allowance 4c.

4. CONSTRUCTING THE NORMAL TEST [DT7]

Fix the normal multidegree dNo and its selected joint isotypic
projector e. The exact projector properties needed are: Fock
self-adjointness, preservation of dNo, image of type ka_j,u at every
nested/disjoint cut, and

  p^D e(BB) subset BB,
  D=sum_(j,u) binom(K,2) floor(log_p(2 degIn(dNo,NSet_(j,u))+K-1)).

Here BB is the weighted coefficient lattice b(F)>=0, where

  b(F)=min_alpha (v(F_alpha)+v_p(alpha!)/2), v(p)=1.

These projector properties are supplied by the explicit denominator
argument and its Fock specialization in IW/PG. In this section w^2=p,
as in the actual local coefficient setting.

Let nu have multidegree dNo, put

  FNo=v_p(prod_x dNo_x!), U=v_p(nu!),
  cY=w^FNo p^D w^(-U), Y=cY e(X^nu).

First w^(-U)X^nu belongs to BB. Apply p^D e, and then multiply by
w^FNo. For every exponent beta of this multidegree,
v_p(beta!)<=FNo because beta! divides prod_x dNo_x!.
The resulting polynomial Y therefore has integral coefficients.
It lies in the prescribed joint types and

  v(cY)=FNo/2+D-U/2.

Use DT4 to approximate Gamma_No(beta)^(-1) modulo p^c by a binomial
polynomial Q(beta) of degree <=2(c-1), for c>=1, and put

  N0=diag(Q)Y.

Then N0 is integral, has the same multidegree, and

  N0-diag(Gamma_No^(-1))(cY e(X^nu)) has coefficient valuation >=c.

DT6 gives, at every normal cut,

  lambda_1(N0)<=ka_(j,u),1+2(c-1)<=ka_(j,u),1+4c.

For c=0 one may choose N0=0; its difference from the integral unit
correction of Y has valuation >=0. This proves NoAt with the exact
source scalar bound. In particular NoAt is no longer an independent
approximation input once IW/PG projector properties are supplied.

5. WEIGHTED COEFFICIENT SELECTION AND PickAt [DT8]

Start with a primitive integral polynomial G of the prescribed
multidegree d. The frame's true-letter indexing is a bijection onto
the selected positions modulo n'; plane and normal coordinates are
disjoint. At every coordinate use the unique Euclidean division

  alpha_v=rho_v+P_v beta_v, 0<=rho_v<P_v.

Choose a unit coefficient of G, with exponent alpha0. Its digit and
block class gives a two-block polynomial H(A,B), with plane variables
A and normal variables B. The coefficient indexed by (betaP0,betaN0)
is the original unit. This is the source's levImg. Its reconstruction
map is asm(A,N)=X^rho upPl(A) upNo(N). Distinct digit classes have
disjoint monomial supports, so the Fock identity in DT5 is exact.

Write VP=v_p(betaP0!), VN=v_p(betaN0!), VC=v_p(C_rho).
The normal coefficient y(B) at betaP0 has b(y)<=VN/2. Since the finite
complete family of joint projectors sums to the identity and b of a
sum is at least the minimum b of its terms, one joint projector e
satisfies b(e y)<=b(y). The source's cuts are nested or disjoint;
their projectors commute, so the selected composition retains the
joint-type and self-adjointness properties used in DT7.

Choose a monomial nu attaining b(e y), and let Pc(A) be the coefficient
of B^nu in (1 tensor e)H. If U=v_p(nu!), then

  b(Pc)+U/2 <= (VP+VN)/2.

Indeed, the coefficient of Pc at betaP0 is the selected coefficient
of e y, so the displayed inequality is the definition of b applied
twice. Pc is nonzero. If beta3 attains b(Pc), choose

  z=(Pc)_beta3 w^{v_p(beta3!)}, F2=z^(-1)Pc.

Then v(z)=b(Pc) and b(F2)=0. The source's separate MemAt hypothesis
places Pc, and hence F2, in the signed-chain space XX. This membership
does not follow from unit-coefficient selection alone; the exact
upstream boundary is recorded in Section 8 below.

Construct N0,cY by DT7 and put

  k0=C_rho cY nu! z.

Let FPl=v_p(prod_x dPl_x!), FNo=v_p(prod_x dNo_x!), and
Ftot=v_p(prod_s d_s!). The scalar cost is

  v(k0)<=VC+FNo/2+D+(VP+VN)/2.

The unit factorial identity gives VC+VP+VN=v_p(alpha0!)<=Ftot.
Thus, if the explicit size budget is

  D+(FPl-VP)/2+(FNo-VN)/2 <= Dn,

then

  v(k0)<=Ftot-FPl/2+Dn.

This is the source's PickAt scalar inequality, with n0=FPl/2. The
source choices already reconstructed in CL8 give Dn<=9Nk^2 log_p(a).

For any integral plane polynomial A of multidegree dPl, Fock
self-adjointness of e and DT5 give the exact identity for the ideal
normal test Nex=diag(Gamma_No^(-1))(cY e(X^nu)):

  <G,asm(A,Nex)>=C_rho cY nu! <Pc,diag(Gamma_Pl)A>
                     =k0 Bform(F2,A).

Replacing Nex by N0 introduces a polynomial with coefficient
valuation >=c. The normalized Fock integrality from CL7 therefore
gives

  v(<G,asm(A,N0)>-k0 Bform(F2,A)) >=c+Ftot-kappa_fl.

All other clauses of PickAt are the block-degree equations, the
joint-type data, b(F2)=0, and the supplied signed-chain membership.
Thus the only upstream content not proved by this coefficient
selection is the correct choice of the signed-chain datum and MemAt
for the actual word closure; these are not silently discarded.

6. INTEGRAL GLUING OF SEGMENT CONDITIONS [DT9]

This section supplies the elementary tensor algebra used in DecAt.
Let L_j be finite free coefficient lattices in polynomial rings on
disjoint sets of variables. Let X_j be a saturated submodule of L_j.
Over a DVR, L_j/X_j is torsion-free and hence free, so a basis of X_j
extends to a basis of L_j. In the resulting product basis,

  intersection_j (X_j tensor tensor_(i!=j) L_i) = tensor_j X_j.

Indeed the left side permits a basis tensor precisely when its factor
at every j belongs to the chosen X_j basis. This proves ATL with
integral coefficients, without any integral isotypic decomposition.

The X_j used here are the intersections of a rational vector subspace
with a coefficient lattice. The rational subspace imposes support,
homogeneity and prefix highest/lowest weight bounds. Such intersections
are saturated: if a nonzero scalar multiple of a vector lies in the
rational subspace, the vector itself lies there. All modules can be
restricted to the finitely many degrees occurring in the polynomial.

Coefficient extraction in variables outside a prefix commutes with
the GL action on that prefix. Thus it cannot introduce an isotypic
type absent before extraction, and preserves its highest and lowest
weight bounds. The same applies to multihomogeneous projection and
to injective, sign-preserving renaming. These prove ValSlice, ValComp
and ValRen for the actual polynomial models. For noninjective
equivariant maps the same assertion follows by passing to an image;
the uses here have the stated disjoint support.

Apply the intersection identity first to plane variables and then to
normal variables. A polynomial satisfying the conditions in every
segment is an integral finite sum of products of segment polynomials
satisfying those conditions separately. A further finite decomposition
into physical multidegrees gives DecAt. The source's plane_dec,
normal_dec and dec_identity are exactly these three operations.

7. ADMISSIBILITY OF THE ASSEMBLED TEST: MembAt [DT10]

The assertion proved here is conditional on the explicit MFH frame
and margin data, Tied signed-chain datum, BlockOK degree identities,
NormTest, and the YY(24,60) conditions delivered by PG. It is the
outgoing symbol-membership assertion MembAt. It is not the incoming
word-closure assertion MemAt discussed in Section 8.

7.1. The two numerical margins.
Order the model positions x_u by the internal letters and then the
current frame letters. Write B_u=Bo(x_u), s_u=sl(x_u), m=mStar, and
a_u for the first coordinate of the normal joint highest weight at
the corresponding cut. The definition thStar is

  T_u=max(B_u/2,t1_u,t2_u),
  t1_u=a_(u+1)+m+s_u when x_u is even, and B_u/2 otherwise,
  t2_u=a_(u-1)+m+s_u when u>=1 and x_(u-1) is odd,
       and B_u/2 otherwise.

Put tau_u=T_u-s_u/2-60c. MFH gives s_u>=128c at the positions used.
The Tied chain threshold is T_u-s_u/8 and its allowed loss is
3s_u/8. Hence YY(24,60) gives a plane chain lower bound

  T_u-s_u/2-24c >= tau_u.

The corresponding link bound is T_u-3s_u/8-60c>=tau_u.
The literal model-prefix preimages are the relevant chain or link
sets, possibly with extra positive vector letters. Adding those
letters cannot lower the minimum weight, as explained in PG's order
calculus. Thus the plane orders immediately before and after an
internal position x_u are at least tau_u and tau_(u+1).
NormTest bounds the corresponding normal first weights by a_u+4c
and a_(u+1)+4c.

At an even position, T_u>=a_(u+1)+m+s_u, so

  tau_u >= a_(u+1)+m+4c,

because s_u/2-60c>=4c. At an odd position the same calculation at
the next cut uses T_(u+1)>=a_u+m+s_(u+1). Choose the separating
integer M(x) to be a_after+4c at an even position and a_before+4c
at an odd one. The plane and normal bounds then give exactly the
even/odd separation clauses in XX'. Finally

  tau_u >= B_u/2-s_u,

since T_u>=B_u/2 and s_u/2>=60c. This supplies its independent
Bo/2-sl threshold clause. These two elementary inequalities are the
substantive margin calculation in restXXG_b.

7.2. Support, degree, and the Frobenius identity.
The model letters are internal letters, labels, carries and tangents.
Only the carry wc(j,m) receives a nontrivial exponent under phi:
it is sent to its true coordinate to the power

  P_j=p^(z_(j+1)-z_j).

The subsequent up-map at physical position x contributes
p^(z_j-x), so the composed carry exponent is p^(z_(j+1)-x),
exactly the physical Frobenius substitution. Internal, label and
tangent coordinates follow directly; periodic representatives give
the same differences. This proves the algebra identity AlgAt by
checking variables and then multiplying.

Goodness gives every Gp monomial the same phi-multidegree dPl.
At an internal physical letter the model has just its one internal
preimage; at an incoming W letter only the carry preimages occur,
and no normal variable occurs. Since all exponents are nonnegative,
BlockOK yields, for the physical degree delta of a supported Gp term,

  p^(z_j-x) (delta(j,x)+dNoOf(j,x)) <= deg(x mod n').

This is the actual segment-degree inequality used by DegAt. In
particular it implies the source's exported bound by 1.01 levDeg;
the extra one percent is unnecessary for this pointwise estimate.
Taking a physical homogeneous component only discards monomials,
so every remaining monomial still has the original phi-degree dPl.
The total assembled degree is d by BlockOK.

The label exponents satisfy 0<=rho<P in every true coordinate.
The true-letter indexing is bijective onto TT. Its periodic degree
identity and these inequalities prove IsLabel for the global label
polynomial, including support in TT. This proves LabAt.

The total normal order is also constant on every supported term.
Writing the sums over the true coordinates of the stated parity, it is

  sum_even (rho_normal + P*dNo)
    + sum_odd (rho_plane + P*dPl).

The parity swap is essential: at odd letters the plane coordinate
is the physical normal coordinate. This is oOf in MembDegF.

7.3. Conclusion.
DT9 decomposes the plane and normal factors into integral sums of
segment products, with the prefix bounds from 7.1 and the support
and degree bounds from 7.2. The finite physical-degree decomposition,
the Frobenius identity, and the label factor then express

  asm(phi(Gp),N0)

as an integral sum of OneSidedLemma4Elements with its required degree
and fixed normal order. The definition of their span proves MembAt.
Thus the four interfaces DecAt, AlgAt, DegAt and LabAt have explicit
pointwise arguments here. Producing the frame/parameter datum that
satisfies MFH is a separate global construction; it has not been
inferred from these local inequalities.

8. THE REMAINING INCOMING CONDITION MemAt [DT11]

MemAt and MembAt have different logical roles. MemAt says that,
after digit extraction and the normal joint projection, the plane
coefficients of the INPUT polynomial belong to the signed-chain
space XX. This is an assumption in DT8 and PG's application. MembAt
says that the OUTPUT test assembled from a constrained Gram test
belongs to the allowed symbol span; that is Section 7 above.

The source proves the incoming condition in two stages:

  Lemma5aAt: initial symbols satisfy MemAtG(0,1/8);
  WordConditionAt: at most c0 allowed operators and the selected
      twist transport this to MemAtG(1/8,3/8).

Theorem memAt_of_wordConditionAt then uses linearity to pass to the
whole word-closure span. Lemma5aGeneratorProducts reduces the first
stage to its actual plane/normal generator products. WordLossNonneg
reduces the second to OneOpStep and WordBoundStep, with cost two
at each touched prefix. These are useful exact reductions, but their
substantive prefix and twisted-substitution estimates have not been
reconstructed in this note. DT11 records them as retained inputs,
not as consequences of the completed outgoing admissibility proof.

The symbol-space dimension estimate and the construction of the full
numerical frame/chain parameter system also remain separate. Closing
the local factorial, normal-test, coefficient-selection and outgoing
membership calculations does not assert a complete Type-II theorem,
nor a complete human proof of RH or GRH.
