# A normalized grid over the given bottom row

Fix an allowed Hida datum with its character frame, its given real chain, its reference tuple and its finite set of twists. Assume the twist primes avoid the rational prime $q_0$ and use the scale $(1,C_F,b)$ in the field request. We construct a normalized grid on these very data. The construction of the grid adds no lower bound to either large parameter. A separate assertion, denoted by **ConstEx**, supplies a compatible real matching and square proportionality constants for every prescribed grid frame. We show where it enters the choice used to request the field. [Quantifiers of the three existence statements][ex] · [Construction at this scale][grid]

## Splitting primes and characters

Let $K$ be the imaginary quadratic subfield of the CM host $E=FK$. The host hypotheses put the primes $g_0,g_1,p_\delta,p'_\delta$ in the split class of $K$. The defining congruence for $q_0$ implies $q_0\equiv1\bmod\operatorname{cond}E$ in the notation of the grid, hence it too is in the split class. The size inequalities separate $q_0$ from the $g_i$, and its defining conditions separate it from the two $\delta$ primes. Choose $v_0$ of $F$ over $q_0$. Let $S_1$ be the places over the four marked primes and put $S_0=S_1\cup\{v_0\}$. At each rational prime in question choose one of its two primes in $K$, respecting the given type at $g_i$ and the prescribed twist orientation at the $\delta$ primes. For every $v$ over this prime there is a unique half over $v$ restricting to the selected prime in $K$, by $E\otimes_F F_v=F_v\times F_v$. Conjugation exchanges the two idempotents. In particular the choices agree at every overlap allowed by the level datum. The twist exclusion keeps $v_0$ outside every twist support. [Compatible orientations and exclusion][places] · [Size comparison at the type primes][size]

For each required row exponent $e=(e_\tau)$ we can now construct a unitary Hecke character of $E$ trivial on the ideles of $F$, of angular exponents $2e_\tau$. On a selected half $w$ above $S_1$, take a nontrivial character of $U_w^1/U_w^2\simeq(k_w,+)$, where $U_w^j=1+\mathfrak p_w^j$. Choose its extension to be trivial on the Teichmüller units and on a uniformizer. It has order equal to the residue characteristic $p$ and is trivial on all roots of order dividing $|k_w|-1$. On the selected half $w_0$ above $v_0$, choose a character of $k_{w_0}^{\times}$ which cancels, on $\mu(E)$, the product of these characters and the archimedean angular character of exponents $e_\tau$. Reduction is injective on $\mu(E)$ and $|k_{w_0}^\times|>\#\mu(E)$ by the bound on $q_0$. A character of a subgroup of a finite cyclic group extends, and its extensions differ by characters of the quotient. Thus one can choose a nontrivial extension even when the character prescribed on the subgroup is trivial. Inflate it to units and take it trivial on a uniformizer. [Wild and tame characters with their witnesses][wild] · [Torsion condition and exact levels][local]

Let $\psi$ be the product of these local characters and the angular character, trivial at the other finite places. For the fractional ideal map $I$ normalized by valuations, define a character on the subgroup of ideals $(a/\bar a)$ by
$$
   (a/\bar a)\longmapsto \psi(a/\bar a).
$$
If two such generators define the same ideal, their quotient is a unit whose every complex absolute value is one. Kronecker's theorem makes it a root of unity, and the choice at $w_0$ makes its value under $\psi$ equal to one. The circle is divisible, so this homomorphism extends to a character $\lambda$ of the fractional ideal group. Then
$$
     \chi(x)=\psi(x/\bar x)\,\lambda(I(x/\bar x))^{-1}
$$
is continuous, unitary, trivial on principal ideles and on the ideles of $F$. The correction is unramified at every finite place. At a conjugate half the unit character is the conjugate-place pullback of the inverse, so the two local kernels have the same levels. The exact conductor exponents are two above $S_1$, one above $v_0$, and zero elsewhere. At infinity $x/\bar x$ doubles the angular exponents. Applying the construction for each entry of the specified finite list gives the grid characters with their prescribed infinity types. [Character extension and its local effect][char] · [Assembly for the rows][rows]

## Spaces, finite frames and vacuum normalization

Keep the entire prescribed boundary $V_{0,j}$. In particular its first lattice in $Y_0$ is self-dual. Adjoin a positive unit line to this lattice to get the first core of rank $r_D+1$. For each of the other core vertices take a Hermitian lattice of the indicated signature. On an incident staircase edge adjoin the indicated anti-diagonal block. If the ideal on coordinate $a$ is $\mathfrak b_a$, the reversed coordinate carries $\overline{\mathfrak b_a}^{-1}$. The dual lattice in the block is therefore the block itself, and each two-coordinate anti-diagonal pair adds one positive and one negative direction. In odd length the sign of the central coefficient determines the remaining direction. Use the level-dependent staircase ideals on $j=0$ and the host ideals on $i=m$, $0<j<m$. Choose the definite corner first and adjoin the positive unit line there. At a free interior vertex take the standard lattice $\mathcal O_E^\nu$ for the diagonal form with the required numbers of $+1$ and $-1$ entries. It is self-dual and has the indicated signature at every real place. This order realizes the actual boundary decompositions, including the two ends where the added block is a line. [Self-duality and signature][vform] · [Boundary, staircases and corner][vertices]

Use the given additive character and its real frames. On the prescribed boundary use the same real majorants, orthonormal bases and Weil representations as the datum, including the last representation landing at the definite group. The other edges use the unitary-pair Weil model for the prescribed splitting characters. Choose finite adelic frames of the top lattices by taking local bases integral almost everywhere. For each staircase ideal choose an idele with exactly its valuations at all finite places. At each of the finitely many active halves its component can be set to the specified signed power of the uniformizer because the required valuation equals that of the ideal. Multiplication by local units effects these finitely many changes at once. The guard on the supports gives valuation zero for the two host ideals there. Appending these coordinates to the top or core frame gives the stipulated block frames. This argument also works for a nonprincipal ideal. [Weil representation on the given boundary][weil] · [Adelic frames and prescribed powers][frames] · [Guard and finite set of places][guard]

In the definite comparison choose the discriminant root $\delta$ and the common two-torsion point of the CM construction. At each definite pair the product of the ranks is even. Its normalized theta section and its oscillator vacuum give a single nonzero scalar $c_{0,p}$ for every monomial, with the orientation of that pair. This is the previously reconstructed vacuum identity. Choosing such scalars, vacua and theta sections over the finite grid gives the normalized choice used for all the jets. [CM vacuum identity](vacuum_identity/main.tex) · [Common point and orientation of the monomial formula][vac] · [Use in the field calculation](ROW_COEFFICIENT_FIELD.md)

## Completing an arbitrary prescribed grid choice

For this completion one may start with any already specified grid on the standing Hida data. The twist-prime exclusion was needed to select a grid in the preceding construction. Now supply the real matching and constants of **ConstEx** for the frames just given, or for any other prescribed normalized grid choice over the same datum. The proportionality has to hold for every pair of tests in the definition of a square constant. Row Haar measures with the prescribed normalization admit fundamental domains. The finite catalogue is indexed by the allowed coset data, classes of the definite endpoint, and a basis of its coefficient module. Each of these sets is finite. The source's two fields on the coefficient module give a scalar-extension isomorphism, and comparison of their highest weights extends the prescribed grid choice to a sum choice using this isomorphism. [Square constant and definition of a base choice][mark] · [Catalogue enumeration][enum] · [Extension of the coefficient map][sum]

It remains to choose an actual nonzero input $w$ for the archimedean chain. On each descending edge the images of the real intertwiner on polynomial and compact-finite tensors span the lower compact-finite module. Start with the identity on the bottom module. Whenever the composition so far is nonzero on a compact-finite vector, write this vector as a finite sum of images on the next edge. At least one image has nonzero composite. Choose its polynomial and continue, applying the same argument to the last edge from the definite space. This gives all the test polynomials and an endpoint vector whose composite is the required $w\ne0$. The given parameter gaps put every edge within the prescribed real theta correspondence. These choices give **IsBase**. Finally apply the [common-real-test theorem](ROW_COMMON_REAL_TEST.md) to replace just the two real test entries. The result satisfies **Is** with the original grid, measures, frames, and constants. The field proof can therefore use a representative for each realized pattern and repeat the completion on the target. The [all-column comparison](ROW_TOWER_INDUCTION.md#passage-to-the-prescribed-grid) now gives **ConstEx** on the standing theta and definite doubling inputs, before any field is requested for the numerical ratios. The [square regularization argument](ROW_SQUARE_REGULARIZATION.md) constructs its common real relation from the two quotient maps and passes from one comparison to all convergent translates. It asks for proportionality on every square; the stronger square assertion also asks for a nonzero witness and controls the later formula.
