# Why the downward theta integral is cuspidal

At an interior step the integral takes a cusp form on a unitary group of rank $n+t$ to a function on one of rank $n$. A constant term of the output would give a theta lift to a group of rank $n-2d$ for some $d\geq1$. A single real place rules this out. In the Harish-Chandra parameter of the input there is a consecutive string of length $t$. A lift to the smaller group requires a longer string with the same center. The two neighboring entries are missing.

**Theorem.** Take a step satisfying the source's parameter condition, with its specified archimedean character exponents, normalized Weil action and ordinary discrete-series models. For every admissible input multiplicity map $F$, smooth tensor $v$ and theta function $f$, the downward integral
$$
 B_f(v)(g)=\int_{U(W)(F_0)\backslash U(W)(\mathbb A)}
             \theta(g,h;f)\,F(v)(h)\,dh
$$
has zero constant term along every proper rational parabolic of the smaller group $U(V)$. Here $F_0$ denotes the totally real ground field, $\dim_E V=n$ and $\dim_E W=n+t$. The assertion applies separately to each finite twist. It includes the case in which the entire theta integral vanishes. ## The local obstruction

Fix one real place. Write $a=\operatorname{expS}$ for the infinity exponent of the character $\chi_V$ determining the splitting on $U(W)$. Define $B_j=\{(j-1)/2,(j-3)/2,\ldots,-(j-1)/2\}$. The parameter specified on the larger side, before taking its contragredient, has the following blocks. Each coordinate displayed is to be increased by $a$.

| Sign of the compact block | Coordinates relative to $a$ |
| --- | --- |
| Positive | $\alpha,\ B_t,\ \delta$ |
| Negative | $\gamma,\ \beta$ |

Every coordinate $u$ in the four outer lists satisfies $|u|\geq(t+3)/2$. Thus neither $(t+1)/2$ nor $-(t+1)/2$ occurs relative to $a$, in either block. The source obtains this description from its node separation hypothesis. [Two parameters, gap and character exponents][par] · [Centered block][block] · [Derivation from node separation][gap]

To identify the representation tested by the kernel, note the absence of a complex conjugate in $B_f(v)$. Its local invariance pairs the oscillator action with the discrete-series model carried by $F$, so the representation sought as a quotient of the oscillator is that model's contragredient. The stored model has parameter $\mathrm{parB}=\mathrm{dualPar}(\mathrm{parA})$, where duality negates each coordinate and retains its compact-block sign. Dualizing it therefore gives exactly the displayed $\mathrm{parA}$. The character $\chi_V$ is $(z/|z|)^{2a}$ there. Its differential contributes $+a\operatorname{tr}(Y)$ on the integrated group. Removing this shift subtracts $a$ from the displayed parameter. This can be done on the determinant double cover even for half-integral $a$, by its integral power of the square-root determinant. The real additive character in both the source and Ichino's §4.1 is $x\mapsto e^{-2\pi i x}$. Changing the basis of a Hermitian form or exchanging its two signature labels does not affect the following multiset test. [Dual parameters and group model][dual] · [Infinity exponent][exp] · [Differentiated pairing with the plus sign][sign] · [Ichino, §4.1 and Theorem 4.1(ii)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2008.06174#page=10)

For a real unitary pair with larger dimension $N$ and smaller dimension $M<N$, Ichino's necessary condition for a nonzero theta lift of a discrete series is that its parameter contain every element of $B_{N-M}$ after subtracting the half-exponent of the splitting character of the smaller space. This follows directly from Theorem 4.1(ii), including the requirement that each entry of the block have positive total multiplicity. Section 9 gives the same criterion on the character exponents of the local parameter. The necessary string already follows in §6.1 of Ichino’s discrete-series paper from Atobe’s nonvanishing criterion. Atobe explains after Theorem 1.7 why this criterion is unconditional for discrete series. [Ichino, §§4.3, 6.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.09148#page=16) · [Atobe, §1.2](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aim.2020.106984) For $V'=I^\perp/I$, where $I\subset V$ is a totally isotropic $E$-space of dimension $d$, we have $N=n+t$, $M=n-2d$. The same $\chi_V$ serves on the smaller space because the rank changes by $2d$ and the required restriction is quadratic. The source uses precisely this same character in the tower. Since $d\geq1$, $B_{t+2d}$ contains both missing neighbors of $B_t$. The proposed local lift is zero. If $M=0$, the oscillator on the zero symplectic space is a line. Its character on $U(W)$ has differential $a\operatorname{tr}$, hence infinitesimal character $a+B_N$. The same missing coordinates exclude it. [Ichino, proof of Theorem 4.1(ii), §9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2008.06174#page=44) · [Same splitting character and lower signature][same]

## From local vanishing to a global constant term

For an irreducible cuspidal automorphic input, the lower global theta lift can be nonzero only if the integrated representation occurs in the oscillator at every place, with the dual convention above. One can apply the same argument directly to the multiplicity spaces in the source, without decomposing a smooth automorphic form into globally irreducible summands. Fix the output point and all inputs away from the chosen real place. A nonzero value on a polynomial times the Fock Gaussian and a compact-finite input would give a bilinear invariant pairing there. Indeed, simultaneously translating both factors on the right in the integral leaves its value fixed. Differentiation gives the Lie-algebra identity, and translation by the maximal compact subgroup gives compact equivariance. A compact-finite polynomial yields a functional with finite compact orbit on the input Harish-Chandra module. Such functionals belong to its contragredient because each compact type has finite multiplicity. The pairing would therefore define a nonzero map from Fock polynomials to that contragredient. Bao–Sun's Theorem 1.3 identifies algebraic and smooth occurrence for real unitary dual pairs, so local vanishing excludes this map. [Bao–Sun, Theorem 1.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1611.06298#page=3) · [The pairing at one real place][slice]

For a polynomial test and an arbitrary smooth input, average the simultaneous invariance identity over the maximal compact subgroup. The compact translates of the test span a finite-dimensional space. Expanding them in a basis replaces the smooth input by finitely many of its compact-type projections. Every resulting pairing is zero. For general tests, finite sums of polynomial times Gaussian vectors are dense at fixed finite level in the oscillator Sobolev seminorms. For fixed smooth cusp input and output point, the integral is continuous in a sufficiently high such seminorm. In the Schrödinger model the rational theta sum is bounded by a Schwartz seminorm, its translates have at most polynomial height growth on a Siegel set, and the fixed cusp form is rapidly decreasing. Integrating this majorant gives continuity and absolute convergence. The [seminorm proof for the original theta integral](THETA_CUSPIDAL_FACTORIZATION.md#convergence-and-the-growth-bound-on-the-prescribed-integral) gives this bound using input decay alone. This proves lower-lift vanishing for all the tests and vectors in the theorem. [Compact averaging][average] · [Fixed-level approximation and the integral bound][density] · [Smooth cusp inputs in the multiplicity space][mult]

Finally, the maximal parabolic stabilizing $I$ has Levi factor $\mathrm{GL}_d(E)\times U(V')$. The Rallis tower property in the unitary case identifies the constant term of $B_f(v)$ on $U(V')$ with the lower theta lift with Schwartz function restricted to zero in the $d$ hyperbolic variables. Chenyan Wu gives the precise formula in Proposition 3.1, (3.2), and gives the factor on the general-linear part in (3.4). The formula also follows by unfolding against the individual smooth cusp form $F(v)$. Nonzero isotropic orbits involve proper constant terms of the input and vanish. Right translation of $f$ covers arbitrary output points. In the rational-lattice model, normalized actions with the same splitting characters agree on the theta functions. Restriction to the lower member commutes with the action on the integrated group. Thus the same tower formula applies to the specified operators and measures. A different Haar normalization multiplies the zero constant term by a positive constant. Every rational isotropic $I$ gives zero by the local obstruction. For an anisotropic target the condition is vacuous. This proves cuspidality on the source's original domain. [Wu, Proposition 3.1 and proof](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1409.0770#page=9) · [Uniqueness on the original functions](../../../research/modules/theta/base_theta/main.tex#L1141-L1226) · [Compatibility under restriction][compat] · [Constant term for the specified quotient and measure][measure]
