# Bibliographical guide to the reconstruction

Levent Alpöge and Claude are the authors of the formal proof. This AI-assisted exposition draws on their Lean archive and on the work of other readers. We credit the account from which we learned a calculation, even when the argument can also be traced to Lean. To cite this exposition, give the edition, the result or section, and the source revision on the title page.

## How the Roadmap is used

The RH Lean Roadmap gives names and numbers to results dispersed through the formal archive. For instance, the list at the end of this chapter points to longer treatments of 63 statements in its Part VIII. The link labelled B8.55b takes the reader to the argument about evaluating algebraic sections at complex points. Our chapter on period heights shows why a relation among the evaluated functions vanishes on all the relevant frames, then uses base change for sections to descend the spanning statement to the comparison field. The number lets the reader match that argument with the Roadmap and its formal declarations. Individual chapters cite the revision they used so the line references remain meaningful. [Part VIII index](../../manuscript/guides/ROADMAP_OUTLINE_CROSS_REFERENCES.md) · [Descent of the spanning identity](../../manuscript/notes/geometry/COMMON_FAMILY_AND_PERIOD_HEIGHT.md#why-a-complex-spanning-identity-descends-to-the-comparison-field)

Elsewhere we follow the Roadmap's mathematical exposition closely. In the even-rank Galois argument, its proof of B9.41 supplies two theta lifts with distinct families of auxiliary characters. We use its choice of characters, work out their Frobenius roots and Hodge jumps, and then use disjointness to cancel the extra summands. The chapter specifies where it still uses the odd-rank attachment theorem. [Roadmap's character calculation](https://github.com/rh-lean-roadmap/rh-lean-roadmap/blob/c7755c1086f05d0918ee8496d95912e4f35e22f9/docs/src/book/main.tex#L61203-L61216) · [Even-rank argument in this edition](../full/even-rank-attachment.md)

A citation can also carry an entire hypothesis. The arithmetic volume argument starts with the tower of integral models in B8.26u. Its boundary section, metrics and frames enter the comparison of section counts. We explain that comparison and cite Moriwaki for the volume inequality on a suitable model. For the existence of the tower with those properties the reader still needs the external construction. The coverage chapter records such dependencies. [Roadmap's construction of the tower](https://github.com/rh-lean-roadmap/rh-lean-roadmap/blob/c7755c1086f05d0918ee8496d95912e4f35e22f9/docs/src/book/main.tex#L49923-L50726) · [Volume comparison in this edition](../../manuscript/notes/periods/PERIOD_VOLUME_BEFORE_SELECTION.md) · [Coverage of the derivations](../audit/completeness.md)

For ramified Galois comparison we follow the Roadmap's construction of a cuspidal CM lift fixing a chosen Weil element. The trace recurrence comes from the corresponding lemma in Alpöge and Claude's Lean proof. For the full tame test we apply Chang Yang, *Gamma factors of level zero supercuspidal representations*, Journal of the Ramanujan Mathematical Society 38 (2023), 295–307. Propositions 4.2–4.3 and 5.1 in the author's preprint identify the tame irreducible parameters and compute their unramified base change. The chapter checks them against the type and determinant laws of the weak local correspondence used here. [Ramified comparison in this edition](../../manuscript/notes/galois/RAMIFIED_LOCAL_COMPARISON.md) · [Roadmap B9.38b–d](https://github.com/rh-lean-roadmap/rh-lean-roadmap/blob/c7755c1086f05d0918ee8496d95912e4f35e22f9/docs/src/book/main.tex#L61026-L61121) · [Yang, journal article](https://www.mathjournals.org/jrms/2023-038-003/2023-038-003-007.html) · [Author's preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.11497v1)

## Formal source and working manuscripts

- **Levent Alpöge and Claude, formal proof archive, November 2026.** The five terminal declarations determine the scope of RH and of Dirichlet GRH here. Alpöge's account explains the use of repeated theta lifts and the choice of their number. The public mirror is cited at a fixed commit so that a declaration and its hypotheses can be retrieved together. [Original account](https://alpo.ge/remark.pdf) · [Formal sources at the revision used in this edition](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/tree/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4)
- **RH Lean Roadmap, book, version 41.** Its numbered statements and its index of declarations connect the host-field construction, the estimates for the theta periods, the frame argument and the conclusions about zeros. The book also distinguishes some arguments given in outline. The index in this volume points to their longer discussions. [Version 41](https://github.com/rh-lean-roadmap/rh-lean-roadmap/tree/c7755c1086f05d0918ee8496d95912e4f35e22f9) · [Index of longer passages](../../manuscript/guides/ROADMAP_OUTLINE_CROSS_REFERENCES.md)
- **Levent Alpöge and Claude, *The Dirichlet generalized Riemann hypothesis: An exposition of Alpöge’s argument*, Lean-to-natural-language translation by Kaiwen Jin.** We use Jin's signed window argument, the classification and estimates for pairs in the spectral sum, and the construction of a cut determinant in Appendix J. The manuscript identifies Alpöge and Claude as authors of the original proof and Jin as translator. [Translation at the cited revision](https://github.com/yuiyuiui/Natural-Language-Proof-Generalized-Riemann-Hypothesis/tree/405237610e58ab9d4a9d5d54d9a0548a0c44227c)
- **Repository account yyyyychen, *The Riemann hypothesis and Dirichlet L-functions: An exposition of Levent Alpöge’s argument* (24 November 2026).** Its treatment of period transport and of the auxiliary prime attached to a word is used in comparing arithmetic and complex realizations. The reconstruction is a working manuscript. [Manuscript at the cited revision](https://github.com/yyyyychen/RH-Natural-Language-Proof/blob/d4dfb1a282dbf857288e73eb1f546dbfcc5899b8/RH-Natural-Language-Proof.pdf)
- **Jimmie Adriazola, *The Alpöge Proof of the Riemann Hypothesis*.** The integral kernel and the counting of mixed and pure factors in its derivatives enter the inert-prime estimate. The chapter on finite derivatives derives the shared degree constraint from that counting argument. [Manuscript at the cited revision](https://github.com/jimjamzola/RH-Alpoge-Reconstruction/blob/9eefc08b0819728d4dd2ef0568b49a61251af111/manuscript/main.pdf) · [Calculation used](https://github.com/jimjamzola/RH-Alpoge-Reconstruction/blob/9eefc08b0819728d4dd2ef0568b49a61251af111/manuscript/sec8_gain.tex#L210-L265)
- **Muralidhara Subbarao, *Ram Calculus and the Alpöge Riemann-Hypothesis Approach: Equivalent Representations, Explicit Analytic Refinements, and Arithmetic Compatibility Conditions*, version 2 (24 November 2026).** Theorem 17.2 provides a precision estimate for a finite derivative test. Theorem 19.1 gives the centered rank inequality for the family of period maps. The numerical refinements in this edition also use the ranks and number of words in the selected family. [Theorem 17.2](https://github.com/muralisubbarao2/Refinements-and-Extensions-to-Alpoge-Claude-proof-of-Riemann-Hypotheisis/blob/4c3cb4f5e43212ec8c0ae759502291ae16e3df02/Ram-Calculus-Alpoge-RH-Nov24-v2.pdf#page=34) · [Theorem 19.1](https://github.com/muralisubbarao2/Refinements-and-Extensions-to-Alpoge-Claude-proof-of-Riemann-Hypotheisis/blob/4c3cb4f5e43212ec8c0ae759502291ae16e3df02/Ram-Calculus-Alpoge-RH-Nov24-v2.pdf#page=38)
- **Paul Nelson, *GRH via exercises*, and Bartosz Naskręcki’s riemann-rev project, *The announced proof of the Riemann hypothesis*.** Nelson's workbook develops comparisons for theta seesaws. Its revised route uses Luke Sernau’s reconstruction with colleagues at Anthropic and Claude, so transfer to an original Alpöge–Claude period requires identifying the objects at the chosen word and level. The riemann-rev notes separate the first-power payment from the other payments in column-chain divisibility. [Lean rewriting used by the workbook](https://github.com/lsernau/grh-lean/tree/72fead102f663d5c7a407964a13dc0cf9d977207) · [Workbook revision](https://github.com/ultronozm/grh-workbook/tree/954f94f32f6a3f7ce2d21c24598d8b9251e41729) · [Column-chain calculation](https://github.com/nasqret/riemann-rev/blob/cd4b6963e1ef31b923c1978ac11bf75c71ec8383/writeup/notes/column-chain-divisibility/column-chain-divisibility.tex#L1414-L1458)
- **Chenxin Huang, *An Arakelov-theoretic perspective on the Alpöge–Claude argument on RH, after Calegari-Dimitrov-Tang*, and Tokuhiro Eto, *Reading Alpöge’s proof of the Riemann hypothesis*.** Both are November 2026 preprints. The overview credits Huang's explanation of the height-two local model and Eto's account of the competing costs in a chain of lifts. Their role at these points is explanatory. Other comparisons, including accounts consulted without importing a theorem, are recorded in the source guide. ## Examples of refereed inputs and their precise scope

- **Bruce Ferrero and Lawrence C. Washington, “The Iwasawa invariant $\mu_p$ vanishes for abelian number fields,” Annals of Mathematics 109 (1979), 377–395.** Vanishing of the cyclotomic Iwasawa invariant for the abelian fields in question is an input to the host argument. Selecting a common interval for the twists and the prime sums still requires the construction in this manuscript. [Journal article](https://doi.org/10.2307/1971116)
- **Jean-Benoît Bost and Huayi Chen, “Concerning the semistability of tensor products in Arakelov geometry,” Journal de Mathématiques Pures et Appliquées 99 (2013), 436–488, Proposition 2.10(3).** The proposition controls the extreme slopes of a nonzero morphism. The manuscript identifies the source and target filtration steps, the common period maps and the squared operator norm to which this slope comparison is applied. [Journal article](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.matpur.2012.09.009) · [Authors' copy](https://webusers.imj-prg.fr/~huayi.chen/Recherche/bost_chen.pdf#page=26)
- **Atsushi Moriwaki, “Continuity of volumes on arithmetic varieties,” Journal of Algebraic Geometry 18 (2009), 407–457, Corollary 6.4.** The volume–intersection lower bound applies on the indicated arithmetic model under its positivity hypotheses. Construction of that model, regularization of its metrics, passage to cusp sections and comparison with the original lattice account for the proof-specific work. [Journal article](https://doi.org/10.1090/S1056-3911-08-00500-6) · [Author's preprint, Corollary 6.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0612269#page=42)

These examples illustrate how to read the citations across the volume. A theorem about a single lattice, a single prime, or one fixed height does not by itself prove the simultaneous statement needed on all the selected words, places and scales. The bridge from a published theorem to that simultaneous assertion must account for those quantifiers.
