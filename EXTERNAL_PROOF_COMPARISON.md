## Special choices with a verifiable benefit

* **Choose the primes in two sets.** The first set carries prescribed Frobenius in a common finite quotient and gives units at the trivial theta character. The second set supplies independent ray coordinates and detects Galois minors. At a toric label the residual order per second prime is zero or two. At an automorphic row of Hecke rank $r$ on $b$ basis columns it is at most $rb$. Both bounds persist at $\ell=2$. [Toric calculation](FIRST_PASS_TORIC_DISTRIBUTION.md) · [Automorphic calculation](FIRST_PASS_AUTOMORPHIC_FACTORS.md)
* **Use the cyclotomic image to decide whether the cofactor argument is necessary.** At every odd coefficient prime unramified in the fitted CM host the first-order calculation already gives a unit coefficient. At two the allowed loss is exactly controlled by the largest group of $2$-power roots of unity contained in that host. This can be tested on the field already chosen. [Proof and exact criterion](FIRST_PASS_GALOIS_PRIMES.md#when-the-simpler-galois-construction-applies)
* **Take one set of interpolation points before the long Frobenius orbits are known.** In Hexagon 2611.00839v1, Theorem 2.1, $0<\varepsilon<1/52$, the finite abelian group has order comparable to $x^{2+8\varepsilon}$ and its distinguished subgroup has bounded index $g$. The construction gives exact coset counts in the ratio $4:3:\cdots:3$ and spacing on every orbit of order at least $x^{\varepsilon/4}$ from every origin. Its nontrivial quotient characters consequently have unweighted mean $1/(3g+1)$. This offers a simplification for that combinatorial input. Subsequent derivative weights vary. Corollary 4.1 of the same paper selects the two outer host primes $q,d$. The inner conductor $Q$ also has independent ray coordinates, determinant requirements, and local Selmer conditions. [Theorem and hypotheses][interp]

## How the determinant fits Alpöge’s remarks

Items 17 and 18 distinguish two costs of twisting. Surviving cohomology contributes through its total length, while congruences change the index of a lattice of forms. On the fitted column the square Selmer determinant measures the first cost and the determinant of the projected theta columns bounds the second. Choosing them at the same conductor is what makes the two estimates usable for the same twist. Items 15 and 16 raise a further question about the positions of several period images in one target lattice. The first-pass determinants concern individual rows and leave those positions to the period and spectral estimates. ## Other literature at the boundary

Poitou–Tate and the local and global Euler characteristic formulas provide the count of obstructions to the square Selmer presentation. Bloch–Kato's local dimension formula applies to the actual de Rham representations once their conjugate jumps and rational invariant vanishings are checked. The integral finite cocycle lattice must still commute with specialization of coefficients. We give that step explicitly. Gross's formulation of Landherr's local-global theorem identifies the residual rational Hermitian space for the flag classification after the finite local lattices and all real signatures are compared. [Square matrix](FIRST_PASS_SELMER_PRESENTATION.md) · [Bloch–Kato, Corollary 3.8.4][bk] · [Milne, *Arithmetic Duality Theorems*][milne] · [Gross, Theorem 3.1][gross]

Waterloo v2 supplies the analytic host-prime selection and a detailed zeta specialization, with its main-inequality dependencies set out in Part III. Naskręcki's three digests address a different Selmer group, the locally trivial negative Tate twists on a cyclotomic tower. Those results are valuable on the window branch. Adriazola's proposed shorter K1 argument in §17.4.3 bounds a global second cohomology group over a field containing the requisite roots of unity. None of these results asserts the inner twisted row determinant for every coprime power on its installed theta conductor. [Waterloo v2][waterloo] · [Digest comparison at the formal pin](continuation_v83/nasqret.md) · [Proposed K1 bound][kone]

Two alternative geometric approaches merit a specific test. Huang's quantitative equidistribution and arithmetic Hilbert–Samuel inputs are stated as hypotheses. The Roadmap's simplification notes suggest using definite Siegel–Weil positivity to estimate the integral theta image. The remaining quantity is the **covolume of that integral image**. Surjectivity on the fraction-field spectral block allows an arbitrarily large integral index. The proposed single-embedding determinant shortcut would also need the signed bound at all embeddings. These alternatives leave concrete quantities to estimate on our [original period lattices](PERIOD_COMPARISON_STEP.md). [Huang, §§1, 6][huang] · [Roadmap simplification notes][simple]
