# The bottom Poincaré pairing with its Haar factor and finitely many real tests

Fix a selected original word $u$ on its linked Bessel realization. Write $S_\infty,S_f$ for the real and finite stabilizers in the arithmetic group's coordinates, $x=x_u$ for the word's finite translate, $K=K_2$ for the marked compact open and $H_x=S_f\cap xKx^{-1}$. Write $e:S_\infty\times S_f\to R(\mathbb A)$ for the native identification with the Bessel subgroup. Give $S_\infty$ its specified measure $\mu_{S,\infty}$, the pushforward of $\mu_N\otimes\mu_H$ under multiplication. Let $\mu_f$ be the ambient finite Haar and $\mu_R$ the Bessel Haar used in the level period. For any compact Borel $D_\infty\subset S_\infty$ of positive measure, put
$$
 C_u=\frac{\mu_f(K)\mu_{S,\infty}(D_\infty)}
 {\mu_R(e(D_\infty\times H_x))}>0.                                      \tag{1}
$$
This is the actual scalar taking the integrated bottom Bessel period to the Petersson pairing of the conjugate row with the original Poincaré column. It is independent of the patch, arithmetic tuple, and chosen column and row vectors. It need not be the same across words.

**Proof.** Give $S_f$ Haar $\nu_f$ with $\nu_f(H_x)=1$. This is possible since $H_x$ is compact open in the closed stabilizer. For a nonnegative function supported on $S_fxK$, integrating over $(s,k)\mapsto sxk$ against $\nu_f(ds)\mu_f(dk)|_K$ gives its ambient Haar integral. Indeed the admissible $s$ for a fixed finite point form one left coset of $H_x$. In these coordinates $e^*\mu_R=b(\mu_{S,\infty}\otimes\nu_f)$ for a unique $b>0$, since both are Haar and $e$ is a topological group isomorphism. Testing on $D_\infty\times H_x$ gives $b=\mu_R(e(D_\infty\times H_x))/\mu_{S,\infty}(D_\infty)$. The real quotient measure $\nu_{Q,u}$ is defined by the original Weil integration formula for $\mu_{S,\infty}$. The product disintegration therefore contributes $b^{-1}$. Unfold the rational stabilizer using its normalized cutoff. The level characters of the seed and row cancel on $K$ so its integral contributes $\mu_f(K)$. Their product is exactly (1). This obtains the scalar by testing Haar measures on a rectangle, even if all the automorphic pairings happen to vanish. [Finite orbit and normalizing subgroup][wp5-fin] · [Product coordinates and Haar factor][wp5-prod] · [Assembly of the two integrations][wp5-fold]

Let $C$ be a native spectral column, $f$ a representative of a permissible bottom row, and $I_y(C,f)$ the Bessel period with scalar column $\operatorname{colForm}(C,W_u(y))$ and row $g\mapsto f(\iota(g)(y,x_u))$. In the native conventions,
$$
 \langle\overline f,P_u C\rangle
        = C_u\int I_y(C,f)\,d\nu_{Q,u}(y).                           \tag{2}
$$
The bar indicates conjugation of the row representative before it enters the first slot of the Petersson product. In this convention (2) is linear in $f$ and in $C$. The compact rational Bessel quotient, the bound on the scalar-column readings and boundedness of $f$ give
$|I_y(C,f)|\le M(C,f)\|W_u(y)\|$ with a finite constant for each pair. The native test satisfies $\int\|W_u(y)\|d\nu_{Q,u}(y)<\infty$. The integrands are measurable, and the same bounds justify the complex-valued unfold. The stabilized original Poincaré seed then gives (2) with these measures. In applying (2) to all twisted rows on a selected word, take $f$ in the finite span of their actual translated, descended readings. The prescribed bottom datum uses one central finite translate $z$ for all placed words. For the chosen word, let $J$ be its bottom level, $\chi_J$ the level character and $k\in J$ the final level factor in the pin. The native identity of point arguments is
$$
 \iota_A(a_0a)b=(1,x_u)\,z t_S k
$$
in split coordinates. The bottom column dictionary supplies one isometry on the ramified column and functionals $\xi(y')$ spanning its coefficient space. Moving $a_0a$ from the column onto the row, then using right level equivariance, multiplies each bottom frame period by $\chi_J(k)$. This same nonzero scalar appears for the reference and every tested tuple, since it is fixed by the one word pin. It cancels from homogeneous relations, including zero inputs. This change of columns and row translation is made before choosing an evaluation; no division by a Bessel value enters it. The global existence of the common $z$ uses the source's placed and framed Bessel data, package hypotheses and $r_S\ge1$. The use of the folded pairing also requires its Bessel character and integrable discrete series test. ## Exact finite evaluation on the selected spaces

Let $V$ and $H$ be finite-dimensional spaces of these permissible columns and bottom row functions, of dimensions $d$ and $h$. For example $V$ may be the ramified word column and $H$ the span of the readings used by all the serving tuples, together with the full reference reading if required. There are real points $y_i$ and positive real numbers $a_i$, independent of both input vectors, with
$$
 \langle\overline f,P_u C\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{N}a_i I_{y_i}(C,f),
 \qquad N\le 2dh+1,\qquad
 \sum_{i=1}^{N}a_i\|W_u(y_i)\|
       =C_u\int\|W_u(y)\|\,d\nu_{Q,u}(y).                          \tag{3}
$$
If the rightmost integral vanishes, the empty sum works. Thus the noncompact integral of real tests is represented by exact positive quadrature on the original finite-dimensional inputs. The weights and points assert existence over the reals.

For the proof, write $w(y)=\|W_u(y)\|$, $J_0=\int w\,d\nu_{Q,u}>0$. On $w>0$ use probability $w\,d\nu_{Q,u}/J_0$ and form the bounded vector $Z(y)\in\mathbb R^{2dh}$ of real and imaginary parts of all basis-pair values $I_y/w(y)$. Its expectation belongs to the convex hull of actual attained values, including for noncompact parameter spaces. Indeed restrict to the smallest affine subspace containing $Z$ almost surely, discarding the exceptional null set. The expectation is in the closed convex hull. A supporting hyperplane at a relative boundary point containing this expectation would force its nonnegative affine distance on $Z$ to vanish almost surely, contradicting minimality of the affine subspace. The relative interior of the closure of a convex set is contained in the set, so finite-dimensional Carathéodory gives at most $2dh+1$ actual points and positive convex coefficients $p_i$. In affine dimension zero one point suffices. Set $a_i=C_uJ_0p_i/w(y_i)$. Equation (2) gives (3) on basis pairs, and bilinearity gives it throughout. The points can be drawn from any prescribed full-measure set. No extra finite level, determinant bound or moment condition is used.

## Integral ramified tests at a serving place

There is a further calculation on the original free marking orbits which also applies at residue characteristics dividing $T_u-1$. Let $A=(\mathbf F_{T_u}^{\times})^f$, put $n=T_u-1$ and choose a generator $s_i$ of each factor. Over the valuation ring $O$ of the serving coefficient place, use the integral class-function lattice with coefficient lattice $L$ transported along one marking orbit. The action changing the marked vector identifies this lattice with $L\otimes_O O[A]$. The two neat auxiliary levels give freeness on the selected word and the natural pointwise coefficient lattice is transported by the markings. The integral vectors satisfying the original ramification equations form precisely
$$
 \bigcap_{i=1}^f \ker\Big(\sum_{a=0}^{n-1}s_i^a\Big)
       = \Big(\prod_{i=1}^f(1-s_i)\Big)(L\otimes_O O[A]).                \tag{4}
$$
No division occurs. Thus testing an $O$-linear period against this whole ramified source amounts to testing signed sums supported at $2^f$ markings at a time. The workbook's central generator and any other primitive generator give exactly the same lattice on common marking data. A change between them costs no serving-prime factor. [Free original marking orbits and ramified projection](PERIOD_FULL_REFERENCE_PAIRING.md#extracting-the-reference-vector) · [Workbook's exact source level and ramification equation](https://github.com/ultronozm/grh-workbook/blob/7b008ea8b334749fb3ad0b2a59c99ef00ba2a41c/20261126T040100--spaces-forms-and-twists__workbook.tex#L108-L112)

Indeed in one cyclic orbit let $e_j=s^j e_0$ and $I=\{\sum c_je_j:\sum c_j=0\}$. The norm carries $\sum c_je_j$ to $(\sum c_j)\sum e_j$. The adjacent differences $e_j-e_{j+1}$ for $0\le j<n-1$ form an integral basis of $I$, and $O[C_n]=I\oplus O e_{n-1}$. Tensor these split decompositions over all factors. The common kernel is $L\otimes_O I^{\otimes f}$, and the image on the right of (4) is that same module. Taking all $f$-fold products of adjacent differences and a basis of $L$ gives an integral basis of the ramified lattice. Each basis element is one signed sum. This works independently on every orbit, over every valuation ring, and after reduction or extension of coefficients. For any bilinear pairing with an integral row, the fractional ideal generated by its values on that row and this source is exactly the ideal generated by its values on these signed sums and row generators. Scaling the source by a fractional ideal scales this content by the very same ideal. For the original unshifted module $N_u$, the defining local conditions give $(N_u)_w\subseteq (M_{\rm ram})_w$ whenever $w\nmid T_u$. Thus, at all such places, a bound on these signed basis sums for the full pointwise ramified lattice suffices on the original unshifted module under its stage-zero class-coordinate identification. This includes every coefficient place over a divisor of $T_u-1$. At the word prime the native module is instead a rational pullback of a Hecke image of the ramified input; the signed basis generates the input to that operator, and its image must still be used. [Actual unshifted lattice and the word-prime branch](continuation_v89/integral.tex#L3-L46)

There is also an exact archimedean cost on these marking coordinates. In the pointwise class-sum metric take the orbit basis vectors to be orthonormal, absorbing any common orbit weight into the metric on $L$. In one factor the Gram matrix of the adjacent differences has diagonal $2$ and neighboring entries $-1$; expanding along an end gives determinant $n$. By the Kronecker determinant formula the signed-corner Gram determinant on $f$ factors is $n^{f(n-1)^{f-1}}$. If the coefficient fibre has rank $b$ and Gram matrix $G_L$, the full determinant is
$$
    n^{b f(n-1)^{f-1}}(\det G_L)^{(n-1)^f}.
$$
Thus the increase in log squared covolume per ramified dimension over the coefficient-fibre contribution is exactly $f\log n/(n-1)$. This metric contribution and the integral equality (4) coexist even at a prime dividing $n$; a vanishing Gram determinant modulo that prime does not change the split-module calculation.

## The integral inverse of the affine incidence matrix on one reduced chart

At a marked split place let $W$ be the $d$-dimensional residue vector space over $k=\mathbb F_q$, $d\ge1$. On a *full* chart of all punctured residue vectors and dual vectors, put
$$
 (\mathcal H b)(g)=\sum_{z\in W:\,g(z)=1} b(z),\qquad
 (\mathcal H^{t}a)(z)=\sum_{g\in W^*:\,g(z)=1}a(g).
$$
Take the integral lattices on either side given by vanishing of the coefficient sum on each punctured line. These are the linewise augmentation lattices in (4). Both operators preserve them, since summing $(\mathcal H b)(ag)$ over $a\in k^\times$ gives the sum of $b$ on all lines outside $\ker g$, and likewise on the dual side. On each of these integral lattices,
$$
              \mathcal H^t\mathcal H=q^{d-1}1,\qquad
              \mathcal H\mathcal H^t=q^{d-1}1.                 \tag{6}
$$
For a fixed $z\ne0$, exactly $q^{d-1}$ functionals satisfy $g(z)=1$. Distinct proportional vectors cannot both take value one. For each $z'$ independent of $z$ precisely $q^{d-2}$ functionals take value one on both. This last contribution sums to zero line by line. For $d=1$ there are no independent pairs. Interchanging the space and its dual gives the second identity. The proof acts componentwise for values in any fixed coefficient module.

In particular the **inverse on the residue chart** is $q^{1-d}\mathcal H^t$ in characteristic zero. The image contains $q^{d-1}$ times the target augmentation lattice and agrees with the latter away from the residue characteristic. Its determinant index over $\mathbb Z$ is $q^{(d-1)D/2}$, where $D=(q-2)(q^d-1)/(q-1)$, because the two linewise integral lattices have the same Gram determinant and (6) scales their Euclidean metrics by $q^{d-1}$. Over a coefficient DVR the determinant valuation is $(d-1)D\,v(q)/2$. These formulas apply to the individual residue reading of the original neighbor operator after any indicated coefficient transports. The original global operator also transports polynomial lattices between different neighbors. A coefficient-lattice comparison for those maps is needed to carry the numerical index to the full automorphic lattice. For the matched arithmetic kernel there is also an exact formula before class averaging. Write $T=T_u$, $s=(t+1)/2$. If $D,D'$ have surjective residue markings $\ell,\ell'$, put $H=\ker\ell$ in $D$ and take $z_0\in D$ with $\ell(z_0)=1$. For an arbitrary linear map $x$, the unique contributing upper neighbor of $D'$ and the unique contributing lower neighbor of $D$ both exist exactly when $xH\subset D'$, $Txz_0\in D'$ and $\ell'(Txz_0)=1$. Their parameters are $Txz_0\bmod TD'$ and $\ell'x|_H$. The determinant increments at the moved place are both one. The native split scalar consequently equals $T^s$, with all away factors one. Summing over $x$ with the common away reader and the native movement law gives the kernel identity
$$
 \sum_{P'\to P'_1} k_i(P,P'_1)
       =T^s \sum_{P_0\to P} k_i(P_0,P').                      \tag{7}
$$
The two neighbor sums are over individual decorated points, before passing to classes. Linearity proves (7) for each signed-corner basis input as well. Its explicit scalar contributes $s\,v_w(T)$ when comparing nonzero values at a serving place; at a place away from $T$ it is a unit. Neither (6) nor (7) identifies the entire set of row and column coefficient lattices.
