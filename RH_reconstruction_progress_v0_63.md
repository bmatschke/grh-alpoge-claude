## The negative weights

Write the weight as $-n$, with $n\ge1$, and let $e\ge1$ be the host exponent. On the absolute Galois group the scalar character is a finite character times $\chi_\ell^{-en}$. Fix a prime $p\ne\ell$. Pass to a finite local extension that kills the finite character. On inertia the cocycle now gives an additive homomorphism. Since its target is pro-$\ell$, this homomorphism factors through the $\ell$-part of tame inertia.

Let $q$ be the residue cardinality of the extension. Arithmetic Frobenius acts on that tame quotient by $q$, but on the coefficient line by $q^{-en}$. These scalars differ in the characteristic-zero coefficient field. Applying the cocycle identity to conjugation by Frobenius forces the inertia homomorphism to vanish. The remaining unramified cocycle has a primitive, found by dividing its Frobenius value by $q^{-en}-1$. Averaging the finite orbit of that primitive under the affine action descends it to the original decomposition group. This argument also applies to special host primes where the original negative-weight condition supplies no inertia primitive. [VHN1–2](hostvan_v63/negative.tex)

At $\ell$, the given primitive on the sign kernel extends by the same finite-index argument. The earlier global rank-one theorem then supplies a primitive in the original fraction field. Surjectivity of the map to the host Galois quotient descends its identity, and clearing the denominator gives the required integral scalar result. [VHN3](hostvan_v63/negative.tex) · [Earlier global theorem RV6–7](rank_one_cohomology/main.tex)

## Weight zero and the integral matrix

At weight zero the character on the sign kernel is trivial. The inertia tests make the reductions of the additive cocycle unramified on the Gaussian field. Class number one kills each reduction. Separation in the coefficient DVR then gives vanishing on the sign kernel, and finite-index descent supplies the integral assertion. [VHZ1](hostvan_v63/assembly.tex)

For a matrix cocycle, pair each entry with its reflection across the opposite diagonal. The two signed combinations are scalar cocycles of the kinds just proved. Taking a common multiple $A$ of their finitely many denominators gives one integral matrix $X$ satisfying $4A\gamma(g)=X-\operatorname{Ad}(r_0(g))X$ for all $g$, with the adjoint action including the sign involution. The calculation includes entries fixed by reflection. This proves the original host proposition for every host frame permitted by its signature. [VHZ2–4](hostvan_v63/assembly.tex)
