# The two arithmetic rows in the homogeneous period comparison

Fix a word on the original linked bundle. Write $t_0$ for its arithmetic reference tuple, $F$ for a vector of rational coordinates in the retained top row at tuple $t$, and $q_t F$ for its image in the good bottom row. Two observations allow one to apply the homogeneous seesaw to these particular rows. The terminal input is prescribed by $F$ at **both** tuples. The finite level average giving the analytic row arrow can be evaluated even when the tuple has nonempty twisting support. This identifies the row in the [good-row argument](PERIOD_GOOD_ROW_WORD_MAPS.md) without requiring surjectivity onto an arbitrary smooth bottom vector.

## The row for a prescribed arithmetic vector

Use a real realization with its level characters, intertwining models, cuspidal theta chain, good idempotent, and the extra top projection as in the source's hypothesis `PkHyp`. On the word use its prescribed Haar measures and geometric rows (`RowOK`). In this statement the geometry at every tuple is generated from the **same** word. The existence of the compatible row choices uses cuspidal rapid decay and the level, model and chain conditions independently of a period comparison. The geometric measure and terminal data supply the other parts of `RowOK`. [Simultaneous choice of the rows][rowchoice] · [Inputs for the row][rowok] · [Measures and simultaneous installation][install]

Let $D_{t,j}$ denote the row arrow going from stage $j+1$ to $j$, with the terminal domain understood at $j=m-1$. For an admitted upper row $G$, polynomial $P$ and upper smooth test $v$, the actual analytic lift with the word's finite test and Haar measure satisfies
$$
   \operatorname{Lift}_{t,j}(G(v);P)
       =i_{t,j}(D_{t,j}G)(I_j(P,v)),\qquad
   i_{t,j}=|H_{\rm lev}|\,\kappa_{j+1}\,\operatorname{volLev}_{t,j}.
   \tag{1}
$$
Here $I_j$ is the indicated real intertwiner, $H_{\rm lev}$ is the finite level group, and $\kappa_{j+1}\operatorname{volLev}_{t,j}$ converts the native quotient Haar measure into the measure in the common word. The level volume may depend on $t$. Equation (1) includes the terminal arrow with its own intertwiner and measure. Jin explains the character cancellation for the reference tuple. [Jin, Appendix E][jinrow] · [Ordinary row scalar][iosc] · [Haar conversion][iohaar] · [Terminal scalar][iotop]

Indeed the prescribed theta test is the sum of translates of the tuple's native test by lifts $k_a$ of the level group elements $a$, weighted by $\chi_{t,j}(k_a)^{-1}$. For each $a$, the native integrated lift, evaluated at $gk_a$, transforms by $\chi_{t,j}(k_a)$ because the projected multiplicity has that level character. Hence each summand is the same function of $g$. Integrability of the individual theta integrands allows the finite sum to pass through the integral. This gives $|H_{\rm lev}|$ before changing the Haar measure. Left automorphy permits replacing the fundamental domain in that change. The terminal domain is the projected range on the definite group, and the terminal character calculation applies to every row in that range. The argument uses the representatives and character at the tuple being tested. It also applies when a row, a polynomial evaluation, or a period vanishes. [Finite character calculation][iosc] · [Terminal projected range][iotop]

For the particular row put $x_t=j_{X,t}(e_{V,t}F)$. In the terminal multiplicity take the vector whose underlying function is the prescribed terminal model applied to $x_t$ (the source's `topOf`). The extra top projection admits this vector. Each $D_{t,j}$ agrees on underlying functions with the canonical theta arrow on the projected input. Induction down the chain, starting with the terminal arrow, therefore gives the native composite $\operatorname{thDn}_t(x_t)$. To identify it with the good model row, let $e$ be the central finite character idempotent. The retained vector $e_{V,t}F$ lies in the image of the good projector $eP$, so $e(eP)=eP$ fixes it. Equivariance of descent with respect to the finite group carries this equality to the bottom. Applying the good projector there fixes the descended function on each decomposable smooth tensor, and linearity gives equality everywhere. In the common smooth realization we have proved
$$
       D_{t,0}\cdots D_{t,m-1}\,\operatorname{topOf}_t(F)
          =\operatorname{rowOf}\bigl(\Theta_t j_{X,t}(e_{V,t}F)\bigr).
       \tag{2}
$$
This is the precise analytic reading of $q_tF$ before the common coordinate isometry and conjugation. Both sides depend on the original $F$, including on the kernel of the arithmetic row map. ## Applying the two-tuple relation

For the joint comparison assume the **bundle** has its full original linkage witness, its marked real readings and its pointwise row data. The latter include the joined row model, real frames, primitive rows and size gaps. Use the published representation inputs on the word (in particular rapid decay, discrete-series cover, Harish-Chandra module theory and strong approximation for the special unitary groups) and $r_S\geq1$ for the common bottom datum. On this domain the source constructs its prescribed characters, twist records, terminal readings, true geometry and its common bottom translate. Equality of the entire linked bundle transports these data. An identification just of its ranks, measures or finite tuple labels would not implement this step. [From a witnessed bundle to the true realization][pack] · [Transport on the selected bundle][link] · [Pointwise row hypothesis and its own upstream inputs][pt] · [Common bottom datum and family][common]

On this realization the twist charts, fixed-type representations of the tests, level-prime correction on retained column readings, upper-row invariances, and terminal class pairing all belong to the family whose rows occur in (1). More explicitly, one first constructs the compatible rows; the finite local comparison supplies the correction and its constants; one constructs the column using exactly these constants with these rows; one then evaluates the finite comparison on that family. The top class reading is checked after this substitution. This order matters because choosing identical abstract row spaces in two different families would only identify the domains in (1). The detailed local calculations are in [the finite factors](PERIOD_INTERIOR_FINITE_FACTORS.md), [the selected Fourier operator](PERIOD_SELECTED_FOURIER_WORD.md) and [the final two lifts](PERIOD_TERMINAL_ROW_DOUBLE_LIFT.md). [Construction in that order][install] · [Evaluation of these tests and the terminal class law][laws]

For clarity, take a finite list of arithmetic inputs $(x_i,F_i,t_i)$, in particular one at $t_0$ and one at $t$. Let $d_t=\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}i_{t,j}$, and let $h_t$ be the product of the terminal class scalar and all the even-pair factors $v_{t,j}k_{t,j}k_{t,j+1}$. Here $v_{t,j}$ is the two-step orbital scalar and $k_{t,j}$ is the scalar of the $j$th definite column lift. Write $s_u$ for the common scalar from commuting the level corrections, and use the inverse of the common word similarity in the bottom column coordinates. Then $c_t=s_u h_t/d_t$ is the arithmetic top normalizer. Let $A_t(x,F)$ be the word's raw top class pairing and $B_u(x,q_tF)$ its bottom automorphic reading in these coordinates, including the common finite translation. The following implication holds:
$$
   \sum_i \lambda_i c_{t_i}A_{t_i}(x_i,F_i)=0
       \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
   \sum_i \lambda_i B_u(x_i,q_{t_i}F_i)=0 .
   \tag{3}
$$

To see the normalization, insert $\alpha_i=\lambda_i/d_{t_i}$ and terminal row $\operatorname{topOf}_{t_i}(F_i)$ into the joint descent. At stage $j$ even the coefficient is
$$
   \alpha_i\prod_{0\le b< j/2} v_{t_i,2b}k_{t_i,2b}k_{t_i,2b+1}
              \prod_{j\le n<m}i_{t_i,n}.
$$
Multiplying the lower coefficient by the two outgoing column scalars and orbital scalar gives the upper coefficient times the two row scalars. At the terminal stage the common real branching map multiplies the top relation, so it vanishes on every required real test. At each intervening pair the finite-frame expansion has coefficients independent of the tuple's input. The upper relation therefore vanishes in each summand, for each remaining pair of real translates. Applying the ordered absolutely convergent period integrals and then continuity on the dense space of real tests gives the relation one pair lower. At the top the arithmetic tail and the column commutation contribute $A_t$ and $s_u$; at the bottom (2) and $\alpha_i d_{t_i}=\lambda_i$ give the desired coefficients. The relation holds for every base frame and smooth tensor, so integration against the common bottom Poincaré test preserves it. In particular one may use the arithmetic construction (2) at $t_0$ itself. Full reference descent onto an arbitrarily chosen analytic vector enters the stronger statement with such a vector prescribed in advance. ## Why the selected family supplies these data

Take a family in the original common selected class, in its stated small positive $\varepsilon$ range. There is one threshold for all indices and words of that family after which the hypotheses concerning the marked real readings, pointwise rows and joined coordinates above are fulfilled. This deduction does not require adjoining a new eventual predicate to an already chosen selector. It uses the individual fields of its actual bundle and its size clauses. The class's existence is a separate quantifier. [Actual bundle and witness](continuation_v87/selection.tex#L3-L35) · [Fields making up the pointwise row][ptdef]

First, the common parameter, including its strengthened twist version, has the form $\mathrm{prmR}(N,S,\Gamma)$. Its row-position condition is exactly the existence data for the marked real readings. The weighted point $q$ already contains the witness $q.x.rx$; this gives the chart readings at *all* tuples with their specified rational-coordinate compatibility. The full bundle equality in X87S1 transports it from this $q$. The witnessed bundle also supplies the six qualitative row assertions in the native pointwise condition. These are the joined row realization, its division-side compatibility, real frames, the seesaw/evaluation realization, existence of the auxiliary real data, and the primitive row and type separation conditions. For the joined evaluation the construction uses the discrete-series cover, Harish-Chandra module theory, smooth globalization, Harish-Chandra finiteness and cuspidal rapid decay. The project proves these five inputs and supplies the joined arithmetic/spectral coordinate isometries on the same eventual members. [Actual parameter](continuation_v77/selection.tex#L35-L82) · [Projection of the marked readings for arbitrary $N,S,\Gamma$][rx] · [Qualitative rows from the one witness][pt] · [Joined coordinates](continuation_v90/join.tex#L117-L186)

The four remaining fields of the pointwise condition follow directly from the family inequalities. Set $r=k+m-1$, $N=k+m(t+1)=\operatorname{rank}X'+1$, where $t$ in this paragraph is the odd rank increment. R89C1 proves spectral admissibility $\mathrm{FinX}$ at every sufficiently late index. For the signature gap the native real parameter is the ordered list $(v_0,\ldots,v_{k-2},-b_0,\ldots,-b_{m-1})$. The first $k$ coordinates have equal sign. If two different-sign positions are $a<b$, their later position is at least $k$ (indices from zero), hence their difference dominates one entire adjacent node gap. The family bound $b_{i+1}-b_i\ge16r^{19}$ exceeds $100r^4$ for eventual $r\ge1801$. The Hida row is the prescribed reindexing of these values and signs. For the node-gap field use the different size clause
$$
  b_{i+1}-b_i\ \ge\ \eta/m\ \ge\ 4N^3+2t+6\ \ge\ t+2N^3+1.
$$
It gives the required nodes for the original tower including its terminal rank. Finally $m\ge x^{1/4}$ and $r_S=m/2-1$ give $r_S\ge1$ uniformly once $x\ge256$. Taking the maximum of these cutoffs with the join and spectral cutoffs proves the assertion. [Eventual adjacent gaps](frame_v61/main.tex#L95-L109) · [Rank, parameters and size inequalities](degree_prime_size/main.tex#L116-L126) · [Cubic clause in the size bounds][sizes] · [Signature comparison][sign] · [Native node requirement][nodes] · [Spectral admissibility](continuation_v89/comparison.tex#L4-L30)

Thus (3) holds on every sufficiently late member of a *supplied* original common family with the stated general representation and strong-approximation theorems. No per-word or per-tuple choice of another family, marked real reading, node bound or joined model is needed. The common bottom translate acts isometrically on the good model row; its inverse is the inverse translate. Dividing the word similarity by its positive norm scale gives the word's unitary column change, and the fixed row conjugation is the same for all words. These are the metric identifications required when using (3) for entry 4. [Transported bottom level](PERIOD_TRANSPORTED_BOTTOM_LEVEL.md) · [Jin on the row and column isometries][unit]

## Consequence for the two height entries

Suppose the common joined coordinates have been installed and the word has nonzero bottom pairing on the good row. Since $q_{t_0}$ is onto, choose a rational reference pair with nonzero bottom reading. By (3) its normalized top entry is nonzero. Cancel the two normalized top entries in (3) for this pair and any second arithmetic input. This identifies the native rational pairing $B_u^{\rm rat}$ from the reference section with $A_t$ on $q_tF$, up to the ratio $c_t/c_{t_0}$. It also shows that $B_u^{\rm rat}$ is a nonzero scalar multiple of the bottom pairing in the joined coordinates, as required for the word in entry 4. The [good-row calculation](PERIOD_GOOD_ROW_WORD_MAPS.md) gives $c_t/c_{t_0}\in\mu(K)$ on these fitted charts, with $K$ the coefficient field; in particular it is a unit at every finite place.

Consequently at each serving place $w$, for the original column lattice $N_u$ and served row lattice $M_{t_w}$, the values of $B_u^{\rm rat}(N_u,q_{t_w}M_{t_w})$ and $A_{t_w}(N_u,M_{t_w})$ generate the same local fractional ideal (with the zero ideal allowed). If the latter values belong to $\mathfrak p_w^{k_{u,w}}\mathcal O_w$, so do the former. This includes reference-served places where the native rational section alone has a denominator. The [arithmetic serving estimate](PERIOD_REFERENCE_INTEGRAL.md#arithmetic-periods-on-the-same-selection) proves the requisite bound on its original constructed-family domain under its correspondence, reciprocity and arithmetic inputs. Thus on their joint domain this proves the serving test of entry 1 for every active word. There is no additional exceptional finite-place test on those words.

## Full reference anchor and the literal native word maps

There is a stronger conclusion on the original selected class when one uses the [full-column diagonal estimate](SPECTRAL_FULL_GRAM_ON_MATCHED_FAMILIES.md) and the [reference row descent](PERIOD_REFERENCE_ROW_DESCENT.md). In particular, the possibility of a nonzero native top functional on a word with zero good pairing, left open by (3) alone, is excluded there.

The diagonal estimate has a simultaneous dimension window. At a good linked member in the original size range one can establish this window *before* using that estimate. The split class count and trivial class stabilizers give $d_u=d_u^\star(T_u^k-1)^f$. The prescribed prime window has $T_{*,u}\le T_u\le(1+1/(fk))T_{*,u}$, where $T_{*,u}^{fk}= D^\star/(d_u^\star\Gamma^2)$. Thus $d_u\Gamma^2/D^\star=(T_u/T_{*,u})^{fk}(1-T_u^{-k})^f$ lies between $1/2$ and $e<3$. This works for every word on this member; it uses the original column dimension and arithmetic prime window. [Linked-member statement and complete count][window] · [Arithmetic proof with class stabilizers](PERIOD_FULL_REFERENCE_PAIRING.md#the-simultaneous-dimension-premise)

Write $P_u$ for the full bottom Bessel operator in this paragraph, $\Pi_u$ for the ramified column projector and $H$ for the full Hilbert target. On the valid window the diagonal estimate gives $P_u\Pi_u\ne0$ for each word simultaneously. Fix $\phi_*$ in that ramified column with $p=P_u\phi_*\ne0$. Full translated evaluation at the reference tuple is onto $H$; choose the smooth multiplicity row evaluating to $p$. Choose **one analytic reference terminal row** descending to that multiplicity row. To justify this choice one uses surjectivity on the full reference row, proved by adjoint doubling on the reached Hecke eigenspaces. Its row tests and levels are the reference instance of (1). The source's exact application uses the realization and row conditions accounted for above, together with unramified Howe correspondence, the unitary-pair Weil representation, the unitary Weil–Siegel and convergent Siegel–Weil formulas, local doubling integrals and unramified split-general-linear doubling in their specified normalizations, as well as the five general representation inputs. The [reference row proof](PERIOD_REFERENCE_ROW_DESCENT.md#the-realization-and-the-analytic-inputs) gives the local support, real pairing and parameter arguments. No projection of $p$ to the good row occurs. [Full evaluation and nonvanishing](PERIOD_FULL_REFERENCE_PAIRING.md#extracting-the-reference-vector) · [Doubling criterion][onto] · [Its application to this reference tower][reach]

The proof by coefficient recursion before (3) applies to this analytic terminal row as its reference term, since the recursion allows any terminal multiplicity input there. Keep the canonical arithmetic terminal row (2) as the second term, for every $t$, *including* $t=t_0$. Let $a_*$ be the normalized top entry for the chosen analytic reference pair and $b_*=\|p\|^2$ its bottom entry. Its upper entry must be nonzero by the one-term implication. Taking the coefficients $(c_t A_t(x,F),-a_*)$ gives
$$
       a_* B_u(x,q_tF)=b_*c_t A_t(x,F)  \qquad\text{for all }x,t,F .
$$
This includes words on which every good-row bottom value is zero. There every arithmetic $A_t$ vanishes as well. At the reference tuple the identity shows $A_{t_0}$ kills $\ker q_{t_0}$ and therefore descends through the prescribed rational section to exactly the native rational word map. Its nonzero comparison factor with $B_u$ uses $a_*,b_*,c_{t_0}$ and the established metric scales. On a zero word both maps vanish identically. At every other tuple it satisfies the unit-ratio identity of the preceding section; for a zero word its local content vanishes. [Two types of reference input in the native relation][specialize] · [Normalization and integral-dual deduction](PERIOD_REFERENCE_INTEGRAL.md#what-the-retained-multiplicity-comparison-proves-for-entries-1-and-4)

Consequently **literal entry 4 holds for all words** on each sufficiently late member of the supplied original selection under the standing spectral, class-count and reference-doubling hypotheses just stated. **Literal entry 1 also holds for all words** on the common domain of the arithmetic serving estimates (B4.31/B8.56) and the original integral identifications (B4.38–45/R88L1–3). These are family-level deductions on the original class. Uniform production of the good points underlying that class, the arithmetic prerequisites to its serving inequalities, and the other height entries are independent tasks. Applying a final selected-family Gram estimate while constructing the class that is to support it would require a separate justification.
