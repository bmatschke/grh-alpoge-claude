# Local conditions for exporting a first-pass column

Fix the common eventual range of the first-pass column and its parameter and point theorems. On **every built first-pass Level Four column with origin** in that range, the setting condition $\mathrm{SetHyp}_i$ holds for every twist index $i$ lying over a nonreference coefficient place. In particular it holds at the chosen index $i=\mathrm{twI}(w)$ for every $w\in W$, including when $W$ is nonempty. This proves the setting-condition clause of the first export with its universal quantifier over built columns. The same proof gives integral self-duality and the Hodge gap for every stored Galois label at such an index. The remaining export clauses ask for a finite full-rank family of theta tests and characters satisfying simultaneous estimates for every coprime power. ## Locating the Galois data on the built column

The source of a built presentation is pinned, has the extra row witness, and carries its normalized extension. Forgetting the origin, list and extension law gives the default extension $e$. In particular there are actual Hecke eigenvalues, the normalization of their actions and the goodness of the marked projectors on this localized column. The residual data, character frame, and its degree-zero local character readings are those of this same presentation. For $w\in W$ the fitted small-field embedding gives the underlying prime at $w$ in the positive reading and at $\bar w$ in the negative reading. Both are nonreference places. The compatibility of the chosen index and the normality of the distinguished coefficient field identify its contracted maximal ideal with this valuation. This proves $\mathrm{NonzeroIndexPlace}(\mathrm{twI}(w))$. [From a built witness to the member][built] · [Default extension][default] · [Proof for the two sides][under]

One can extract the Galois triple needed here without the global series-integrality assumption sometimes included in the member theorem. We detail the extraction because replacing a uniformly quantified hypothesis by a verification on one distinguished row would change the export statement.

* The character-field values, roots of unity and conjugation stability follow from the fitted base and its character frame. The local parameter for a good row follows from the good cuspidal parameter theorem, existence of an index over a twisted place and the localization's split comparison at that index. These two member proofs discard the auxiliary series hypothesis. [Coefficient field][field] · [Row parameters][rowpar] · [First-pass cuspidal row](FIRST_PASS_CUSPIDAL_ROW.md)
* For the Hecke eigenvalue and occurrence tests, start with the actual finite omitted set and the eigenvalues read by $e$. Enlarge the omitted set inside the common excluded places to include the low and marked primes. The Hecke and identity-coordinate fields of $e$ read the rational Hecke operators there. Read the marked additive character from the stored level. Its identity and the preceding enlargement are obtained pointwise from the member data. The rational row reading now identifies the eigenvalues on the transported row and witnesses the finite type of each occurring summand. Both subsidiary proofs used for these two operations discard the global series hypothesis. [Allowed enlargement][hecke] · [Actual level character][psi] · [Eigenvalue and occurrence comparison][eig]
* The commuting Hecke actions and fullness on the required rows follow from the geometric characterization at the pinned family, its integral identity and the Hecke field of $e$. The marked primes are in every indicated exclusion set. Again the proof uses the given member. Together with the residual ties this identifies the integral Galois matrices, row parameter and normalizing character of each triple with those on the localized presentation. Substitution of the four preceding member results into this construction eliminates the unused global series hypothesis. [Commutation and fullness][comm] · [Assembly of the Galois triple][trip]

The normalized extension separately supplies the toric labels, their critical exponents and their class-sum comparison. Extract the Weil–Deligne identification, the true automorphic-parameter reading and the tame label identification from the built core. The column size range supplies $r\ge100$ and $g_0,g_1\ge2r$. These give all the fields of the Galois-side record. The normalized LLC, polarized cuspidal Galois representations and their local–global compatibility, and de Rham potential semistability produce the Galois–Hodge comparison for this reading. The [pointwise Galois comparison](FIRST_PASS_POINT_COMPARISON.md) checks the local-global normalizations at these places. Full degree of the tame label gives trivial wild inertia for the indicated local parameters: the dimension already accounted for by the tame part is the whole dimension. The attached triple then gives the marked residual comparison. ## The Hodge gap and integral duality

Write $T_s=\chi_s^{-1}\rho_s$ for the integral twist on a stored label $s$, $c$ for host conjugation and $\epsilon$ for the cyclotomic character in the record's convention. At a place above $\ell_i$ and a chosen embedding $\tau$, the de Rham comparison identifies the jump multiset with
$$
 J_\tau=\{-z+(t-1)/2 : z\in\mathrm{Inf}_\tau(\pi)\},
$$
where $\pi$ is the rank-$r$ cuspidal constituent normalized for $T_s$. The parity of $r,t$ and algebraicity of $\pi$ make the entries integral. The weight window of the attached row puts every entry outside $[-1,t]$; $J_{\bar\tau}$ contains $t-1-a$ with the same multiplicity as $a\in J_\tau$. The identification counts graded multiplicities at every local embedding. Thus an actual jump satisfies $a<-1$ or $a>t$. [Weight computation and row window][weights] · [Passage to each local filtration][njump]

At a split unramified Frobenius, conjugate self-duality of the parameter gives equal characteristic polynomials for $g\mapsto\epsilon(g)^t T_s(g)$ and $g\mapsto \epsilon(cgc^{-1})\,{}^{t}T_s(cgc^{-1})^{-1}$. Split Frobenius density and continuity extend the characteristic-polynomial identity to the whole Galois group. The two marked type primes give absolute irreducibility of the residual constituent, preserved by twisting with scalars. In particular the residual image spans the full matrix algebra over the residue field. One obtains an intertwiner over the fraction field by the characteristic-polynomial identity and irreducibility, or by the trace pairing on that matrix algebra. Multiply it by a scalar so that the entries are integral and at least one survives reduction. Its residual kernel is an invariant proper subspace of an irreducible representation, hence zero. Equal dimensions imply the reduced intertwiner is invertible. Its determinant is therefore a unit of $O_{0,i}$, so the normalized intertwiner lies in $\mathrm{GL}_r(O_{0,i})$. This proves the required self-duality **on the stored lattice**. ## Nonvanishing at empty auxiliary support

For a toric label $\lambda$, the stored comparison selects indices $j,a$, a positive integer $h$, an embedding $\iota$, and a scalar $u\ne0$. It gives
$$
   s_2(i,\lambda)=2h,\qquad
   1\le h\le N_{\rm fun}+1,\qquad
   \sum_\delta\theta_{i,\varnothing,\lambda,\delta}=u\,\iota(T_0)
$$
after embedding the left-hand side in the same local algebraic closure. Here $N_{\rm fun}=k+m(t+s_{\rm fun}+1)$, and $T_0$ is the base-conductor toric sum for $\lambda_0=\mu_{0,j}\delta_{\ell_i}\chi_a^{-1}$. Summing over $\delta$ collects every order class once, since the fibers of the class map partition the finite order class group. The toric identity identifies $T_0$ with the unitary Hecke $L$-value at $h$ with the conductor and normalization fixed in that identity. At every chosen embedding the first-pass grid gives $\kappa=\lfloor e_j\rfloor+c_a-h\ge1$, using the integrality of $e_j$. The angular exponent of $\lambda_0$ in this CM type is $\kappa+h=e_j+c_a$, hence even at $h=1$ the character and its square have nonzero angular type. [Range for $h$][range] · [Angular exponent and strict grid inequality][angular] · [Characters, scalar and class-map fibers][toric] · [First-pass toric normalization](FIRST_PASS_TORIC_COEFFICIENTS.md)

For $h>1$ the Euler product gives nonvanishing. At $h=1$, remove the finitely many bad Euler factors; all removed factors are nonzero there. For real $x>1$, the logarithm of
$$
  \zeta_E^S(x)^3\,|L^S(x,\lambda_0)|^4\,|L^S(x,\lambda_0^2)|
$$
is a sum of nonnegative terms by $3+4\cos\theta+\cos2\theta=2(1+\cos\theta)^2$. If $L^S(1,\lambda_0)=0$, its fourth power would cancel the at-most-third-order pole from $\zeta_E^S(x)^3$ and force this product to tend to zero. The square character has nonzero angular infinity type too, so $L^S(x,\lambda_0^2)$ is holomorphic at one by Hecke continuation. The product is at least one, giving a contradiction. Thus the prescribed empty-support sum is nonzero at every label. This uses only base-conductor values, including the endpoint, and supplies no estimate on the valuations at new conductors. [Toric sum and nonvanishing at the prescribed integer][empty]

The endpoint calculation and the Hodge gap, integral duality and size bounds prove all seven conjuncts of $\mathrm{SetHyp}_i$, the first being the tautology in its definition. The proof applies to every nonzero index, whether or not it is selected by the origin’s representative rule. The remaining first-export assertion requires, in particular, one auxiliary character at each required accuracy and avoidance set whose every power prime to $\ell_i$ satisfies the simultaneous bounds.
