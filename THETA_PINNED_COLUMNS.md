# The initial geometric core and the eventual theta construction on a prescribed column

Take the original arithmetic stages, their marked pair of primes over $g_0,g_1$, and their embedding $e:L\hookrightarrow\mathbf C$. The initial geometric core can be constructed on these stages. Under the analytic hypotheses of the [fixed-stage construction](THETA_FIXED_STAGE_REALIZATION.md), in the eventual range of the host and coefficient-field requests, **every supplied pinned column** with the required group identifications and volume has a fine theta datum. It gives both coefficient readings and the separating tests at both levels. This is the assertion called `DatExS`. Its quantifier over columns is universal. The [first-pass construction](FIRST_PASS_COLUMN.md) produces an initial column. The [extension theorem](THETA_FINE_EXTENSION.md) preserves a prescribed old fine datum satisfying its lower-row laws when the coefficient field changes. [Exact fine-datum assertion and its enlargement counterpart][stat]

## Rational boundary components on the marked family

Fix an active row $j$, write $r=p_j+q_j$ and $\rho=q_j$, and use its actual genus representative with period Gram matrix $G$. The distinguished period convention gives $\rho$ positive signs, $r-\rho$ negative signs and $2\rho<r$. These hold on every component and at all places of the chosen CM type. We need a totally isotropic *rational* $\rho$-frame in each component. A real flag by itself would give no Fourier lattice over $E$. [Stage signatures and trace pairing](PEL_STAGE_PARAMETERS.md) · [Required rational frame and exponent laws][cusp]

Start with the diagonal sign matrix $S$ of this signature. Set $d=\det(G)/\det(S)\in F^\times$, where $F=E^+$. The signs of the two determinants agree at each real place, so $d$ is totally positive. Pair $\rho$ positive entries of $S$ with $\rho$ negative entries. Pick one of the remaining entries, which exists by the strict inequality, and multiply it by $d$. The new matrix has exactly the determinant of $G$ and the same signatures. Local classification at finite places and Landherr’s local-global theorem give a rational isometry between them. In the diagonal model take the sum of the two coordinate vectors in each pair. These $\rho$ sums have disjoint supports, vanish under all mutual pairings, and are linearly independent. Carry them through the isometry to obtain the required ordered rational cusp frame. At the definite endpoint $\rho=0$ the empty frame works. No condition on a basis of the *integral* lattice is needed to choose this frame. [Gross, §3 and Theorem 3.1](https://people.math.harvard.edu/~gross/preprints/incoherent.pdf#page=6) · [Source determinant correction and isotropic vectors][diag]

For each such cusp, and for every other rational cusp frame on the same component, intersect its rational span with the actual component lattice. In an adapted pseudobasis write the intersection as $\bigoplus_i\mathfrak a_i e_i$ and let $\mathfrak D=\mathfrak D_{E/\mathbf Q}$. Denote the component's imaginary polarization scalar by $\xi$. A central skew-Hermitian translation $\beta$ lies in the component group precisely when
$$
      \beta_{ij}/\xi\in M\mathfrak a_i\mathfrak D\bar{\mathfrak a}_j,
      \qquad M=g_0g_1.
$$
Indeed the displacement of a lattice vector must lie in $M$ times the cusp intersection, by the principal-level condition. Trace self-duality gives the displayed independent coefficient conditions. They define a full lattice in the space of skew-Hermitian matrices, of integral rank $[F:\mathbf Q]\rho^2$. For a Hermitian frequency $H$ the normalized phase is, up to orientation, one half the absolute trace of $\sum_{ij}H_{ij}\beta_{ij}/\xi$. Testing opposite off-diagonal entries in conjugate pairs and a diagonal entry $s+\bar s$ gives the separate trace-dual conditions. In the reverse direction the sum lies in $F\cap\mathfrak D^{-1}$, and the host supplies $w_0\in\mathcal O_E$ with $w_0+\bar w_0=1$. Its half-trace is therefore integral. The condition is precisely
$$
     M\sum_{ij}u_i H_{ij}\bar v_j\in\mathcal O_E
       \qquad\bigl(u,v\in\textstyle\prod_i\mathfrak D\mathfrak a_i\bigr).
$$
This proves the Fourier translation and frequency laws with the level of the section on this component, including the places above two. The basis-independent calculation is PUN16–19. [Center lattice and integral phase](principal_uniformization_v48/main.tex#L677-L906) · [Normalized charts on the same families](normalized_frame_laws_v51/main.tex#L1210-L1277)

## Assembling the initial core

Use the [stage's coefficient field and moduli data](PEL_STAGE_PARAMETERS.md), its [unitary components and principal level](PEL_ADELIC_COMPONENTS.md), and the [section and integral-frame laws](PEL_SECTION_LAWS.md). For a good embedding and active row use the signed orthogonal reference frame in its adelic dictionary. Use the previously constructed framed point for unused indices or embeddings. Choose the initial determinant character, adelic multiplier, and every component scalar to be one. Then the conjugation law has the coefficient witness $1$ at every embedding. The half-level and the model and basepoint clauses use precisely the completed marked lattice and original coordinate map. The trace pairing has comparison scalar $+1$ in the original lattice coordinates. In period coordinates both Gram convention and imaginary scalar have been changed as recorded in the component calculation. The initial character is sufficient here since the twist-dependent multipliers are installed after this core is chosen. [The initial choice and its output][init] · [Geometric, character and frame laws][joint]

The quotient measure used on sections comes from Haar measure with the source’s self-dual-lattice and real density normalizations. In positive rank Harris–Li–Skinner §0.2, (0.2.1) gives Tamagawa number $2$, and GV9 converts this to the positive finite mass for the prescribed density. In rank zero the quotient is a point of mass one. The normalized quotient probability has full support since the countably many rational translates of a null fundamental-domain slice cannot cover a nonempty Haar-open preimage. The adelic reading of a cusp section is bounded on the orthogonal frames by its bounded invariant norm, and is continuous on the quotient. It is therefore square-integrable, and zero squared norm forces pointwise vanishing. Every admissible frame is represented up to the arithmetic and triangular actions. Their invertible weight factors propagate vanishing, and injectivity of chart evaluation gives zero section. Finally
$$
       |\tau(\xi)|=\frac{1}{\mathrm N(\mathfrak c_K)\sqrt d}
$$
for all $\tau$, since $\xi$ is the period-sign choice of the stage scalar $-1/(\mathrm N(\mathfrak c_K)\sqrt{-d})$. This verifies the same scale in the Lie normalization on every component. These arguments establish the core norms before one compares a separately stored numerical volume with the quotient volume. The latter equality is an explicit hypothesis in the eventual fine-datum theorem. [Harris–Li–Skinner, §0.2](https://ems.press/content/book-chapter-files/27474#page=8) · [Conversion and quotient probability](geometric_volumes/main.tex#L357-L416) · [Reading and full-support argument](THETA_MARKED_FRAME_COMPARISON.md) · [Source construction without the numerical volume equality][novol]

Together these choices give the common evaluation and integral tests, the model and signed reference frame, the quotient norm, the cusp multiplication, and the three boundary clauses on the same family. In particular the ordered primes remain the supplied pair, and the parameters remain the canonical ones. Its algebra $F_O=e(K_s)\to L$ is the inverse of $e$ on this image. The character-field calculation now applies to this *constructed* core. For any admissible untwisted level choice, principal idèles, weak approximation and the even infinity exponents put every character value at an idèle of trivial infinite part in this same $F_O$. ## One eventual range before the columns

The host estimates choose the small parameter and the thresholds on $c_D,c_h,x$ in the source's nested order. They give standard lattice frames, positive definite source spaces, interlacing and the required real weight gaps for **every** stage over the admitted context. Its rank specification gives $n_V=8fr+4+\mathrm{TOv}(s)\mathrm{nWv}(D.R,s)$, hence the stable range for the rational tests. Intersecting a finite number of these conditions takes the minimum admissible small parameter, the componentwise maximum of constant bounds, and successive maxima of thresholds after their permitted earlier arguments have been fixed. [Host estimates](THETA_COEFFICIENT_EXTENSION.md) · [Stable rank and selected primes](continuation_v77/stage.tex#L3-L66) · [Quantifiers of the eventual range][range]

Make the finite coefficient requests before choosing a column. Roots for the finitely many ramified character primes give the local additive characters. The finite Hecke splitting and Weber requests for the subsequent tests can be included in the same last finite union, a function of the prehost, frame, two specified small fields and twist-character set. A pinned column has the required prime contractions, common half and character range. Its exact small-field and character-value pins put the base-character values in the field used above. The set of coefficient places over the specified twist primes is finite and conjugation-stable. All these assertions hold simultaneously for the allowed levels of that column. [The requests in dependency order](continuation_v77/request.tex#L3-L106) · [Formal order of fields and choices][large]

Now fix any such column $w$, any permitted later stages $Z$, and a field witness, under the group's two comparison hypotheses and the stipulated numerical volume equality. Apply the [decorated-lattice readings](THETA_DECORATED_HECKE.md) and the [preliminary construction](BASE_THETA_CONSTRUCTION_STEP.md) with this core. Use the [eight maps](THETA_FIXED_STAGE_REALIZATION.md) on the weak data. The [phase and frame calculation](THETA_MARKED_FRAME_COMPARISON.md) puts the geometric evaluations in the actual models with reference phase one. Q77Z2 installs the final coordinates, applies the eight-map result again to those stored choices, and carries the preliminary level, group and reading equalities across. Its output $z$ simultaneously has the eight maps, the geometric comparison, the pinned core, and both preliminary arithmetic readings. These are the four clauses of $\mathrm{Fine}(z)$. Q77Z1 on this very $z$ gives the integral cusp lattice and its separating rational tests at each of the two levels. [Fine record and tests](continuation_v77/column.tex#L3-L100) · [Pinned assembly and exact order of the two applications][fine]

The field requests and thresholds preceded $w$. Restricting the resulting assertion to the good contexts and additional base hypotheses in the definition of $\mathrm{StepW}$ proves the stated $\mathrm{DatExS}$. An independent application to an enlarged column produces some fine datum there. The next assertion $\mathrm{DatExt}$ additionally prescribes an *old* fine datum satisfying its operator laws and requires a new one extending that particular datum. It compares distinguished realizations, frames and integral rows, and the nonzero-index places. The [extension proof](THETA_FINE_EXTENSION.md) makes these identifications for the prescribed old datum, including when the old intermediate field is contained in the new one. The two-pass construction has the additional task of supplying the pinned columns and linking a bundle at the requested size to the previously chosen reciprocity record.
