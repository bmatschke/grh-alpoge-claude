# The core theta norm and the prescribed level in bottom-row separation

On the restricted second-pass grid, the core ranks force the convergent Siegel–Weil identity at every upward arrow. In the global doubling quotient the only factors evaluated at the edge of absolute convergence are explicitly identified degree-one Hecke values. Both facts hold for every bottom spherical eigensystem satisfying the project's elementary Satake bound. For the separate detection of a bottom form, the prescribed level is deep enough to make the identity orbit dominate once the archimedean coefficient and the lower bound for lattice minima have been obtained. These deductions apply with the grid fixed before the row and its smooth vector. [Restricted realization](SECOND_PASS_BOTTOM_TESTS.md) · [Statement of B8.15][b15]

## Which Siegel–Weil identity occurs

Let $m$ be the length of the chain, $r=k+m-1$ the bottom row rank, $n=r+1$ the bottom **core** rank and $d=r+5=n+4$ the increase in core rank. At the arrow $i\to i+1$ put $a=n_i=n+id$ and $b=n_{i+1}=a+d$. At each infinite place the negative index of the target core is $\lfloor(m-i)/2\rfloor$. A rational totally isotropic subspace stays totally isotropic after extension to a real place, where projection to the negative subspace is injective. Thus its rational Witt index $q_i$ satisfies $q_i\le\lfloor(m-i)/2\rfloor<d$ and $b-q_i>a$. [Grid signatures][sig] · [Native core ranks][rk]

Use Yamana's Theorem 2 with division algebra $E$, index $\delta=1$, Hermitian dimension $b$, and doubled skew-Hermitian space $V_i\oplus(-V_i)$ of split rank $a$. The section is the flat section with value at $s_0=d/2$ given by evaluating the doubled Weil action on the doubled test at zero in its Schrödinger model. A rational polarization takes the finite tests to Schwartz–Bruhat functions and the polynomial Fock tests to Schwartz functions. The splitting character in normalized induction restricts to $\eta_{E/F}^b$. Yamana gives absolute convergence of the theta integral and an Eisenstein value equal to its quotient **average**, with coefficient one. For an arbitrary Haar measure multiply the average by the covolume to recover the integral. At $i=0$, $b=2a+4$ and the defining Eisenstein series itself converges absolutely. For $i\ge1$, $2a-b=n+(i-1)d>0$ and the holomorphic value of its continuation is used. This proves the global Siegel–Weil input on these tests in both ranges. [Yamana, Theorem 2, pp. 236–237][yam] · [Project section and ranges][sw] · [Polarization and convergence][pol]

## The global factor in the upward norm

Write $S$ for the core exceptional places of $F$, and omit the places above them from products for $E$. Write $\chi_i=\widetilde\chi_i$ in the chain convention and $B(w)$ for the base-change multiset of the conjugate bottom eigenvector. The grid gives unitary $\chi_i$, unramified outside $S$, with mutually distinct angular exponents. The spherical calculation gives $|B(w)|\le n$ and $|\beta|\le (Nw)^{(n-1)/2}$ for each $\beta\in B(w)$. In the normalized spherical chart, give the hyperspecial compact volume one, pair the spherical vectors to one, and take the section of value one there. Eischen–Harris–Li–Skinner §4.2.1 gives the local integral in both the split and inert cases. Its denominator has factors $L_v(2s+n_i-l,\eta_v^{b+l})$, $0\le l<n_i$. The twist of the old roots is $\chi_i^{-1}$ in the conjugate convention. The [inert vacuum calculation](SECOND_PASS_BOTTOM_TESTS.md#a-literature-calculation-inside-the-separation-theorem) and its split counterpart adjoin the fixed length-$d$ segment at each prior arrow. [Spherical parameters][sat] · [Character data][chr] · [Local doubling, (53)][ehls] · [Global numerator and denominator][prod]

Let $H_i(s)/D_i^S(s)$ denote the resulting global quotient in B8.15c. Put $\kappa_{ji}=\chi_j\chi_i^{-1}$ and $A_K=\prod_{k=2}^\infty\zeta_K(k)$ for a number field $K$. The latter converges since $\zeta_K(k)\le\zeta(k)^{[K:\mathbb Q]}$ and $\sum_{k\ge2}(\zeta(k)-1)<\infty$. At $s_0$, remove the endpoint values and write
$$
 W_i=
 \frac{H_i(s_0)}
 {D_i^S(s_0)\prod_{j<i}L_E^S(1,\kappa_{ji})}.
$$
Then
$$
 \left(\frac{\zeta_E(6)}{\zeta_E(3)}\right)^n A_E^{-i}A_F^{-1}
 \ \le\ |W_i|\ \le\ \zeta_E(3)^n A_E^i A_F .
$$

Indeed, each old root times its Euler variable has modulus at most $(Nw)^{-3}$, since $(s_0+1/2)-(n-1)/2=3$. Its inverse Euler factor in absolute value lies between $(1+(Nw)^{-3})^{-1}$ and $(1-(Nw)^{-3})^{-1}$. Multiplication gives the displayed bottom bounds, with the lower bound valid even if Euler factors are omitted or some multisets have fewer than $n$ entries. A previous segment gives the arguments $d-l$, $0\le l<d$, exactly once each. For $k=2,\ldots,d$ a partial unitary character value has modulus between $\zeta_E(k)^{-1}$ and $\zeta_E(k)$. The denominator at $s_0$ runs from $d+1$ to $d+n_i$, again without repetition. Its inverse contributes between $A_F^{-1}$ and $A_F$. These estimates need no temperedness assumption.

For $k=1$, both $\kappa_{ji}$ and its square have a nonzero angular exponent, so their Hecke functions are holomorphic at one. To see nonvanishing directly, for $\sigma>1$ expand logarithms in
$$
 \zeta_E^S(\sigma)^3
 |L_E^S(\sigma,\kappa_{ji})|^4
 |L_E^S(\sigma,\kappa_{ji}^2)|\ge1.
$$
Each prime-power coefficient is nonnegative by $3+4\cos\theta+\cos(2\theta)=2(1+\cos\theta)^2$. A zero at one for the middle function would force the product to tend to zero, since the zeta pole is simple. Thus the global quotient is finite and nonzero on each rung. This proves qualitative nonvanishing for all these eigensystems, with a quantitative estimate for the factors away from the endpoints. It uses no lower bound uniform in the family for the endpoint values. [Hecke continuation and local omissions][hecke] · [Native segment calculation][seg]

## How the prescribed depth pays for detection

The adjoint-functor reconstruction gives an explicit proof of B8.15b at the actual principal level. Its formula agrees with the pinned definitions. Write $f=[F:\mathbb Q]$, $b_0$ for the grid weight bound, and $q_0$ for its auxiliary split rational prime. At the selected prime above $q_0$ the modulus $\mathfrak m$ has exponent
$$
 1+\left\lfloor\frac{C_F f^2n^6
  \log(b_0 n g_0g_1p_{\rm de}p'_{\rm de}|\Delta_E|)}{\log q_0}\right\rfloor .
$$
At the other selected split places the depth is two. Thus $N=N\mathfrak m$ satisfies $\log N>C_Ff^2n^6\log b_0$. These places and exponents are chosen before the bottom multiplicity map $t$ and the smooth tensor $w$. [Reader, proposed detection and exact depth][reader] · [Native depth][depth] · [Places][places]

Here is the numerical implication used by that argument. Write $L=\log b_0\ge1$, $Q=Cfn^4L$ for the loss of its archimedean test, and $Y=r^2f g_0g_1\operatorname{rd}(E)$. On the row size range $f,m\le n$, $\log Y\le n^3+21L$. Choose $s=\tfrac12(2e^QY)^{-m}$. Then $e^QY(2s)^{1/m}=1/2$. To get $R=s^f\sqrt N\ge 2e^{Q+(6n+9)f}$ it suffices that
$$
 (1+f)\log2+Q+(6n+9)f+fm(\log2+Q+\log Y)
 \le \tfrac12\log N .
$$
For $n\ge15$ the left side is at most $(2C+50)f^2n^5L$ by substituting the three bounds above. Hence $n\ge(4C+100)/C_F$ suffices. Eventual growth of $n$ allows this single threshold independent of the row and vector. If the unfolded identity term is $cu(x_*)$ with $|u(x_*)|>\|u\|_\infty/2$, and the nonidentity absolute sum is bounded by $|c|\|u\|_\infty e^{Q+(6n+9)f}/R$, the identity term survives. This verifies the size and level implication in the reader's proposed construction for $C_F\ge6510$. On the admitted Hida grid the [branching proof](../construction/ROW_CORE_BRANCHING.md) gives the reproducing coefficient, transverse decay and its first derivative with $C=10872$. Cuspidality along the short unipotent direction and that derivative give the lattice minimum by the [short-vector argument](../construction/ROW_TEST_CONVERGENCE.md#detecting-a-row-by-a-poincar%C3%A9-series). [Reader's choice of scale][reader-scale] · [Roadmap identity term and nonidentity estimates][tail]

## First occurrence and the norm on a whole Hecke eigenspace

At an even-rank core arrow use the signed, centered parameter of the smaller group. Its old entries have modulus at least $(d+3)/2$. The splitting exponents agree, and the new string $(d-1)/2,\ldots,-(d-1)/2$ lies in one compact block of the larger group. There are two distinct first-occurrence checks.

* Upward, the old parameter has no entries $\pm1/2$. In the equal-parity convention of Ichino §6.1 the string invariant is $k_\pi=0$. Write $r_\pi,s_\pi$ for the counts of positive entries on the positive side plus negative entries on the negative side, and the complementary count, in the oriented frame. The successive signatures in the chosen real Witt tower are $(r_\pi+d+l,s_\pi+l)$. In Theorem 6.1(ii) take $t=d/2>0$. At $l=0$ both test sets $C_\pi^\pm(d/2)$ are empty by the bound on the old entries. For $l<0$ the necessary inequality $l\ge k_\pi$ fails. For the opposite orientation, Ichino's Remark 6.2 interchanges the signatures and replaces the representation by its conjugate with the prescribed splitting-character twist.
* Downward, for the larger parameter the string invariant is at most $d$, since the entries $\pm(d+1)/2$ are absent. A target strictly below the smaller core in its Witt tower would require a drop $k\ge d+2$. Corollary 6.5 permits only $k\le k_\pi$ or the exceptional $k=k_\pi+2$ subject to the three conditions of Theorem 6.1. The exceptional case could only have $k_\pi=d$ and requires both missing entries. Thus all smaller lifts vanish in this direction as well. The nonzero pairing in the actual two ranks follows from Lemma 8.2 and Proposition 7.1 in the orientation of the [core computation](../construction/ROW_TEST_CONVERGENCE.md#the-real-zeta-integral-on-the-prescribed-core-coset).

Any rational lower Witt member gives a lower real Witt member. The constant-term formula and cuspidality of the input now imply cuspidality of the lifts in the two indicated directions. At the finite depth places the core coset is a surjection modulo the depth ideal. Two points in that coset which differ by a source-group element force that element to be the identity modulo the same ideal. Thus the oscillator autocoefficient is supported on the prescribed principal compact subgroup. The real test can vary without changing this support. [Centered core lists and first occurrence][reader-core] · [Ichino, Theorem 6.1, Corollary 6.5 and Proposition 7.1][ichino] · [Coset proof and archimedean tests](../construction/ROW_TEST_CONVERGENCE.md)

Fix a nonzero multiplicity map $u$ in a single spherical Hecke eigenspace. Choose a real tensor $v$ and Fock polynomial with nonzero real zeta pairing as above, and put $F=u(v)$. At the allowed finite level and a real argument $g_\infty$, Schur's lemma gives the Petersson coefficient $\|u\|^2\langle\pi_\infty(g_\infty)v,v\rangle$ in its coefficient convention. The semilocal integral therefore has the nonzero real pairing times $\|u\|^2$, the squared norms of the nonzero finite tests and positive Haar factors. At a good finite place with compact group $K_v$ of volume one, compress a translation $R(g_v)$ by the orthogonal compact projector $e_v$ on both sides. The operator $e_vR(g_v)e_v$ is the normalized characteristic measure of $K_vg_vK_v$ and acts on this whole eigenspace by its spherical coefficient. Operators at distinct places commute. Applying this identity at the finite support of a good-place translation factors its coefficient into the scalar spherical factors and the semilocal coefficient. In particular multiplicities or cross terms within the eigenlabel introduce no new local nonvanishing condition. [Independent reader's eigenspace argument][reader-norm] · [Local doubling, (53)][ehls]

Unfold the cuspidal doubling pairing in its right half-plane of absolute convergence. The proper-orbit terms integrate a constant term of the cusp form and vanish. On the open orbit the preceding compression gives $H_i(s)Z_{S,\infty}(s)/D_i^S(s)$. For the chosen depth coset the exceptional finite part is compact; the real doubled section has bound $C h(g)^{b/2}$ at $s_0$, where $0<h(g)\le1$. After multiplication by $h(g)^{s-s_0}$ it is bounded on $\Re s\ge0$. More precisely, $b/2-s_0=a/2$, so on $\Re s\ge0$ the integrand is bounded by $C h(g)^{a/2}|\langle\pi(g)v,v\rangle|$. The discrete-series coefficient is square integrable and $h^a$ is integrable by the Cartan-coordinate bound. Cauchy–Schwarz gives an integrable majorant independent of $s$, hence $Z_{S,\infty}$ is holomorphic on $\Re s>0$. The cuspidal pairing with the continued Eisenstein family also continues by rapid decrease and uniform moderate growth. At $s_0$ the convergent Siegel–Weil identity identifies this pairing with the squared norm of the upward theta lift divided by the target covolume. This is the convergent-range Rallis identity with the same doubled Schwartz vector and conjugate input, extended to a Hecke label by the preceding calculation. It gives
$$
 \|\Theta^{\rm up}F\|^2=
 \operatorname{vol}[U(V_{i+1})]\,
 \frac{H_i(s_0)}{D_i^S(s_0)}\, Z_{S,\infty}(s_0)\ne0.
$$
The real bound, pole-free value and nonzero semilocal coefficient have all been checked on this test. By Petersson adjunction this upward value is a value of $A_i^*u$, where $A_i$ is the fixed downward map of multiplicity spaces. Indeed pairing the upward value with a target occurrence gives the product of its downward multiplicity pairing with $u$ and the local real intertwiner pairing on the chosen vectors. Thus $A_i^*u\ne0$. At each successive arrow this applies to the transferred eigenlabel; adding the same string to two multisets preserves their distinction. The composite adjoint is therefore injective on the bottom multiplicity space, and the downward composite is onto it by finite dimensionality. This proves the required core-chain assertion on the admitted grid, allowing arbitrary multiplicities in each Hecke label. [Rallis inner-product formula, §8.2][ichino] · [Gan–Qiu–Takeda, §§11.3–11.5][rallis] · [Eigenlabel argument and adjunction][norm]

## One real test after weak detection

Suppose the prescribed columns and finite coset tests weakly detect every nonzero $t$ at a given $w\ne0$, and the resulting periods are continuous in the terminal smooth archimedean vectors. Jiang–Sun–Zhu's Theorem A applies to the terminal discrete series and the irreducible coefficient dual of the compact group. The project has a flag of isotropic lines, a distinguished negative line and a positive definite remaining middle space. Its character is nontrivial on each simple flag coordinate. These give their Bessel subgroup and generic character. In each real factor the continuous equivariant forms therefore span at most one line. The theorem imposes no numerical gap for this uniqueness step. Weak detection of one row shows each line is nonzero. Pick generators and local arguments on which they take value one. With finite data fixed, evaluate at those arguments successively. The global functional on the terminal tensor factors as their product $\ell$ times one scalar $c_{t,\Psi,\Phi}$. This works on pure tensors and extends linearly to the project's smooth tensor space. [Jiang–Sun–Zhu, Theorem A and §2][jsz] · [Terminal group and character][terminal]

Choose a weak witness for one nonzero row, so $\ell(\xi,v_\rho(w))\ne0$. Each real Fock polynomial is a finite linear combination of monomials in its chosen frame. Expand also $\xi$ in any fixed basis of the permitted coefficient dual. Multilinearity, or conjugate linearity where the kernel is conjugated, shows that a single tuple of monomials and one basis covector still has nonzero evaluation. Fix that pair. A weak witness for any other nonzero $t$ forces its scalar $c_{t,\Psi,\Phi}$ to be nonzero for some finite data. The fixed real pair therefore detects that $t$ with those data. At most $\dim\mathcal E_0$ such finite tests suffice for the entire bottom row space, by successively decreasing the dimension of their common kernel. If the space is zero the separation assertion is vacuous. This chooses a pair for each $w$ without a new field request or an asserted bound on the monomial degrees. [Allowed real polynomials and cosets][tests] · [Factorization on the terminal tensor][factor]

On a restricted fine datum take the temporary Hida stage of the [bottom-row construction](SECOND_PASS_BOTTOM_TESTS.md#obtain-the-tests-without-a-prior-fitted-row). Its pins, exact maps, frame and true finite group give the level characters, residue idempotents and finite series of the base index. The separation uses the reference column of the grid. Enlarging the catalogue by other tuple characters leaves this column and all of its depth tests available. The depth primes must satisfy the host coprimalities with $Q_S q\,N\mathfrak c_K$. In particular on the actual stage $p_{\rm de},p'_{\rm de}$ are both in the level set prime to $Q_S$, split away from $q$, and prime to the CM conductor. The normalized grid has the two marked levels and the auxiliary prime with their own coprimalities. Apply the preceding estimates at that level. The [mixed-model period ascent](../theta/ROW_PERIOD_ASCENT.md) on its standard auxiliary frames carries one reachable core period to the prescribed top cosets. The common real test then gives B8.15 on this realization. The argument uses the standing global Weil and mixed-model identities, local theta correspondence, cuspidal reduction theory and finite-level models throughout. The separation is a deduction on these inputs for each eligible stage. Producing all the stages and obtaining arithmetic values on the other tuples involve additional arithmetic and geometric assertions.
