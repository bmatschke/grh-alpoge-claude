\section{The discrete series on the determinant double cover}

% BEGIN D90S1
\paragraph{D90S1. The two sets of parameters are related by a character.}
Let $n=p+q\ge1$. The group in this input is
\[
 \widetilde G=\{(g,z):g\in U(p,q),\ z^2=\det g\}.
\]
Indeed the two specified homomorphisms give a bijection onto this
group and an embedding into the ambient matrix group times
$\mathbb C^\times$. This identifies both the topology and the
compact subgroup $\widetilde K$, the preimage of $U(p)\times U(q)$.
The prescribed exponential along $tX$ is the unique continuous lift
of $\exp(tX)$ through the identity. Its second coordinate is
$\exp(t\operatorname{tr}X/2)$.

The square root gives a unitary character $\chi(g,z)=z$. In an
irreducible unitary representation, the central involution $(1,-1)$
acts by a single sign, since its two eigenspaces are closed and
invariant. The representations with sign $+1$ descend to $U(p,q)$.
Those with sign $-1$ descend after tensoring by $\chi^{-1}$. A
finite covering pushes Haar measure to a Haar measure, and twisting
by a unitary character preserves the absolute values of matrix
coefficients. Consequently this gives two copies of the ordinary
discrete series. It also treats the formal degree in the source's
Hilbert-space definition. Discrete-series Schur orthogonality gives
\[
 \int_{\widetilde G}|\langle y,\pi(g)x\rangle|^2\,dg
       =d_\pi^{-1}\|x\|^2\|y\|^2
\]
with $d_\pi>0$ for the specified Haar measure. Conversely any
representation in the source has a nonzero square-integrable
coefficient by this identity and its nonzero Hilbert space.

In the ordinary Harish-Chandra parametrization one chooses $n$
distinct coordinates in $\mathbb Z+(n-1)/2$, decreasing within
each of the two blocks. These conditions choose one representative
of each compact Weyl-group orbit. See Harris--Kobayashi--Speh,
\emph{Translation functors, branching problems, and applications to the
restriction of coherent cohomology of Shimura varieties},
\S2.3, (2.22)--(2.24), for the unitary-group convention.
The differential of $\chi$ is $\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}$, so
tensoring by $\chi$ adds $1/2$ to every coordinate, including the
central coordinate of the infinitesimal character. The union of the
two lists consists precisely of the regular block-decreasing tuples
for which $2\lambda_a\in\mathbb Z$ and all
$\lambda_a-\lambda_b\in\mathbb Z$.

In each Hilbert realization take its $\widetilde K$-finite vectors
with their derived complex Lie-algebra action. They are smooth by
admissibility of the discrete series. Their compact orbits span
finite-dimensional modules and split into torus weights. Indeed
the diagonal torus of the cover has characters generated by its
ordinary coordinate characters and $\chi$. Twisting by $\chi^{-1}$
descends any character nontrivial on $(1,-1)$. All weights are
therefore rational as required. This supplies the source's
Harish-Chandra module, its inclusion and the specified differentiation
on every real Lie-algebra direction. Choosing one such representation
for every parameter gives a family exhausting all the given discrete
series up to unitary intertwiner.
% END D90S1

% BEGIN D90S2
\paragraph{D90S2. The minimal compact type and the cone of all types.}
Order the coordinates by decreasing $\lambda$. The positive roots
are $e_a-e_b$ for $\lambda_a>\lambda_b$. Those within one block
are compact and those between the blocks are noncompact. Denote
their respective half-sums by $\rho_c$ and $\rho_n$. The $a$th
coordinate of a half-sum is half the number of roots beginning at
$a$ minus half the number ending there. Thus the weight called
$\mathrm{blattner}(\lambda)$ in the source is exactly
$\mu_0=\lambda+\rho_n-\rho_c$.

Atiyah--Schmid, \emph{A geometric construction of the discrete
series for semisimple Lie groups}, Theorem 8.5, gives both facts
needed here. The compact type of highest weight $\mu_0$ occurs
with multiplicity one, and every other compact highest weight is
$\mu_0$ plus a sum of positive roots. To apply their semisimple
theorem for $pq>0$, use the finite central surjection
$U(1)\times SU(p,q)\to U(p,q)$. The scalar circle acts by a
character. The remaining factor acts irreducibly, and its matrix
coefficients are square-integrable because the circle is compact.
Restriction from $U(p)\times U(q)$ to $S(U(p)\times U(q))$
keeps each compact type irreducible. The common circle character
fixes its missing central coordinate. Thus the multiplicities and
root differences given by Theorem 8.5 on the derived group give the
stated assertions on $U(p,q)$. Twisting by $\chi$ translates
every weight and its parameter by the same vector, preserving all
root comparisons. If $pq=0$, the whole group is compact and the
usual highest-weight theorem gives its sole compact type of weight
$\lambda-\rho_c$, including when $n=1$.

The simultaneous kernel of the positive compact matrix units at
weight $\mu$ is the sum of the highest-weight lines of copies of
that compact type. Consequently it has dimension one at $\mu_0$.
For any nonzero highest-weight vector of weight $\mu$, count how
often $e_a-e_b$ occurs in the sum supplied by Theorem 8.5. This gives
the nonnegative integer coefficients for the exact cone in the source.
% END D90S2

% BEGIN D90S3
\paragraph{D90S3. The Casimir normalization and the full classification input.}
The quadratic operator used here is the Casimir of the trace form
on $\mathfrak{gl}_n$, namely $C=\sum_{a,b}E_{ab}E_{ba}$. We include
its elementary normalization since the scalar direction matters
when passing to the double cover. In the order given by
$\lambda$, let $h$ be the highest weight of a Verma module. For a
positive root $e_a-e_b$, the contribution of the two off-diagonal
terms on a highest-weight vector is $h_a-h_b$, by
$[E_{ab},E_{ba}]=E_{aa}-E_{bb}$. The diagonal terms contribute
$\sum_a h_a^2$. Hence the central character at
$\lambda=h+\rho$, with $\rho=\rho_c+\rho_n$, sends $C$ to
\[
  \sum_a h_a^2+2\langle h,\rho\rangle
     =\sum_a\lambda_a^2-\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}
                             \bigl((n-1)/2-j\bigr)^2 .
\]
The Harish-Chandra parameter specifies the same infinitesimal
character on the discrete series. This computation uses the Verma
module only to evaluate a central element. In particular the
scalar holds on every compact-finite vector of each representation
in D90S1, including the determinant twist.

D90S1 provides a single exhaustive family indexed by all of the
required parameters for each prescribed group. D90S2 and the
Casimir calculation give all three required conditions on its
members. Together they prove the original
$\mathrm{Published.DiscreteSeriesCover}$ at every positive rank.

\paragraph{D90S4. Existence with prescribed lowest compact type.}
Two further existence statements recognize the parameter by the
compact type minimizing the squared Vogan norm
$\|\mu+2\rho_c\|^2$. For the representation just constructed
this follows directly from the root cone. Write $\mu=\mu_0+A$ as
in D90S2. Since $\mu_0+2\rho_c=\lambda+\rho$ and
$\lambda+\rho$ pairs strictly positively with every positive
root, we have
\[
 \|\mu+2\rho_c\|^2-\|\mu_0+2\rho_c\|^2
   =2\langle\lambda+\rho,A\rangle+\|A\|^2\ge0,
\]
with equality only at $A=0$. This proves the required minimum
for the double cover.

For the ordinary unitary group the input gives a decreasing
$\lambda$ in the ordinary lattice and an arbitrary ordered list
of signs specifying the Hermitian form. Group the positive and
negative coordinates into their two blocks, retaining the order
inside each, and transport the representation from D90S1 by this
permutation. Every coefficient lies in $L^2$ by Schur orthogonality.
Extend the action by zero off the unitary group if it is written as a
function on all complex matrices. The representation laws and
strong continuity in the statement are imposed on the unitary group.
The source recognizes a compact highest weight as an occurring
torus weight dominating every other torus weight in its finite
irreducible compact subspace by a sum of positive compact roots.
This is exactly the compact highest-weight theorem. Thus D90S2
supplies the required subspace and the displayed inequality treats
every competing compact type.

Both existence statements also allow $p=q=0$. Then the ordinary
group is trivial and its determinant double cover has order two.
In each case the trivial representation on $\mathbb C$ has the
empty highest weight, finite Haar integrals, and squared Vogan
norm zero. This completes both
$\mathrm{Published.DiscreteSeriesExists}$ and
$\mathrm{Published.DiscreteSeriesCoverExists}$.

\paragraph{D90S5. The ordinary classification in the source's central coordinates.}
The ordinary classification additionally specifies the eigenvalues
of every cyclic product of matrix units summed around a closed
path. In degree $k\ge1$ this element is
$Z_k=\operatorname{tr}(E^k)$, where $E=(E_{ij})$ is the matrix
of generators in the enveloping algebra of $\mathfrak{gl}_n$.
The identity
\[
 [E_{ab},(E^k)_{ij}]
 =\delta_{bi}(E^k)_{aj}-\delta_{ja}(E^k)_{ib}
\]
follows by the product rule and induction. Taking the trace proves
centrality. Evaluate on a Verma highest vector $v$ of weight
$h=\lambda-\rho$, using zero-based indices in decreasing
$\lambda$ order. Write $(E^k)_{ii}v=d_i^{(k)}v$; the top weight
space is a line. For $i<j$, $(E^k)_{ij}v=0$ since its weight
would exceed $h$ by a positive root. Expanding
$(E^{k+1})_{ii}=\sum_j E_{ij}(E^k)_{ji}$ and commuting
$E_{ij}$ past $(E^k)_{ji}$ for $j>i$ gives
\[
 d_i^{(0)}=1,\qquad
 d_i^{(k+1)}=(h_i+n-1-i)d_i^{(k)}-\sum_{j>i}d_j^{(k)}.
\]
Thus $Z_k$ acts by $\mathbf1^t A^k\mathbf1$, where $A$ is upper
triangular with diagonal $\lambda_i+(n-1)/2$ and every entry
strictly above the diagonal equal to $-1$. This is precisely the
source's $\mathrm{gelfVal}(\lambda,k)$. The Harish-Chandra
infinitesimal character transfers the scalar to the discrete
series. On the smooth coefficient $g\mapsto\langle w,\pi(g)v\rangle$,
differentiation along $g\exp(tX)$ acts on $v$ by $d\pi(X)$.
Complexifying and composing in the order of the cyclic word therefore
gives the required eigenfunction identity on every compact-finite
coefficient. Smoothness on the embedded group supplies the local
ambient smooth extensions by submanifold coordinates.

Choose the ordinary representation from D90S4 for each admissible
ordered parameter and sign sequence. At all other indices choose,
for example, the trivial line. Multiplicity one in D90S2 gives a
unique compact subspace of the indicated type, and its root cone
uses positive roots $e_a-e_b$ with $a<b$. Conversely, any
irreducible square-integrable representation has a Harish-Chandra
parameter decreasing separately in the positive and negative
blocks. Sort its coordinates into a single decreasing list.
The sorting permutation preserves the original order within each
sign. Relabelling rows and columns by that permutation identifies
the original representation with the chosen model for the sorted
parameter and the permuted signs. At rank zero the trivial
representation is the sole irreducible representation, every
positive-degree cyclic sum is empty and the same assertions hold.
These facts prove $\mathrm{Published.DiscreteSeriesClassification}$,
including its prescribed differential operators.
