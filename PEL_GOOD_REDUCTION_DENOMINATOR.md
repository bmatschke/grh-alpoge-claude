# One denominator on all good marked traits from the generic PEL row

Let the small-field marked PEL scheme and its cusp sections be the ones in [the interior construction](PEL_MODULI_COMPARISON.md). Let $F$ be its coefficient field, $O$ its integer ring and $E$ the CM endomorphism field. On each row, with the original marking and its prescribed rigidified theta line, **every finite list of algebraic sections has one scalar denominator on every good-reduction integral framed trait**. The trait can have arbitrary residue field, ramification, or residue characteristic. This supplies the good-reduction step of [the full integral evaluation lattice](RETWISTED_INTEGRAL_EVALUATIONS.md) directly from the generic small-field data. No integral compactification or universal semiabelian boundary family is an input for this step.

Take auxiliary full symplectic levels $N=3$ and $4$ in turn. Work initially over $O_N=O_{F(\zeta_N)}[1/N]$. The fine Siegel scheme $S_N$ of principally polarized abelian schemes with this auxiliary level is quasi-projective of finite type and carries its universal polarized abelian scheme. Over the fraction field $F_N$ take $X_N$ to be the given generic marked row with the extra level, retaining its original half-marking. It is a finite étale cover of the base change of the marked row, hence smooth, with finitely many normal components. The forgetful map $X_N\to (S_N)_{F_N}$ is finite. Indeed it is separated of finite type. Its geometric fibres are zero dimensional by rigidity of homomorphisms of abelian schemes and by étaleness of the torsion marking in characteristic zero. For the valuative criterion over $F_N$, start from the given polarized abelian scheme over a DVR. Each generic CM endomorphism extends uniquely to it. The identities in the order and the Rosati relation persist by uniqueness. The original markings on finite étale torsion extend and retain their pairing and rank. The Lie signatures are constant on the trait by splitting the characteristic-zero CM action, and the prescribed paired integral Tate type is constant by smooth proper base change for each prime. The generic theta line is determined by the graph of $w_0$ and the polarization, so it also extends. This gives the required lift. Quasi-finite proper morphisms are finite. For each irreducible component of $X_N$ take the reduced closure in $S_N$ of its image and normalize that closure in the component's function field. Call the resulting disjoint union $Y_N$. The schemes involved are of finite type over a localization of a number ring, hence Nagata. The map $Y_N\to S_N$ is finite, and its generic fibre with map to $S_N$ is $X_N$ by generic normality and the preceding finite map. Pull back the universal abelian scheme to $Y_N$. Full faithfulness for semiabelian schemes over a normal noetherian base extends the generic CM action and the Rosati identities uniquely. The Poincaré graph formula gives the rigidified theta line for that action. Its polarization is the pulled-back principal polarization. Its direct image is an invertible sheaf with arbitrary base change. Let $V$ be a good integral test with $N$ invertible. The proper good witness and the actual semiabelian model bearing its frames have isomorphic generic fibres. Full faithfulness over the trait identifies the two, including the action, and their rigidified lines agree by the rigidification comparison over a DVR. A finite unramified extension $V'/V$ supplies the auxiliary level and the compatible root of unity, since the $N$-torsion is finite étale. The abelian scheme with auxiliary level gives $S_N(V')$, and the original marking gives the lift of its generic point to $X_N$. Finiteness supplies a point of $Y_N(V')$ extending that lift. In this construction no integral marking is required at residue primes dividing the original $M$. The generic marking is retained by the finite extension of schemes. For the precise line comparison, use the rigidified generic isomorphism from the given frame to the graph line. On the common abelian scheme over $V'$, the divisor of this isomorphism as a rational section is a multiple $m$ of the special fibre: the scheme is regular and the special fibre geometrically integral. Pulling back along the zero section gives order $m$, while the rigidifications identify the pullback with $1$. Thus $m=0$, and the specified generic comparison extends invertibly. Its map of direct images therefore carries the *actual* theta generator to a generator. The Lie comparison respects the actual eigenspace lattices, whose saturated intersections and bases are preserved by finite flat extension. The scheme $Y_N$ handles the good family; the added frame-coordinate charts need only be of finite type. The bounded matrices below give their lifts explicitly.

Use finitely many affine charts on $Y_N$ trivializing its Lie and theta direct-image bundles. On each chart take the integral frame scheme used in [the original denominator argument](RETWISTED_INTEGRAL_EVALUATIONS.md). More explicitly, clear the denominators of all generic CM idempotents with one nonzero element $d\in O$. Adjoin an eigenframe matrix $U$ with its ordered multiplicities, a matrix $B$ with $UB=BU=dI$, and a generator of the theta direct image and its inverse. The saturated eigenlattices of the test contain the columns of each $dP_\tau$, so its actual basis matrices give such a point over $V'$. Over $F_N$ this is an ordinary framed chart of the given marked PEL row with auxiliary level. Pullbacks of the specified algebraic section rules are regular there. On the reduced closure of the generic framed chart, a common nonzero scalar clears the denominators of these finitely many functions. The result holds on every $V'$-point above a good test. The denominator can be chosen to be a nonzero rational integer, by clearing the finitely many field denominators using integers, because $O_N\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathbb Q=F_N$. Its integrality conclusion descends from $V'$ to $V$. Multiply over the finitely many charts and both $N$. Every residue characteristic is covered by at least one of $3,4$, including the primes of the original marking and the discriminant.

On the selected level $M=g_0g_1$, the prime-to-$\ell$ part of $M$ is at least three at every $\ell$. For the nonnegative dominant row weight, nonnegative theta degree, supplied complex uniformizations and cuspidality at each designated embedding, apply **Roadmap B8.43n** separately above every coefficient place to each multiple $c f$ just obtained. It gives integrality on all of the original finite-residue integral frames, including the degenerating ones. Its comparison between the marked toroidal arc and the actual theta generator is retained. Applying clearance to a finite basis, and the existing finite-generation bound ICL8 to the opposite inclusion, proves that the original $M_j^F$ is a full $O$-lattice under those hypotheses. Its equality on the retwisted copy then follows from the original section and frame identifications. The denominator is qualitative and may depend on the row and sections. The construction starts with the algebraic small-field row and the identification of the evaluated sections with its regular generic frame rules. Construction of those identifications on every required input and production of the good weighted points remain open.

## The denominator on every terminal-row trait

In the [prescribed signature on row $j$](PEL_STAGE_PARAMETERS.md) put $p_j=k+j-1$ and $q_j=m-j$. On **any** of the original complete discrete valuation tests on this row, the torus in the semiabelian special fibre has dimension $[E:\mathbb Q]s$ with $0\le s\le m-j$. In particular every terminal-row evaluation is covered by the good-reduction denominator constructed above.

Pass to a finite extension over which the torus splits and the generic coefficient field contains the embeddings of $E$. The semiabelian scheme carrying the test has generically abelian fibre $A$. The functorial Raynaud extension $0\to T\to G^\natural\to B\to0$ over the complete trait has the same formal completion along the special fibre as the identity component of the original model. Consequently their Lie modules agree. Indeed one can evaluate the isomorphism of formal groups at the infinitesimal identity over each Artinian quotient of the valuation ring and take the inverse limit of the finite free Lie modules. The inclusions and these identifications commute with the integral CM action by functoriality. On the fraction field $K$ they give an $E$-equivariant injection $\operatorname{Lie}(T_K)\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Lie}(A_K)$. [Raynaud extension, formal completion and functoriality, SGA 7 I, Exp. IX §7.2](https://grothendiecksga.com/read/sga7/en/9-7.html)

The rational cocharacter space $X_*(T)\otimes\mathbb Q$ is a unital module over the field $E$; write $s$ for its dimension over $E$, allowing zero. As the torus is split, tensoring that rational space with $K$ gives its Lie algebra, equivariantly. It has multiplicity $s$ at **each** embedding of $E$. The signature of the receiving module has smaller multiplicity $q_j$ at a conjugate embedding, giving the claimed bound. This calculation uses the generic Lie algebra in characteristic zero, including when the test residue characteristic ramifies in $E$.

At $j=m$ the torus has dimension zero. Under Raynaud uniformization the period lattice also has rank zero, so $A$ has good reduction over the finite extension. The good witness and original framed model compare by the full-faithfulness and rigidified-line argument above. If a multiple of a section evaluates integrally after the extension, it does so before extension, by the valuation criterion. Thus on a supplied marked terminal row the finite basis denominator and the existing opposite finite-generation bound give the full integral lattice directly. On row $m-1$ the only further case has torus $E$-rank one. The [extension of the primitive theta generator](PEL_ALL_FRAME_DENOMINATORS.md) handles this case and all higher torus ranks for a qualitative denominator. The [good-frame theorem](PEL_GOOD_FRAMES_AT_ALL_PRIMES.md) proves the stronger same-scalar conclusion B8.43n at every prime.
