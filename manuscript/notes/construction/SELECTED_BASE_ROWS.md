# The base rows of the selected theta lift

A theta lift is represented here by matrices between spaces of algebraic modular forms with chosen rational bases. To use the dimension of the lift, the rational rows must describe the very complex subspaces on which the lift acts. The construction supplies this identification on every bundle selected by the original design. In particular, the base rows, the theta matrix and the tests used to read its coefficients can all be taken from one realization. [Exact base row assertion](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/HilbertRowConditions/ColumnsFunctionAtSubspaces.lean#L108-L113) · [Theta square in these coordinates](../../../archive/rounds/v74/continuation_v74/bundle.tex#L142-L169)

**Theorem.** For every family $F$ belonging to the selected construction class, every $x>F.x_1$ and every index $a$ at $x$, let $b$ be the bundle returned by the common selector. Then its prescribed row family $b.P$ satisfies all five assertions of $\mathrm{RowsHold0}_3$. Its base row datum equals $b.P.S_0$. All choices of coefficient field, subspaces and bases in this identification belong to the realization used for the theta map of $b$. The single eventual threshold $F.x_1+1$ works for every index and lift.

**Proof.** In the source the common selector is the function `pick` on $\mathrm{Fc.cls}(Q,\mathrm{IsLinked}(p),E)$, where $Q$ includes the prescribed good-point condition and the additional conditions imposed by the rows. The construction proves the selection property for that function. Thus at the specified $x,a$ the chosen bundle comes with two equalities of triples, each identifying a datum, its object and its bundle. Composing them identifies $b$ with a linked bundle of a weighted point. Applying this argument to the output of the common selector keeps exactly the bundle that enters the theta square. The linked bundle identifies its row family, faces, periods, scales and real realization with those built from one datum $R$ and its face $F_0$. That equality includes the dependent entries recording the bases. Write $X=R.\mathrm{toCx3}(F_0)$. The linkage supplies the primitive row identities for $R,F_0$, the admissibility of the family at parameter $1$, and separation of its types. The weak characterization belongs to the same $R,F_0$. Use the base row construction on these primitive data. Its rational matrix entries can be read by evaluating the separating tests on the chosen frame and solving a full-rank minor over the given embedded field. Orthogonality of the complex type spaces and injectivity of the complexified frame give the dimension bounds on their rational parts. Summing the bounds over all types and using spanning gives equality for each type. This is how the primitive construction retains the original eigenspaces and bases. The source identifies the underlying datum of its resulting row record with $X.\mathrm{data}(F_0.\mathrm{cr},\mathrm{false})$. Transporting the bundle equality identifies it with $b.P.S_0$. Stability and membership of the chosen subspaces in the assigned blocks transport by the same equality. These two assertions, the row record with the stated underlying datum, admissibility and separation give all five conclusions. ## The square used by both endpoints

Fix the same family, index and selected bundle as in the theorem. Its witness identifies the row model, its face and its realization in one dependent equality. On its used base tuple $t_0$ the stored tuple equals $\mu_0$. The phase of the reference theta operator, the two Hilbert-space readings and the target coordinate inclusion therefore evaluate the lift on that tuple in the same realization as the row theorem. The weak and primitive row identities supply the smooth extensions and the [separating tests on the prescribed base](../theta/SELECTED_THETA_RATIONAL_TESTS.md). In particular the tests apply to rational inputs and to the basis in which the base matrix is read. [Realization and base tests](../../../archive/rounds/v74/continuation_v74/bundle.tex#L3-L69)

At lift $j$, write $\Pi_j$ for the normalized base projection, $E_j$ for its inclusion, $M_j$ for the stored base matrix and $Z.jW(j)$ for the target coordinate inclusion. Decompose $v=E_j\Pi_jv+(v-E_j\Pi_jv)$. The full-level theta evaluation on the first summand is $Z.jW(j)(M_j\Pi_jv)$ by the coordinate comparison. The orthogonal second summand maps to zero by the good-type projection law. This gives the required commuting square for every $v$. Applying the row theorem above to exactly this bundle supplies the row datum used in the rank argument. The quantifier $x>F.x_1$ in the selector precedes all the indices and all lifts, so $F.x_1+1$ works throughout. [Full square and eventual rank on the linked selection](../../../archive/rounds/v74/continuation_v74/bundle.tex#L142-L193)

The square gives the dimension comparison on each selected bundle. To obtain a prime estimate one needs such bundles for every scale on an arbitrarily late window.
