# A common finite set of frames for the coupled definite lifts

The two theta lifts on the definite column can have a coupled real test. In the period comparison for entries 1 and 4 one needs the same linear combination of output evaluations for every tuple, including tuples with different sets of finite charts. On the marked class data used for the terminal test, this assertion holds at every double step. In fact the **support** of the combination can be fixed before even the coupled test and incoming frame are known.

## Statement on the prescribed column

Fix a word, its marked real package, and a stage $j$ with $j+2\le m$. Assume the actual common and tuple laws, the marked definite class data at these stages, and equality of the archimedean Weil actions. These are the class formulas for the level, kernel, and readings on the prescribed finite charts. The original construction of those data takes the valid real package, the standard twist comparison, the leading row reading, and the row and column conditions as inputs. A finite-order twist is trivial on a connected real unitary circle. The real splitting exponents, and hence the two real actions, consequently agree for all the tuples of a valid column. The lattice vacua before the finite translations are common. [Class-data construction][family] · [Real actions][real] · [Class formulas][classes]

Let $R_{t,h,\beta}(\phi;y)$ denote the function on the definite adelic group at level $h$ obtained by reading section $\phi$ in chart $\beta$ at the archimedean frame $y$. Let $K_{t,h}$ be the arithmetic column step and $c_{t,h}$ its class-sum scalar. Let $\mathcal L_{t,j}^{C}$ be the *analytic double lift* with the smooth real two-variable vacuum test $C$ allowed in the source, including the two prescribed finite chart operations. There are frames $z_1,\ldots,z_d$ in the $j+2$ frame coordinates, all given by nonsingular integer matrices at each embedding, such that for every such $C$ and every incoming frame $y$ there are complex $a_i(C,y)$ with
$$
 \mathcal L_{t,j}^{C}\big(R_{t,j,\bullet}(\phi;y)\big)_\beta
 = c_{t,j}c_{t,j+1}\sum_{i=1}^d a_i(C,y)
   R_{t,j+2,\beta}(K_{t,j+1}K_{t,j}\phi;z_i).                 \tag{1}
$$
This is equality of functions, for every attained tuple $t$, every permitted ramified column input and every output chart. The number $d$ is the dimension of the common space of determinant-twisted polynomial frame functions of the output weight. The word and the stage fix this space. In particular neither $d$ nor the list of frames is multiplied by the number of tuples or charts. The coefficients in (1) are archimedean interpolation coefficients. The separate row-lift measures and the local period-unfolding scalar do not occur in this column identity. [Precise one-step and double-step statements][twox]

## Passing from one variable to a coupled test

On a class representative the frame reading is a determinant twist of a polynomial of the fixed weight. Translation by a central archimedean element with scalar coordinates $z_\tau$ multiplies the reading by $\prod_\tau z_\tau^{\sum_i\lambda_{\tau,i}}$. The class transformation law extends this identity to every point and shows continuity and rational left invariance. This central character is the same for every chart and every tuple. [Frame laws and central character][rd]

Average the first argument of $C$ over the input center against the character required by the incoming form. Translation in the definite quotient integral shows that projecting onto this character preserves the double lift, including every finite chart operation. To see which projection to make at the middle center, take the *same scalar* in the incoming and the middle unitary groups. Its action on the matrix variable $X\mapsto bXa^{-1}$ is trivial. The Weil action on the vacuum line is scalar, and it commutes with every polynomial creation operator for this pair. Thus on the projected first factor the middle center acts by a single character. Project the second argument onto the matching character in the middle integral. Haar invariance again preserves the integral. Both projections use only the real actions. Compactness of the definite quotients and the smooth theta bounds justify averaging under these integrals. If every incoming function of the specified character vanishes, the zero combination already proves (1). [Two projections and uniformity][fock] · [Middle-center character][middle] · [Earlier calculation on the definite oscillator](../construction/ROW_MIXED_CELL_UNFOLDING.md)

At a definite pair, a central character on a lattice-vacuum theta function fixes the polynomial degree at each real place, up to the vacuum character and the sign of the definite model. Only finitely many Fock monomials have these degrees. Orthogonality of distinct characters and completeness of the polynomial Fock system give the finite-dimensional slice space also for the smooth test. Apply this to each argument of the projected kernel $C_2$. If its left slices lie in a finite-dimensional function space $E$ and its right slices in $E'$, choose evaluations at $\dim E$ points giving a dual basis on $E$. For the dual interpolation functions $e_h$ one has $C_2(u,v)=\sum_h e_h(u) C_2(u_h,v)$. Each right factor lies in $E'$. Taking Fock polynomial preimages expresses $C_2$ as a finite sum of products with polynomial pairs $(P_i,P'_i)$. The original $C$ can have infinite tensor rank. It is the *integral after projection* that admits this finite expansion. The projected kernel and its polynomial pairs were chosen before inserting the finite tests of any one tuple. [Degree bound][degree] · [Finite tensor expansion][slices]

For one of these polynomial tests and one incoming frame, the definite class-sum formula gives a finite coefficient function $b_{j,P,y}$ on the next frames. Its identity holds for the sum over all input charts ending in each output chart. In the quotient integral, the input level character cancels its inverse on the kernel. The source class contributes the volume of its level divided by the order of its stabilizer. The marked kernel formula converts that level volume times the theta reading to $c_{t,j}$ times the prescribed class functional. The remaining compact unitary average acts on polynomials in the common frame model. The substitution identity for this average expresses it by evaluations at invertible frames simultaneously for every input polynomial and every class functional. The incoming determinant factor and the determinants of the scaled outgoing frames are included in $b_{j,P,y}$. This explains why $b$ is common although the class functionals, stabilizers and $c_{t,j}$ can vary. [Substitution in the compact average][avg] · [Evaluation on each marked class][oneclass] · [Determinant factors in the one-step formula][one]

Apply that identity twice to each polynomial pair. The arithmetic first step preserves the permitted column space, so its image is an allowed input for the second identity. Linearity lets the finite intermediate sums pass through the second lift. The resulting output coefficient function is
$$
 b(z)=\sum_i\sum_w b_{j,P_i,y}(w)\,b_{j+1,P'_i,w}(z).
$$
The two tuple scalars factor out as $c_{t,j}c_{t,j+1}$. No division by a period or a coefficient is involved. Empty chart sums contribute zero. This proves (1) initially with a finite list depending on $C,y$. [Composition for the original kernels][compose]

## Fixing the support before the test

Put $V=\mathrm{ValC}(\lambda_{j+2})$ in the common marked coordinates. For every tuple, output chart, allowed section and fixed adelic point, the resulting function of the outgoing frame lies in $V$. This includes the change of archimedean representative and the finite level character. The space is finite dimensional, and a common product of determinant powers clears the denominators of a basis. The nonsingular integer matrix tuples are Zariski dense in the product of general linear groups. Indeed, multiplying a polynomial vanishing on those tuples by the product of their determinants gives a polynomial vanishing on every integer tuple, hence the zero polynomial. It follows that evaluations on those nonsingular tuples span $V^*$. Select a basis $\operatorname{ev}_{z_i}$, once for the output weight, and let $h_i\in V$ be the interpolation functions with $h_i(z_k)=\delta_{ik}$. Then $\operatorname{ev}_z=\sum_i h_i(z)\operatorname{ev}_{z_i}$ for every output frame. Taking $a_i(C,y)=\sum_z b(z)h_i(z)$ proves (1) on the fixed support. For $V=0$ the empty sum suffices. This argument supplies no bound on the heights of the interpolation coefficients. [Full frame-function assertion][scalar] · [Finite-dimensional determinant-twisted model][frame]

For the final two column lifts, pairing (1) with a fixed smooth top row gives the same finite sum of terminal periods. The [terminal comparison](PERIOD_TERMINAL_COMMON_TEST.md) turns each summand into its common branching scalar times the tuple's own volume and arithmetic terminal reading. Thus these two operations preserve a homogeneous terminal relation once the equality that unfolds the **row** double lift into this coupled column integral is established. For an interior pair the corresponding row equality is needed at every real translate. These are the distinct statements with their integration measures and scalar $c_{t,j}^{V}$ in [the period definitions][values]. Identifying the descended row with the prescribed bottom row and bounding the served arithmetic maps remain necessary for entries 1 and 4.
