# A nonzero small-field reading at the untwisted reference tuple

Fix a nonzero original cusp row and the coefficient frame, embeddings, lowest-type vector $u$ and coefficient extension $c_x$ used to test a fresh normalization. Write $M=g_0g_1$ for the original level, $F_0=\operatorname{emb}(LL_0)\subset\mathbf C$, and $\mu_0$ for the reference tuple. Take the common geometric correcting phase $\phi_0=(\alpha\beta)^{-1}$ defined by the fixed extremal-vector harmonic map and the vacuum identity at the reference frame. These are the same maps and phase used to geometrically read the distinguished generators. Under the first-pass rank, class-sum, marking, and small-field hypotheses there are an integral polynomial in the prescribed source representation, a definite argument $h$, and a target argument $g_*$ such that the reference series $F$ for these data satisfies $F(g_*)\ne0$ and $\phi_0 F$ has an expansion *as a function of every target argument* in original cusp readings with coefficients in $F_0$. Only the source translates entering this existence statement are needed in the fixed-field test. [Choice of the common data][norm] · [Definition of geometric phase before any cover or translate is chosen][phase]

**Keep the support of the nonzero term.** With $8fr+4\le n_V$, the second-field cusp row is nonzero and is spanned by the definite class-sum sections of the original one-map construction. Some such section $s$ is nonzero. The central marked-level translation used by the one-map identity is invertible, and its uniformized reading is nonzero at some frame. The Lie factor is positive, the component factor is a unit and the scalar from the root of unity is nonzero. The one-map identity therefore makes the integrated theta series nonzero there. Unfold at this point into the finite sum over the *actual* chosen representatives $h_k$. Some term is nonzero. Each $h_k$ has split support away from the integer recorded for this tuple. In particular its support avoids $2M$. This last conclusion uses the full representative pin, from which the statement of the existence lemma retains only that the translate is finite-adelic. Next expand the coefficient vector of this same summand in the standard integral polynomial basis of the given highest-weight representation. By linearity one basis polynomial $P$ keeps the value nonzero, with exactly the same $h_k$ and target $g_*$. Transport $P$ to $LL$ and to $\mathbf C$, giving $v,x$. Their coefficients correspond under the embeddings. Compatibility of the two coefficient maps gives $c_x(x)=c_{0,L}(v)$. This vector belongs to the $F_0$ rational vector space in the pinned realization. The extraction can be made at the original point $g_*$ throughout. **Read this summand over the reference field.** Choose the good cover with relative conductor $1$, half ideal the unit ideal, full level $M$ and embedded field precisely $F_0$. The original coordinate field is contained in $F_0$, the level root of unity is in that coordinate field, and the given larger ray field contains the ray field of $M$ by divisibility. The half-normalized reference character values needed at unramified primes are in $F_0$ by the small-field hypothesis. At $\mu_0$ the variable-conductor set is empty and the finite level datum has half $\overline{P_g}$. The opposite translating half is $P_g$, precisely the marked primes of the level-$M$ ideal. The pinned finite function is periodic on the selected lattice modulo $M$ and takes values in $F_0$. Its finite Fourier coefficients also belong to $F_0$ since the quotient is killed by $M$. The fixed distinguished character used in the outer series does not enter this reference test. In particular $h_k$ is allowed to have support above the outer distinguished prime, here absent from the level. [Level-one reference cover][cover] · [Finite level datum][ref] · [Marked half and residue Fourier calculation](FIRST_PASS_DISTINGUISHED_COVER.md)

Choose an auxiliary prime $p$ outside $2M$ and the rational primes below the support of $h_k$. Take bad integer $2Mp$ for the lattice calculation. The same split support avoids it. The conjugate moved lattice $\Lambda$ is unimodular and standard at $M$. There is an odd multiple $N$ of $M$, prime to $p$, with $NS\subseteq\Lambda\cap S$ for the standard lattice $S$. At its own level the cover thus admits the arithmetic short section for each of the marked Fourier terms. The level-one residue sections use the constant weight-zero section $1$. The marked-point finite delta weights have the same normalization as the normalized short-series sum. On each reached component the common vacuum pin identifies the analytic generator, and the coefficient phase of the harmonic vector gives a polynomial over $F_0$ after it is removed. With $I=\Lambda\cap S$ the ratio of the two index factors of the short sum is $[\Lambda:I]^r$. The leftover class factor is
$$
 \chi_W(y)^{-1}[\Lambda:I]^{-r/2}.
$$
For the determinant ideal $\mathfrak c$, the split elementary divisors give $[\Lambda:I]=N(\mathfrak c)k^2$ and $\chi_W(y)=\chi(\mathfrak c)$. Thus for $r=2t+1$ the factor equals $(\chi(\mathfrak c)\sqrt{N\mathfrak c})^{-1}(N\mathfrak c)^{-t}k^{-r}\in F_0$. The harmonic sign is rational. These identities give a cover section whose adelic reading is exactly $\phi_0 F$ on all target arguments, including points where that section vanishes. [Lattice and denominators][lattice] · [Harmonic and finite coefficients][harm] · [Arithmetic section and analytic identity][identity] · [Determinant and index][index]

The reference series and the dictionary character are invariant under the original target level. By the holomorphic cusp spanning theorem at the given rank and positive last weight, this section's reading is in the complex span of readings of original sections. To retain the coefficient field, pull a finite spanning family for this reading to the cover, choose a basis of its $F_0$-span, and use preservation of linear independence by the complex uniformization of sections. If the cover section lay outside that $F_0$-span, the enlarged family would be linearly independent over $F_0$ and hence its readings would be linearly independent over $\mathbf C$, a contradiction. The original sections with the bounded holomorphic cusp reading are cusp sections in the original model. Consequently, for all $g$,
$$
 \phi_0 F(g)=\sum_s e_s\,\operatorname{lieFac}\,O.\phi(c_E,f_s;g),
 \qquad e_s\in F_0,\quad f_s\in \operatorname{cuspA}.
$$
The general phase-expansion lemma in the pin assumes rationality for every good cover and every allowed source translate. In its proof that premise is used to produce the one cover section above. The subsequent level descent and comparison of dictionaries apply directly to the constructed section. ## A single conjugation correction for the untwisted reference lift

Fix a first-pass row $j$ and set $p=\operatorname{cj}(\mathrm{phs}_j)$ at the reference embedding. Write $\Theta(y)$ for the lift at $\mu_0$ with its prescribed real vector. There is a single root of unity $r$ such that for every allowed coefficient automorphism $\tau$ and every conjugate pair of definite inputs $y,y'$, the cusp expansion of $rp\Theta(y)$ conjugates to that of $rp\Theta(y')$. Conjugation applies $\tau$ to the coefficients of any expansion in readings of cusp sections over the core. This applies to every $\tau$ fixing the embedded $L_0$ under the first pass's normality, normalized unramified character values, ray and type-place root data. These give the required fixation of the core, host embeddings, CM marking, normalized prime factors and type-place characters. [Fixing conditions][fixsmall] · [Actual row hypotheses][actual]

**Keeping the old phase.** The old geometric row supplies an identity with the stored component constants and phase $p$. Its phase tie takes the existence of any normalized one-map identity on that row to an identity with those specified constants. The antecedent follows from the geometric core, fixed weight and frame comparison, chart independence and extension of the polynomial coefficient map. The actual first pass invokes the old geometric phase together with uniformity. Pass to a finite normal coefficient field $M\subset\mathbf C$ containing the normalized cover data, with the same level as the old row and the same extended polynomial coefficient map. Comparison with the old row gives one root of unity $\zeta$ and one central level action $z$ such that
$$
                  p\Theta(y)=\zeta\,\mathcal R(z f_x).
$$
Here $x$ gives the polynomial values of $y$ on the definite classes, $f_x$ is its geometric class section, and $\mathcal R$ includes the stored component constants, Lie factor and adelic multiplier. The two normalized maps use the same auxiliary component constants and archimedean scalar after accounting for their respective finite-order factors. In the prescribed rank range the old reference class sections span the old cusp space. The comparison with the stored constants therefore holds on the whole old cusp space and extends by base change. Its resulting multiplier is a product and quotient of three roots of unity. These are all old geometric or normalized class-section data. [Old phase tie][old] · [Normal coefficient model][model] · [Finite-order factors][compare] · [Span and common normalization][normspan]

**The scalar on the reference cover.** The relative determinant character $\xi_{\mu_0}$ is $1$ by the reference level pin and the existence of determinant roots. The normalized weight-zero cover section thus takes the same root-of-unity value $c_0$ at every point of the uniformization dictionary. The dictionary reaches every underlying complex point, and changing frames preserves a weight-zero value. The identity adele supplies a framed point. Every automorphism over $M$ carries it to another point with the same value and sends $c_0$ to its conjugate. Thus $c_0\in M$ by separability and algebraicity. Sections can be compared on framed points, so $c_\delta=c_0{\bf1}$ after identifying the cover coefficient field with $M$. Hence the cover section transforms under $\tau$ by $\tau(c_0)/c_0$. [Pointwise calculation and field descent][constdes] · [Constant section and conjugation][constant]

Use the same cover and definite representatives on both sides. In the finite Fourier table $\tau$ raises the relevant root of unity to a unit power. Inverse dilation reindexes the finite sum and equals the identity modulo the fixed type primes because $\tau$ fixes the old level root. The remaining determinant factors combine into one value of $\xi_{\mu_0}$, which equals $1$. The same computation includes zero finite tests. The volume is a reciprocal index. For each class a numerator ideal prime to the bad primes computes its lattice factor using normalized prime values and a positive rational factor uniquely determined by its square. Thus the volume and lattice factors are fixed. Conjugating the harmonic polynomial coefficients and the finite class formula now gives the class section for the conjugate values $x'$ as
$$
                   f_{x'}=(\tau(c_0)/c_0)^{-1}\operatorname{gal}_\tau f_x.
$$
The inverse occurs because the character section multiplies the pulled-back class section in that formula. [Finite Fourier dilation][dil] · [Volume and class factors][vol] · [Reference class formula][sum]

**Cancellation.** Write $\mathcal C_\tau$ for conjugation of cusp expansions. Base change expresses $f_x$ as a sum of cusp sections over the core. The fixed level action $z$ is defined over the core and preserves the cusp space. All relations among their readings are defined over the core, so coefficient conjugation is independent of the expansion. In particular $\mathcal C_\tau\mathcal R(z f_x)=\mathcal R(z\operatorname{gal}_\tau f_x)$. Take $r=(\zeta c_0)^{-1}$. Then
$$
  \mathcal C_\tau(rp\Theta(y))
  =\tau(c_0)^{-1}\mathcal R(z\operatorname{gal}_\tau f_x)
  =c_0^{-1}\mathcal R(z f_{x'})
  =rp\Theta(y').
$$
The same $r$ works for every $\tau$ and every test. This calculation first applies to inputs with values in the normal coefficient model. The old rational inputs span the complex definite space, and definite conjugation is semilinear. Expanding a conjugate pair with coefficients $\alpha_k,\tau(\alpha_k)$ gives the identity for all pairs. A nonzero reference reading enters only in the subsequent fixed-field comparison of the geometric phase with $rp$. Triviality of the relative character here is specific to the old reference tuple. The active base generator can carry its prescribed distinguished character. [Galois action on readings][reading] · [Common root and orientation][root] · [All definite forms][all]

## An admissible scale for the actual base class sums

Take the representative and polynomial from the first part. At the reference tuple their coefficient conjugates give the same underlying series because the polynomial has rational coefficients and the source argument is the same on the two sides. The realization of a single source translate by a conjugate pair of definite forms lets us apply the common-root law to this series. Put $a=rp/\phi_0$. Every $\tau$ fixing $F_0$ fixes the coefficients of the expansion of $\phi_0F$ from the first part. The conjugation law for $rpF$ gives
$$
           a\,\phi_0F(g_*)=\tau(a)\,\phi_0F(g_*).
$$
The factor on both sides is nonzero. Thus $a$ is fixed by every automorphism of $\mathbf C$ over $F_0$. The fixed-field assertion follows since a nontrivial algebraic conjugate extends to an automorphism of $\mathbf C$, and a transcendental element can be included in a transcendence basis and moved before passing to the algebraic closure. Hence $a\in F_0^\times$. The argument uses Galois conjugation of coefficients of rational sections. It only evaluates the resulting identity of functions at $g_*$. [Realizing the conjugate pair and proving the power identity][fix]

Now let $c$ be the nonzero scalar comparing the harmonic coordinates of the current coefficient map with the prescribed polynomial reading for this triple $(i,j,b)$. For a polynomial generator $P$ represented by $G$ in the rational coefficient space, equality of harmonic evaluations and injectivity on the allowed harmonic subspace give $\mathrm{ioP}(u,c_{0,L}G)=cP$. The theta kernel is linear in this polynomial. Thus the geometric reading gives $(\phi_0 c)\Theta(h,P)$ in the unscaled rational cusp reading. The installed reading at scale $\nu$ is $\nu^{-1}$ times that unscaled reading. We can take
$$
                      \nu_g=a\phi_0c=rpc .
$$
Since $a\in F_0^\times$, the [distinguished-conductor calculation](FIRST_PASS_DISTINGUISHED_COVER.md) reads all the required base generators at this scale. If $s$ is the order of $r$ then $\nu_g^s=(pc)^s$. This is precisely the required power in the original definition of an admissible scale. The geometric comparison can be made in the standard frames. The standardization uses the original uniformization, geometric lattice stability and cusp decay. Moving the frame by a central element acts inversely on the vector used in the coefficient identification and directly on its geometric evaluation. These two actions cancel first on original forms and then on simple tensors. Hence the coefficient reading at the same $\nu$ agrees pointwise in the two frames. The scale predicate itself is preserved. [Polynomial scaling and phase][fock] · [Reading on a simple tensor][scale] · [Exact equality after the frame change][move] · [Preservation of admissibility][movenorm]

Choose the B1 scale once per triple. If a full admissible rationality witness exists, take its scale. Its base clause then reads all these generators. Otherwise use $\nu_g$ and the full-rationality implication in the scale specification is vacuous. If the original cusp space is zero, all the tested series vanish by their geometric expansions. In that case take any full witness when it exists and take the prescribed phase times an allowed coefficient scale otherwise. This gives a nonzero admissible scale in every case. The geometry and the unscaled row used for this choice precede the installation of the theta maps. The ensuing Hecke identities are homogeneous, and B2 transports the chosen series with its reading. Finite summation on the definite classes and scalar extension give the class-sum identity for every tested source vector on each active row of this freshly chosen pass. This constructs the required pass. The specification for an independently chosen earlier scale still quantifies over a larger family of twists.
