0. RESULT AND EXACT SCOPE

We prove the actual GeneratorModuloUniformiser assertion. For every
coarse generator u in Dat.lamGen, there is an allowed integral generator
g in Dat.gen such that

                 reduction(Delta*u)=reduction(g).               (CG0.1)

Here Delta is the source Dl, not the larger localizer SPR. The generator
g belongs to the full allowed Gen set; choosing a primitive independent
subfamily of that set is the separate, already proved SD1 selection.
In particular (CG0.1) is an existence assertion about an individual
allowed product, stronger than membership in its residue-field span.

The hypotheses are the literal Dat.Hyp and UniformizedDVR data, with
C0>=32 and CLa,Ck>=C0. The final application may retain C0=1088. Write
k=r+K, with r>=2, K>=1, p prime, p>=13 and even n'>0. The coefficient
ring O is a DVR with uniformizer w, residue field f of characteristic p,
and the source UniformizedDVR structure. Its completeness is not used
in this finite congruence proof.

The valid periodic frame has r increasing even selected positions
f_(j,0)<...<f_(j,r-1)=z_j, z_j<f_(j+1,0), and
f_(j+J,i)=f_(j,i)+n', J>=1. Set

  W_j = selected letters in [f_(j-1,0),z_j],
  Wold_j = selected letters in [f_(j-1,0),z_(j-1)],
  L_j = selected letters in (z_(j-1),f_(j,0)),
  T_j^late = selected letters in (z_(j-1),z_j],
  d_j(x)=z_j-x on W_j.

No selected letters lie strictly between consecutive positions of one
frame, so Wold_j consists of its r even frame letters. For a late x,
y_j(x) is the r-th most recent even selected letter before x. Validity
requires x-y_j(x)>L1. The other valid-frame conditions remain exactly
as stated in SegRing.Valid; our arguments use their consequences below.

The scalar data satisfy, for every selected physical residue t and j,

  a<=deg_t<=2a,  E'_t<=E0,
  #W_j<=Ns,  Ns<=N1,  Ns<=2|TT|,  Ns+r<=K,
  p^(d_j(x))<=Q for x in W_j,  Y>=1,
  Ck Ns^2 log(a)<=k,
  128 k^2 Ns Y^2<=p^(L1-1),
  32 p Y k^2 Ns Q^2 (E0+mStar+1)<=a.                 (CG0.2)

All real powers/floors are literal source ones. In particular a>0,
Ns>=1, k>=5 and Q>=1 follow from these hypotheses. Put

  La=CLa Ns^2 log(a),
  B_x=deg_(y_j(x) mod n')/p^(d_j(y_j(x))),
  s_x=floor(B_x/(k^2Y)),  m_x=mStar+s_x,  theta_x=B_x/2,
  o_x=La*a/(p*k*p^(d_j(x)))
       +(10 if x even, 1 if x odd)*a/p^(L1+d_j(x)).   (CG0.3)

Natural floors in the source agree with ordinary floors because B_x>=0.
Write XX_j=XX'(O,r,K,F,j,m,theta,o). Its definition and the common
integral basis needed to reduce it are proved in CG1, not assumed under
the name NestedTypeBound.

DEPENDENCIES ALREADY RECONSTRUCTED. IP3--IP4 give the universal integral
signed-path basis and its simultaneous prefix minimum/maximum adaptation.
SD3 gives Delta nonzero modulo w, hence Delta!=0 over O. Companion RS2/RS3
give the determinant degree budget and scale estimates recorded in CG3.
For clarity their short calculations are also supplied here, so that no
numeric wrapper is left unexpanded. We do not redo the SD3 nonvanishing
argument. The result does not count coarse generators or construct the
global frame and parameter data, and does not by itself complete RH/GRH.


1. THE ACTUAL PAIRED-PATH LATTICE AND ITS REDUCTION [CG1]

Let R be any integral domain. List W_j increasingly as x_0,...,x_(N-1),
with vector sign at even positions and covector sign at odd positions.
For a plane polynomial A and normal polynomial G write v_<x(A),v_<=x(A)
for the minimum generic rank-one torus orders, and M_<x(G),M_<=x(G)
for the corresponding maximum orders. The definitions use a generic
invertible matrix, not evaluations on the finite group GL(f).

For integer margins m_x and real theta_x,o_x, XX'_R is the intersection
of the following two R-submodules. First take the span of separated
products A G, where A uses plane variables at W_j, G uses normal
variables only at T_j^late, and there are integers M_x with, at every
late x,

  x even: M_<=x(G)<=M_x,  v_<x(A)>=M_x+m_x;
  x odd:  M_<x(G)<=M_x,   v_<=x(A)>=M_x+m_x;
  always: v_<x(A)>=theta_x.                           (CG1.1)

Intersect that span with the monomial support condition

  normal degree at x <= o_x,
  where normal degree means degree in the last K coordinates for
  even x and in the first r coordinates for odd x.   (CG1.2)

This is exactly SegRing.XX'. It is important that (CG1.2) is imposed
on the span, not prematurely imposed on each arbitrary separated
product in a chosen expansion.

Use the already proved IP basis independently in the r plane and K
normal coordinates. Its elements have paths nu^0,...,nu^N and
kappa^0,...,kappa^N, with respective letter degrees b'_i,b''_i, and
standard terminal column indices I',I''. The paired products

  B_(nu,kappa,I',I'')=Psi_(nu,I') * Psi_(kappa,I'')     (CG1.3)

form an integral basis of the polynomial ring on these segment letters.
Each path is indexed once; its degrees are determined by its successive
weight differences. The two variable blocks are independent, so this
assertion is the ordinary tensor-product basis assertion, followed by
the injective renaming i->x_i. It is not an integral isotypic splitting.

The allowed index subset is precisely:

  b''_i=0 if x_i is in Wold_j;
  if x_i is late and even:
       kappa^(i+1)_1+m_(x_i)<=nu^i_r,
       b''_i<=o_(x_i);
  if x_i is late and odd:
       kappa^i_1+m_(x_i)<=nu^(i+1)_r,
       b'_i<=o_(x_i);
  at every late x_i: theta_(x_i)<=nu^i_r.              (CG1.4)

These are the literal inequalities in segPairs. We claim that XX'_R
is exactly the span of the subset (CG1.3) satisfying (CG1.4).

Proof of the inclusion from XX'_R. First ignore (CG1.2). Split each
separated product A G into its plane and normal letter-multihomogeneous
components. Every twist preserves these degrees, so the components
retain (CG1.1). For each component, apply IP4 to A with the indicated
lower bounds, and to G with the indicated upper bounds. Real lower
bounds use their ceilings. At unused prefix cuts one can use the
automatic bounds between plus and minus the total letter degree.
Every plane basis element in the expansion has the required minima,
and every normal basis element has the required maxima. Multiplying
the expansions therefore gives the gap inequalities in (CG1.4), without
their o-bounds. The zero normal degrees on Wold_j hold because the
original G has no such variables; every basis vector has its fixed
letter degrees.

Now apply the monomial projector retaining (CG1.2) to this expansion.
On a paired basis vector every support monomial has normal degree
b''_i at an even x_i and b'_i at an odd x_i. Thus the projector either
keeps that whole basis vector or kills it. On a polynomial in XX'_R it
is the identity. This proves the inclusion with all of (CG1.4).

For the reverse inclusion, take an allowed paired basis vector. IP4
gives its plane/normal prefix bounds. Choose
M_(x_i)=kappa^(i+1)_1 at an even letter and kappa^i_1 at an odd letter.
Then (CG1.4) gives (CG1.1), while its exact multidegrees give (CG1.2).
Its normal old-frame degrees vanish. Hence it belongs to XX'_R.

Consequences over O and its residue field f:

  (a) XX'_O is spanned by a subset of an ambient O-basis. Add the
      excluded paired basis elements and monomials involving an
      outside-segment variable to obtain a complement. In particular
      XX'_O is saturated for multiplication by any nonzero a in O.
  (b) Reduction sends every integral basis polynomial to the identical
      path polynomial over f, because wedge/contraction and renaming
      have integral coefficients. Thus

             reduction(XX'_O)=XX'_f.                 (CG1.5)

      A direct lift is obtained by expanding in the allowed f-basis
      and lifting its finitely many scalar coefficients to O.
  (c) Projection to any fixed total letter degree preserves XX'_O:
      each paired basis polynomial has a fixed sum b'+b''.

This proves exactly the first, third and sixth clauses of the actual
NestedTypeBound package needed here and by RestLam. The residue-field
sixth clause first puts the separated pair in XX'_f by (CG1.1)--(CG1.2),
then uses (CG1.5). We are not claiming its separate characteristic-zero
joint-isotypic description (the fourth clause) as a consequence of
this argument. Under the source's valid-frame, N+r<=K and m>=0
hypotheses all statements above apply. The direct basis proof itself
does not need the last two inequalities.


2. COARSE DATA, UNIQUE LATE LETTERS, AND THE LABEL DIGITS [CG2]

For each late x let D_(j,x) be the determinant of the first-r-coordinate
rows at the last r even selected predecessors of x. All these rows
are distinct and belong to W_j. Set

  D_j=product_(x in T_j^late) D_(j,x)^(s_x),
  Delta=product_(0<=j<J) phi_j(D_j),
  phi_j(X_(x,c))=Z_(x mod n',c)^(p^(d_j(x))).         (CG2.1)

The plane embedding is implicit. Write E_t for the physical degree of
Delta at row t; CG3 gives E_t<=d_t, where
d_t=deg_t-E'_t on TT and d_t=0 outside TT. Thus

                 a'_t=d_t-E_t.                     (CG2.2)

For the coarse scale define

  T_j=a/[8Yk^2 Ns p^(d_j(f_(j-1,0)))],
  R_j=floor(log_p(floor(T_j))),  P_j=p^(R_j),
  m0=k Ns+1,
  Qc_j=p^max(z_(j+1)-z_j+R_(j+1)-R_j,0).            (CG2.3)

The floor-log description is valid because CG3 proves T_j>=1; the
source uses Nat.log and the natural floor exactly. This P_j is the
coarse exponent scale; it is distinct from the global physical size Q
and the carry ratio Qc_j.

For y=y_j(x), define

  Bw_j(y)=floor(a'_(y mod n')/p^(d_j(y)+R_j))-(k+r+1),
  theta^c_(j,b)(x)=(2/3)*
    {Bw_j(y)-m0, if y is in Wold_j;
     b(y),       otherwise},
  o^c_j(x)=La*a/[p*k*p^(d_j(x)+R_j)]
       +1_(x even)*4a/p^(d_j(y)+R_j).                (CG2.4)

The coarse segment module M_j(b) is XX' with margin m0, lower threshold
theta^c and normal-degree bound o^c, intersected with total letter
degree b and the restrictions that every plane exponent at a current
frame letter is less than Qc_j. This is the literal Dat.Mj. The current
frame exponent restriction is useful in counting/injectivity elsewhere;
the congruence proof uses only the other two conditions.

A coarse generator has the form

  u=Z^ell product_(0<=j<J) phi_j(expand_(P_j)(y_j)),
  y_j in M_j(b_j),  physical multidegree of u = a'.   (CG2.5)

Here expand_P replaces each variable by its P-th power and FIXES
coefficients. The coarse label satisfies
ell_(x mod n',c)<p^(d_j(x)) P_j at every late x.

The intervals (z_(j-1),z_j], 0<=j<J, partition (z_(-1),z_(-1)+n'].
Every selected residue consequently has exactly one late representative
x in this period. Periodic translation supplies the representatives for
all integer j and preserves d_j and R_j. At the coordinate of that x,
perform Euclidean division

  ell_(t,c)=ell0_(t,c)+p^(d_j(x)) e_(j,x,c),
  0<=ell0_(t,c)<p^(d_j(x)),  0<=e_(j,x,c)<P_j.       (CG2.6)

Set e_j=0 off T_j^late, and keep ell0=ell outside TT. This proves both
the fine-label condition and IsEll, with the exact monomial identity

             Z^ell=Z^ell0 product_j phi_j(X^(e_j)). (CG2.7)

No assumption of injectivity on the whole overlapping set W_j is used.
Only late representatives are unique. This is CoarseLabelFactorisation.


3. THE DETERMINANT BUDGET AND THE COARSE SCALE [CG3]

These are the completed RS2/RS3 companion estimates; we record their
proofs to make the constants used in CG6 explicit.

Each W_j lies in the union of the two consecutive half-open cells
(z_(j-2),z_(j-1)] and (z_(j-1),z_j]. Summing over one frame period,
each physical residue occurs at most twice among all W_j, even if a
single W_j contains repeated residues. The r predecessor rows of any
one determinant are distinct as integers. If y=y_j(x) and a row v is
one of those predecessors, then v>=y and therefore d_j(v)<=d_j(y).
Consequently

  s_x p^(d_j(v))
    <= [deg_(y mod n')/(p^(d_j(y)) k^2Y)] p^(d_j(v))
    <=2a/(k^2Y).                                    (CG3.1)

For a fixed physical residue there are at most two row occurrences
among the segment sets, and each occurrence can occur in at most Ns
of the determinants of that segment. Summing (CG3.1) gives

                      E_t<=4Ns*a/(k^2Y).             (CG3.2)

The last inequality in (CG0.2) implies a>=32(E0+mStar+1)>=32, since
p,Y,k,Ns,Q are at least one. Thus log(a)>=1. As Ck>=32,
Ck Ns^2 log(a)<=k implies k>=32Ns. With k>=5 and Y>=1 this gives
k^2Y>=160Ns. Hence E_t<=a/40 and E0<=a/32. On TT, deg_t>=a and
E'_t<=E0 imply

  E_t<=d_t,   a'_t=deg_t-E'_t-E_t >= (9/10)deg_t.     (CG3.3)

Indeed the removed amount is at most a(1/32+1/40)<a/10.
Outside TT every determinant row is absent, so E_t=0=d_t.
This proves the full TopCountLowerBounds input, including natural
subtraction agreeing with the displayed ordinary subtraction.

The first position f_(j-1,0) belongs to W_j, so its power is at most
Q<=Q^2. Divide the last inequality in (CG0.2) by the denominator in
T_j to obtain

  T_j>=4p(E0+mStar+1),
  P_j<=T_j<pP_j,
  P_j>4(E0+mStar+1)>mStar.                            (CG3.4)

The middle pair is the defining property of the largest power of p
not exceeding floor(T_j); integrality upgrades floor(T_j)<pP_j to
T_j<pP_j. Every y in W_j is later than f_(j-1,0), so

             8Yk^2 Ns p^(d_j(y)) P_j <= a.          (CG3.5)

These prove PowerExceedsMaxMultiplicity and the exact scale inequalities
used below. None requires a claim that Frobenius is a characteristic-zero
intertwiner.


4. THE ACTUAL COARSE GENERATOR SUPPLIES THE INTERNAL DEGREES [CG4]

An intermediate letter z in L_j belongs to no other segment letter set
modulo a period. To see this, place a representative between z_(j-1)
and f_(j,0). Earlier segment sets end no later than z_(j-1); later ones
begin no earlier than f_(j,0). Periodic translation gives the same
conclusion for residues. This is the exact LL_unique assertion.

Assume u in (CG2.5) is nonzero. At t=z mod n' only its y_j factor
contributes a segment degree, namely p^(d_j(z))P_j b_j(z). Its label
degree is sum_c ell_(t,c), strictly less than k p^(d_j(z))P_j. Since
u has degree a'_t, equality of multihomogeneous degrees gives

  a'_t=p^(d_j(z))P_j b_j(z)+sum_c ell_(t,c),
  a'_t/[p^(d_j(z))P_j]-k < b_j(z).                   (CG4.1)

Nonzeroness is used only to identify the common degree with a';
cancellation in a sum of polynomial monomials does not invalidate a
fixed row-degree calculation. Equation (CG4.1) is exactly Step2.

For every late x the r preceding vectors exist because the previous
frame already supplies r. The oldest of these, y_j(x), belongs to W_j.
If it is not in the old frame, it lies in L_j. Indeed, if it lay in the
current frame, its last r predecessors together with x would give
r+1 distinct selected positions in the current-frame interval, which
has exactly r. Thus (CG4.1) applies precisely in the non-old-frame
branch of theta^c.


5. THE TWO NUMERICAL TRANSFERS [CG5]

Fix a late x, write y=y_j(x), P=P_j and K0=k Ns. Define

  c=deg_(y mod n')/[p^(d_j(y))P],
  q=a'_(y mod n')/[p^(d_j(y))P].

By (CG3.3),(CG3.5),

       q>=(9/10)c,   c>=8Yk^2Ns>=40K0.              (CG5.1)

If y is intermediate, (CG4.1) gives b_j(y)>q-k. Then

  theta^c(x)-K0=(2/3)b_j(y)-K0
      >(3/5)c-(2/3)k-K0 >= c/2.                     (CG5.2)

The last inequality follows from c/10>=4K0 and K0>=k.
If y lies in the old frame, floor(q)>q-1 yields

  theta^c(x)-K0
   =(2/3)[floor(q)-(k+r+1)-(K0+1)]-K0
   >(3/5)c-(2/3)(k+r+3)-(5/3)K0 >= c/2.            (CG5.3)

For the last inequality, c/10>=4K0, r<=k<=K0 and k>=5 give
4K0-(2/3)(k+r+3)-(5/3)K0 >= k-2>0.
Since Pc/2=theta_x, both cases prove the source transfer

                  P(theta^c(x)-k Ns)>=theta_x.      (CG5.4)

For the normal-order transfer, validity gives
d_j(y)>=d_j(x)+L1+1. Put H=a/p^(L1+d_j(x)). From P<=T_j and
d_j(f_(j-1,0))>=d_j(y),

  kP<=H,
  4a/p^(d_j(y))<=H,                                (CG5.5)

where the second inequality uses p>=13>=4. Multiplication of the
definition of o^c by P cancels P from its denominators, so

  P o^c(x)+kP <= La*a/[p*k*p^(d_j(x))]
                    +(2 if x even, 1 if x odd)*H
               <=o_x.                              (CG5.6)

The source coefficient 10 at an even letter is retained. The displayed
2 is merely unused spare room in this local calculation; no downstream
normalization is changed. Equations (CG5.4),(CG5.6) prove the precise
ThresholdPowerLowerBound and OrderNumericBound premises.


6. ONE CANONICAL COLUMN PRODUCT AFTER REDUCTION [CG6]

Take one allowed coarse paired basis product B=A0 G0 from CG1, with
paths nu,kappa and per-letter degrees b',b''. Its coarse margin is
m0=k Ns+1. Let e=e_j, supported on late letters with every exponent
less than P=P_j, and split X^e=ellP*ellN into its plane and normal
monomials. Over f define

  A=A0^P ellP D_j,   G=G0^P ellN,                    (CG6.1)
  dS=sum_(late odd x,c<r) e_(x,c),
  dV=sum_(late even x,c>=r) e_(x,c).

There are at most Ns late letters and k coordinates at each, so

                  dS+dV <= k Ns P.                 (CG6.2)

The exact reduction identity is

  reduction[D_j expand_P(B) X^e]=A G.                (CG6.3)

This is where characteristic p is used. The canonical path polynomials
have integer coefficients, by their wedge/contraction definition.
Their reductions have coefficients in the prime field, where c^P=c.
The Frobenius identity therefore gives expand_P(B)=B^P. It is not
being applied to arbitrary residue-field coefficients. For a general
y=sum c_B B, expand_P(y)=sum c_B expand_P(B) is linear in the original
coefficients; no surjectivity or inverse of coefficient Frobenius is
needed. This distinction is essential when f is larger than F_p.

Support is correct: A uses only W_j; G uses no old-frame variables,
since G0 has degree zero there and ellN is late-supported. Every D_j
uses only plane vector letters, so it contributes zero normal degree.

We prove all the target prefix conditions. Fix a late x=x_i. In the
even case let nu*=nu^i_r and kappa*=kappa^(i+1)_1; in the odd case
let nu*=nu^(i+1)_r and kappa*=kappa^i_1. The relevant prefix is <x
for A and <=x for G in the even case, with these exchanged in the odd
case. IP4 and the generic Laurent support rules give

  minimum(A0^P)>=P nu*,   maximum(G0^P)<=P kappa*;
  minimum(ellP)>=-dS,     maximum(ellN)<=dV.          (CG6.4)

The latter bounds count only covector plane exponents and vector normal
exponents; all other factors have the favorable sign. Multiplication
adds finite Laurent lower/upper bounds, and zero polynomials satisfy
them automatically.

Every determinant factor of D_j has nonnegative minimum order at any
prefix, since all its variables are vectors. The specific D_(j,x)
uses r distinct vector rows strictly before x. Twisting them all
multiplies their determinant by the rank-one torus determinant t, so
that factor has order 1; its s_x-th power contributes s_x. Hence

  minimum(A)>=P nu*-dS+s_x,
  maximum(G)<=P kappa*+dV.                           (CG6.5)

Choose M_x=P kappa*+dV. The coarse path gap nu*>=kappa*+k Ns+1,
(CG6.2), and P>mStar from (CG3.4) imply

  P nu*-dS+s_x >= M_x+mStar+s_x = M_x+m_x.          (CG6.6)

For the always clause use nu^i_r>=theta^c(x) and (CG5.4):

  v_<x(A)>=P nu^i_r-dS+s_x
           >=P(theta^c(x)-k Ns)>=theta_x.            (CG6.7)

Finally every support monomial of AG has target normal degree

  even x: P b''_i+sum_(c>=r)e_(x,c),
  odd x:  P b'_i +sum_(c<r)e_(x,c).                  (CG6.8)

There is no determinant term in the odd case because all determinant
letters are even. Coarse path membership gives b''_i<=o^c(x) at an
even x and b'_i<=o^c(x) at an odd x. Each label coordinate is <P, so
(CG6.8) is bounded by P o^c(x)+kP<=o_x, by (CG5.6).

Thus AG satisfies all actual support, prefix gap, lower threshold, and
normal-order conditions over f. CG1 puts it in XX'_f for the fine
parameters and supplies an integral lift in XX_j. This proves the
substantive content of all four premises of column_word_reduction_of_four
and the resulting ColumnWordReductionLifts assertion.


7. GENERAL SEGMENTS AND PRESERVATION OF MULTIHOMOGENEITY [CG7]

For y in M_j(b), define the actual polynomial

                 z_j=D_j expand_(P_j)(y) X^(e_j).   (CG7.1)

By CG1, y is an O-linear combination of allowed coarse paired basis
products. The operation in (CG7.1) is O-linear in y. Apply CG6 to every
basis product and take the same integral linear combination of their
lifts. This gives h_j in XX_j with reduction(h_j)=reduction(z_j).

The polynomial z_j is homogeneous in each whole letter. Its degree
at letter v is explicitly

  beta_j(v)=P_j b(v)+sum_c e_(j,v,c)
       +sum_(x late) s_x #{i: predecessor_row(j,x,i)=v}.           (CG7.2)

Every determinant term has one variable from each of its rows, so its
letter degree is independent of the determinant permutation. The other
two terms in (CG7.2) are respectively exponent expansion and the label.
This directly proves SegmentImageMultihomogeneous.

Project h_j onto its total letter-multidegree beta_j component. By CG1(c)
this projection stays in XX_j. It commutes with residue reduction and
fixes z_j. Therefore we may choose an integral x_j such that

  x_j in XX_j,  deg_letter(x_j)=beta_j,
                    reduction(x_j)=reduction(z_j). (CG7.3)

The degree equality means membership in the indicated homogeneous
submodule, so it includes x_j=0. This is exactly
SegmentReductionLiftsMultihomogeneous; no unproved homogeneous-lift
principle is being invoked.


8. ASSEMBLY INTO ONE ACTUAL GENERATOR [CG8]

Take a coarse u. If u=0, choose g=0: because J>=1, the product with
one zero segment factor and label zero is an allowed Gen element.
All label inequalities hold since the relevant powers of p are positive.

Suppose u!=0. Split its label by CG2, obtain the needed internal
degree lower bounds from CG4, and choose all x_j by CG7. The exact
polynomial identity before reduction is

  Delta*u=Z^ell0 product_(0<=j<J) phi_j(z_j).         (CG8.1)

It follows by distributing products and by
phi^c_j=phi_j composed with expand_(P_j). Define

                    g=Z^ell0 product_j phi_j(x_j). (CG8.2)

It has a fine permitted label and every segment factor belongs to XX_j.
Its physical multidegree is the same as the nonzero polynomial Delta*u:
for any letter-multihomogeneous factor of degree beta_j, substitution
by phi_j gives physical degree at t equal to

       sum_(v mod n'=t) p^(d_j(v)) beta_j(v).        (CG8.3)

Thus replacing z_j by x_j preserves every row degree, irrespective of
whether the replacement product vanishes. By SD3 and the domain property,
Delta*u!=0. Its row degree is E+a'=d by CG3. Consequently g has the
required degree d, and g belongs to the literal Dat.gen=Gen set.

Residue reduction commutes with substitutions and products. Applying
(CG7.3) in (CG8.1)--(CG8.2) now gives (CG0.1). This proves
GeneratorModuloUniformiser(C0) under the actual hypotheses C0>=32.

The two occurrences of nonzeroness in the source are accounted for:
u!=0 identifies the Step2 internal degrees; Delta*u!=0 identifies the
target total physical degree. Neither asserts a nonzero residue for u
or for g. No choice of a new frame, no limit, and no kernel replay is
hidden in this finite construction.
