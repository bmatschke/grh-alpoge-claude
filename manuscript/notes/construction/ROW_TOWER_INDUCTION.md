# Comparing the two theta integrals throughout a row square tower

Fix one prescribed square of the row grid and one tuple of characters. A stage is obtained by removing hyperbolic planes from its sources and target. Write $a,n$ for the current source ranks, $p,q$ for their defects, $d=a+n$, $h=p+q$, and $e=(a-n)/2$. At the real places the signed source signatures are $(a-p,p)$ and $(q,n-q)$ and the target signature is $(d-h,h)$. The row inequalities are $n,e>2h+1$ and $\kappa_w=m_{\alpha,w}-m_{\beta,w}>d$ at every real place. Under the standing local theta and Weil identities, the definite doubling comparison with nonvanishing, and the row character hypotheses, the two integrated theta functions are proportional on every matched test at every stage. The constant is nonzero, and at least one matched pair has a nonzero integral. On the small side of the analytic comparison the finite test is primed at the specified split place. On the prescribed grid one uses the regularized interpretation below.

There are two inductions. The first proves a vanishing statement for differences of **arbitrary** upper tests. The second identifies the constant for **matched** upper tests. This distinction matters because restriction of the unwanted coefficient to a Levi subgroup generally produces two lower tests for which there is no matching relation.

## Geometry and the hypotheses carried through a removal

A beta removal replaces $(a,n;p,q)$ by $(a,n-2;p,q-1)$ and an alpha removal by $(a-2,n;p-1,q)$. The target loses one hyperbolic plane in either case. The latter geometric removal is available whenever $p>0$ even though the numerical alpha recurrence is used only when $q=0$. The rank difference $e$ changes by $+1$ or $-1$, respectively. The lower small rank stays at least $n-2$, the target rank drops by two and $h$ drops by one. The strict row inequalities, parity of the restrictions of the two splitting characters, and $\kappa_w>d_{\mathrm{lower}}$ all persist. [Stage inequalities][range] · [Different uses of the two removals][step]

The [rational splitting and integral partner argument](ROW_BETA_RATIONAL_PLANES.md) applies whenever a plane is requested. The rank inequalities keep each indefinite residual space in the isotropic range at every finite completion. The signatures bound its rational Witt index from above. Choose the rational planes and self-dual complements before varying the tuple. Construct the auxiliary lower triples also on the branch which removes a big-source plane to test pure alpha. Only finitely many descendants occur for any given original square. At a fixed removal the lower split basis can be extended by primitive plane columns and the lower real Cartan involution by the hyperbolic interchange. Fields and characters stay the same. Markings needed on two different branches can be chosen separately. The [change of presentation](ROW_FIRST_BETA_GRID_RATIOS.md#from-the-fixed-model-to-the-prescribed-grid) carries polynomial identities up to an invertible scalar. Equivalently, a change of positive real oscillator metric carries Schwartz witnesses to Schwartz witnesses. Continuity, polynomial density and priming in the untouched finite slot recover a nonzero polynomial witness in the new realization. [Transfer of nonvanishing and order of the choices][moving]

One may take any of the normalized actual local quotient maps on these triples. In the real Fock construction the two source-character models have a nonzero common simple quotient, with the cyclic compact types and infinitesimal actions fixed by the row exponents. This uses the two polynomial models and their generator relations as well as their weights. At a split finite place the normalized maps are the two rectangular zeta transforms. At an inert place the two local spherical realizations identify the nonzero vacuum classes. These constructions depend on the local spaces and actions and precede the comparison of the integrated theta functions. The same real maps can be used for twists with the same infinity type. ## Elimination of the pure alpha term for every $q>0$

For fixed $q>0$ we prove by induction on $p$ that a polynomial theta difference with a pure alpha line constant term is identically zero. Here pure alpha means that the full constant term along one rational line transforms by $\alpha(l)|l|_E^{n/2}$ on the line Levi, at all right arguments. Witt extension carries this assertion to any rational line needed in the proof. The [axis calculation](ROW_TOWER_AXES.md) gives the case $p=0$, including its cuspidal orthogonality argument.

Suppose $p>0$. Remove a plane from the **big source** and a plane containing the chosen target line. Put $b=a-2$ and denote the remainder of the target by $W_1$. Let $C_L$ be the line constant term of the upper difference. For each upper compact target component $k$ the function
$$
 F_k(z)=C_L(j(z)k),\qquad z\in U(W_1)(\mathbb A_F),
$$
is a difference of lower theta functions on $(p-1,q)$, where $j$ is the complementary Levi embedding. Indeed its two summands are the big alpha cone and the primed ordinary small evaluation. The convergence, polynomiality and primed correction for this statement are the ones proved in the [single-plane unfolding](ROW_TOWER_BOUNDARY_MAPS.md#unfolding-over-a-noncompact-lower-source). At this point their **inputs may be arbitrary**. On the lower target a line exists because its defect is $(p-1)+q\ge1$. Its constant term has at most the two laws $\alpha(s)|s|_E^{n/2}$ and $\beta(s)|s|_E^{b/2}$ by the same orbit calculation, including the definite-source case.

Here the [opposite dilations on two lines](ROW_MIXED_LINE_REMAINDER.md) apply without a condition on the rest of $W_1$. For clarity, the two successive line averages are the average along an isotropic flag of length two. On the embedded $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb A_E)$ acting on that plane, the same flag average is the upper-unipotent mean of the plane constant term. Opposite dilations $u,u^{-1}$ on the two lines fix the alpha-alpha component and multiply the alpha-beta component by $\lambda=\alpha(u)\beta(u^{-1})|u|_E^{(n-b)/2}$, where $|u|_E>1$ and $b>n$. The upper-unipotent mean thus satisfies the two-root recurrence with roots $1,\lambda$ at every right argument. Reduction on $\mathrm{SL}_2/E$, smoothing by a compact bump and induction on the growth exponent force the mean to be fixed by the dilation. This is the one-variable argument in the cited proof. Its assumptions are continuity, rational invariance, moderate growth and the two-root law. They follow here from compact unipotent averaging and the [source estimates](ROW_TOWER_INTEGRABILITY.md). It follows that the lower beta component is zero. The recurrence has been applied to the plane average before making any assertion of cuspidality. [Pure-alpha induction on the unchanged small defect][vanind]

Thus each $F_k$ is pure alpha. The induction hypothesis on $(p-1,q)$ gives $F_k=0$. Parabolic Iwasawa and the known alpha law now give $C_L=0$ everywhere. The [induction on the dimension of an isotropic subspace](ROW_TOWER_PARABOLICS.md) then kills every other constant term. Both sources here have a rational hyperbolic plane. The reverse pairing over either one vanishes against the cuspidal difference, using its local realization through the opposite rectangle at a good split place. Hence its Petersson norm is zero and the upper difference vanishes. This proves the induction for arbitrary upper differences on the prescribed towers.

## Nonzero scalar and its recurrence

Induct on $h$ with the quantifier **for every compatible presentation and every normalized matched triple on the descendants in question**. The assertion includes a nonzero constant, a nonzero big polynomial lift and a nonzero primed small polynomial lift. This permits the induction hypothesis to be used on the actual markings chosen for the next boundary map. At $h=0$ the [definite comparison](../theta/ROW_DEFINITE_THETA_COMPARISON.md) and the [definite nonvanishing tests](ROW_MATCHED_SQUARE_TESTS.md#nonzero-integrated-kernels-at-the-definite-bottom) supply these assertions.

If $q>0$, apply the [beta boundary comparison](ROW_TOWER_BOUNDARY_MAPS.md) to the lower triangle $(p,q-1)$ with its nonzero scalar $c_1$. Take
$$
 c=\frac{c_1}{C_\beta D^\beta L^{S_0}(e+1,\nu)} .
$$
The Hecke product is absolutely convergent and nonzero and the local marked factors are nonzero. The beta terms agree after every simultaneous compact target translation of a matched pair, since the real and finite projections intertwine that translation. Their common dilation law and Iwasawa extend the identity to every right argument. The difference $I_b-cI_s$ has pure alpha line constant term, hence is zero by the first induction. The nonzero beta boundary obtained by ordinary evaluation detects the big lift, and a matched small partner detects the small lift.

If $q=0$ and $p>0$, the [axis calculation](ROW_TOWER_AXES.md#the-large-source-when-the-small-defect-is-zero) kills the beta terms of the individual lifts. Apply the alpha comparison on $(p-1,0)$ and put
$$
 c=C_\alpha D^\alpha L^{S_0}(e,\nu)c_1 .
$$
The same translation and Iwasawa argument makes the alpha terms agree everywhere. The zero line constant term then implies cuspidality by the parabolic induction. Orthogonality on that axis gives zero norm for the difference. Ordinary small evaluation detects a nonzero boundary and the matched big partner has a nonzero image. Full rank at a prescribed place can be imposed on the definite small source by replacing the finite vector by a representative of its source coinvariant class. All terms converge on this compact quotient. In either case the witness makes $c$ unique. Simultaneous polynomial approximants in the prescribed matching relation give the matched Schwartz identity by the continuous bounds. ## Passage to the prescribed grid

Perform the successive beta removals and then the alpha removals. For $\{t,0\}$, with $0$ the reference tuple, the [fitted base and bottom-up frame extensions](ROW_MIXED_GRID_RATIOS.md#one-fitted-base-and-compatible-planes-for-the-two-removals) iterate verbatim. The hypotheses on exponents and character restrictions persist at every removal as checked above. Extend the affected source and target frames on one plane at a time, copying the unaffected frame. The chosen rational complements are fixed for the family, and the numerical local comparison for each removal uses its actual plane ideals. At **every stage** the assigned finite pair represents the same nonzero restricted tensor up to the calculated change of frame. In the standard frame the final $n$ columns of the small test are invertible at the permanent marked place, even at the reference twist. Apply the full-rank bound there to all real polynomials. While that place supplies convergence one can replace a vacuum at the designated auxiliary place by its primed representative without changing the integral. Since the lower and upper individual lifts are nonzero on their simple quotient, they are injective there. Thus a fixed nonzero real quotient image detects the standard tests for each twist. This extends the [standard-test factorization](ROW_STAGE_STANDARD_TESTS.md) to the whole tower.

For the top grid flip, its small input may require regularization. Use a representative with full rank at the designated place. A single fresh split-place regularizer works on every term of the finite sum of source-translation differences from the original representative. Its nonzero degree identifies their common regularized value. The [commuting-regularizer identity](ROW_SQUARE_REGULARIZATION.md) then gives $d(z)I_b=g_{ij,t}I_s(z)$ for **every** allowed convergent translate combination, including degree zero. The comparison of vacua, finite flip, endpoint characters and quotient measures uses only the normalized local zeta maps, as in the [top-stage calibration](ROW_FIRST_BETA_GRID_RATIOS.md#from-the-fixed-model-to-the-prescribed-grid). By [prescribing the local tests before finding a block endpoint](ROW_MATCHED_SQUARE_TESTS.md#prescribing-the-finite-vectors-before-finding-the-global-endpoint), the polynomial nonvanishing witness yields an allowed seed and endpoint with nonzero big value. This makes $g_{ij,t}$ nonzero on the prescribed grid. Real quotient maps and their tied relation were fixed before choosing the twist. Changing the common real normalization multiplies the resulting constants by one scalar independent of the twist.

The hypotheses of the [cancellation calculation](ROW_MIXED_GRID_RATIOS.md#conditional-cancellation-throughout-an-original-square-tower) now hold for every column on these standing inputs. The product of the square laws also gives the [opposite-path identity](ROW_SQUARE_PATH_FLIP.md) with an invertible multiplier. This compares two actual iterated lifts. The [mixed-cell unfolding](ROW_MIXED_CELL_UNFOLDING.md) evaluates the resulting period and the [full product calculation](ROW_GRID_PRODUCT.md) gives its normalized square factor on the first pass.
