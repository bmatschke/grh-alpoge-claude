# Coefficient rings and anticyclotomic class groups for the first list

Fix the fitted intermediate state $x$. Let $E$ be its CM field, $L_0$ its coefficient number field with complex embedding $e_0$, and $\Omega$ its chosen algebraic closure of $E$. Use the original character frame, its value conditions in $e_0(L_0)$, the stability of $L_0$ under complex conjugation, and the integrality of its archimedean exponents $e_{j,\tau}$. The twist rings and class groups can be constructed simultaneously for **every nonzero prime** $i$ of $\mathcal O_{L_0}$. Write $\ell$ for the rational prime under $i$. Distinct primes above $\ell$ retain their separate coefficient embeddings. [Ring and character requirements][spec] · [Exact order of the construction][assembly]

## The three coefficient rings

Complete $L_0$ at $i$ and embed the completion in $\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell$. Its restriction $J_i$ satisfies $x\in i\iff |J_i x|_\ell<1$ for every $x\in\mathcal O_{L_0}$. For any coefficient embedding $J$ at $\ell$, take the finite compositum $c_{\rm rings}(\ell,J)$ of $\mathbf Q_\ell J(L_0)$, $\mathbf Q_\ell(\mu_\ell)$, and the normal closures of the embedded fields $E_v$ for the finitely many $v\mid\ell$. For the last factors use prolongations identifying the given valuation rings $A_v\subset\Omega$ with $\{x:|j_vx|_\ell\le1\}$. Extension to completions identifies the decomposition group with $G_{E_v}$, and adjoining the normal closure includes all conjugate images of $E_v$. Thus any larger coefficient field supports the required local identifications for every $v\mid\ell$. The lower bound works for every $J$ with the same quantified rule. [Construction of the finite lower bound][low] · [Valuation and decomposition-group comparison][bridges]

The ring construction permits independent finite extensions $K_0=b_0(\ell,J_i)$ and $L'=b_1(\ell,J_i)$ of $\mathbf Q_\ell$, requiring only $K_0\supset c_{\rm rings}(\ell,J_i)$. In the first list take $b_0=b_1$ equal pointwise to the compositum of this lower bound and the one supplied by the cuspidal multiplicity calculation. Hence $K_0=L'$ at that index. Define inside $\mathbf C_\ell$
$$
 U=K_0L'\mathbf Q_\ell^{\rm nr},\qquad
 O_0=\mathcal O_{K_0},\qquad
 O=\mathcal O_{\widehat U},\qquad
 O_k=\mathcal O_{\widehat{U(\mu_{\ell^k})}}\quad(k\ge 0).
$$
Here $\mathbf Q_\ell^{\rm nr}$ denotes the algebraic maximal unramified extension and hats denote closures in $\mathbf C_\ell$. The ring $O_0$ is finite free over $\mathbf Z_\ell$ with finite residue field. The union of unramified extensions in $U$ preserves the value group of $K_0L'$ and gives residue field $\overline{\mathbf F}_\ell$. Completion preserves that discrete value group and residue field. Thus $O$ is a complete DVR with algebraically closed residue field and $\ell$ in its maximal ideal. Its fraction field embeds in $\mathbf C_\ell$ with $O$ exactly the inverse image of the closed unit ball. Explicitly, $a/b$ with $b\ne0$ is integral precisely when $|a|_\ell\le |b|_\ell$. [Rings as embedded closures][defs] · [DVR and unit-ball assertions][dvr] · [Fraction-field embedding][norm]

The same proof applies to the finite starting field $K_0L'(\mu_{\ell^k})$. A primitive root $\zeta_{\ell^k}$ is a unit and generates the extension of completed fraction fields, since a finite extension of a complete valued field is complete. In a power basis the coordinate maps are bounded, by equivalence of norms in finite dimension over the complete base field. The unit ball $O_k$ is therefore an $O$-submodule of one fixed denominator multiple of the free power-basis lattice. Since $O$ is Noetherian, $O_k$ is finite over $O$. For each fraction $z\in\operatorname{Frac}(O_k)$ expand it in powers of $\zeta_{\ell^k}$ over $\operatorname{Frac}(O)$ and clear the finitely many coefficient denominators. This gives $dz=P(\zeta_{\ell^k})$ with $0\ne d\in O$ and $P\in O[X]$. At $k=0$ the root is $1$ and $O_k=O$. The two coefficient embeddings into these fraction fields are induced by $J_i$ and $J_i\circ c$, where $c$ is conjugation on $L_0$. Abstract field isomorphisms from $\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell$ and, separately, $\mathbf C_\ell$ to $\mathbf C$ can be chosen to restrict to $e_0$ on $J_i(L_0)$ by extending transcendence bases. These comparisons are algebraic. The integral norm test uses the preceding valued embedding into $\mathbf C_\ell$. [Cyclotomic generation and finiteness][cycl] · [Comparison of embeddings and inclusions][emb]

## The original frame characters

Put $p(i)=p_\delta'$ when $\ell=p_\delta$, and $p(i)=p_\delta$ otherwise. The chosen finite-order Hecke character $\delta_\ell$ is the corresponding member of the original pair. Geometric Artin reciprocity gives an $O_0^\times$-valued character $\Delta_i$ with open kernel on $G_E$. At every $v\nmid\ell$ where $\delta_\ell$ is unramified, its arithmetic Frobenius value is $J_i(e_0^{-1}(\delta_{\ell,v}(\varpi_v)^{-1}))$. Reciprocity identifies the local unit images with inertia. The defining conditions of the frame therefore give ramification precisely at all $v\mid p(i)$, inertia image of order $15$ at each such $v$, and $Nv\equiv1\pmod {15}$. The conjugation law of the original character gives $\Delta_i(cgc^{-1})=\Delta_i(g)^{-1}$, using the fixed lift of CM conjugation on $\Omega$. [Choice of the auxiliary prime][switch] · [Local and global reciprocity conventions](LOCAL_RECIPROCITY.md) · [Inertia calculation][inertia]

For $1\le j\le m$ take the two $\ell$-adic readings of the algebraic Hecke character
$$
 h_{i,j}=\mu_{0,j}\delta_\ell\eta^{-1}|\cdot|_{\mathbb A_E}^{-r/2}.
$$
Here $r$ is odd. The integral exponents of $\mu_{0,j}$ and the angular exponent one of $\eta$ give integral infinity exponents for $h_{i,j}$. Away from ramification its reciprocal uniformizer value is $(\mu_{0,j}\delta_\ell\eta^{-1})_v(\varpi_v)^{-1}(Nv)^{-r/2}$. At these unramified places the frame puts the uniformizer values of $\mu_{0,j}$ and $\delta_\ell$ in $e_0(L_0)$ and puts $\eta_v(\varpi_v)(Nv)^{-1/2}$ there. Since $(r-1)/2$ is integral, the displayed value also belongs to $e_0(L_0)$. Form the usual $\ell$-adic avatar by moving the algebraic infinity component to the places over $\ell$. On the two sides identify coefficients by $J_i$ and $J_i\circ c$, respectively. Frobenius density and closedness of $K_0$ put the entire character image in $K_0^\times$. Compactness of $G_E$ puts it in $O_0^\times$, since the valuation of a compact subgroup of $K_0^\times$ is a finite subgroup of $\mathbf Z$. At $v\mid p(i)$ the other factors are trivial on units. The two sides give $\Delta_i$ and $\Delta_i^{-1}$ on inertia. At $v\mid g_k$, $v\nmid\ell$, the split type and the prescribed tame character make the inertia exponent divide $g_k-1$. Elsewhere away from $\ell$ the character is unramified outside these listed places. This gives the reference readings with weight $-r$ on both sides. [Algebraic avatar and its field of values][avatar] · [Two sides and all inertia conditions][inertia] · [Norm twist in the row convention](LOCAL_AUTOMORPHIC_SPECIALIZATION.md)

## Finite class groups and excluded places

For any finite set $Q$ of finite places of $E$, form the quotient $A_i(Q)$ of $G_E$ by the intersection of the kernels of all continuous maps to finite abelian $\ell$-groups which intertwine conjugation with inversion and kill inertia outside $Q$ and at every $v\mid\ell$. It is finite. Indeed, put $\mathfrak m=\prod_{v\in Q,\ v\nmid\ell}v$. At each $v\mid\mathfrak m$ the principal units form a pro-residue-characteristic group, so a map to a finite $\ell$-group kills them. At every other finite place inertia is already trivial, and $E$ has only complex infinite places. Thus each map factors through the same finite ray class group modulo $\mathfrak m$ by global reciprocity. The quotient by the intersection remains finite, abelian and $\ell$-primary. This argument also works when $Q$ is not conjugation stable. The natural map onto $A_i(Q)$ has open kernel and satisfies the conjugation law. When $Q$ avoids $\ell$ it is universal for *all* such anticyclotomic maps unramified outside $Q$. For $Q'\subset Q$ the admissible kernels for $Q'$ form a subfamily, giving the compatible surjection $A_i(Q)\to A_i(Q')$. [Exact universal property][groups] · [Finite-index argument][finite] · [Artin's reciprocity theorem](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02952531) · [Idelic and ideal formulations, Milne, *Class Field Theory*, V.3–5](https://www.jmilne.org/math/CourseNotes/CFTc.pdf)

One can compute the quotient even when $Q$ contains only one member of a conjugate pair. Write $Q^*=(Q\cap cQ)\setminus\{v\mid\ell\}$ and $C=\mathrm{Cl}_{\prod_{v\in Q^*}v}(E)[\ell^\infty]$. Ramification of an anticyclotomic quotient is stable under conjugation, because the images of conjugate inertia groups are inverse subgroups. Its conductor thus divides this smaller, conjugation-stable modulus. On $C$ the relation that conjugation act by inversion is precisely $a\,c(a)=1$ for every $a$. Artin reciprocity and the universal property give
$$
 A_i(Q)\simeq C/\langle a\,c(a):a\in C\rangle .
$$
The quotient formula also applies at $\ell=2$, without dividing by $2$ or choosing a minus eigenspace. [Universal quotient][groups] · [Functoriality of reciprocity](LOCAL_RECIPROCITY.md)

The ring construction allows any extra finite set $S_x$. In the application choose it to contain **all** places over $\mathrm{lowAll}$, namely the places over $2,q_0,g_0,g_1,p_\delta,p_\delta'$ and the rational prime divisors of $N\mathfrak c_K$ and $|\operatorname{disc}(E)|$, with union interpreted literally. This is exactly $S_x$ and is stable under conjugation. The full excluded set at $i$ is
$$
 S_0(i)=S_x\cup\{v\mid\ell\}\cup\{v\mid g_0g_1p_\delta p_\delta'\}
       =S_x\cup\{v\mid\ell\}.
$$
This formula retains both auxiliary primes even though $\Delta_i$ uses only one. [Low rational primes][lowpr] · [All four added primes][lowall] · [Choice of the exact finite union][sx] · [Excluded-place formula][exc]

## Integral coordinates for the spaces of forms

Suppose the multiplicity calculation supplies the finitely generated full $O$-lattices $B^{\rm sup}_{i,j,b}$ in the form spaces over $\operatorname{Frac}(O)$ for the two sides $b$. Each lattice is torsion free over a DVR and hence free. Any integral basis becomes a fraction-field basis, by clearing denominators in a relation and using fullness. A form belongs to this lattice exactly when every basis coordinate has norm at most one in $\mathbf C_\ell$. Use the disjoint union of the two basis-index sets as the evaluation set for $(i,j)$, extending each side's coordinate functions by zero on the other side. Evaluation is injective and the coefficient matrix in that basis gives the evaluation identity for every Hecke endomorphism. Rank zero gives an empty index set on the corresponding side. This constructs the two auxiliary coordinate records called `NTh` for the empty and the toric character families, using the same finite-generation and fullness statements. Their bare construction permits a zero theta-evaluation tensor. The separate identities equating evaluations with the actual theta maps, when called for, require the corresponding comparison. The pointwise first-list call uses the two coordinate records together with its independent side-data implication.
