RESULT AND SCOPE
===============
Under the literal Std(C0,Cde,CE1,CE2,H,X) with H>=1, the upper
rank-on-digits inequality is proved for every segment, every compatible
digit class, every margin M>=0, and the actual characteristic-zero
coefficient DVR. There is no further representation, first-row, matching,
or finite-count premise. The formula is

 rankOnDigits <= h_(r+1)(A_*) product_i[h_(r+1)(A_i)h_(r+1)(C_i)]
                           h_(r+1)(B),
 h_d(g)=binom(g+d-1,d-1).

Here r=X.m, k=r+1, A_*=S.A0(p), A_i=coarseA_i, C_i=coarseC_i,
and B=S.bPlus. These are the exact source mmB, not bounds replacing its
degrees. The report closes SW8's upper-rank input. The lower estimate is
the separate RD component. Root's uniform numerical component supplies
Std from HIext and the nested constant choices; RU8 spells out the
composition with that result without claiming to reprove it here.

RU9--RU11 additionally close the actual segment-to-HH assembly. They
use RD9's proved fixed-digit lower estimate and RL11's proved pair
lifts, sum the compatible digits, recover the full tensor dimension,
extract block-supported partners, and remove the pairing error. The
conclusion is precisely HerrExtensionClass / aux_3 for supplied
GoodShare data, with depth sum_j D_j+4 kappa_0 and mass
(1-kappa_P-sum_j kappa_j) dimHH/gaussian(q,k). Supplying the final
particular GoodShare depths and averaging their losses is the
separate SW/RD/NP numerical application, not an unstated consequence
of this assembly lemma.

No source file was edited and no Lean compilation or kernel replay was
performed. The short proofs below are checked against declarations in
provenance.json. Already proved shared inputs are SW1, SW10, IP3--IP4
and CB3; every use and its exact interface is explained.

NOTATION AND THE LITERAL DATA
============================
O is a DVR, K its fraction field. For the path dimensions K has
characteristic zero. In the source's UniformizedDVR p O, this follows
from p being nonzero and lying in the maximal ideal: positive
characteristic p contradicts p!=0; positive characteristic q!=p makes
p an integer unit, also impossible. There is no need to identify K
with Q_p or to assume integral semisimplicity.

A segment has s interior positions i=0,...,s-1, split at sigma into
early positions i<sigma and late positions i>=sigma. For actual segment
data 0<=sigma<=s. Its physical distances d_i are strictly increasing,
and P_i=p^(G-d_i), Q_j=p^G. A compatible digit class consists of
rho_i^z,rho_i^y in {0,...,P_i-1}^{r+1} with sums at most the relevant
physical degrees b_i,a_i and with the differences divisible by P_i.
Consequently

 A_i=(b_i-sum_c rho^z_(i,c))/P_i,
 C_i=(a_i-sum_c rho^y_(i,c))/P_i

are nonnegative integers. A_*=floor((a-E0)/Q_j) is an INTEGER in the
definition; its positivity is proved below. The endpoint degree is B.

The actual block data consist of nonnegative o_i,w_i,s_i and o_+.
We write s_i for the source field sg_i, to avoid confusing it with the
segment length. The relevant exact Kept clauses are:

 early i: o_i=0, w_i<=A_i, s_i<=C_i,
          s_i<=2 lambda A_*/k;
 late i:  o_i<=A_i, w_i=s_i=0.

Kept also imposes its displayed finer upper bounds on w_i+s_i, o_i
and o_+. Those conditions are not dropped from the source set; the
upper-bound proof simply works for the larger set obeying the clauses
used here. Define e_i=w_i+s_i early and e_i=o_i late, and

 c_n=A_*+sum_(i<n)(A_i-e_i).                              (R.1)

These are exactly oEff and cap. The polynomial chain is
V_0,E_0,...,V_(s-1),E_(s-1),U: V_i and U are vector letters,
E_i are covectors. A level is (j_i for late i, j_+), where
0<=j_i<=min(o_i,C_i) and 0<=j_+<=min(o_+,B).
Extend j_i=0 on early positions. The residual chain degrees are

             degree V_i                 degree E_i
 early       w_i                        C_i-s_i
 late        o_i-j_i                    C_i-j_i,
 degree U=o_+-j_+.                                      (R.2)

All displayed differences are ordinary nonnegative differences; this
checks the source's natural subtraction rather than interpreting it
as an unrestricted integer difference.

The actual prefix constraints are the first-weight cutoffs
floor(c_i-M) just after V_i for every late i, and floor(c_s-M)
at the full word. The auxiliary xi polynomial has degree B-j_+.
This is literally blockData.lettersAt, blockData.T and LevelOK.
The powers z_i=A_i-o_i for late i and z_+=aPlus-o_+ enter only as
fixed Z-variable multipliers. Even where the latter is the source
natural subtraction, none of the dimension or matching steps changes it.

RU1. THE ACTUAL BLOCK RANK, NOT AN AMBIENT SUBSTITUTE
==================================================
For any source BlockData D, let Y_(j,j_+) be the constrained residual
chain space over K and Y_(j,j_+),O its intersection with O[X].
Let Xi_b be the degree-b polynomial module in the r independent xi
variables. A level maps by multiplication into the actual polynomial
ring as

 (f,g) -> (product_i Ehat_i^j_i) Eplus^j_+
          (product_i Z_i^z_i) Z_+^z_+ f(X)g(xi).          (R.3)

The Ehat_i are Z_i S_i plus their actual vector-covector pairing;
Eplus is Z_+ S_+ plus the terminal pairing with xi.

SW1's proof is valid for this literal map: take the lexicographically
largest S-degree (j_1,...,j_t,j_+) in a finite relation. Its coefficient
is the nonzero fixed Z monomial times the corresponding coefficient
polynomial f(X)g(xi); smaller levels cannot contribute to it. The X
and xi variable sets are disjoint, so multiplication identifies their
tensor product. The coefficient ring is a domain. Therefore (R.3),
summed over all legal levels, is injective.

Its integral image is the definition of SSZ. Every rational f in Y
has an integral multiple in Y_O by clearing its finitely many
coefficients; scalar multiplication preserves the rational prefix
conditions. The same holds for the xi factor. Thus extension to K
gives precisely the direct sum of all Y_(j,j_+) tensor Xi_(B-j_+).
There are finitely many levels by LevelOK, and

 blockRank(D)=sum_(legal j,j_+) dim_K Y_(j,j_+) h_r(B-j_+). (R.4)

This proves BlockRankAt, with the actual integral span in its left
side. It works over any DVR at this step. It also is the rank of the
actual blockLat: blockLat lies in K SSZ and contains SSZ because
ordinary integral coefficients satisfy the factorial-weighted
coefficient inequalities. Equivalently, multiplying a rational
basis by a sufficiently high uniformizer power gives full rank in
that weighted lattice. No equality between the weighted lattice and
the coefficient lattice is asserted.

RU2. THE EXACT PREFIX DIMENSION AND UNRESTRICTED PATH MASS
=======================================================
For a dominant integer r-tuple lambda define

 D_r(lambda)=product_(0<=a<b<r)
                 (lambda_a-lambda_b+b-a)/(b-a).

A signed strip path starts at kappa^0=0. At a vector step of degree d,
kappa^(l+1)/kappa^l is a horizontal strip of size d; at a covector
step the reverse difference is a horizontal strip. In coordinates
the first condition is

 kappa^l_a<=kappa^(l+1)_a,
 kappa^(l+1)_(a+1)<=kappa^l_a,
 sum_a(kappa^(l+1)_a-kappa^l_a)=d.                         (R.5)

This is exactly CountDefs.Strip and IsChain. It implies dominance
at every stage. Each coordinate stays between minus the total
covector degree and the total vector degree, so the path set is
finite, independently of any formal infinite-sum convention.

IP3 is the integral basis by the literal signed path polynomials and
standard terminal column choices. IP4 says the simultaneous prefix
maximum conditions select exactly the basis paths with
kappa^(J_l)_0<=M_l. The source YCond is the largest Lie-stable
subspace with that coordinate-weight cutoff. In characteristic zero
this equals the generic-group weight condition in IP4: a Lie-stable
subspace is stable under exponentials of the nilpotent root actions;
the diagonal operators decompose it into weight spaces, and the
torus and elementary root subgroups generate GL_r. Conversely a
group-stable space is Lie-stable by differentiation. Thus IP4 applies
to the literal Yjt, with no new filtration identification assumed.

For fixed path, the number of standard terminal choices is
D_r(kappa^N) by IP3's tableau identification and CB3's finite
Vandermonde count. Adding a common integer determinant shift to make
the endpoint a partition changes neither that number nor D_r.
Therefore

 dim_K Yjt(LL,J,M)
   = sum_(signed paths with prefix bounds) D_r(kappa^N).  (R.6)

This is the exact DimYAt, for arbitrary finite prefix constraints,
including infinite thresholds. Prefix J=0 is either unrestricted
or zero according to its cutoff; this agrees on both sides.
For no constraints, the left side has its ordinary monomial basis:

 sum_(all paths) D_r(kappa^N)=product_l h_r(d_l).          (R.7)

This proves ChainDimAt. Alternatively (R.7) follows by iterated
signed Pieri insertion from IP3; no new representation dimension
formula is required. Combining (R.4)--(R.6), and summing over kept
blocks in a fixed digit class, proves exactly rankOnDigits_cast.
Every source path carries weight D_r(kappa^N)h_r(B-j_+).

RU3. INTERLACING BRANCHING AND THE TERMINAL SUM
=============================================
For every dominant integer (r+1)-tuple nu,

 D_(r+1)(nu)=sum_(nu_(a+1)<=lambda_a<=nu_a) D_r(lambda).   (R.8)

This is NumBranchAt. It follows directly from CB3: removing the
largest tableau entries gives the interlacing shape, and the discrete
Vandermonde determinant evaluates the count. Explicitly, for strictly
decreasing x_0>...>x_r,

 sum_(x_(a+1)<=y_a<x_a) Delta(y)=Delta(x)/r!.

Write Delta(y) as a determinant of powers, sum each row using a
polynomial antiderivative for the forward difference, and take
successive differences of the rows of the augmented determinant
with final column 1. Its leading coefficients multiply to 1/r!.
Set x_a=nu_a+r-a and y_a=lambda_a+r-1-a. A common integral shift
handles signed nu. If r=0, both sides are 1.

For r>=1 the remaining terminal sum is the exact hockey-stick identity

 sum_(j_+=0)^B h_r(B-j_+)=h_(r+1)(B).                    (R.9)

It counts monomials of total degree B in r+1 variables by the
exponent j_+ of the last variable. Both (R.8) and (R.9) keep the
actual terminal symmetric-power degree; it is not replaced by a
cruder upper bound.

RU4. THE EARLY PARTIAL WIDTH REALLY FITS THE ACTUAL CAP
====================================================
Here use the literal Std hypotheses with H>=1. Put
lambda=X.La C0, epsilon=N1 lambda/k, delta=X.de C0 Cde, beta=be.
SW10 supplies, with the exact physical-distance geometry,

 k>=5, N1>=1, lambda>=1, p>=k^2, s<=N1,
 40epsilon<=delta<1/6, 0<=beta<=delta/2-18epsilon,
 E0>=0, E0+Q<=epsilon a, b'>=k^3 Q, a>=b',
 A_i(1+delta)<A_* for early i.                         (R.10)

The last assertion is the early half of the already proved coarse
degree alternative, not an added numerical premise. These also show
epsilon<1/240 and beta<1/12. Since Q_j<=Q,

 A_* > (a-E0-Q_j)/Q_j >= (a-E0-Q)/Q >= a/(2Q).

Thus A_*>=k: indeed a>=b'>=k^3 Q and k>=5. The source's slightly
weaker non-strict half-ratio estimate gives the same conclusion.
This checks the positivity of A_* before converting its integer value
to the natural degree in mmLetters.

Compatible digits give the exact coarse inequalities

 A_l P_l<=b_l,              b_i-kP_i<A_i P_i.             (R.11)

For l<i, strict increase of the integer physical distances gives
P_l>=pP_i. The degree window b'(1-beta)<=b_i<=b'(1+beta)
therefore implies

 A_l <= 2(A_i+k)/p.                                      (R.12)

Indeed A_l pP_i<=b'(1+beta), whereas
b'(1-beta)<(A_i+k)P_i. Divide by positive quantities and use
(1+beta)/(1-beta)<=13/11<2. This proves the source
coarse_ratio_small with a strict improvement that is not needed.

For early i, Kept bounds s_l<=2lambda A_*/k for every l<=i.
Hence

 sum_(l<=i)(A_l+s_l)
 <= A_i+s[2(A_i+k)/p+2lambda A_*/k].                     (R.13)

To bound this by A_*, use the actual gap in (R.10). Since
A_i(1+delta)<A_*, with A_i>=0,

 (A_*-A_i)(1+delta)>A_*delta>=40epsilon A_*,
 A_*-A_i>34epsilon A_*.

Also A_i<=A_*, k<=A_*, p>=k^2, lambda>=1 and k>=5 give

 2(A_i+k)/p<=4A_*/k^2<=lambda A_*/k.

The added term in (R.13) is at most 3epsilon A_*, strictly below
the available gap. Thus sum_(l<=i)(A_l+s_l)<=A_*.

Finally, for every n<=i+1, (R.1) and w_l<=A_l show

 sum_(l<=i)w_l-c_n
 = sum_(l<n)(2w_l+s_l-A_l)+sum_(n<=l<=i)w_l-A_*
 <=sum_(l<=i)(A_l+s_l)-A_*<=0.                          (R.14)

This is the actual partial_width_le_cap, simultaneously for all
n<=i+1, including n=i and n=i+1. It does not assume that cap is
monotone on early positions; in general it need not be.

RU5. ALL THE FIRST-ROW CONDITIONS, INCLUDING THE ENDPOINT
========================================================
Let kappa be a residual path satisfying the actual prefix cutoffs.
During any vector strip its first coordinate increases by at most
the strip size; during a covector strip it cannot increase. Thus at
an early V_i,

 kappa^(2i+1)_0<=sum_(l<=i)w_l<=min(c_i,c_(i+1)).          (R.15)

For late i, the literal cutoff gives
kappa^(2i+1)_0<=floor(c_i-M)<=c_i because M>=0.
Here c_(i+1)-c_i=A_i-o_i>=0 by Kept, so it is also <=c_(i+1).
The full-word cutoff gives

 kappa^(2s+1)_0<=floor(c_s-M)<=c_s.                      (R.16)

These prove every FirstRowsAt clause. The early argument is needed
precisely because those earlier cuts are absent from the source TieData.

RU6. THE ACTUAL MATCHING MAP AND ITS INVERSE ON THE IMAGE
=======================================================
Insert an extra first row into the residual path, with a one-step
delay:

 nu^0=0,
 nu^(2i+1)=(c_i,kappa^(2i))                    (0<=i<=s),
 nu^(2i+2)=(c_i+A_i-d_(2i),kappa^(2i+1))       (0<=i<s).  (R.17)

The target signed word has degrees

             +A_*, +A_0, -C_0, ...,+A_(s-1),-C_(s-1).

The first state is (A_*,0,...,0). The rest follows from the elementary
strip extension rule: if nu/mu is a horizontal strip of size d,
f<=f', and nu_0<=f, then (f',nu)/(f,mu) is a horizontal strip of
size d+f'-f. This is checked directly from the adjacent interlacing
inequalities and the difference of the row sums.

For a positive target step, d_(2i)<=A_i. The first-row inequality
needed by the extension rule is kappa^(2i+1)_0<=c_i, given by RU5.
Its total added size is exactly A_i.

For the following negative target step, put
b_i=c_i+A_i-d_(2i). Then

 b_i-c_(i+1)=e_i-d_(2i)=
      s_i on early positions, j_i on late positions,
 (b_i-c_(i+1))+d_(2i+1)=C_i.                            (R.18)

Both differences are nonnegative; these equalities use (R.2)
and its checked nonnegative subtractions. The first-row bound
kappa^(2i+1)_0<=c_(i+1) supplies the remaining interlacing inequality.
Thus nu is literally an IsChain for mmLetters.

Set lambda=kappa^(2s+1). The final positive residual strip says
kappa^(2s)_a<=lambda_a and lambda_(a+1)<=kappa^(2s)_a.
Together with lambda_0<=c_s from RU5 this is exactly

 nu^(2s+1)_(a+1)<=lambda_a<=nu^(2s+1)_a.                (R.19)

The target is the triple (nu,lambda,j_+). It preserves the weight
D_r(lambda)h_r(B-j_+) exactly, and j_+<=B.

It is injective on actual source data. From the tail r coordinates
of nu^(l+1), recover every kappa^l through l=2s. The stored lambda
recovers its endpoint. Strip row-sum differences recover all residual
degrees d_l. Digits fix A_i and C_i, so

 early: w_i=d_(2i), s_i=C_i-d_(2i+1), o_i=0;
 late:  j_i=C_i-d_(2i+1), o_i=d_(2i)+j_i, w_i=s_i=0;
 end:   o_+=d_(2s)+j_+.                                 (R.20)

These formulas recover the complete SegBlock and every level. They
are valid at zero degrees and the extremes of LevelOK. This proves
SrcInjAt and MatchUp for the actual block family, rather than merely
postulating a weight-preserving injection.

RU7. THE FINITE WEIGHTED UPPER COUNT
==================================
Enlarge the image of (R.17) to all triples (nu,lambda,j_+) with nu
an unrestricted target-word path, lambda interlacing its endpoint,
and 0<=j_+<=B. Every such path is finite in number by the coordinate
bounds of RU2, every interlacing range is finite, and all weights are
nonnegative. RU6 is injective and weight preserving; therefore the
source total is at most this target total. No interchange of an
infinite divergent sum is involved.

Sum first over j_+ using (R.9), then over lambda using (R.8), and
then over nu using (R.7) in rank r+1. The result is

 h_(r+1)(B) sum_nu D_(r+1)(nu^end)
 =h_(r+1)(B) h_(r+1)(A_*)
        product_i[h_(r+1)(A_i)h_(r+1)(C_i)].              (R.21)

This is exactly Gt_sum plus prod_kLetters and countUp_of, now with
all their combinatorial inputs proved. The coefficients in the sum
are positive rational Weyl products, in fact integers by RU2--RU3.
The source finite-support convention consequently creates no hidden
zero branch. If a constrained source space is zero, it contributes
zero, and the same argument applies.

RU8. RETURN TO THE ACTUAL LATTICE AND THE UNIFORM STATEMENT
=========================================================
RU1--RU2 identify the source weighted total with the actual
rankOnDigits. RU7 gives (R.21), whose right side is the literal mmB:
symDim(k,A_*) equals h_k(A_*) because A_*>=0, and the remaining
coarse degrees and B are natural. Rational-to-integer casting
preserves the inequality. Thus

 rankOnDigits O C0 CE1 CE2 X j M rho^z rho^y
       <= mmB C0 CE1 CE2 X j rho^z rho^y                 (R.22)

for every M>=0 and compatible digit class under Std(H), H>=1.
In particular M=0 is exactly the ExtendedDigitRankBound upper
inequality used in SW8.1. The proof is independent of the actual
finite residue field, the choice of chart, and the valuation depth.

For transparency, the source's RestUp is the following ordered
constant package: for every Cc>=0 choose T0>0, then for every C0>=T0
choose T1>0, then for every CE1,CE2,Cm>=T1 choose T2>0, after which
the result holds for every CYb,Cde,Ck,CD,Cb>=T2. The proven root
Std-supply theorem with H=1 permits the explicit choices

 T0=T1=1,
 T2=1000(CE1+CE2+1)(Cc+1).                              (R.23)

RU itself does not reprove that numerical supply; it takes the

RU9. SUM THE DIGITS AND RECOVER THE ACTUAL HH DIMENSION
======================================================
This part uses RD9's exact LowAt, with its original lower-margin
condition. It does not infer a lower rank from RU8's upper bound.
For the fully reconstructed application, RD9 is supplied under
C0>=880 and Std(H=4). The conversion itself only needs Std(H),
H>=1, once that LowAt inequality is available. Let J be the number
of segments, N=card(TT),
q_j=p^(segG_j), q=p^n', M>=0 and Ckap>=3. Write

 eta=exp(-lambda/8), eta2=exp(-lambda/3),
 Bcal=eta+k Q(M+N1 k)/b'+1/Y+k E0/a,
 kappa_P=Ckap N Bcal.                                    (R.24)

These are precisely brk and kappaMarginBound; at M=m0 they are the
source Loc.kapP. Denote the actual segRank at margin M by R_j.

9.1. Exact digit sum.
For a physical degree g and positive integer radix P, coordinatewise
division gives a bijection

 {e in N^k:sum e=g} <->
 disjoint union_(rho in [0,P)^k, sum rho<=g,
                 P divides g-sum rho)
       {u in N^k:sum u=(g-sum rho)/P},
             e=rho+P u.

Therefore the sum over compatible rho of h_k((g-sum rho)/P)
is exactly h_k(g), not just an asymptotic estimate. Apply this
independently to every interior vector and covector digit row.
Every kept block has exactly one such digit class. Summing RD9
gives, whenever ceil(M)<=A_*j N1 lambda/k,

 R_j >= L_j h_k(A_*j)
        product_(i interior j)[h_k(b_i)h_k(a_i)] h_k(b_next),
 L_j=(1-eta)(1-eta2)(1-r ceil(M)/A_*j).                   (R.25)

All sums here are finite. Digits are bounded by P_i, o_i,w_i by
the physical b_i, and sg_i by a_i. Also
cap_s<=A_*j+sum_i b_i; substituting this in the explicit Oplus
bound gives a finite upper bound for o_+. Hence the entire kept
set is finite even before fixing a digit class. This establishes
the actual finite support needed by segRank and segLat.

9.2. The first coefficient and the small-loss case.
Assume Bcal<1/6. Put

 S=kQ/a, t=kQ(M+N1 k)/b',
 delta_f=2/(kY)+(E0+Q)/a, theta=1-delta_f.

Std gives k>=5, N1 lambda>=1, N1 k>=5, b'<=a,
A_*j>=a/(2Q), and Q>=q_j>=1. Consequently
t>=S(M+5), and

 k ceil(M)/A_*j < 2S(M+1) <= 2t < 1/3.

In particular the margin condition in (R.25) is automatic. Set
x_j=r ceil(M)/A_*j. We have 0<=x_j<1 and

 k delta_f=2/Y+kE0/a+S<=2 Bcal<1/3,
 eta+eta2+x_j+r delta_f
 <=2eta+S(2M+3)+2/Y+kE0/a <=3 Bcal.                    (R.26)

The last constant 3 matches the source. The displayed elementary
bound even permits 2, but no downstream constant is changed.
All four loss factors lie in [0,1]. Bernoulli and the elementary
product inequality therefore yield

 L_j theta^r >= (1-eta)(1-eta2)(1-x_j)(1-r delta_f)
                >=1-3 Bcal.                            (R.27)

The actual head degree g=aT(segStart_j) satisfies
a<=g<=a+2a/(kY). Since A_*j=floor((a-E0)/q_j), for u=1,...,r,

 theta(g+u)
 <=a+2a/(kY)+u-delta_f a=a-E0+u-Q
 <=a-E0+u-1<q_j(A_*j+u).

Multiplying these r inequalities and dividing by r! gives

 theta^r h_k(g) <= q_j^r h_k(A_*j).                     (R.28)

Combining (R.25)--(R.28), with nonnegative factors, yields

 (1-3 Bcal) T_j <= q_j^r R_j,
 T_j=h_k(a_start) product_(i interior j)
            [h_k(a_i)h_k(b_i)] h_k(b_next).              (R.29)

9.3. Literal cyclic geometry and the tensor dimension.
The even positions of TT, in their increasing cyclic order, are
the segment starts. Consecutive starts partition the cycle into
disjoint half-open intervals; each selected odd position lies in
exactly one of their interiors. The cyclic successor permutes the
starts. Thus every selected a-factor and b-factor occurs once in
product_j T_j. There are at least two starts under Std, so the
cyclic distances are positive and telescope to n':
sum_j segG_j=n', product_j q_j=q. Also J<=N and N>=2.

The actual VHH is a tensor product, in disjoint matrix variables,
of dual-Weyl modules with vector weight (d,0,...,0) or covector
weight (0,...,0,-d). In the latter case nuOf gives
(d,...,d,0). CB3's standard-monomial basis and its Vandermonde
dimension give h_k(d) in both cases: all Weyl factors cancel
except respectively product_(u=1)^r(d+u)/u or the same product
in reverse order. Tensor products of those bases are independent
because their variables are disjoint. This proves the source's
DimHHAt for its literal polynomial VHH, rather than substituting
a different symmetric-power model:

 product_j T_j=dim_K VHH=product_(s in TT)h_k(a_s)h_k(b_s).
                                                               (R.30)

If kappa_P>=1, the desired rank lower bound has nonpositive left
side and is immediate. Otherwise Ckap>=3 and N>=2 imply Bcal<1/6,
so (R.29) applies. Multiply it over segments, use Bernoulli and
gaussian(q,k)=1+q+...+q^r>=q^r to obtain

 (1-kappa_P) dimHH
 <=(1-3 Bcal)^J dimHH<=q^r product_j R_j
 <=gaussian(q,k) product_j R_j.                          (R.31)

This is the exact ExtendedModelRank, valid for every M>=0. No
extra smallness or ceil(M) premise remains in its statement: the
nontrivial case itself implies the condition needed by RD9.

RU10. THE ACTUAL WEIGHTED LATTICE AND BLOCK-SUPPORTED PARTNERS
============================================================
Return to the actual ramified coefficient setting with uniformizer
w, w^2=p and v_p(w)=1/2. For each kept block beta, let V_beta be
the rational span of its literal SSZ generators and let

 L_beta=V_beta intersect bbSpec(specConst_beta).

Every legal level has fixed finite polynomial degree; there are
finitely many levels. Hence V_beta has finite dimension and only
finitely many monomials occur in a basis, and thus in any vector.
The coefficient definition of bbSpec is exactly

 v_p(c_alpha)>=-(v_p(alpha!)+v_p(specConst_beta))/2.

Its allowed coefficients at those finitely many monomials lie in
the free fractional module with basis
w^(-v_p(alpha!)-v_p(specConst_beta)) X^alpha. Thus L_beta is a
submodule of a finite free O-module. A DVR is Noetherian, so this
is a finite free O-module, and RU1's denominator clearing gives
rank_O L_beta=dim_K V_beta. The kept support is finite by 9.1;
therefore L=segLat is their finite direct sum and

 rank_O L=sum_beta blockRank_beta=segRank.                (R.32)

This proves RkAt without assuming that a rational rank definition
automatically equals the rank of its weighted lattice.

The actual form is block diagonal:
beta_L(f,g)=sum_beta specConst_beta fock(f_beta,g_beta).
It is O-valued on L. Indeed a coefficient contribution has valuation

 v_p(specConst)+v_p(alpha!)+v_p(f_alpha)+v_p(g_alpha)>=0.

The Fock sum is finite, so the ultrametric inequality preserves
integrality. In particular ordinary Smith normal form can be
applied separately to the pairing matrix on each finite free
L_beta. Concatenating the resulting two bases produces bases
a_i,b_i of L, each supported in a single block, with
beta_L(a_i,b_l)=delta_il d_i and d_i in O (possibly zero).
This conclusion also follows by clearing denominators first, as
the source does; the direct integrality check saves that step.

Let L1 be an actual GoodSub of L at depth D>=0 and g=rank L1.
Call a diagonal index good when d_i!=0 and v_p(d_i)<=D.
If fewer than g indices were good, the coordinate map from L1
to the good coordinates would have a nonzero kernel. This follows
after tensoring with K and clearing denominators. Choose a nonzero
kernel vector and divide all its coordinates by their largest
common power of w. The divided vector still lies in L, and lies
in L1 because L1 is saturated in L. At least one coordinate is
a unit, so the vector is primitive in L and consequently in L1.
Its good coordinates remain zero. Every pairing against L then
has valuation strictly greater than D (zero has valuation infinity),
contradicting the defining primitive-partner property of GoodSub.
Thus at least g indices are good. Selecting any g of them proves
the literal BlockDiag and PartnersToFactorsAt, including single
block support, nonzero integral diagonals, and the unchanged depth D.
