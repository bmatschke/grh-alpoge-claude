# A simple trace formula on the actual lower rows

The two arithmetic applications have a useful common feature. Their totally real field has **at least two real places**. For the ordinary host its degree is $2^{e+1}$. For the Dirichlet host it is $2|A|$, where $A$ is the finite group of the character construction. More generally the stage identities give $[F:\mathbf Q]=f=2n$ and $n\ge1$. Thus the trace formula with two cuspidal local factors applies even when the marked type has no single finite place at which it kills *every* proper parabolic. **Proposition.** On either actual row let $G=U(V)$, let $M_\infty$ be its specified product of discrete-series models with the source's separated weights, and take a normalized discrete-series pseudocoefficient at each real place. Choose the usual compactly supported, smooth, maximal-compact-finite representatives. At the finite places use the original marked type, the strictly dominant split Iwahori test, and the remaining factors of the finite test. With the product Haar measures of the row, the invariant trace is its cuspidal trace. Its geometric expansion is the sum of the ordinary rational orbital terms for the regular elements $\gamma$ whose characteristic polynomial over $E$ is irreducible, with their centralizer covolumes. This is the equality of traces needed before comparing the two rows. It applies to the chosen tests on these hosts. ## Why the support is regular

At the auxiliary split place $u$ the test is the indicator of $I t I$ in $\mathrm{GL}_r(F_u)$, where $I$ is the inverse image of the upper triangular Borel and $t=\operatorname{diag}(\varpi^{a_1},\ldots,\varpi^{a_r})$ with $a_1>\cdots>a_r$. Conjugating $i_1t i_2$ by $i_1^{-1}$ reduces the characteristic polynomial to that of $t b$, $b=i_2i_1\in I$. The coefficient of degree $r-k$, up to sign, is the sum of the principal $k$-minors. The last $k$ diagonal positions give a minor of valuation $a_{r-k+1}+\cdots+a_r$. The corresponding principal minor of $b$ is a unit because its reduction is upper triangular with invertible diagonal. Every other summand has higher valuation. Thus the Newton polygon has consecutive sides of horizontal length one, and the valuations of the roots, with multiplicity, are the distinct integers $a_i$. A rational matrix meeting this support consequently has separable characteristic polynomial after embedding $E$ in $F_u$, hence already over $E$. In particular all rational elements that occur geometrically are regular semisimple. ## The marked idempotents eliminate proper induction

Write $K_v=\mathrm{GL}_r(\mathcal O_v)$ at a marked split place and $e_{\tau_v}$ for the normalized idempotent cutting out its inflated residue type. The actual test is $e_{\tau_v}*h_v*e_{\tau_v}$ for arbitrary allowed $h_v$. Fix a proper rational parabolic. Its isotropic flag has a proper nonzero dimension $a$ defined over $E$. At a split place the parabolic in the chosen $\mathrm{GL}_r$ contains the flag data of that cut, possibly in the dual orientation. Choose the marked witness for the dimension in that orientation. For a rational isotropic $a$-space the split flag contains both dimensions $a$ and $r-a$ when these differ, since it also preserves the orthogonal complement. Over the local integers a basis adapted to the induced flag exists: intersections of the subspaces with the free lattice are saturated direct summands. Thus a $K_v$-conjugation puts the parabolic in standard position, and its integral unipotent radical reduces onto a subgroup containing the entire upper-right $a$-by-$(r-a)$ block. The [marked-type theorem](LOWER_ROW_MARKED_TYPE.md) says that the residue type has zero fixed vectors under this subgroup.

For any smooth representation $\eta$ of the Levi, inflate $\eta$ to the parabolic and form normalized induction. Iwasawa decomposition and compact Frobenius reciprocity identify its $\tau_v$-maps with maps from $\tau_v$ to the inducing representation on $P_v\cap K_v$. Such a map factors through the coinvariants by the integral radical. On the finite residue image, averaging identifies coinvariants with invariants over $\mathbb C$, so these maps vanish. This holds for every unramified twist of the inducing parameter. Projection to a fixed compact type is exact by compact averaging. Hence $e_{\tau_v}$ kills the induction and each of its subquotients. The place can be chosen separately for every proper rational parabolic, before specifying the inducing representation or parameter. The actual two-sided convolution therefore acts by zero as well. [Parabolic and marked-type calculation in the source](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/TwistedOperators/TypeIdempotentConstantTermVanish.lean#L93-L155)

Use the invariant trace formula with its ordinary automorphic Haar normalization. The invariant discrete distribution can in general contain traces of intertwining operators on properly induced spaces. Each such term here contains the test operator on one of the just-described global induced spaces, which is zero. The same local type kills residual constituents from proper cuspidal data, by the residual spectral decomposition. The surviving ordinary discrete trace is therefore the cuspidal trace. Equivalently, on the row's regular coefficients Dalal's Proposition 6.3.3 identifies the invariant spectral distribution with the ordinary discrete multiplicity sum, and the preceding type argument removes the residual terms. For this application take restriction of scalars to $\mathbf Q$. The center is the restriction of a norm-one torus and is compact at infinity, so the split central parameter of that proposition is trivial. ## The two real places eliminate nonelliptic terms

At each real place the orbital integral of the chosen pseudocoefficient vanishes on regular elements with noncompact centralizer. The center of $U(p,q)$ is compact, so this is the invariant cuspidality required at that place. Two distinct real places supply the two cuspidal factors. By Arthur's Theorem 7.1(b) the invariant geometric expansion has no proper-Levi distributions. With regular support at $u$, the surviving geometric terms are the regular elliptic orbital integrals. This particular formulation, including the degree and finite-support hypotheses, is Labesse's Proposition 3.10 and Corollary 3.11. At a regular elliptic element the contribution is the ordinary orbital integral times the covolume of the rational centralizer. Its product is independent of the choice of measure on that centralizer, since scaling that measure scales the covolume and quotient integral inversely. [Arthur, Theorem 7.1(b) and Corollary 7.2](https://personal.math.ubc.ca/~cass/arthur/pdf/27.pdf#page=39) · [Labesse, §3.9](https://webusers.imj-prg.fr/~michael.harris/CBCM.pdf#page=21) · [Pseudocoefficient normalization](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Definitions/Foundations/ContinuousOfMemCcSmooth.lean#L216-L229)

There are regular elliptic elements with reducible characteristic polynomial. To evaluate their contribution put $A=E[\gamma]$. The involution on this étale algebra induces $\gamma\mapsto\gamma^{-1}$ and conjugation on $E$. If it exchanges two simple factors, their norm-one group contains an $F$-split torus (scalar $s$ on one factor and $s^{-1}$ on its mate). The centralizer is then nonelliptic. If it preserves all factors and $A$ is a product, take the idempotent of one proper factor. It is self-adjoint, so $V$ splits as its image and kernel, an orthogonal $\gamma$-invariant decomposition of dimensions $a,r-a$. At the marked place for $a$, identify the split group with $\mathrm{GL}_r$. In a basis for this decomposition $\gamma=\operatorname{diag}(A_1,A_2)$, where $A_1$ and $A_2$ have disjoint spectra by regularity.

To see the resulting orbital vanishing, integrate first along the unipotent radical $n(X)$ of this two-block parabolic. Conjugating $\gamma$ by $n(X)$ replaces its upper-right block by $A_1X-XA_2$. This is an invertible linear transformation of the rectangular matrix space because the two spectra are disjoint. Its Jacobian is a nonzero constant. The invariant orbital integral thus contains the constant-term integral of the marked test over the radical, with left and right translates. It vanishes for all these translates: in the idempotent itself average over $N\cap x^{-1}K_vx$ in $\int_N e_{\tau_v}(xny)\,dn$. In any nonempty support coset this inserts the averaging projector onto the invariants of the residue subgroup of $K_v\cap xNx^{-1}$. An integral basis adapted to the conjugate cut identifies this subgroup's reduction with a conjugate of the full block radical. The projector is zero. Convolution on either side by a compactly supported function preserves the vanishing by Fubini. Iwasawa integration on the centralizer quotient now gives zero for the local ordinary orbital integral, and hence for the global class. What survives has $E[\gamma]$ a field. Conversely for an irreducible characteristic polynomial this centralizer is the restriction of scalars of a quadratic norm-one torus over the fixed field of the involution; it is anisotropic over $F$. These are exactly the field terms in the proposition. Their comparison between the two rows uses the classification and the real-place character calculation in the [elliptic transfer proof](LOWER_ROW_ELLIPTIC_TRANSFER.md). For an arbitrary totally real field of degree one the two-real-place argument is unavailable. The pinned source then treats the proper-Levi weighted orbital terms by splitting the truncation weight between the marked and remaining places. Both RH and the Dirichlet application have the degree required above.
