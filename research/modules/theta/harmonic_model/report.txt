1. HM1: THE EXPLICIT TWO-LETTER MODEL AND ITS DIMENSIONS

Let Z be a k by k matrix of variables, with indices 0,...,k-1. Define

  c_i(Z)=(-1)^i det Z_{rows other than i, columns 0,...,k-2}.

With two independent matrices Z^+,Z^-, the source map PsiXY is exactly

  Psi: F[x_0,...,x_{k-1},y_0,...,y_{k-1}] -> F[Z^+,Z^-],
  x_i |-> Z^+_{i0},   y_i |-> c_i(Z^-).

It is injective, including in positive characteristic. Indeed the leading
monomial of c_i in column-lex order is the diagonal monomial of that minor,
with coefficient (-1)^i. In a product prod_i c_i^{b_i}, the exponent at
Z_{t+1,t}, 0<=t<=k-2, is sum_{i<=t} b_i. The total degree in column 0 is
sum_i b_i. These quantities recover all b_i. Distinct y-monomials therefore
have distinct leading monomials. The x variables lie in a disjoint block,
so distinct x/y monomials retain distinct leading monomials as well. This
also proves integral saturation: eliminating the largest leading monomial
shows that Psi(f) has integral coefficients only if f does.

Let P_ab be polynomials homogeneous of degree a in x and b in y. CB2 identifies
the actual vector-letter module with polynomials in Z_{i0}. The covector-letter
module has normalized column shape (b,...,b,0): its tableaux have b columns
of height k-1. Such a tableau is exactly a multiset of omitted rows, sorted
in decreasing order, so its basis consists of the signed-minor monomials
prod c_i^{b_i}. Hence Psi(P_ab) is literally twoSp, and Psi(P_ab(O)) is
twoLat at the standard lattice. No dimension comparison is needed for this
identification or for its integral version.

Identify the two matrix blocks by the source deltaB. The resulting map is

  beta_ab(f)(Z)=f(Z_{00},...,Z_{k-1,0}, c_0(Z),...,c_{k-1}(Z)).

Its image is the actual dualWeyl module of normalized shape
(a+b,b,...,b,0), corresponding to weight (a,0,...,0,-b). Every tableau
minor product of that shape has a first-column factors and b height-(k-1)
factors. Therefore it is in the image, integrally, by CB2/CB6.

Put h=sum_i x_i y_i. Expansion of a determinant with its first column
repeated gives beta(h)=sum_i Z_{i0} c_i(Z)=0. Multiplication by h is
injective. CB3's Weyl product in this particular shape is

  ((a+b+k-1)/(k-1)) binom(a+k-2,k-2) binom(b+k-2,k-2)
    = S_a S_b-S_{a-1}S_{b-1} = H_ab.                         (HM1.1)

The equality also holds when a=0 or b=0. Rank-nullity now gives, over F,

  ker beta_ab = h P_{a-1,b-1},                              (HM1.2)

where a negative degree means the zero space. This is a kernel statement
for the displayed map, not an isomorphism inferred merely from equal ranks.

There is also an exact description at EVERY lattice Lambda=g O^k. In the
source convention let g act on x by g and on y by g^{-transpose}. The signed
minor identity

  c(g^{-1}Z)=det(g)^{-1} g^transpose c(Z)

shows

  twoLat(Lambda)=Psi(glAct(g^{-1}) P_ab(O)),
  H0((a,0,...,-b),Lambda)=beta_ab(glAct(g^{-1}) P_ab(O)).    (HM1.3)

For example the covector letter is det(g)^b times rowAct(g^{-1}) of the
standard covector lattice; this determinant factor cancels the det(g)^{-b}
introduced by the minors. CB7/EL2 justify basis independence of H0 and its
identification with the actual integral invariant module. Thus (HM1.3)
contains the correct inverse matrix and determinant twist, not a generic
Schur-module convention.

At an even s in TT the two degrees are (A_s,B_s); at an odd s they are
(B_s,A_s), since lamAt reverses and negates the weight. The number H_ab is
symmetric in a,b. Off TT the factor is the one-dimensional constant module.
Disjoint-block multiplication and the monic CB2 bases therefore give

  dim HH(A,B)=prod_s S_{A_s}S_{B_s},
  dim V(flatW(A,B))=prod_s H_{A_s B_s}.                    (HM1.4)

This proves the actual RestDims. The scalar telescoping identity
sum_{j=0}^{min(A,B)} H_{A-j,B-j}=S_A S_B, multiplied over s, gives

  sum_{0<=j_s<=min(A_s,B_s)} dim V(flatW(A-j,B-j))/Ne
       = dim HH(A,B)/Ne.                                  (HM1.5)

2. HM2: HARMONIC PROJECTIONS WITH THEIR INTEGRAL DENOMINATOR

Let Delta=sum_i partial_{x_i} partial_{y_i}; write H_ab^harm=ker Delta in
P_ab. Direct differentiation gives [Delta,h]F=(a+b+k)F on P_ab. Consequently
for harmonic W of degree (a,b),

  Delta(h^i W)=i(a+b+k+i-1)h^{i-1}W.                     (HM2.1)

For F in P_AB define

  pi_AB F = sum_{l=0}^{min(A,B)}
       (-1)^l binom(A+B+k-2,l)^{-1} (h^l/l!)(Delta^l/l!)F.

To verify this formula, the coefficient of h^{l-1}Delta^l F in its
Delta-image is c_{l-1}+l(A+B+k-l-1)c_l=0, where
c_l=(-1)^l/[l!(A+B+k-2)(A+B+k-3)...(A+B+k-l-1)]. Also pi F-F is a multiple
of h. If hG is harmonic, the commutator forces G to be divisible by h;
repeating this finitely many times proves h P_{A-1,B-1} intersects H_AB^harm
trivially. Thus pi is the projection onto H_AB^harm along h P_{A-1,B-1}.
Induction gives the unique decomposition

  P_AB = direct_sum_{j=0}^{min(A,B)} h^j H_{A-j,B-j}^harm.

Its jth projection is the SOURCE formula

  E_j = binom(A+B+k-j-1,j)^{-1}
        (h^j/j!) pi_{A-j,B-j} (Delta^j/j!).              (HM2.2)

Indeed apply (HM2.1) to each h^i W. Terms i<j vanish after Delta^j; terms
i>j are killed by pi; the remaining coefficient at i=j is precisely the
binomial in (HM2.2). Hence sum_j E_j=1.

Here is a short quantitative proof with the actual ordinary-coefficient
lattice. The auxiliary lattice with basis x^alpha/alpha! times y^beta is
preserved by both h^l/l! and Delta^l/l!. For the former, expansion over
multiindices nu has coefficients prod binom(alpha_i+nu_i,nu_i); for the
latter they are prod binom(beta_i,nu_i). These are integers. Further,

  v_p binom(n,r) = sum_{a>=1}(floor(n/p^a)-floor(r/p^a)
                                     -floor((n-r)/p^a))
                 <= floor(log_p n).                     (HM2.3)

Each summand is 0 or 1 and vanishes beyond that logarithm. Therefore, if
L=floor(log_p(A+B+k)), p^L pi and p^{2L} E_j preserve this auxiliary lattice.

On x-degree A the Gauss lattice G and that auxiliary lattice B satisfy

  A! B subset G,
  p^{(k-1)L}(A!)^{-1} G subset B.                        (HM2.4)

The second inclusion follows because A!/alpha! is a product of k-1 binomial
coefficients with upper arguments at most A, so its valuation is at most
(k-1)L; the first is immediate. The scalar A! cancels on conjugating any
degree-preserving map. Thus

  p^{(k+1)L} pi(G) subset G,
  p^{(k+1)L} E_j(G) subset G.                            (HM2.5)

All maps commute with glAct(g): h and Delta are invariant under the vector/
covector substitutions. So the same bounds hold on glAct(g)G for every g.
There is no claim that glAct(g) preserves G itself.

Write E_jF=h^j D_jF. Since h has a monic leading term, Gauss's elementary
leading-coefficient argument gives h^jG integral iff G integral. It applies
also after glAct(g), which fixes h. Consequently p^{(k+1)L}D_j maps G into
P_{A-j,B-j}(O). Define actual quotient maps

  pr_j=beta_{A-j,B-j} D_j Psi^{-1},
  io_j(beta f)=Psi(h^j pi_{A-j,B-j} f).                 (HM2.6)

The second is well-defined by (HM1.2), since pi kills ker beta. Equations
(HM2.2)/(HM2.6) give sum io_j pr_j=1. They give both integral bounds in
SingleWeightLatticeComparison with exponent

  U=ceil((m+2) lgA(p,a,m)),  lgA(p,a,m)=log_p(8a+m)+1,

provided A+B+1<=8a: (k+1)L<=(m+2)log_p(8a+m)<=U. Multiplication by the
additional integral power of p only enlarges the bound. This proves the
integral comparison directly; field-level complete reducibility is unused.

7. HM7: THE ACTUAL HARMONIC DENSITY AND FINITE-BOX AVERAGING

Divide (HM6.3) by the positive dimHH/Ne. Its weight at a scalar coordinate is

  P1(alpha,beta,j)=H_{alpha,beta}/(S_{alpha+j}S_{beta+j}).

For fixed alpha,beta put R_j=S_alpha S_beta/(S_{alpha+j}S_{beta+j}). Since
S_{d-1}/S_d=d/(d+k-1) increases with d,

  H_{alpha,beta}/(S_alpha S_beta)
    >=1-(alpha+j+1)(beta+j+1)
                 /((alpha+j+k)(beta+j+k)).

Multiplying by R_j gives P1(alpha,beta,j)>=R_j-R_{j+1}. Thus the exact,
finite lower bound

  sum_{j=0}^J P1(alpha,beta,j)
     >=1-S_alpha S_beta/(S_{alpha+J+1}S_{beta+J+1})       (HM7.1)

holds for all nonnegative alpha,beta, including zero. For L=J+1,

  R_L=prod_{i=1}^{k-1}(alpha+i)/(alpha+L+i)
                         * (beta+i)/(beta+L+i)
      <=exp(-(k-1)L/(beta+L+k-1)).                       (HM7.2)

The inequality follows from 1-z<=exp(-z), using only the beta factors;
retaining both products gives a sharper endpoint bound if desired. It is
uniform over a finite rectangle by substituting its upper endpoints.

8. HM8: ACTUAL DUAL-HEIGHT BASIS AND PERFECT EDGE PAIRING

For completeness, the later source interfaces DualHeightBasis,
DualHeightInclusion, BePerfect and HLSpans follow from the already proved

  H_src = direct_sum O x_i,
  H_tgt = direct_sum O p^{pi_i}x_i,
  H_mid = direct_sum O w^{pi_i}x_i,
  R_e   = direct_sum O w^{-o_i}x_i,
  R'_e  = direct_sum O w^{-o_i}p^{-pi_i}x_i^*.           (HM8.1)

For a single factor pi_i is the row degree in the first r rows plus
