# The bottom row on the first untwisted column

The [saturation argument](../arithmetic/UNTWISTED_AUXILIARY_PRIMES.md) supplies the fine datum on which to begin. The weight assertion on the first pass is evaluated on its restriction. Let $z$ be the datum over the places above the permitted residue characteristics, put $W=z.W_1(F_0)$, and restrict $z$ to $W$. Write $z'$ for this restriction. Its exact model readings, geometric membership, core, standard reading and equality $W=z'.W_1(F_0)$ are available in the construction. The constructor applies the weight assertion to **this** $z'$, supplies the row-lift family, and puts both the family and $z'$ into the starting state. On a pinned datum the extra input used to pass from the lower-row comparison to the full weight assertion is the bottom rational row test on $z'.R.\mathrm{toCx3}(z'.F)$. ## Which tuples actually occur

Write $p_\delta,p_{\delta'}$ for the two frame primes and $\delta,\delta'$ for their frame characters. Let $t_\delta,t_{\delta'}$ be the corresponding tuples in the full column, and let $t_0$ be its reference tuple. The set of underlying tuples on the restricted **untwisted** first pass is exactly
$$
 \{t_0\}\ \cup \{t_\delta\mid \exists v\in W,\ \ell(v)\ne p_\delta\}\ \cup \{t_{\delta'}\mid \exists v\in W,\ \ell(v)=p_\delta\},
$$
where $\ell(v)$ denotes residue characteristic. In particular $W=\varnothing$ gives one tuple, a nonempty $W$ on just one side of the residue-characteristic split gives two, and $W$ meeting both sides gives three. This computation applies also before restriction by substituting the original place set for $W$. [Untwisted auxiliary character][untw] · [Character evaluated at a place][twist] · [Tuple set and selected indices][used] · [Adjoining the reference tuple][adjoin]

**Proof.** The auxiliary character $\psi_v$ equals one in the untwisted datum. The place-dependent character is therefore $\delta'$ if $\ell(v)=p_\delta$ and $\delta$ otherwise. Its frame is the one recorded on the column. The column's character range contains $1,\delta,\delta'$, and its character map is injective. There is consequently one index for each of these characters, and the index for $1$ is the reference index. The three are distinct. At any place above $p_\delta$ the restriction of $\delta$ to the local units has order 15, while $\delta'$ is unramified there because its allowed ramification is supported above the different prime $p_{\delta'}$. Interchanging the two primes distinguishes $\delta'$ from one as well. Finally the tuple map itself is injective. Taking its image on the selected indices and adjoining the reference tuple gives the displayed equality. [Frame and range][col] · [Reference and injectivity][inj] · [Local character orders][orders] · [Distinct frame primes in the local argument][primes]

All of these tuples land in a single finite-dimensional complex bottom space $E$. For the specified coefficient embedding $k\hookrightarrow\mathbb C$, the required linear forms $\lambda_i\in E^\vee$ jointly separate $E$ and satisfy $\lambda_i(\Theta_t j_t x)\in k$ for every permitted $t$ and every rational source vector $x$. The intermediate rational lift tests supplied by Q77Z1 have targets $W_j$ and source maps $\theta'_{j,t}$. The [common-target period argument](COMPOSITE_ROW_RATIONALITY.md#rational-tests-on-the-actual-weight-supported-column) now gives the simultaneous tests in $E$ on this smaller index set. ## Absolute convergence of the final Bessel period

Fix an admissible row-test choice on the datum, any one of its finite column tests, and a continuous automorphic output $F$ on the row node $(m,0)$. Here automorphy includes invariance on the left under rational points and a finite right level. The terminal Bessel integral for these particular inputs is absolutely convergent, with the indicated Haar measures and fundamental sets. In fact, if $Q(h,n)$ is its integrand, there is a finite constant $C$ such that
$$
 \int_{[H]}\int_{[N]} |Q(h,n)|\,dn\,dh\le C\,\operatorname{vol}([H]).
$$
Here $H=U(V_{\mathrm{top},0})$, $N$ is the flag unipotent group on the $(m,0)$ node, the base field is the totally real field $F^+$ of the stage, and brackets indicate the corresponding rational left quotients, with the source's fundamental sets understood in the integral. The inner measure has mass one. The bound may depend on the column test and on $F$. [Period and measures][bdef] · [Convergence on the column][bcol]

**Proof.** Write $s:H\to U(V_{m,0})$ for the isometric embedding, $\Psi$ for the chosen coefficient-valued finite column, $\rho$ for its coefficient action and $\xi$ for the chosen linear functional. With the given additive character the integrand is
$$
 Q(h,n)=\xi\big(\rho(h^{-1})\Psi(h_f)\big)\,F(n s(h))\,\overline{\psi_N(n)}.
$$
The coefficient is continuous because $\Psi$ is locally constant at the finite level and $\rho$ acts continuously on its finite-dimensional coefficient space. For $\gamma\in H(F^+)$, the column transforms on the left by $\rho(\gamma)$, which cancels against $\rho((\gamma h)^{-1})$ in the coefficient. The Bessel character is continuous of modulus one. Thus $Q$ is continuous on the product before taking the indicated integrals. [Coefficient evaluation and column equivariance][coef] · [Continuity][cont]

The form of $V_{\mathrm{top},0}$ is positive definite at each real place, so it has no rational isotropic line. The adelic quotient of this anisotropic unitary group is compact. The adelic quotient of the unipotent radical by its rational points is compact as well. For the latter, filter the unipotent group by rational normal subgroups with successive vector-group quotients and use compactness of the additive adelic quotient at each stage. Choose compact sets $K_H,K_N$ of representatives covering the respective quotients. To bound $Q$ on the two fundamental sets, first write $h=\gamma k$ with $k\in K_H$. The element $s(\gamma)$ normalizes the radical, so write $s(\gamma)^{-1}ns(\gamma)=\eta k'$ with $\eta\in N(F^+)$ and $k'\in K_N$. Then $n s(h)=s(\gamma)\eta k's(k)$. Rational left invariance of $F$ removes $s(\gamma)\eta$. The coefficient has the same value at $h$ and $k$, and the character does not change the absolute value. Hence $|Q(h,n)|=|Q(k,k')|$. Continuity on $K_H\times K_N$ supplies $C$. Both fundamental sets have finite measure, giving the claimed bound, absolute convergence of the inner integral for every $h$, and integrability of its value over the outer variable. The conjugation step accounts for the dependence of the inner quotient on the outer representative. [Compact representatives and the integration argument][compact]

## The theta integrals preceding that period

For an admissible test $a$ and an arbitrary bottom vector $u$, start with $F_0=\mathrm{ch.form}(u)$ and write $F_{i+1}$ for the prescribed theta lift of $F_i$ along $(i,0)\to(i+1,0)$. Only $0\le i<m$ occurs. On the matching auxiliary grid the core puts the initial form in the cusp multiplicity space at $(0,0)$. On the first edge, the fixed Schwartz test has a polynomial height bound in the integration variable. On a Siegel set the initial cusp vector decays faster than every negative power of that height. Taking a decay exponent large enough to absorb the kernel gives an integrable majorant, for each fixed output point, and hence absolute convergence of this first integral. The argument transfers to the choice's domain and measure through the grid match. [Original growth proof](../geometry/THETA_CUSPIDAL_FACTORIZATION.md) · [Initial cusp membership][start] · [Matching the actual domains, measures and kernels][match]

A precise sufficient input for the *rest* of the integrals is cuspidal propagation for the reference character on the same edges. At each $i$, for a cusp multiplicity vector representing the actual $F_i$, require that the prescribed finite and Fock test produces a cusp multiplicity vector representing $F_{i+1}$ and that its defining integral converges. The matched data identify the kernels on the nose, including any signed change of model, as well as the Haar measure and fundamental set. Induction gives convergence at all $m$ steps and a continuous automorphic $F_m$. The Bessel result just proved now applies, giving the full convergence condition for $a,u$. This reduction uses the reference character for the **horizontal chain of test integrals**. The rationality condition on the resulting functionals still evaluates **all** the tuples computed above. ## Separation by the actual bottom periods

Fix one admissible marked choice on the scale of the period injectivity assertion (`InjK`), with the prescribed Hilbert model identification. Write $H=R.\mathrm{HE}.H$, $E_0=R.\mathrm{EEat}(R.\mathrm{triv})$. The model map $e$ identifies $H$ with $E_0$. The choice fixes a nonzero smooth tensor $w$ in the bottom model, the actual Weil representation, measures and finite list $(\Psi_a,\Phi_a)$. For $t\in E_0$, let $t(w)$ mean the value of its tensor lift at $w$. Write $P_{\rho,\xi}(\Psi,\Phi;t)$ for the actual iterated theta–Bessel period of $t(w)$ with the indicated column, finite datum and real inputs. In this notation $\rho$ is the archimedean test vector, $\xi$ is the coefficient functional, and the choice specifies the pair $(\rho_0,\xi_0)$. Assume the defining integrals of the catalogue periods converge for every replacement $(\rho,\xi)$ and every $u\in H$. [Marked choice and real substitutions](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Definitions/TwistedInnerProducts/ExistsMatchOver.lean#L167-L215) · [Scale and original injectivity requirement](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Statements/HeckeThetaIntegrals/FormulaAt.lean#L65-L76)

**Proposition.** The chosen catalogue separates $H$ if and only if for each $0\ne t\in E_0$ there exist a real pair $\rho_t,\xi_t$, an allowed column $\Psi\in\mathrm{Cols}$ and a finite datum $\Phi$ for which $P_{\rho_t,\xi_t}(\Psi,\Phi;t)\ne0$. The real pair may be chosen after $t$. If $E_0=0$ both assertions hold.

**Proof.** If the marked catalogue separates, apply it to $e^{-1}t$ and take its detecting index with the original real pair. Each catalogue column belongs to the allowed space. Conversely, suppose $u$ is killed by every marked test. The defining `RealOK` property of this choice implies that every catalogue index still vanishes on $u$ after every real substitution. If $u\ne0$, choose $\rho,\xi,\Psi,\Phi$ witnessing the proposition for $t=e(u)$ and keep these fixed. The catalogue columns with datum exactly $\Phi$ span all of $\mathrm{Cols}$. Indeed the catalogue enumerates, for this $\Phi$, each definite double class paired with every member of a spanning coefficient family. In class coordinates these columns have the specified coefficient on their own class and vanish elsewhere. On this span, vanishing extends to linear combinations: after applying the linear coefficient functional, the inner and outer Bessel integrals are linear in the column, since integrability at both stages is preserved by sums and scalar multiples. The theta input remains fixed in this argument. Thus $P_{\rho,\xi}(\Psi,\Phi;t)=0$, a contradiction.
