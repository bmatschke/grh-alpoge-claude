1. PHYSICAL MONOMIALS AND THE EULER MARGIN (RL1)

A segment has odd positions i=0,...,s-1, an initial top, and its next top
as terminal position. Write d_i for the distance from the initial top,
G_j for its length, P_i=p^(G_j-d_i) and Q_j=p^G_j; thus p^d_i P_i=Q_j.
The coarse block b records digits r_z(i,a),r_y(i,a)<P_i, early degrees
w_i,sg_i for i<sigma, late order o_i for i>=sigma, and terminal oPlus.
The total coarse vector/covector degrees are A_i and C_i. Terminal
vector/covector degrees are aPlus,bPlus. All these names denote the
literal source quantities, not freely chosen replacements.

Its auxiliary polynomial variables consist of normal chain vectors W_i,
covectors E_i, terminal vector U and covector xi, and scalar pairs Z_l,S_l
for late i and for the terminal tie. The tied expressions are

  E_l = Z_l S_l + q_l,    q_l=sum_a W_(i,a) E_(i,a),
  E_plus = Z_plus S_plus + sum_a U_a xi_a.                      (1.1)

A generator of SSZ at margin mu is a legal product

  prod_l E_l^j_l E_plus^j_plus prod_l Z_l^z_l Z_plus^z_plus
       Y(chain) xi^gamma,                                    (1.2)

where Y has the literal multidegree and satisfies every prefix bound
floor(cap_b(l)-mu). The vector/covector tie pairs are disjoint and adjacent.
The physical map io_b sends a monomial x^alpha to X^I_b(alpha), where

  I_b(alpha)=rho_b+sum_v P_v alpha_v e_(iota(v)).                (1.3)

Here iota is injective, P_v=P_i at odd positions and 1 at the terminal
position. The base rho_b consists of the digits and, at early positions,
the additional coordinate-0 exponents P_i(A_i-w_i), P_i sg_i.
On the image of iota the remaining residues lie in [0,P_v); any larger
early fixed coordinate-0 exponents lie outside that image. Consequently
I_b is injective. In particular the Fock form after io_b is diagonal:

  Fock(io_b u,io_b v)=sum_alpha u_alpha v_alpha I_b(alpha)!.    (1.4)

Balanced gradings assign the same degree to all coordinates of one chain
letter, and assign to Z_l,S_l degrees whose sum is the sum of the two tied
letter degrees. Every monomial in the rational span of SSZ has the same
value for each such grading. This follows term by term from (1.1), (1.2).
Two important choices are the physical-letter grading and the indicator
of a fixed radix P_v. Thus

  sum_(iota(v) in letter ell) alpha_v=c_ell,
  sum_(P_v=P) alpha_v is constant on the block.                (1.5)

For a nonterminal auxiliary variable v, put

  C_(v,n)=v^n partial_v^[n],   C_(v,n)x^alpha=binom(alpha_v,n)x^alpha.

It costs at most n in every prefix bound and preserves all balanced
degrees. Here is the actual tied-polynomial check. C_(v,n) is the
coefficient of t^n after v is replaced by (1+t)v. If v is a chain
coordinate in q_l, then E_l is replaced by E_l+t v v', and binomial
expansion gives E_l^(j-a)(vv')^a times an order n-a coefficient operator
on the remaining chain polynomial. The new tie level is j-a. Multiplying
by a coordinate pair costs at most a in the prefix bound, and the
remaining Euler coefficient costs at most n-a. The latter assertion is
the already proved word estimate: conjugating a coordinate Euler
operator by the generic GL_m matrix gives a sum of x_a partial_b, each
of prefix weight at most 1. In characteristic zero the divided Euler
operator is a polynomial of degree n in that Euler operator, so the
same rational subspace bound follows. Integrality follows separately
from the coefficient substitution, not division by n!.

If v=Z_l or S_l, first expand E_l -> E_l+t Z_l S_l, then replace
(Z_l S_l)^a by (E_l-q_l)^a. The resulting tie levels are at most j,
the new chain factors are powers q_l^h with h<=n, and all coefficients
are integers. A pair q_l has prefix cost 0 when both letters lie inside
the prefix, and at most 1 when the prefix separates them. Untied normal
variables are easier. Terminal variables are excluded from this step;
the factorial correction below acts only at odd positions.

It follows, by applying the commuting C_(v,n) one at a time, that for
any O-linear combination G of products of total order <=4c,

  G SSZ(mu) subset SSZ(mu-4c).                                (1.6)

The floor convention causes no loss: if n is integral then
floor(x)+n=floor(x+n). Rational-span membership with integral coefficients
can equivalently be converted back to SSZ by SW1. This is the actual
GThetaAt assertion, not an integral isotypic decomposition.

2. AN EXPLICIT FACTORIAL CORRECTION AND ITS VALUATION (RL2)

Let sp_b be the positive integer specConst of the block and put

  C_b=prod_ell c_ell!,     N_b=C_b sp_b,     v_b=v_p(N_b).

Equation (1.5) gives alpha! | C_b, hence alpha! sp_b | N_b. Let
alpha0 be the distinguished monomial obtained by putting each chain
letter's entire degree in normal coordinate 0, putting the excess scalar
degree in Z_l and putting no degree in S_l. The terminal xi degree bPlus
also lies in coordinate 0. This is a monomial satisfying every balanced
grading, whether or not it occurs in a particular SSZ vector.

Let R_b be the product of the fixed factorials outside iota's image, and
let rho_v be the residual digit at iota(v). Define the positive integer

  Phi_b=R_b prod_v rho_v! (P_v!)^(alpha0_v).                   (2.1)

By the second equality in (1.5), alpha0 may be replaced by any allowed
alpha in (2.1). DT's factorial-unit lemma, applied to the inverse units,
gives a polynomial in binomial functions

  g_b(alpha)=sum_nu q_nu prod_v binom(alpha_v,nu_v),             (2.2)

with q_nu in O, |nu|<=4c, nu supported at odd variables, such that

  I_b(alpha)! g_b(alpha)
       =Phi_b alpha! (1+p^c h_alpha),    h_alpha in O.          (2.3)

The proof from DT even has degree <=2(c-1); keeping <=4c matches the
source. No terminal inverse-unit approximation is needed because P_v=1
and rho_v=0 there. Formula (2.3) follows by multiplying the individual
factorial identities, including R_b; omitting R_b would give a wrong
normalization.

Write D_j for the product of all physical letter factorials in the
segment, L_j=sum_ell Nat.log_p(d_ell), and z_j=p^(k L_j)/D_j. Set

  kappa_b=z_j p^v_b Phi_b/sp_b.                               (2.4)

This lies in O and is nonzero. Its valuation can be checked without
an unspecified unit transport. At each physical letter alpha0 uses at
most two auxiliary variables, mapped to physical coordinates 0 and 1.
Consequently

  M_coarse=C_b/alpha0!,       0<=v_p(M_coarse)<=L_j,
  M_fine=D_j/I_b(alpha0)!,    0<=v_p(M_fine)<=(k-1)L_j.         (2.5)

The multinomial bound used here is elementary: at each p-power place
the difference between floor(total/p^a) and the sum of the individual
floors is between 0 and r-1 for r summands, and vanishes above the
largest nonzero place. Thus it is <=(r-1) Nat.log_p(total).
The coarse totals c_ell are at most the true physical degrees d_ell.
This last identification uses the actual terminal oPlus legality, as
well as the kept block's digit and degree conditions.

Legendre's formula gives

  v_p(I_b(alpha0)!)=v_p(Phi_b)+v_p(alpha0!).

Indeed v_p((rho+p^g n)!)=v_p(rho!)+n v_p((p^g)!)+v_p(n!)
for 0<=rho<p^g; this follows by splitting the Legendre sum at g.
Substitution in (2.4) now yields

  v_p(kappa_b)=k L_j+v_p(M_coarse)-v_p(M_fine),
  0<=v_p(kappa_b)<=(k+1)L_j.                                 (2.6)

The lower estimate actually gives >=L_j, but the source needs only
nonnegativity. Combining (2.3), (2.4), and p^c=w^(2c) proves the exact
FactAt congruence modulo w^c, with all factorial normalizations retained.

  z0_i=floor(B_(+,0)/P_i), y0_i=floor(B_(-,0)/P_i),

8. THE RAW TAIL ESTIMATE AS A FINITE FACTOR EXPANSION (RL8)

Write the constructed product as Ftot=f_R prod_a f_a^m_a. Its rigid
factor has Phi(f_R)=w^oR sigma_R exactly. For every other factor,
Phi(f_a) has order >=o_a and leading term w^o_a sigma_a modulo
w^(o_a+1), where sigma_a is homogeneous of normal degree o_a.
Set o=oR+sum m_a o_a and

  raw=w^(-o)Ftot,       t_a=w^(-o_a)Phi(f_a)-sigma_a.

For a monomial M, its coefficient in t_a is divisible by

  w^max(1, nd(M)-o_a).                                      (8.1)

The first bound is the own-chart leading congruence. If nd(M)>o_a,
the coefficient of M in sigma_a is zero, and the integral f_a acquires
exactly w^nd(M) under the identity chart. This proves the second bound
without asserting that a whole tail is normal-homogeneous.

Expand Phi(raw)=sigma_R prod_a(sigma_a+t_a)^m_a. Choose u_a tails
from each factor, and a monomial M from their product. Its coefficient
is divisible by w^h for

  h=max(sum_a u_a, nd(M)-sum_a u_a o_a).                     (8.2)

This follows by adding the bounds (8.1). Its physical degree is
sum_a u_a deg(f_a). If h>=n, the term belongs to w^n O[X].
For 1<=h<n, at most h factors were removed and
nd(M)<=sum u_a o_a+h, so RL6--RL7 put the remaining leading term
times M in

  SSg(mm+(n-h)A, (c-n+h)A, o+h).

Grouping the finitely many coefficients with the same h gives

  Phi(raw)=sigma+sum_(h=1)^(n-1)w^h tau_h mod w^n,
  tau_h in SSg(mm+(n-h)A,(c-n+h)A,o+h).                     (8.3)

Also w^o raw=Ftot is integral, has the correct multidegree, and every
other-plane chart of raw is divisible by w^c because Ftot contains
the localizer L_R^c in addition to factors of total all-chart order o.
For a generator the o in this calculation is its actual homogeneous
normal order. In SSg the displayed o is only an upper bound: if a
generator has order o_gen<=o, its height bound follows by multiplying
by w^(o-o_gen), and SSg is increasing in its order bound, so its
correction belongs also to the space with bound o+h. Linearity then
extends (8.3) to the O-span, even when its summands have different
normal orders. This is exactly RawTailBody and therefore RestRawTail1
at the supplied local parameters.

10. EXACT SOURCE AND DUAL CHART TRANSPORT (RL10)
