ARITHMETIC REALIZATION: EXPANDED PROOF AND SCOPE REPORT
Version 0.19. Component arithmetic_realization. Claims AR1–AR22.
Source commit: 7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4.

1. What this component establishes

This component reconstructs the local arithmetic comparisons that v0.18
left upstream of its conditional LevEq and RankTie theorem. The new work
does not replace the actual global representations by abstract spaces of
the same dimension. One datum D, one NExpTied family X, its actual cells,
its actual MtReal, and the specified geometric/period data remain fixed.
Under their explicit hypotheses, AR1–AR11 supply the bad-place bound,
the local parahoric and unipotent descriptions and the actual Joint
intertwining assertion. AR12–AR14 establish the coefficient-field weight
comparison and CellWt, conditional on two named complex representation
inputs. AR15–AR21 construct the harmonic coefficient map and the reading
whose installation gives the nontrivial SerAt.join equation. AR22 joins
these conclusions with the earlier RC local-to-global rigidity proof.

The resulting endpoint is substantial but conditional. The actual
LevCmp.rank_of_levEq comparison now has human proofs of its local shape,
Joint and CellWt suppliers under the specified global realizations,
normalized characters and representation-theoretic premises. The
component does not construct a nonempty compatible NTw/local family,
the full MtFam or NExpTied realization, or the entire simultaneous
ColW/Pre2/RowPk/St system. The empty-Tw and empty-Loc bare stage of LS
cannot supply the all-place coverage used here.

The arithmetic character field here is also not the full NumbersIn
field in the twisted-inner-product construction. That stronger field
has additional periods, normalized jets and integral values and is
addressed separately by TT. No claim about RH or GRH follows simply
from this conditional comparison.

2. Fixed conventions and the data that must stay identical

The base is a CM host E with real subfield F. The finite adelic unitary
group is G_f and the rational subgroup is Gamma. At a selected split
place s, its chosen component is GL_n(F_v). Replacing the selected prime
by its conjugate changes that component by inverse transpose, so
selection and side are part of the data.

The cell is X.mtV(i,j+1,b,k,Q,psi), with its literal coefficient module,
action, level, character, realization and selected halves Q_P,D_P.
The global position is j+1, not j. The twist character eta is the same
mu0_j delta_l psi_H as supplied by the actual tuple identity. Auxiliary
delta_l changes at the specified residue characteristic. These are
equalities on the actual objects; they are not choices of isomorphic
character data made separately for different lemmas.

Valuations are multiplicative: integral means valuation at most 1 and
maximal-ideal membership means strictly less than 1. Reciprocity uses
the source's inverse-arithmetic-Frobenius convention. At the type primes
the local base-character value is the positive tau value; the inverse
arising from the false geometric side is cancelled later by RatAd.psi.
That inverse is not inserted into the local tau statement.

The coefficient field is K_{i,k}=Frac(O_{i,k}), with the exact embedding
e that reads the selected side and the host. A complex embedding is
algebraic; no continuity of a p-adic-to-complex embedding is assumed.
Continuity is used before complex reading, on the actual adic character
or on a finite/open-kernel character as appropriate.

3. AR1–AR2: exact ramification, halves and separation

AR1 begins with exact ramification rather than an upper bound on a
conductor. IsMu0 says that mu0 is unramified away from the two type
primes. IsPsi identifies exactly Q as the ramification of psi_H.
IsDelta supplies the auxiliary ramification and an inertial image of
order 15. The tuple decomposition then makes cancellation on local
units sufficient: an unramified factor cannot remove the ramification
of a factor with a nontrivial local unit character.

At conductor primes both other factors are unramified. At auxiliary
primes the twist is unramified, by the literal prime-norm exclusion
in IsTwist, and mu0 is unramified. At type primes admissibility excludes
Q, and distinct rational residue characteristics exclude auxiliary
primes. The Bezout proof that a proper prime ideal cannot contain two
distinct rational primes is enough for these exclusions. This proves
badV subset gPl union qPl and also the unramifiedness of the extra
finite-order twist at each type prime.

AR2 identifies the source's two half constructions. A split real
prime has exactly P and conjugate P above it. The series half consists
of the selected P_q(v) in the conjugation-stable set. The alternative
clause in qpOf cannot select the other prime, because conjugation
stability would put the omitted companion back into the set. Thus
half(range P_q,S)=qpOf(P_q,S) literally.

The same equal-or-conjugate dichotomy proves separation of Q_P,D_P
and the type primes. Both selected halves contain no conjugate pair;
a cross-pair would force a forbidden auxiliary prime into Q. The
covering clauses then show every actual qPl place is represented by
the appropriate selected half. The result concerns the exact place
and selection, not a conjugacy class that could be reoriented later.

4. AR3–AR4: tame values and the Artin convention

The finite local ramification argument in AR3 is explicit. An
open-kernel character factors through a finite Galois quotient.
Successive positive ramification quotients embed in additive residue
groups, so wild inertia has p-power image. If all the relevant
inertial values have order dividing powers of m with p coprime to m,
the wild image is trivial. Frobenius acts on tame inertia by q-th
power, and an abelian target forces exponent dividing q-1.
Consequently the tame residue value does not depend on the permitted
choices and is 1 on a residue unit equal to 1.

For the precise Artin comparison, take x/u in 1+m and extract a
(q-1)-st root z in 1+m. The two Frobenius lifts fixing the selected
uniformizer roots have ratio in inertia, and the ratio's tame residue
is u. Its abelianization is the Artin value of u, including the
source's norm and inverse-Frobenius convention. Correct by the
(q-1)-st power of an inertia lift of Art(z). This preserves the
tame residue while making the abelianization Art(x). Reading the
character now gives its actual local Hecke value. This is not a
sign-free appeal to local reciprocity; the particular Artin map is
retained and used throughout.

AR4 treats auxiliary delta. RC3 supplies a finite-order Galois
character for the corresponding finite Hecke character. DeltaRead
identifies its Frobenius values with the specified delta coefficient
character after the side exponent epsilon_b. RC4's Frobenius
detection then gives equality of the Galois characters. On the false
side the inverse in the packet and complex conjugation cancel because
the complex character is unitary. This produces epsilon_b, not an
extra arbitrary inverse. The auxiliary norm is 1 modulo 15, hence
coprime to 15. Since that norm is a power of its residue
characteristic, the latter is neither 3 nor 5. AR3 therefore applies
with m=15.

At a conductor prime the other two factors are unramified; at an
auxiliary prime mu0 and the conductor factor are unramified. Thus
the exact scalar in the local parahoric can be written as the
specified local component of eta in both cases.

5. AR5–AR8: actual local levels, not abstract group shapes

AR5 computes the conductor-prime level directly. An adelic element
supported at the chosen real prime has identity reductions at all
other places. On the selected side the defining block conditions
reduce to the integral upper parahoric condition. The lower block is
invertible modulo the maximal ideal, so its determinant is a local
unit. Every other factor in the global level character is 1, leaving
the local eta value on that determinant. The split completion
identification and tame congruence show that this is exactly the
literal local component in ParaShape.

AR6 constructs the additive completion character rather than assuming
it exists. An additive character of the residue field gives one on
the integral ring. The nonzero complex numbers are divisible because
every nonzero complex number has an m-th root. A maximal-extension
argument extends a homomorphism from an additive subgroup: if a new
element has a least positive multiple in the subgroup choose the
corresponding root; if no such multiple exists choose its value
freely. Maximality forces the extension to the whole completion.
The constructed character has the required residue values. Its
triviality on the maximal ideal and nontriviality on the integral
ring are exact; no continuity stronger than that required by the
source is claimed.

AR7 isolates the arithmetic step that cannot be made vacuous. To
identify the type character tau, the literal proof chooses a rational
prime larger than the fixed type data, a place above it, and then an
index supplied by Bs.cover. The normalized first Galois character is
identified with the adic character of the exact algebraic Hecke
character using the source's block-character construction and RC4.
Its inertial evaluation at the type prime gives tau equal to the
mu0 local value. The source requires actual normalized character
compatibility and a continuous adic realization. A complex HFrame
alone does not provide these, and an empty twist index type cannot
provide Bs.cover.

AR8 computes the type level and its character. The residue condition
is the specified unipotent first block with arbitrary invertible
lower block. Its character is the product of the superdiagonal
additive character and the local base character evaluated on the
lower-block determinant. The rational-datum nontriviality field,
tested at a single superdiagonal elementary matrix, proves that the
additive character is nontrivial. For the small-block case the
literal empty sum is handled separately. The extra twist is
unramified at this place by AR1 and so contributes nothing on those
determinant units.

The order of choices matters: the additive character psi0 and the
local multiplicative character xi are selected once before the
universal quantifier over all allowed complex embeddings e. They
are derived from the same rational residue character and mu0;
they are not chosen anew to fit each embedding.

6. AR9–AR11: identity-frame transport and actual Joint

AR9 verifies the residue comparison entry by entry. The identity
frame preserves the host matrix entries. Integrality can be tested
after embedding into the completion, and equality of residues is
equivalent to a difference in the maximal ideal. The source's
localization and quotient maps have exactly this kernel. The
identity-frame matrix is the identity, so no unnamed change of
basis appears in the result.

AR10 compares the two UniDat presentations. The group membership
conditions use the same block sizes, selected places and residue
maps, and hence are literally equivalent. The unipotent factors
may initially be written using different lifts, but their residue
data agree. The remaining lower-determinant factors take values
in the type residue field. Raising to g-1 kills these units. This
proves the exact power equality in Joint; it does not prove that
the two raw characters agree before that power.

AR11 takes the actual intersection of the two global scalar-action
groups as J1 and uses the identity automorphism theta of G_f.
The parahoric and unipotent containment statements are supplied by
the concrete reduction calculations. RatAd supplies the first scalar
law; the actual series law and SerAt.join supply the second on the
coefficient image. The coefficient map is the semilinear map with
the source's actual fraction-field inclusion, and injectivity follows
from the actual realization and reading fields.

The false geometric side is handled explicitly. Its additive
character is conjugated; RatAd.psi identifies the rational
character with the inverse of psi0. Since the values are unitary,
these two inversions cancel. The common unipotent factor is then
the positive one used in AR8. The lower determinant factors are
killed by g-1 as in AR10. This gives every clause of Joint, including
the exact scalar power relation, with no assumption of a general
representation isomorphism on the full adelic group.

7. AR12–AR14: coefficient-field descent and CellWt

AR12 gives a useful elementary replacement for a field-of-definition
shortcut. Suppose a nonzero complex matrix intertwines two families
of K-matrices, even an infinite family. Choose a K-linear functional
C to K nonzero on one nonzero matrix entry, by extending that entry
to a K-basis. Apply it entrywise. It commutes with every coefficient
in K, so all intertwining equations survive simultaneously, and the
selected entry remains nonzero. No finite intersection or
finite-generation assumption on the group is needed.

Polynomiality of the original representation follows explicitly
from polynomiality of its dual. On the rational unitary group,
g inverse has entries conjugate to the transposed entries of g.
Thus replace each polynomial variable by its conjugate-embedding,
transposed-index variable, transpose the resulting matrix, and
insert the constant change-of-basis matrices. This is why the
literal HWAtF polynomial presentation is used; a highest-weight
vector alone would not establish IsAlgWeight.

AR13 retains two specific representation-theoretic inputs: complex
classification of an irreducible algebraic rational-unitary
representation by the canonical VV module, and the canonical
contragredient highest-weight uniqueness theorem. It does not
label those deep conclusions as proved merely because they have
source declarations.

Given these inputs, complexify the actual K representation. Choose
a nonzero invariant subspace of smallest dimension. Its action is
algebraic: a constant inclusion matrix and a constant left inverse
express its matrix as a product with the ambient polynomial matrix.
Classification applies to that subrepresentation. The inclusion
of its canonical coefficient module descends by AR12 to a nonzero
K-intertwiner. Its image is all of W by irreducibility. The original
complex inclusion gives the opposite dimension inequality, so the
K-intertwiner is an isomorphism. In particular no unproved assertion
that complexification preserves irreducibility is used. The second
named input identifies the dominant weight with the prescribed
dual weight.

AR14 identifies the weights of the actual cell and global module.
The entries of infOf(lambda) are lambda_i-i+(n-1)/2. After removing
the common shift, these form a strictly decreasing list. Equality
of the finite multisets forces equality of their successive largest
entries and hence of the dominant weights. SideRead identifies the
actual CM embeddings and the specified CharSide parameter; the
host involution agrees with complex conjugation through that
embedding. The actual Mt weight and cell rhoW/parameter fields
then provide the two equal multisets.

The field isomorphisms are turned into matching matrices using
canonical coefficient bases whose scalar extensions agree
entrywise. Finally the cell's actual bc field is transferred to
that basis using inverse change-of-basis matrices. This proves
CellWt for every permitted embedding e. It retains the existence
of the actual representations and weight fields, rather than
constructing them from the resulting dimension equality.

8. AR15–AR17: the harmonic comparison and its sign

The source coefficient module is the dual Weyl module nabla. The
earlier integral comparison CS uses divided-power Weyl Delta.
AR15 uses the characteristic-zero identity between these modules
over a field as an explicit input. It never identifies the two
integral lattices.

The new orbit argument is elementary. If a subspace is stable under
row substitutions and contains F, substitute I+zE_ba into F. For
enough distinct nonnegative integers z these are invertible matrices
and their values recover every coefficient by a Vandermonde
inverse. The coefficients are exactly the row divided powers.
Iterate to obtain every divided-power word on the highest
polynomial. The retained characteristic-zero equality supplies
the whole dual Weyl module. Tensoring over the embeddings and
absorbing the nonzero determinant shifts proves that the
coefficient module is the span of the extremal orbit.

AR16 computes the extremal harmonic value using complementary
minors. The plus minors evaluate as leading minors of y. The
negative minors evaluate as reversed complementary minors of
y inverse. The reversal contributes
(-1)^{i(i+1)/2}; the inverse minor contributes the reciprocal
determinant. Consecutive row-length drops telescope to the
correct dominant-weight exponents and determinant shift.
The total scalar is exactly the product of these reversal signs,
with the negative row-length drops as exponents. It is 1 or -1,
not a free proportionality constant.

Both the harmonic action and the coefficient action translate
the evaluation variable by inverse multiplication, after the
specified inverse-transpose change. That change is a bijection.
The extremal calculation and AR15 identify their full images.

AR17 independently proves injectivity on the harmonic orbit span.
The Fock pairing makes monomials orthogonal with squared norm
alpha!, and is positive definite. The literal mixed substitution
uses inverse-matrix coefficients on the plus variables and
transpose coefficients on the minus variables. A multinomial
calculation gives T(g) adjoint=T(g*) in that convention.

The extremal vector is an eigenvector of the lower triangular
group and is fixed by its unipotent subgroup. On the upper-times-
lower big cell, this proves that its Fock coefficient functional
is a fixed nonzero scalar multiple of evaluation at the identity.
The big cell is dense: on (1-z)g+zI the necessary principal minors
are nonzero polynomials because they are nonzero at z=1. Avoid
their finitely many roots and let z tend to zero. Continuity
extends the functional identity to every g.

If a harmonic vector evaluates to zero everywhere, all its
translates have zero evaluation at the identity. The functional
identity and adjointness make it orthogonal to every translate
of the extremal vector, hence to itself. Positive definiteness
forces it to be zero. For coefficient polynomials, multiply by
the product of determinants: vanishing on all invertible tuples
then gives a polynomial function zero everywhere; over an
infinite field the polynomial is zero. The two injective maps
with the same image give a canonical harmonic isomorphism Psi.

9. AR18–AR21: integral coefficient map and installed reading

AR18 uses the actual common base-change bases. Express a vector
in the K-basis, apply the specified embedding to its coordinates,
and combine the corresponding canonical polynomial basis
vectors. This defines an injective semilinear map Omega; composing
with Psi defines omega. The entrywise matching matrices prove
equivariance and the image lies in the harmonic space.

The two lattice properties are separate from injectivity.
The geometric coefficient lattice is defined by O-linear
combinations of the harmonic functions of the actual nabla
lattice, where O=e(R). Its generators lift to the smaller
coefficient field and then to W through Omega. Hence that lattice
is contained in the image of omega. Conversely a full lattice
spans its coefficient module; sandwiching the underlying
lattice between integral multiples of a standard lattice,
then taking the finite polynomial tensor construction and
clearing determinant shifts, proves the needed spanning.
The finitely many coefficients of a vector can be cleared by
a product of nonzero R-denominators. Thus some nonzero c in R
makes omega(cw) integral at the specified point. These are all
five WtMap clauses, not just a complex vector-space comparison.

AR19 computes LevMatch globally. AR9 transfers all the residue
entries, AR2 identifies the actual halves, and the literal
global level membership is equivalent term by term. Character
equality follows from every type, conductor and auxiliary factor.
The proof is for an arbitrary adelic g in the level, not only
for elements supported at one place. This avoids an unsupported
generation argument. The false auxiliary side uses the actual
delta_tame relation with its side exponent.

AR20 defines the reading by applying omega pointwise to the
actual realization, after the exact finite-adelic frame map.
Covariance follows from equivariance and LevMatch, and
injectivity follows from both original injectivities. An
integral geometric form takes values in the image of omega;
choose their unique inverse values. Applying omega proves
their left and right covariance, so they form a source form.
This proves that the reading range contains every integral form.

To show the integral preimage spans, choose finitely many
double-coset representatives and one nonzero denominator at each.
Their product c works at every representative: c/c_a is the
product of the other denominators and belongs to R. Covariance
then transports integrality everywhere. The level scalars are
roots of unity because they arise from finite residue groups.
Their preimages in the fraction field are integral over R and
belong to R because the actual coefficient ring is integrally
closed. Therefore r(cf) is globally integral and f=c^{-1}(cf)
lies in the field span of the integral preimage.

AR21 makes these choices for each admissible cell. At an
inadmissible parameter the source allows the zero map because
all conclusions are conditional on TwOK. The resulting reading
satisfies its pointwise join by construction. When the literal
series constructor installs rdV equal to this reading, its
equality produces SerAt.join. Two identity frames induce the
same adelic map because they preserve the same entries.

The installation condition is indispensable. We have not proved
that an arbitrary pre-existing rdV equals this map, nor have we
constructed every field of exists_preTie. The pinned good setup,
scalar normalization, faithful reading, theta/class-sum series,
Hecke tie and universal-character fields remain their named
suppliers. Transport theorems such as SeriesAtOfExpRelation
do not independently supply a new join; they transport an old one.

10. AR22 and the exact remaining arithmetic frontier

RC19 already proves global LevEq from actual local shapes,
nonzero forms, Joint and the bad-place condition. AR supplies
those comparison inputs under the stated global realization
and character premises. AR14 supplies CellWt. RC1–RC2 then give
the actual rank equality and RankTie. This is the strongest
conditional rank endpoint established here.

The next_targets.json distinguishes that completed conditional
work from its still-substantive sources:

  * The exact published global Artin reciprocity map.
  * The algebraic adic-character realization used by the normalized
    block character, including actual coefficient-ring containment.
  * Complex classification by VV and the canonical contragredient
    highest-weight theorem.
  * The characteristic-zero dual-Weyl/divided-power-Weyl identity
    (not an integral equality).
  * One actual nonempty twist/local system, its normalized Galois
    characters and all-place coverage, compatible with the same D.
  * Actual common MtReal/MtAd/NExpTied and their local/weight fields.
  * Actual pinned setup and series installation for the constructed
    reading, not an unrelated old rdV.
  * The exact local IsGalOf, twOf and twSide clauses for LocsCarry.
  * The actual same-object ColW/Pre2/RowPk/St and full choice inputs.

The complete complex HFrame supplied by HF addresses character
existence on the complex side. It does not, without these joins,
create normalized adic data or the nonempty coverage. The actual
NChoice construction in NC and the MuIsW verification in RC
remain available once their exact hypotheses are supplied. No
bare stage, tuple reset, rank count or independent existential
witness can silently replace a common compatible construction.

11. Verification and reproducibility

The builder reads immutable Git objects at the fixed commit.
For each source link it records the exact inclusive one-based
range, declaration, full-file SHA-256 and range SHA-256. Present
checkout files are checked against the Git blob without changing
the checkout. Proof paragraph hashes bind the individual AR
claims to main.tex. The builder also creates the source manifest,
the human-readable locators and the completed/retained target
map. It checks consecutive claim IDs, dependency ordering and
accidental control characters.

The independent review is attached only for current paragraph
hashes explicitly present in its review file. The source ledger
is evidence about the exact supplier interfaces, not a claim
that a Lean build was run. No Lean build, source edit, PDF
compilation or new computational RH check is part of this
component.
