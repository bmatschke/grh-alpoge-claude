# Bibliographical guide to the reconstruction

Levent Alpöge and Claude are the authors of the formal proof. This AI-assisted exposition draws on their Lean archive and on the work of other readers. We credit the account from which we learned a calculation, even when the argument can also be traced to Lean. To cite this exposition, give the edition, the result or section, and the source revision on the title page.

## Integral character lines and residual kernels

Raynaud’s fundamental character lines and Fargues’s integral Hodge–Tate complex enter the good-prime comparison. GRT27–34 use Lan’s orthogonality theorem on the polarized degeneration, including at the residue prime, and his relative Zarhin construction on each quotient. The theorem numbers below refer to the author’s revised thesis. The high-jet argument in GRT35–36 uses the section-slope formula of Bost and the sup norm and matrix lemma in Gaudron. The absolute Siegel lemma then gives the maximal slope with the required uniform constant. [Raynaud, §1.4](https://www.numdam.org/item/10.24033/bsmf.1779.pdf#page=16) · [Lan, Theorem 3.4.2.4 and Proposition 3.4.4.1](https://www.kwlan.org/articles/cpt-PEL-type-thesis-revision-dense.pdf) · [Lan, Lemma 4.1](https://www.kwlan.org/articles/cpt-ram-flat.pdf) · [Bost, Theorem 4.2](https://www.numdam.org/article/SB_1994-1995__37__115_0.pdf) · [Gaudron, §2.3 and Theorem 3.2](https://lmbp.uca.fr/~gaudron/art19.pdf) · [Gaudron–Rémond](https://lmbp.uca.fr/~gaudron/art10.pdf)

FBE12–32 draw on the signed group entries, the coalescing-block germ and the rank recursions in Kaiwen Jin’s real-restriction chapter. The two residual moments and the differentiated cutoff in the original coefficient are now written out. Miličić gives the gap-dependent decay on compact types and Bernstein–Krötz gives uniqueness of the smooth globalization used in the section norm bound. [Jin, residual integrals](https://github.com/yuiyuiui/Natural-Language-Proof-Generalized-Riemann-Hypothesis/blob/405237610e58ab9d4a9d5d54d9a0548a0c44227c/sections/g-real-restriction.tex#L786-L1260) · [Miličić](https://www.math.utah.edu/~ptrapa/math-library/milicic/milicic-asymptotics-duke-1977.pdf) · [Bernstein–Krötz](https://arxiv.org/abs/0812.1684) · [Smooth-vector deduction](../../manuscript/notes/spectral/SPECTRAL_SMOOTH_DECAY.md)

## Common covers and real periods

The component-volume argument adapts Chen's common finite cover to the actual trace polarization and level, using Landherr's theorem and strong approximation. The boundary metric argument uses Chen's toroidal growth estimate, Demailly's regularization theorem, and Bedford–Taylor convergence for bounded potentials. The geometry module records each published theorem with the hypotheses it supplies. [Chen on component volumes](https://github.com/yyyyychen/RH-Natural-Language-Proof/blob/d4dfb1a282dbf857288e73eb1f546dbfcc5899b8/tex/covolume-component-transfer.tex#L18-L109) · [Theorems used in the geometry chapter](../../research/modules/geometry/good_root_tower_comparison/published_inputs.json)

Nelson's Bessel workbook supplies the marking-group average and Haar conventions. The earlier signed-word chapter of Jin's translation supplies the cofactor and reflected-pivot computations. His revised real-cell chapter gives a separate scalar Plancherel check. The signed chapter in this edition follows the coordinates of the current Roadmap and works out the quotient and period-lattice factors in that normalization. [Nelson's Bessel workbook](https://github.com/ultronozm/grh-workbook/blob/49fe050552850f18c51af2e3875ce94702aec99c/20261126T081500--a-bessel-function-at-a-real-place__workbook.tex#L157-L228) · [Jin's earlier signed calculation](https://github.com/yuiyuiui/Natural-Language-Proof-Generalized-Riemann-Hypothesis/blob/405237610e58ab9d4a9d5d54d9a0548a0c44227c/sections/g-real-restriction.tex#L1455-L1516) · [Jin's revised real-cell calculation](https://github.com/yuiyuiui/Natural-Language-Proof-Generalized-Riemann-Hypothesis/blob/163ad1f1289299b14dad7f48aa292cd5f85c69ca/sections/real-cell.tex#L1482-L1516) · [Detailed comparison](../../manuscript/guides/EXTERNAL_PROOF_COMPARISON.md)

## How the Roadmap is used

The RH Lean Roadmap gives names and numbers to results dispersed through the formal archive. For instance, the list at the end of this chapter points to longer treatments of 63 statements in its Part VIII. The link labelled B8.55b takes the reader to the argument about evaluating algebraic sections at complex points. Our chapter on period heights shows why a relation among the evaluated functions vanishes on all the relevant frames, then uses base change for sections to descend the spanning statement to the comparison field. The number lets the reader match that argument with the Roadmap and its formal declarations. Individual chapters cite the revision they used so the line references remain meaningful. [Part VIII index](../../manuscript/guides/ROADMAP_OUTLINE_CROSS_REFERENCES.md) · [Descent of the spanning identity](../../manuscript/notes/geometry/COMMON_FAMILY_AND_PERIOD_HEIGHT.md#why-a-complex-spanning-identity-descends-to-the-comparison-field)

Elsewhere we follow the Roadmap's mathematical exposition closely. In the even-rank Galois argument, its proof of B9.41 supplies two theta lifts with distinct families of auxiliary characters. We use its choice of characters, work out their Frobenius roots and Hodge jumps, and then use disjointness to cancel the extra summands. This account of the formal construction assumes odd-rank attachment for the definite unitary lifts. For the general linear representation on the cohomological window we use the published theorem identified below. [Roadmap's character calculation](https://github.com/rh-lean-roadmap/rh-lean-roadmap/blob/c7755c1086f05d0918ee8496d95912e4f35e22f9/docs/src/book/main.tex#L61203-L61216) · [Even-rank argument in this edition](../full/even-rank-attachment.md)

The integral volume comparison uses the good root tower of B8.26u. The reference flag height, comparison of cutoffs and transition of the Newton powers are derived here. At a boundary valuation, Bosch–Lütkebohmert–Raynaud’s §7.3, Lemma 2(a) and Zariski’s main theorem give the finite flat open in the torsion. Fargues’s §5.4, Theorem 3 then applies to the Cartier dual of the induced finite flat group at depth four. Polarization identifies its generic characters and Nakayama removes the finite-level error. The underlying boundary charts and the quantitative metric hypotheses are recorded where they enter. [Néron Models, §7.3](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-51438-8) · [Zariski’s main theorem](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/05K0) · [Fargues, Ann. Sci. Éc. Norm. Supér. 44 (2011)](https://www.numdam.org/item/10.24033/asens.2157.pdf#page=32) · [Proof on the flag model](../../manuscript/notes/periods/GOOD_ROOT_TOWER_COMPARISONS.md) · [Moriwaki’s volume bound and the comparison of sections](../../manuscript/notes/periods/PERIOD_VOLUME_BEFORE_SELECTION.md)

At a crystalline place we use Bhatt and Scholze’s Theorem 5.6 and their filtered modification in Construction 6.5 together with Wu’s description of the completed cyclotomic evaluation. The lower-root action, equality of the integral lattices, height inclusions and primitive jets are calculated in the book. In the spectral argument the finite positive-kernel comparison and cutoff exhaustion use B8.10e and the residual coefficient comparisons of the Roadmap and Jin. [Bhatt and Scholze, pp. 534–535](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10433702#page=28) · [Wu, Theorem 5.2 and Corollary 5.8](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/26632?nt=1#page=21) · [Local calculation](../../manuscript/notes/galois/CYCLOTOMIC_WACH_COMPARISON.md) · [Flag estimate](../../manuscript/notes/spectral/FLAG_BLOCK_ENERGY_AND_CUTOFF.md)

For ramified Galois comparison we follow the Roadmap's construction of a cuspidal CM lift fixing a chosen Weil element. The trace recurrence comes from the corresponding lemma in Alpöge and Claude's Lean proof. For the full tame test we apply Chang Yang, *Gamma factors of level zero supercuspidal representations*, Journal of the Ramanujan Mathematical Society 38 (2023), 295–307. Propositions 4.2–4.3 and 5.1 in the author's preprint identify the tame irreducible parameters and compute their unramified base change. The chapter checks them against the type and determinant laws of the weak local correspondence used here. ## Formal source and working manuscripts

- **Levent Alpöge and Claude, formal proof archive, November 2026.** The five terminal declarations determine the scope of RH and of Dirichlet GRH here. Alpöge's account explains the use of repeated theta lifts and the choice of their number. The public mirror is cited at a fixed commit so that a declaration and its hypotheses can be retrieved together. - **RH Lean Roadmap, book, version 41.** Its numbered statements and its index of declarations connect the host-field construction, the estimates for the theta periods, the frame argument and the conclusions about zeros. ## Examples of refereed inputs and their precise scope

- **Thomas Barnet-Lamb, Toby Gee, David Geraghty and Richard Taylor, “Potential automorphy and change of weight,” Annals of Mathematics 179 (2014), 501–609, Theorem 2.1.1, p. 538.** Parts (2)–(4) give the local parameters, Hodge–Tate numbers and crystallinity needed for B9.38. The spherical attachment chapter checks the polarization and dual highest-weight conventions, computes the inverse-Frobenius roots and transports the period assertions to every permitted comparison. [Journal article](https://doi.org/10.4007/annals.2014.179.2.3) · [Published theorem](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v179-n2-p03-p.pdf#page=38) · [Application to B9.38](../../manuscript/notes/galois/SPHERICAL_GALOIS_ATTACHMENT.md)
- **Bruce Ferrero and Lawrence C. Washington, “The Iwasawa invariant $\mu_p$ vanishes for abelian number fields,” Annals of Mathematics 109 (1979), 377–395.** Vanishing of the cyclotomic Iwasawa invariant for the abelian fields in question is an input to the host argument. Selecting a common interval for the twists and the prime sums still requires the construction in this manuscript. [Journal article](https://doi.org/10.2307/1971116)
- **Jean-Benoît Bost and Huayi Chen, “Concerning the semistability of tensor products in Arakelov geometry,” Journal de Mathématiques Pures et Appliquées 99 (2013), 436–488, Proposition 2.10(3).** The proposition controls the extreme slopes of a nonzero morphism. The manuscript identifies the source and target filtration steps, the common period maps and the squared operator norm to which this slope comparison is applied. [Journal article](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.matpur.2012.09.009) · [Authors' copy](https://webusers.imj-prg.fr/~huayi.chen/Recherche/bost_chen.pdf#page=26)
- **Atsushi Moriwaki, “Continuity of volumes on arithmetic varieties,” Journal of Algebraic Geometry 18 (2009), 407–457, Corollary 6.4.** The volume–intersection lower bound applies on the indicated arithmetic model under its positivity hypotheses. Construction of that model, regularization of its metrics, passage to cusp sections and comparison with the original lattice account for the proof-specific work. [Journal article](https://doi.org/10.1090/S1056-3911-08-00500-6) · [Author's preprint, Corollary 6.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0612269#page=42)

These examples illustrate how to read the citations across the volume. A theorem about a single lattice, a single prime, or one fixed height does not by itself prove the simultaneous statement needed on all the selected words, places and scales. The bridge from a published theorem to that simultaneous assertion must account for those quantifiers.
