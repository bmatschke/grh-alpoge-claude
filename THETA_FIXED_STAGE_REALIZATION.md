# The theta maps on a fixed arithmetic stage

**Theorem.** Fix an original arithmetic column at the stage of [the preliminary construction](BASE_THETA_CONSTRUCTION_STEP.md), with its coefficient fields and embeddings, marked primes, finite characters and levels. Assume the preliminary datum satisfies its level, inert-prime, tuple and all-place identifications and its retained frame and coefficient conditions. Its ordinary and lift readings on decorated lattices intertwine the full-level split and inert Hecke operators by the [Hecke-neighbor comparison](THETA_DECORATED_HECKE.md). Its Hilbert spaces, Haar measures, Weil actions and polynomial tests can be chosen together on that stage. The original level identifications, ordinary and definite readings and their Hecke comparisons pass to this choice. If the host satisfies the source's interlacing and gap hypotheses, all eight analytic map-existence assertions hold on this realization. More generally the eight assertions hold on every weak realization satisfying the same level, family, model, group and host laws. This last quantifier lets one apply the result again after installing the geometric coordinates and scalars. [Fixed-stage selection](continuation_v77/seed.tex#L51-L98) · [Host interlacing and gaps][gap] · [Universal map assertion][eight]

There are two mathematical operations here. First one forms spaces of actual cusp forms with their Petersson inner products and places the prescribed test functions in the oscillator representation. Then one integrates those test functions and projects to the specified automorphic representations. Distinguishing the operations matters because two different choices of a scalar in the first could change the answer in the second.

## Putting the oscillator on the chosen lattices

Use the [global Weil action](WEIL_AUTOMORPHIC_MODEL.md) with the specified splitting characters. At an odd nonsplit place with unramified characters the lattice stabilizer fixes every theta function fixed by the local Heisenberg lattice. The CM extension in the given host is unramified at each finite nonsplit place. For the action normalized on those lattices the fixing assertion follows from the local lattice projector. Doubling the lattice leaves it unchanged at this place. The canonical auxiliary character is unramified here. For a change to the specified characters, a norm-one determinant has the form $x/\bar x$ with $x$ a local unit. Indeed, Hilbert 90 gives a nonzero $x$, whose valuation can be adjusted to zero by a power of the ground-field uniformizer since the extension is unramified. Both character ratios on this unit are one. One can perform the projector calculation using *any* of the self-dual lattices and their frames in the assertion. Reconstruct the action using those lattices and the same splitting characters. Uniqueness on theta functions identifies it with the action already chosen. This proves the spherical law for that single action on every lattice under test. [Local projector](canonical_weil/main.tex#L241-L255) · [Uniqueness and passage to the specified splitting](base_theta/main.tex#L1142-L1226) · [Local unit calculation](base_theta/main.tex#L1287-L1343) · [Exact inert and real assertions][full]

At a real place the Lie action can also be checked in the literature. Let $n=\dim_E S$, $m=\dim_E T$. In signed orthonormal frames write the real-linear endomorphism $A:u\mapsto X_Tu-uX_S$ in coordinates for the **positive** complex structure as $Az=Mz+N\bar z$. This requires conjugating the coordinates on the negative Hom lines. Equivalently $M$ and $N$ are the framed coefficient matrices of $(A-JAJ)/2$ and $(A+JAJ)/2$, respectively. The infinitesimal metaplectic action is the symmetrized quadratic in the Weyl algebra. Using multiplication by $z_k$ and differentiation $\partial_k$ gives
$$
 \sum_{k,l}\left(M_{kl}z_k\partial_l+
 \tfrac12 N_{kl}z_kz_l-\tfrac12\overline{N_{kl}}\partial_k\partial_l\right)
 +\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}M .
$$
Here $N$ is symmetric, and the last scalar is the contribution of $[\partial_l,z_k]=\delta_{kl}$ when a symmetrized mixed pair is reordered. These are exactly the source's creation coordinates, including its signs of double creation and double differentiation. Funke and Hofmann give the Weyl-algebra construction for a general central character and the unitary pair of arbitrary signatures in Appendix B. [Funke–Hofmann, Appendix B, (B.1)](https://www.mathi.uni-heidelberg.de/~hofmann/files/umillson32b.pdf#page=38) · [Signed coefficients and the full polynomial operator][quad]

It remains to identify the scalar added by the **given** splitting. Let $A_+,A_-$ be the two blocks on the positive and negative Hom lines for compact diagonal generators, written in the original complex coordinates. The canonical action on a vacuum has differential $\operatorname{tr}A_+$ there. The oscillator half-trace in positive coordinates is $(\operatorname{tr}A_+-\operatorname{tr}A_-)/2$. Their difference is $\tfrac12(n\operatorname{tr}X_T-m\operatorname{tr}X_S)$. On the common covering group the two lifts implement the same Heisenberg action, so their scalar difference is a character. Its differential is determined on these compact diagonal generators, since the abelianized Lie algebra of each unitary factor is measured by the trace. Passing from the canonical splitting to characters with infinity exponents $\alpha_S,\alpha_T$ adds
$(\alpha_S-n/2)\operatorname{tr}X_T-(\alpha_T-m/2)\operatorname{tr}X_S$.
For example the determinant root of $e^{itb}$ near the identity is $e^{itb/2}$ modulo a real unit, and the character of exponent $\alpha$ reads $e^{it\alpha b}$ there. Thus the scalar to add to the displayed metaplectic operator is exactly
$$
                 \alpha_S\operatorname{tr}X_T-\alpha_T\operatorname{tr}X_S .
$$
The computation applies to a function satisfying the vacuum equations at this place alone. In the Schrödinger model their common kernel here is the Gaussian line, tensored with the remaining variables. The other real creation operators commute with this local calculation. Smoothness in the theta-function topology permits evaluation after differentiation at every adelic argument. This also shows that applying any polynomial in the real creation operators to a smooth theta function preserves its theta law and its finite open level. [Canonical vacuum determinant](canonical_weil/main.tex#L495-L534) · [Determinant roots](joint_harmonic_io/main.tex#L1027-L1068) · [Smoothness and differentiation](theta_extension/main.tex#L75-L297) · [All frames, real generators and single-place vacua][der]

The [normalized lattice vacuum](THETA_LATTICE_VACUUM.md) and the [two finite Fourier tests](THETA_FINITE_LATTICE_TESTS.md) now give actual theta functions at each tuple. Even an abstractly specified vector characterized by its common value $f$ in every allowed finite model must be a theta function. To see this, let $\ell$ be any linear functional on all functions that kills the theta subspace. Put $D_\ell(g)(w)=\ell(H_wg)$, where $H$ is the Heisenberg action. The product law shows that $D_\ell$ commutes with $H$, and $D_\ell(f_0)=0$ because all translates of the vacuum are theta functions. For an allowed normalized model $T$, the map $T+D_\ell T$ is therefore another such model. Equality on the prescribed test gives $D_\ell(f)=0$. Evaluating at zero gives $\ell(f)=0$. Algebraic linear functionals separate a subspace from its complement, proving the assertion. [Universal characterization and separation argument][theta]

## The automorphic Hilbert spaces and finite levels

For a unitary group $G$ on the stage, normalize finite Haar measure to give its prescribed compact open level mass one. At each real place choose Haar measure with the specified positive density in the local Lie coordinates. The left-invariant volume density of a Lie group is determined by its value at the identity, so this choice exists and is unique. Where compact probability is prescribed, take volume one on that compact real group. Their product is the required adelic measure. A change of signed orthonormal frame conjugates the real group and preserves Haar measure by unimodularity. The archimedean evaluations are the fixed extensions from the dense rational coordinates. These facts give the same normalization for every compatible frame in the rectangle formula. The rational subgroup is countable and discrete in the second-countable adelic group. It has a Borel fundamental domain, and the finite-volume theorem applies since this unitary group has no nontrivial rational characters. [Measure normalization][measure] · [Existence and uniqueness of its density][density] · [Finite-volume proof and fundamental domains](finite_volume/main.tex#L241-L354)

Use smooth equivariant maps from the local discrete-series factors to cuspidal forms at this level. Evaluation on one tuple of nonzero vectors in their lowest compact types is injective. Indeed, each such vector generates its irreducible Harish-Chandra module, and those modules are dense in the smooth representations. Equivariance and continuity of point evaluations then propagate vanishing one variable at a time. The resulting compact types and infinitesimal characters are fixed. Harish-Chandra finiteness bounds the dimension at the prescribed open level (and more generally whenever the level character contains an open kernel as required in the source). Rapid decay makes the quotient pairing convergent. Schur's lemma in the smooth topology gives, for any two multiplicity maps $T,T'$,
$$
 \int_{[G]}\overline{T(v)(g)}\,T'(w)(g)\,dg
    =(T,T')_{\mathrm{mult}}\prod_\tau\langle v_\tau,w_\tau\rangle.
$$
Putting smooth unit vectors in both arguments defines the Petersson form on multiplicities. A nonzero map has a nonzero continuous automorphic value, whose square has strictly positive quotient integral. This makes the finite-dimensional multiplicity spaces Hilbert spaces with exactly the required metric. Smooth nonzero vectors exist by convolution with an approximate identity. The prescribed dominant compact weights give nonzero types by the Weyl dimension formula. [Fixed-type finiteness and decay](continuation_v89/decay.tex#L49-L152) · [Schur pairing on smooth vectors](continuation_v90/join.tex#L64-L115) · [Discrete series and lowest type](continuation_v90/discrete.tex#L101-L170)

The finite levels are compact open. In an integral frame they restrict a lattice stabilizer by conditions on finitely many residue matrices. A principal congruence subgroup lies in the kernel of their character. At a marked split place lift the invertible residue block to an integral matrix whose determinant is a local unit, use identity on the other diagonal blocks, and take the inverse adjoint on the conjugate half. This realizes every marked finite-group element. Two such lifts differ by an element of the level kernel of reduction. Right translation divided by the extended flag character therefore acts independently of the lift on principal-level forms with the prescribed type. The product of lifts lifts the product, so these operators constitute a representation. The character expansion of the good projector has conjugacy-invariant coefficients, and its operator commutes with that action. At each twist the two splitting characters restrict to the appropriate powers of the quadratic character by the parities of the ranks. The chosen oriented split primes are disjoint from their conjugates, furnishing the half idempotent used in the tests. [Residue-group action][act] · [Projector and group identification](BASE_THETA_CONSTRUCTION_STEP.md) · [Character parities and primes][seed]

Finally the signed frames give the Cartan involutions, with identity on a positive-definite space. The commensurability bounds in a base frame sandwich its lattice between $N$ and $N^{-1}$ times a full reference lattice. Noetherianity then gives finite generation, the lower bound gives fullness, and the stage's duality equality gives self-duality. At the last tower index the negative signature has size $\lfloor(m_0-(m_0-1))/2\rfloor=0$, where $m_0$ denotes the number of steps. The terminal space is also definite by the stage signatures. This supplies the compact real measures where required. [Cartans, lattices, last signatures and positivity][easy]

## Choosing and identifying the eight maps

Install the preceding Hilbert spaces and measures on the preliminary column, using its definite coefficient readings. Install the chain and row kernels on their given lattices. For each chain step choose its real intertwiner before choosing the multiplicity map at any twist, then take the bottom projection. For each row choose its common polynomial map before taking its theta integral and good projection. In the weak realization one chooses a witness when its defining property is satisfiable and uses the source's zero choice otherwise. These choices only replace the indicated analytic components. The ordinary and definite coefficient readings, their Hecke comparisons, the residue-group identification and the tuple and character assignments carry through by the fixed-stage selection. This constructs the weak realization on the preliminary column under its stated assumptions. [All analytic ingredients in the source][inputs] · [Selection in dependency order][choose] · [Preservation of the two readings](continuation_v77/seed.tex#L51-L98)

Now impose the host conditions in the theorem. Each assertion below is made about this same weak realization, or about any other one satisfying the indicated laws. In the latter case the stored Weil operators agree on theta functions with the normalized operators for those characters by uniqueness. The weak kernel laws identify their finite tests in every normalized place model. The model laws identify the input and output multiplicity spaces and their measures with the ones used in the integral. [Uniqueness on theta functions](base_theta/main.tex#L1142-L1226) · [Passage from these data to the interior integral][passage]
