# Counting the complementary kernels at fixed saturation and image index

Use the local data and notation of the [trace mass computation](SPECTRAL_TRACE_LATTICE_MASS.md). Put $a=v_F(\det\operatorname{Gram}M)$ in an integral basis of the difference lattice, so $|M^\#/M|=q^{2a}$. It is independent of the frame class on the source's nonsingular point. Write $\mathcal W$ for the total relative inverse stabilizer weight of the integral frame classes, $b=1$ (split) or $2$ (inert), and $\epsilon=7q^{-(k-2r-4)}$. On the native moved-frame, phase and class identifications the signed contribution $a_N$ of all positive-defect terms satisfies
$$
 \|a_N\|_{L^2(w_d)}^2
 \le 2^{32}\,\mathcal W q^{2am} n_0
 \begin{cases}
     q^{-(k+1)},&\text{split},\\
     q^{-2(t-m-1)-6},&\text{inert}.
 \end{cases}                                                     \tag{1}
$$
Here $t=k-1,\ m=2r+2$, $k\ge4r+7$, $r\ge1$. The corresponding zero-defect bound is $\|a_Z\|^2\le\mathcal W q^{2am} n_0$, including when $a=0$. The gain in (1) comes from counting kernels by their index and retaining the exact discriminant of the *saturated* frame lattice.

## The integral count

Fix a datum $d$ and one frame class. Write $P=\Lambda\cap Y$ for the saturated frame lattice with $\beta=|P/M|=q^i$, and $N=\Lambda\cap Y^\perp$. The local model supplies a free rank-$t$ lattice $B_d$ on $Y^\perp$ and its gluing map to $P/M$. Its kernel is $N$, and the cardinality of its image is the source's $q^l$. Thus
$$
                         [B_d:N]=q^l.                             \tag{2}
$$
The common $B_d$ in (2) matters: it is fixed before the lattice $\Lambda$ is counted. In frame coordinates the map assigns to $w\in B_d$ the class of $(x'-x)/\theta$, using integral lifts $w+x,w+x'$ in the two isotropic prefix directions with $x'-x\in\theta\Lambda$. The graph defining $B_d$ supplies the lifts. If they are changed, the two changes in the flag directions are integral prefix combinations. Replace the primed combination by the identical combination in the unprimed frame; their difference divided by $\theta$ lies in $M$. The remaining unprimed difference divided by $\theta$ is integral and lies in the first prefix, whose intersection with $\Lambda$ is generated by the initial frame there. This proves independence of the class. Sums and integral multiples add the corresponding lifts. A vector of $N$ has the zero pair of lifts in the flag directions. Conversely, if the class is zero, write its representative as an integral combination of $g_i$ and $(g'_i-g_i)/\theta$. Therefore $x'-x$ can be written $z'-z$ with $z$ in the integral first full frame and $z'$ in the primed full span. Uniqueness in the direct sum of the two full frame spans forces $x=z$. Since $w+x$ is integral, $w$ is integral, hence in $N$. [Native graph relation and existence][tau] · [Zero kernel criterion and image][ker]

For fixed $i,l$ there are at most $A_m(i)$ possibilities for $P$, by (3) of the trace mass computation. By (2) there are at most $A_t(l)$ choices of $N$, regardless of the quotient's module type or of the embedding of its image in $P/M$. For fixed $P,N$, an ambient self-dual $\Lambda$ is an integral gluing of the perpendicular lattices $P,N$. Its projection to $Y$ is $P^\#$: any integral functional on $P$ extends to $\Lambda$ since $P$ is saturated, and $\Lambda$ is self-dual. Similarly its projection to $Y^\perp$ is $N^\#$. Therefore $\Lambda/(P\oplus N)$ is the graph of an isomorphism $P^\#/P\to N^\#/N$, which determines $\Lambda$ and reverses the discriminant pairings. Both modules have cardinality
$$
                |P^\#/P|=|M^\#/M|/\beta^2=q^{2a}/\beta^2.
$$
Indeed $[M^\#:P^\#]=[P:M]$ by the dual pairing. The source module has at most $m$ generators over $O$, including over the split product ring by taking bases in each factor. There are consequently at most $(q^{2a}/\beta^2)^m$ possible gluings. This upper bound counts all homomorphisms and so includes every allowed anti-isometry. [Native discriminant and gluing count][glue] · [Frame determinant][det]

For $b_0=0$ or $b$ let $M_i(d;b_0)$ be the unsigned mass of terms at order $q^i$ with $l\le i-b_0$. The source disintegration at fixed $d$ multiplies each class count by its actual inverse stabilizer weight. From (2) and the preceding count,
$$
 M_i(d;b_0)
 \le\mathcal W\, A_m(i)(q^{2a}/\beta^2)^m
                         \sum_{l=0}^{i-b_0} A_t(l).               \tag{3}
$$
In particular this reconstructs the fixed-datum bound without a uniform estimate on the number of kernels of maps into all possible defect modules. For $i=l=0$ the two lattice counts are both one. For any positive represented order, $i\ge b$. Proper image implies $l\le i-b$, since all inert lattice indices have even exponent. By the bound on $A_d$ and summation of a geometric progression,
$$
 M_i(d;b_0)\le 512(i+1)^2\mathcal W q^{2am}
                    q^{i(t-m-2)}q^{-b_0(t-1)}.                  \tag{4}
$$

The uniform frame weight used here satisfies $\mathcal W\le q^{r(r+1)(v_\theta+v_D)}$, where the local point height is $H=q^{a+v_\theta+v_D}$. We recall the comparisons used for that substitution. Normalize the integral frame class by the common block stabilizer so that entries of the first unipotent matrix in each diagonal block are integral. Its stabilizer in the normalized position is contained in the reference compact group; the inverse stabilizer weight is the size of the compact-group orbit. The normalized orbits used for distinct classes are disjoint. The source leading-minor elimination clears the two triangular matrices by $\bar\Delta,\Delta$, with $v_D=v_E(\Delta\bar\Delta)$ in its normalization. Classes are counted by their two integral right cosets and by one upper unipotent residue matrix modulo $\theta$ to compare the changes of frame. For a triangular matrix whose entries are in $z^{-1}O$, count its right cosets modulo the integral upper unipotent group column by column: reduce a column by earlier columns from its last possible nonzero off-diagonal entry upwards. Every step uses an integral multiple, and keeps all entries in $z^{-1}O$. This gives at most $|O/zO|^{r(r+1)/2}$ cosets. The two clearings together and the residue matrix give the stated bound with the source index convention. Using (3) or (1) with the actual $\mathcal W$ does not require this last substitution. ## Squaring after fixing $i$

Use the two types of the [elementary move estimate](SPECTRAL_POSITIVE_DEFECT_ENERGY.md). At fixed $d,i$ let $P_i,F_i$ be their unsigned masses. The first type has proper image, hence $P_i\le M_i(d;b)$. The second type satisfies $F_i\le M_i(d;0)$; its phase-weighted subtotal has norm at most $\epsilon F_i$ under the [moved-witness comparison](SPECTRAL_MOVE_CHARACTER_CONDUCTOR.md). Cauchy–Schwarz bounds the squared total at this $d,i$ by $(P_i+F_i)(P_i+\epsilon^2F_i)$. Sum the first bracket over $d$ with the actual weights, using $n_i\le64(i+1)q^{i(m-1-t)}n_0$ from the trace mass computation. Bound the second by (4) before summation. This yields
$$
 \|a_i\|^2\le 2^{15}(i+1)^3\mathcal W q^{2am}\,n_0
               q^{-3i}\,[q^{-b(t-1)}+\epsilon^2].                \tag{5}
$$
The exponent $-3i$ results from adding $i(t-m-2)$ to $i(m-1-t)$. It is useful when many defect orders occur at a high-discriminant point.

By the triangle inequality, summing (5) costs the square of $\sum_{i\ge b}(i+1)^{3/2}q^{-3i/2}$. This is at most $36^2 q^{-3b}$, since $\sum_{j\ge0}(j+3)^2 2^{-j}=36$. For $b=1$ the square bracket is at most $50q^{-(t-1)}$ because $2(k-2r-4)\ge t$. For $b=2$ it is at most $50q^{-2(t-m-1)}$. Together with $2^{15}36^2\,50<2^{32}$ these give (1). The bound for $a_Z$ follows from (3) at $i=l=0$ by taking one unsigned mass outside the squared sum and summing the other to $n_0$.

## The finite product with class normalization

For sufficiently large selected ranks, for instance $k\ge60$ and $f=[F:\mathbb Q]\le k$, the finite factor of the nongeneric corank-one squared class amplitude, divided by the real factor $J_2(\gamma)>0$, is at most
$$
 9\cdot 2^{32} f^2\, 2^{-(k+1)}
 H(\gamma)^{\,2+\max(2r(r+1),\,2m)} n_{\rm gen}(\gamma).         \tag{6}
$$
At $J_2=0$ the corresponding class vectors are zero. For the proof normalize each subset vector by $\sqrt{V_0J_2(\gamma)}$, where $V_0$ is the common class mass factor. The squared orbit-to-tuple bound for this subset gives a factor $\sqrt{\mathcal W q^{2am} n_0}$ in its norm at each place, and an additional $2^{16}q^{-(k+1)/2}$ at each positive place. The inert exponent in (1) is at least $k+1$ throughout the stated range. For $z=(k+1)/2$, the sum of the extra factors is at most $S=2^{16} f\,2^{-z}(1+2/(z-1))$, by the integral comparison for residue norms. It is at most one from $k=60$ onwards. Hence the squared union factor is at most $(e^S-1)^2\le 9\cdot2^{32} f^2 2^{-(k+1)}$. Use $n_0\le4\mathcal W n_{\rm gen}$ on the active places and the exact height-one normalization elsewhere. Since $4^{|\Omega|}\le H(\gamma)^2$ and $\mathcal W^2q^{2am}\le H^{\max(2r(r+1),2m)}$, restoring $V_0$ yields (6). One can instead keep the exact Euler factors of (1) and the exact weights for a given point. The remaining real-energy, global orbital measure and other-corank comparisons are the ones in [the selected energy deduction](SPECTRAL_POSITIVE_DEFECT_ENERGY.md#global-finite-product-and-corank-one-energy). Neither the finite bound nor its insertion proves the entire spectral obligation.
