## How the theta tower uses the oscillator representation

At a real place the chain records two signatures and four blocks of weights. The classical unitary theta correspondence exchanges two blocks and inserts a string of $t$ weights in a prescribed compact factor. The smaller discrete series has positive Plancherel mass. Its pair with the larger discrete series therefore gives a Hilbert-space inclusion. Taking its adjoint on polynomials constructs the same real operator for every finite twist. On the reference tuple the coefficient field can grow while the normalized polynomial and the actual projected theta integral use the same test, measure and rational forms. [Real discrete-series argument](THETA_INTERIOR_DISCRETE_SERIES.md) · [Weil model](WEIL_AUTOMORPHIC_MODEL.md) · [Reference kernel](THETA_REFERENCE_WEIL.md) · [Reference lift](THETA_REFERENCE_LIFT.md)

In the direction used by the integral, a proper constant term would be a lift to a group at least two ranks smaller. This would require the two missing neighbors of the new string in the input parameter. The resulting cuspidality permits projection onto the discrete automorphic spectrum. Howe’s multiplicity-one theorem then identifies the kernel of the global integral on each constituent with a relation already killed by the stored real operator whenever that projection is nonzero. To prove the images agree inside the automorphic space, subtract the scalar multiple given by their mixed Petersson form and reverse-lift the difference. The reverse lift is constant and its average vanishes. The doubled norm at $-a/2$ forces the orthogonal difference to vanish. ## Local Bessel uniqueness has a direct citation

At one real place take $G=U(k+r,r+1)$. Its defining Hermitian space consists of $r$ hyperbolic planes and a middle space of signature $(k,1)$. Fix a negative vector $z$ in that middle space. Its orthogonal complement there is positive definite of rank $k$, so the compact group in the Bessel subgroup is $G_0=U(k)$. Let $N$ be the unipotent radical for the indicated isotropic flag and $S_r=N\rtimes G_0$. **The local question is whether two continuous Bessel maps from a discrete series of $G$ into the same irreducible representation of $U(k)$ can be linearly independent.** [Source group, flag and character](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Formalism/FormalDegreeBessel/StatementG.lean#L13-L58) · [Exact local conclusion](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Formalism/PeriodIntegrals/BesselMapUniq.lean#L15-L59)

Here is a proof suitable for the text. Write $\sigma^\infty$ for the smooth vectors of the given discrete series and $\tau$ for the finite-dimensional irreducible target. A Bessel map $B$ gives the functional $(v,\ell)\mapsto\ell(Bv)$ on $\sigma^\infty\widehat\otimes\tau^\vee$. The source’s bound by a finite sum of derivative seminorms gives continuity. Equivariance gives the Bessel character on $N$ and invariance on the diagonal $G_0$. The successive isotropic lines and the last line generated modulo them by $z$ are the flag in Jiang–Sun–Zhu, §2.1. The nonzero coefficients in the additive character give a nontrivial character on each successive simple coordinate, exactly the genericity condition in §2.2. The two representations are in the smooth admissible Fréchet category of moderate growth. **Jiang–Sun–Zhu, Theorem A, gives dimension at most one for this space of functionals.** Evaluation on the dual of $\tau$ separates its vectors, so the same conclusion holds for the original maps on smooth vectors. This replaces a local uniqueness proof. Existence, the value on a prescribed test vector and estimates uniform as the parameters vary are separate questions. [Jiang–Sun–Zhu, Theorem A and §§2.1–2.2](https://www-users.cse.umn.edu/~jiang034/Papers/JSZ09.pdf) · [Smooth globalization of the discrete series](https://arxiv.org/abs/0812.1684) · [Application in the pinned argument](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/CompanionTheory/AutomorphicTheory/LocalBesselUniqueness/BesselModels/MultiplicityOneUnitaryBranchingOfUniqGap.lean#L10-L26)

Applying this theorem independently at each real place and pulling the resulting line back along the real theta intertwiners gives a [common archimedean test](ROW_COMMON_REAL_TEST.md). It preserves the joint kernel of the full finite catalogue, so one can choose the real polynomials and covector for the arithmetic field construction before proving injectivity.

There is an earlier arithmetic example of this method. Thorne’s Theorem 6.1 gives $H^1_f(F^+,\operatorname{ad} r_{\pi,\iota})=0$ for the polarized Galois representation attached to a regular algebraic cuspidal representation of $\mathrm{GL}_n$ over a CM field $F$, $n\ge2$, with irreducible restriction to $G_{F(\mu_{p^\infty})}$. The work in applying it is identifying the polarized adjoint over $F^+$ and verifying the restriction hypothesis. Once these agree, the Selmer vanishing can be cited in one sentence. [Thorne, Theorem 6.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2207.04925#page=24) · [The project’s identification](conditional_main_v42/adjoint_matching.tex)

## A short reading of the denominator’s multiplicity step

Let $E$ be a good upper Hecke eigenspace and $T$ the prescribed upward theta map from its lower point space. The question is whether $\operatorname{im}T=E$. It suffices to show that the prescribed adjoint lift detects every $0\ne f\in E$. This last quantifier matters when an eigencharacter has multiplicity. [Pointwise formulation](continuation_v87/return.tex)

1. **A string of Satake parameters forces a pole.** Write $r$ for the lower rank, $n=r+t$ for the upper rank, and $t$ for the string length. After twisting out its character, the string contributes $t$ shifted partial Dedekind zeta functions to the standard $L$-function. At $u=(t+1)/2$ their arguments are $1,2,\ldots,t$. Exactly one factor has a pole. The bound on the remaining Satake roots makes their Euler product absolutely convergent and nonzero there. [Pole calculation](continuation_v86/pole.tex)
2. **The residue forces a theta period on the specified lower space.** Choose a scalar component of this $f$ and a polynomial test. At infinity their matrix coefficients give an integral of an absolute square on a compact group, positive since the coefficient is nonzero at the identity. A congruence test supplies the nonzero factor at the excluded finite places. At ordinary places cite the spherical doubling formula of Eischen–Harris–Li–Skinner, §4.2.1, (53). Its numerator uses $u=s+1/2$, so the doubling integral has a nonzero residue at $s=t/2$. Adjoining $t$ hyperbolic planes to the given lower space produces the complementary dimensions $r+2t$ and $r$ in one Witt tower, with sum $2n$. Gan–Qiu–Takeda, Theorem 7.3(i), identifies the Eisenstein residue with a nonzero constant times a theta-integral coefficient on the complementary space. If every downward lift of this component vanished, the pullback pairing with that coefficient would vanish, also in the regularized case. Hence some Schwartz test detects $f$ on the required space. [Unramified formula](https://doi.org/10.1017/fmp.2020.4) · [Complementary first term identity](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1207.4709#page=34) · [Project’s local tests, pullback and regularization](continuation_v87/residue.tex)
3. **The prescribed test detects the same vector.** After the good projection, the needed functions at ordinary nonsplit places are generated by target translates of the lattice vacuum. Here the pair is unramified, both lattices are self-dual and the residue characteristic is odd. This is the setting of Mœglin–Vignéras–Waldspurger, Chapter V, Theorem I.4, p. 103. The split places, prescribed ramified types and real polynomial comparison have their own short propositions in a detailed account. Together they make vanishing for the prescribed test imply vanishing for every test. The preceding residue rules this out. Now $(\operatorname{im}T)^\perp=\ker T^*$ on the point space proves surjectivity. [MVW](https://doi.org/10.1007/BFb0082712) · [Takeda’s statement of the lattice setting, §§1 and 6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1206.3589) · [Prescribed-vector comparison](continuation_v87/fixedtest.tex) · [Transfer to the denominator bound](continuation_v87/denominator.tex)

A tempting shorter citation illustrates how to check the direction of an implication. Jiang–Wu, Theorem 3.3(1), applied at this standard $L$-pole gives an upper bound of $r$ for *lowest occurrence*, with their character twist. Their definition takes the minimum over Witt towers of the relevant parity. It explains why this rank appears. Selecting the given Hermitian space and detecting each vector with a designated test require the additional comparisons above. [Jiang–Wu, §3.2 and Theorem 3.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1409.0767#page=17)

## Which theta vectors share a field of definition?

Fix the prescribed embedding of the coefficient field and write $k$ for its image in $\mathbb C$. Fix a lift index and a level. In the complex vector space $W$ put into one set $S$ all the required images of rational source vectors under the tuple-dependent theta maps, together with the stored target-coordinate columns at this level. **The separating-test condition says precisely that these vectors lie in a single $k$-form of $W$.** The theta maps retain their original normalizations. [Definition of the tests](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/IndefiniteReal/RationalAdjoints/RatZOfTests.lean#L16-L30)

Indeed, let $U=\operatorname{span}_k S$. A finite separating collection of complex-linear tests embeds $W$ in $\mathbb C^N$ and sends $U$ into $k^N$. Any finite $k$-independent family in $U$ remains complex-independent, as witnessed by a nonzero minor of its test-value matrix. Thus $\mathbb C\otimes_k U\to W$ is injective. Conversely, given this injectivity, extend a $k$-basis of $U$ to a complex basis of $W$ by adjoining suitable vectors. Its coordinate functions are the tests and its $k$-span is the desired form. This includes $U=0$ and the case where $S$ does not span $W$. For the full source condition one collection works for *all* the tuples at fixed index and level. The small-level application in BTH8 uses the base tuple alone. The two levels can be treated separately when both are requested. [Base-tuple application](base_theta/main.tex) · [All-tuple result under the geometric inputs, RTG21](rational_theta/main.tex)

There is also a coordinate test a reader can use. Pick vectors of $S$ that form a complex basis of its complex span. The condition is exactly that every other specified vector has all its coordinates in $k$ in that basis. These coordinates are ratios of minors. This formulation makes visible what a period normalization would have to accomplish.

The analytic map in this comparison is now available on each supplied arithmetic column under its field, level, reading and host hypotheses. Its construction uses the lattice vacuum and two different residue-matrix tests. The real theta correspondence identifies the interior representations, and a missing pair of weights forces all proper constant terms of the downward integral to vanish. The remaining comparison identifies the resulting automorphic forms with the same marked algebraic sections at both levels. [Fixed-stage proof](THETA_FIXED_STAGE_REALIZATION.md) · [Next geometric calculation](NEXT_STEPS.md)

Harris’s corrected Theorem E.2.1 is a natural result to compare. It proves rationality of a specified cohomological theta lift after dividing by a period, over the field stated there involving $\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}$. Its Galois-equivariance law transforms the finite additive character as well as the Schwartz function. To use it here one must match the source and target realizations, the direction and range of the lift, and the finite and infinite tests. One must then compare the period-normalized vectors with the **stored columns over this particular $k$**. For example, in $W=\mathbb C$ each singleton $\{1\}$ and $\{\sqrt 2\}$ lies in a $\mathbb Q$-form, while their union does not. The arithmetic task is the relative normalization and descent. [Harris, corrected Theorem E.2.1, pp. 5–6](https://webusers.imj-prg.fr/~michael.harris/periodrelations.pdf#page=5)

An expansion principle addresses another part of the question. Lan’s Proposition 7.1.2.14 says that Fourier–Jacobi expansions jointly detect the relevant algebraic modular forms when the strata meet every irreducible component of every fibre as required. On an eligible finite-dimensional complex space, finitely many scalar tests then suffice for separation. To turn these into the project's tests one must first realize the target as the appropriate space of algebraic sections and show that the expansions of the specified vectors have their coefficients in $k$. Separation and coefficient rationality have different hypotheses. [Lan, Proposition 7.1.2.14, p. 539](https://www.kwlan.org/articles/cpt-PEL-type-thesis-revision.pdf#page=567)

## Do the two constructions give the same periods?

For each label $u$, write the arithmetic and analytic complex period pairings as matrices $B_u,S_u:M_u\to E^\vee$ in orthonormal coordinates. The remaining part of the Bessel identity asks for one unitary $V$ on the common target and, for every $u$, a unitary $U_u$ on the source and a scalar $a_u\ne0$ such that

$$B_u=a_u V S_u U_u\qquad\text{for all }u.$$

The transpose associated with a bilinear pairing has been absorbed in $V$ since the transpose of a unitary is unitary. Edition 0.88 identifies the arithmetic pairing on rational vectors. This further assertion controls the relative positions of the period maps used together in the Gram calculation. [Original Bessel identity](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/GlobalTheoremInputs.lean#L65-L68) · [Rational identification](continuation_v88/rows.tex)

**What can be cited.** Match the zero rows, divide each remaining matrix by its Hilbert–Schmidt norm, and absorb its scalar phase into the source unitary. These normalized maps form a finite system with one common target. Theorem 1(a) of Futorny–Horn–Sergeichuk says that two such systems are unitarily equivalent exactly when the traces along all closed walks after adjoining the adjoint arrows agree. Here those traces are the mixed traces of the normalized target Gram matrices $B_uB_u^*/\operatorname{tr}(B_uB_u^*)$ and their $S$ counterparts. This replaces the classification argument in B87H3. If its exact finite bound is needed, the paired Gram matrices generate an algebra in $M_d(\mathbb C)\oplus M_d(\mathbb C)$, $d=\dim E>0$. The spans of words of length at most $j$ start in dimension one and cease growing permanently at the first equality of consecutive spans. Consequently length $2d^2-1$ suffices. This last count is a separate elementary argument. [Futorny–Horn–Sergeichuk, Theorem 1(a)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.laa.2017.01.006) · [Project’s criterion](continuation_v87/periodbessel.tex)

**What still needs proof.** Two lists of unit columns in $\mathbb C^2$, $(e_1,e_1)$ and $(e_1,e_2)$, have the same singular value at each label. For the two different labels the mixed Gram trace is $1$ in the first list and $0$ in the second. This gives the obstruction even for rational entries. Beuzart-Plessis–Chaudouard, Theorem 1.3.6.1, gives a squared automorphic Bessel period for a factorizable vector under temperedness, base-change and measure hypotheses. Using a norm formula here would require a common realization in which to polarize and compare the arithmetic maps. The theorem itself supplies no such identification. [Bessel period formula, §1.3.6](https://doi.org/10.1017/fmp.2025.8)

There is a more direct mathematical target than enumerating the mixed traces. In a seesaw of dual pairs, restricting the two theta kernels gives one common kernel. Pairing the two ways amounts to exchanging the integrations, with the usual convergence hypotheses. The source uses precisely a seesaw input on its route to the mixed-period identity. To apply it, identify the arithmetic evaluation and the analytic period as the two pairings of that kernel, with their prescribed tests, splittings and measures. In the source’s sufficient version the target isometry works for all labels and tuples, the source isometry for each label works for all its tuples, and the tuple phases are roots of unity normalized to one at the base tuple. That is a concrete comparison of automorphic forms to try to prove. [Kudla, §1, (1.14)](https://wiki.epfl.ch/tan/documents/1984%20kudla-1.pdf) · [Source’s seesaw input](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/ThetaCorrespondence/ThetaPairLifts/ThirdPlaceSeries/MiStar2OfFamilies.lean#L15-L26) · [Exact mixed-period identity](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/IndefiniteReal/IsometricPeriodsOfHypotheses.lean#L80-L105)

### Two exact matches with classical theorems

Two of the five inputs in the source's construction of the compatible real model can be discharged in ordinary mathematical language. For rapid decay, restrict the smooth cusp form to each arithmetic component. The source gives uniform moderate growth for all right derivatives, and every proper constant term vanishes. Arthur truncation therefore fixes the form and gives rapid decay by Borel–Labesse–Schwermer. Comparing the adelic quotient height on Siegel representatives gives the exact exponent statement for every power of that height. For finite dimensionality, the project fixes a compact type and eigenvalues of the cyclic differential operators. At each place their leading symbols are the first $r$ matrix power sums, which generate the invariant polynomials for $\mathfrak{gl}_r$. The central operators thus fix one finite-codimension ideal, exactly the hypothesis of Harish-Chandra's finiteness theorem. [Proofs of both matches](continuation_v89/decay.tex) · [Borel–Labesse–Schwermer, §4.4](https://www.numdam.org/item/CM_1996__102_1_1_0.pdf#page=13) · [Harish-Chandra's theorem in Borel, §8.4](https://homepages.math.uic.edu/~rtakloo/papers/borel/borel3.pdf#page=23)

### Why corrected heights hardly vary

The level contribution to the height allowance of a word is exactly $-A\log T_u$. Add it back, compare two words, and the remaining variation comes entirely from the interior of their first weight blocks. These exponents move in an interval of width $S$. Applying the mean value theorem to $y\log y-y-\lambda y$ on each moving coordinate bounds the diameter by $4B_0/(nX')$ on every sufficiently late member. This proves entry 9. The absolute bound for each individual map is entry 6 and remains open.
