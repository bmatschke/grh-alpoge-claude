# The toric denominator from the normalized elliptic jet

For the toric character on a built column, the individual order-class coefficients and the sums of their finite character twists admit one denominator bound at $\ell$ for every admissible set $Q$ disjoint from the places above $\ell$. There is a route in the pinned source that works directly with the order lattice and the period pair in these coefficients. Its two named partial-zeta inputs are rationality of the ordinary ideal-class values of the totally real field and Hecke's functional equation for those partial zeta functions. The analytic work between the functional equation and the local bound is an explicit Hilbert Eisenstein jet calculation in the source. Below we identify the exact intermediate estimate and derive the arithmetic bound from it. [Target and local exponent][target] · [Construction on a host][host]

## The field, order and partial zeta values

Write $F=E^+$, $n=[F:\mathbb Q]$, $d$ for the host prime, and $K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-d})$. The host gives an abelian CM extension $E/\mathbb Q$, $[E:\mathbb Q]=4\,\mathrm{ord}$, $2\mid\operatorname{disc}E$, and $\operatorname{disc}E=(\operatorname{disc}F)^2$. The relative different of $E/F$ is consequently the unit ideal. The CM type has $n\ge2$ members and induces a single embedding of $K$, with a choice of sign for the generator $\sqrt{-d}$. The product of its embeddings on roots of unity is one by the host's CM-type property. Here $d>4$ and $d\equiv3\bmod4$, and $\Lambda$ has basis $1,(1+i\sqrt d)/2$. Thus $\Lambda$ is the embedded integer lattice of $K$. The normalized extension on the built origin supplies the toric conductor, the unitary character, its infinity type $\kappa_i+s$ in ratio notation, the positive integer $s$, and the comparison with the coefficients of that very column. The fixed conductor $\mathfrak f$ is nonzero and proper. Its prime divisors split in $E/F$, have exponent at most two, and lie over odd rational primes that are unramified and split completely in $E$. The additional real primes in $Q$ split in $E/F$, are prime to $\mathfrak f$ and avoid $\ell$. The calculation uses the lattice of the order $\mathcal O_F+\mathfrak f(\prod_{q\in Q}q)\mathcal O_E$. [Host field and CM type][host] · [Integer period lattice][lat] · [Conductor and character conventions][cond] · [Toric labels on the built origin](FIRST_PASS_EXPORT_LOCAL_CONDITIONS.md)

Choose $z_C=\zeta_F(C,1-2s)$ for each ordinary ideal class $C$. The toric deduction uses only existence of these rational numbers. Its nominal hypothesis on their denominators is projected to this single clause in line 42. The number $e_\ell$ used below is the maximum actual denominator exponent of $z_C/2^n$. It exists since the class group is finite. Siegel's formula at the positive even argument and the functional equation give the desired rationality. For the class convention, take a fractional ideal $\mathfrak a$ of class $C^{-1}$. The integral ideals in $C$ are $(x)\mathfrak a^{-1}$ for $0\ne x\in\mathfrak a$ modulo units. Poisson summation exchanges $\mathfrak a$ with $\mathfrak d_F^{-1}\mathfrak a^{-1}$. This exchanges $C$ with $[\mathfrak d_F]C^{-1}$. The completion is $|\operatorname{disc}F|^{u/2}\Gamma_{\mathbb R}(u)^n$, with $\Gamma_{\mathbb R}(u)=\pi^{-u/2}\Gamma(u/2)$. Both agree with the pinned functional equation, including at $u=2s$. [Hecke, 1917, pp. 77–89](https://geodesic.mathdoc.fr/item/NGWGMP_1917__1917_58974/) · [Formula in the ordinary-ideal convention, Estala Arias, p. 9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1112.1146#page=9) · [Siegel and the gamma factors](FIRST_PASS_TORIC_COEFFICIENTS.md) · [Pinned equation][fe] · [Projection used in the deduction][ded]

## The integral jet on the order lattice

Put $a=a_0(\mathfrak f,\ell)$, the maximum conductor exponent above $\ell$. Put $c=c_{\rm vp}(\mathfrak f,\ell)$. It is $a$ when a place above $\ell$ divides $\mathfrak f$ and $v_\ell(\operatorname{disc}E)/[E:\mathbb Q]$ otherwise. Put $j=\sum_i\kappa_i$, $k=2s$, $w=kn+2j$, and $\epsilon_d=\tfrac12{\bf1}_{\ell=d}$. Fix a representative of the order class whose local components at $\ell$ are one. For a chosen $\ell$-adic embedding, write a bound $B$ on an algebraic number for the inequality $v_\ell(x)\ge-B$, normalized by $v_\ell(\ell)=1$ and extended to zero. The nonvanishing lattice discriminant gives $\Omega^{12}=\Delta_\Lambda$ with $\Omega\ne0$. The same $\Omega$ occurs in the definition of $\beta$. [Valuation conventions and geometry][target] · [Nonvanishing of the actual Weierstrass discriminant][dis]

The source's jet theorem applies to the defining analytic order-lattice sum $g$, and to its iterated derivative $D^\kappa g(0)$ in the prescribed coordinates. For every $\ell$ and every embedding it produces nonzero $a_i,b_i$ such that the following three quantities have the following bounds.

| Quantity | Bound |
| --- | --- |
| $a_i/\Omega$ | $a$ |
| $(a_i b_i\sqrt d/(2\pi))^{-1}$ | $c+\epsilon_d$ |
| $U=D^\kappa g(0)\prod_i (b_i/a_i)^{\kappa_i}/a_i^k$ | $e_\ell+knc+jc$ |

The last row is the bound for the ordinary derivative. The same estimate applies for all $\kappa$ once the local coordinates have been chosen. One can take $a_i=\Omega/\ell^a$ and $b_i=(2\pi/\sqrt d)\gamma/a_i$, where $\gamma$ is an algebraic scalar of valuation $c+\epsilon_d$. Such a scalar is obtained by writing $c+\epsilon_d=p/q$ and choosing a $q$-th root of $\ell^p$. This realizes the first two rows. [Integral jet statement][jet] · [Choice and bounds for the two coordinates][coords]

The [diagonal derivative calculation](FIRST_PASS_CM_DIAGONAL.md) proves the last row for this order lattice. The order lattice is expressed in the basis $1,\theta$ for $E/F$. One introduces nonzero torsion of order dividing $\ell+1$ in the auxiliary elliptic level. On the diagonal the conjugate derivative is a finite sum over $\mu\le\kappa$. A term is an integer coefficient times a holomorphic elliptic modular evaluation of weight $kn+2|\mu|$ times the $(j-|\mu|)$-th power of the normalized weight-two correction. The integral $j$-invariant and the valuation of the Weierstrass correction at that prime-to-$\ell$ point control these evaluations. The two weights add to $w$. The source then puts back the change of lattice and the chosen coordinates and bounds the discriminant contributions. The pinned proof of the jet theorem retains only the ordinary partial-zeta functional equation from the four named arguments of its public statement. The [weight-two continuation](FIRST_PASS_CM_WEIGHT_TWO.md) shows exactly where the ordinary partial-zeta functional equation enters the Fourier expansion. The [Fourier bound at every cusp](FIRST_PASS_CM_FOURIER_BOUND.md), [evaluation at the integral CM invariant](FIRST_PASS_CM_MODULAR_VALUES.md) and [torsion correction](FIRST_PASS_CM_TORSION_CORRECTION.md) give the three estimates needed on this diagonal. ## Passing from the derivative to each class coefficient

To identify the derivative with the class-sum value, put $t_i=\phi_i(x)$ for a nonzero lattice vector. The unregularized factor $(t_i+z_i\bar t_i)^{-k}$ has $m$th derivative at zero equal to $(-1)^m k(k+1)\cdots(k+m-1)t_i^{-k}(\bar t_i/t_i)^m$. Multiply these identities over the CM type and sum over the original unit orbits. For $k>2$ the undifferentiated positive majorant still works on compact subdiscs, since $|t_i+z_i\bar t_i|\ge(1-|z_i|)|t_i|$. At $k=2$ do this first for the Wirtinger derivatives of the regularized summand on $\Re\sigma>0$. The rising factors there start at $2+\sigma$, and the product of angular ratios has modulus one at the origin. The [continued Fourier family](FIRST_PASS_CM_WEIGHT_TWO.md) permits every fixed spatial derivative to continue in $\sigma$. Uniqueness of holomorphic continuation identifies it with the corresponding scalar lattice sum at zero. At that parameter the germ in the deformation variables is holomorphic, so its Wirtinger derivatives are the ordinary complex derivatives used above. [Weight-two derivative identity][wder] · [Both weight ranges and the class laws][dall]

Let $S$ be the analytic continuation of the lattice sum defined using the original lattice and $\mathcal O_F^\times$-orbits. The defining-coordinate derivative formula and the value of $C_0$ give
$$
 X=\frac{C_0 S(0)}{\Omega^w}
 =(-1)^j U\prod_i
 \left(\frac{a_i b_i\sqrt d}{2\pi}\right)^{-\kappa_i}
 \left(\frac{a_i}{\Omega}\right)^{k+2\kappa_i}.
$$
To check the factor at one $i$, multiply $(b_i/a_i)^{\kappa_i}/a_i^k$ by the two powers on the right. All $a_i,b_i$ cancel and the result is $(2\pi/\sqrt d)^{\kappa_i}/\Omega^{k+2\kappa_i}$. The sign is the product of the signs of the rising-factor derivatives. This identity in the proof of the unramified case holds for every $\ell$. Its derivation uses $a_i b_i\Omega\ne0$, the derivative formula, and the period constant. The condition $c=0$ is used in that file to estimate the factors. [Derivative and period identity][ident] · [Production of the derivative formula from weight-two continuation][deriv]

For every $\ell$ the three local estimates therefore give directly
$$
 -v_\ell(X)\ \le\ e_\ell+knc+j(2c+\epsilon_d)+wa .
$$
Here and in this inequality $v_\ell(0)=+\infty$. Taking the twelfth power bounds $\beta$ by twelve times the displayed exponent. When $c=0$ the host forces $\ell\ne d$, so this gives the required $12(e_\ell+wa)$. When $c\ne0$ it improves the pinned exponent by $12j(1+{\bf1}_{\ell=2})$. Indeed the prescribed exponent is $12(e_\ell+knc+j(2c+1+{\bf1}_{\ell=2}+\epsilon_d)+wa)$. The pinned proof first passes to a divided derivative in this branch and estimates the inverse factorial. Retaining the undivided identity avoids that loss and suffices for the requested inequality. These comparisons apply at every embedding and hence give the stated rational-prime integrality predicate. [Unramified implication $\ell\ne d$][unr] · [Prescribed ramified bound][ram] · [Divided derivative construction][fac]

By weak approximation choose a rational multiplier that brings each local component above $\ell$ into the unit group of the order there. This group is open even at primes of the conductor. Multiplication by these order units then gives a representative literally equal to one above $\ell$. Class invariance moves a normalized class coefficient to this representative with an $\ell$-integral unit factor. A finite character of the order class group preserves the angular type, since $\kappa_i+s>0$. The Hecke continuation used for the lattice sums therefore has a nonzero infinity exponent also at $s=1$. Finally the power bound passes to finite sums by the ultrametric inequality on the *twelfth roots* $X$ using the common $\Omega$; raising the resulting sum to the twelfth power restores $\beta$. There is no class-number division in this argument. The bound is independent of the auxiliary set $Q$. The passage to the installed coefficient also involves its fixed comparison scalar. The valuation theorem supplies the individual class terms and their finite character sums. ## The denominator in the installed toric coefficient

For the fixed host, character and weight put $P=\Omega^w/C_0$. This is nonzero. The preceding estimate for each $T/P$ holds at every embedding above $\ell$, with the same right-hand side. Round the bound up to a nonnegative integer $n$. Thus $\ell^n T/P$ is an algebraic number integral at all such places. Equivalently one can clear its remaining denominators by an integer prime to $\ell$. The exponent $n$ precedes $Q$ and its order classes.

At a nonzero twist index $i$, the built comparison for a toric label identifies $\theta_{i,Q,\lambda,\delta}$ in the algebraic closure of the fraction field of $O_i$ with $u$ times the embedded sum of the individual $T$ over the fibre $\pi(q^{-1})=\delta$. Here $u$ and the comparison embedding $\iota$ are fixed before $Q$. Choose $m\ge0$ such that $\ell_i^m u\,\iota(P)$ is integral over $O_i$. This is possible for a single element of the algebraic closure of a discretely valued fraction field by clearing the coefficients of its monic polynomial. Every embedded $\ell_i^n T/P$ is also integral over $O_i$, since its prime-to-$\ell_i$ denominator is a unit there. Consequently $\ell_i^{m+n}\theta_{i,Q,\lambda,\delta}$ is integral over $O_i$. It lies in the original fraction field, so it lies in $O_i$ by integral closedness. For each label admitting an allowed critical integer fix one such integer for its comparison. The element of the installed record is independent of that choice. Take the maximum of $m+n$ over the finitely many such labels, taking zero for an empty list. This gives the export exponent $N_0(i)$ simultaneously for every admitted $Q$ and $\delta$.
