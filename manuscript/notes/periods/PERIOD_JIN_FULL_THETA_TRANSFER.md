# Transferring the signed reference bound through the measured theta operator

The original-family application below is completed by the [source-volume and core estimates](PERIOD_VOLUME_BEFORE_SELECTION.md), [vertical pole and Petersson comparison](PERIOD_VERTICAL_TRAIT_DETECTION.md), and [signed finite estimate](PERIOD_FINITE_ERRORS_ON_THE_DESIGN.md). Their combination gives [the seventh height inequality](../geometry/COMMON_FAMILY_AND_PERIOD_HEIGHT.md#the-absolute-height-on-this-class). The alternative comparison between independently chosen families remains optional.

Entry 7 uses the sum of a target covolume, two theta-image indices at conjugate places, and a pairing index. Kaiwen Jin bounds these terms on a member produced by his structural selection. Here is a way to transfer their *sum* to an original linked member. Its finite-place input is a determinant of the full measured theta operator on the prescribed base level. It can be compared before choosing retained bases, integral models, residual projections or the two auxiliary runs. The comparison still concerns a particular member and its metrics. [Signed base identity, equation (5)](PERIOD_SIGNED_LEVEL_TRANSFER.md) · [Jin's intermediate bounds and their hypotheses](PERIOD_JIN_PAIRED_REFERENCE.md)

## The determinant on the original member

Fix a linked member, a stage $j$ of reference rank $D>0$, and a tuple $t$. The term *base level* specifies the level for every tuple, including tuples different from the reference $t_0$. In rational bases let $H$ be the nonsingular Hermitian Gram of the whole base-level definite space $C_{jt}$, $Z$ the retained basis insertion and $\theta$ the square invertible matrix from its coordinates to the target $V_j$. Keep the actual level normalization of $H$, including its class stabilizer factors. Let $*$ be the algebraic conjugate transpose, $H_r=Z^*HZ$, and $P=H_r^{-1}Z^*H$. Let $A_{jt}=\theta P:C_{jt}\to V_j$. On the coefficient field $K$, define
$$
 a_{jt}=\det(A_{jt}H^{-1}A_{jt}^*)=\det(\theta H_r^{-1}\theta^*).
 \tag{1}
$$
Indeed $PH^{-1}P^*=H_r^{-1}$. Thus $a_{jt}$ is nonzero and fixed by the involution. At the distinguished embedding it is the positive Gram determinant of the pullbacks by $A_{jt}$ of the target coframe, in the dual of the full source. This includes the case where the full source has a kernel of positive dimension. [Original level form, insertion and normalized projector][level] · [Earlier retained-space formula](PERIOD_FRAME_TRACE_DETERMINANTS.md)

The full operator in (1) has a measured interpretation on the original bundle. Its single linked witness gives the prescribed oscillator and quotient-measure reading with phase one at all $j,t$, and tests rational on all tuple inputs that also read the stored target frame. Primitive base links give the rational retained matrix and its common target at that level for every $t$. On retained inputs the native identity is precisely the equality of the target reading of $\theta$ and the projected Hilbert theta of the inserted form. To see why extension by $P$ gives the measured operator on the whole level, take the orthogonal projection to the adjoint image of the Hilbert theta. This projection commutes with the source action since the kernel is invariant and the action unitary. The level idempotent fixes the given input, and the central type idempotent fixes its theta image. Applying both idempotents and the adjoint-image projection thus preserves the theta reading and gives a vector in the retained space. The latter is the complex span of its rational cut: the kernel is rational by the separating tests, its orthogonal complement is rational by the class form, and the idempotents are rational. A vector orthogonal to the rational cut consequently has theta zero (pair its adjoint-image representative with that vector as in BTH9). This argument uses the tests and type action for the chosen $t$ and the positive Hilbert form at the distinguished embedding. It gives the all-tuple use of (1) on the selected bundle without identifying it with a second selection. ## The joint covolume and index calculation

Write $\ell_w=\log Nw/[K:\mathbb Q]$, and let $t(w)=t(\bar w)$ be the serving tuple, using $t_0$ off the twist support. In the chosen rational target basis let $b_{jw}$ be the valuation of the determinant ideal of the target lattice $M_j$. If $i_{jw}$ is the sum of its two signed theta-image indices and $q_{jw}$ the pairing index at $w$, then
$$
 i_{jw}+q_{jw}=v_w(a_{j,t(w)})-b_{jw}-b_{j,\bar w}.                 \tag{2}
$$
For example, if the determinant valuations of the source lattice in retained coordinates are $u_w,u_{\bar w}$, its dual at $w$ has determinant valuation $-v_w(\det H_r)-u_{\bar w}$. Subtracting $u_w$ gives $q_{jw}$. In the image indices the two $u$'s have positive sign and cancel. This works over the semilocal ring at $w,\bar w$, including a self-conjugate place.

Let $G_{j,\sigma}$ be the prescribed negative-member positive metric in the same target basis, and let angle brackets denote the normalized average over embeddings. The squared covolume of $M_j$ is $\langle\log\det G_{j,\sigma}\rangle+2\sum_w\ell_w b_{jw}$. The original off-support condition gives $i_{jw}+q_{jw}=0$ there at $t_0$. Adding (2) over *all* places cancels both lattice terms against the squared covolume, yielding
$$
 Z_0^{(0)}=\frac1D\sum_j\left(
 \left\langle\log\det G_{j,\sigma}\right\rangle+
 \sum_w\ell_w\,v_w(a_{j,t(w)})\right).                             \tag{3}
$$
Each sum has finite support. Transport to a common coefficient extension with compatible involution and pulled-back selectors preserves (3), by local ramification and the normalized degree weights. Away from the distinguished embedding the positive metric uses ordinary complex adjoints at that embedding. Formula (1) uses the field involution. ## Comparison with Jin's selected realization

Suppose a comparison member $J$ and the original member have the same reference rank, with rational stage targets identified in common bases after compatible coefficient extension. Each member uses its own serving tuple, also on primes where only the other member serves a nonreference tuple. Subtracting (3) gives the exact difference
$$
 Z_0^{(0)}-Z_{0,J}^{(0)}
 =\frac1D\left[
 \sum_j\left\langle\log\frac{\det G_{j,\sigma}}{\det G^J_{j,\sigma}}\right\rangle+
 \sum_w\ell_w\,v_w\!\left(\prod_j
       \frac{a_{j,t(w)}}{a^J_{j,t^J(w)}}\right)\right].             \tag{4}
$$
In particular, on a common marked realization the finite term is zero if, for each served pair of tuples, the two full measured operators agree under an isometry of their source class forms and the fixed target identification. Equality of Gram determinants at the distinguished embedding is already sufficient by injectivity of the field embedding. An arbitrary common unit-modulus scalar on the full operator for a given $j,t$ cancels in its complex Gram. Both algebraic determinants are then equal even if the scalar itself has not been shown algebraic. The reference target determinant metrics also have to be compared in (4).

There is also a version that does not require the two expositions to agree on a rational target frame, or even to identify their target spaces. In each member separately form
$$
 r_{jt}=a_{jt}/a_{j,t_0},\qquad
 h_j=\left\langle\log\det G_{j,\sigma}
                     +\log|\sigma(a_{j,t_0})|\right\rangle .
$$
Changing that member's target basis by $C$ multiplies every $a_{jt}$ by $1/(c\bar c)$, $c=\det C$. The metric determinant at $\sigma$ is multiplied by $|\sigma c|^2$. The ratios $r_{jt}$ are therefore unchanged, and the two changes of average logarithms in $h_j$ cancel because $\sigma\mapsto\sigma\circ\mathrm{bar}$ permutes the embeddings. The product formula in (3) gives
$$
 DZ_0^{(0)}=\sum_j h_j+
    \sum_{w\in{\rm Tw}}\ell_w\,v_w\!\left(\prod_j r_{j,t(w)}\right).
 \tag{5}
$$
In comparing two members, their scalar sums in (5) make sense even over different coefficient fields. With compatible embeddings, equality of the *ratios* of the two full Gram determinants, each read at its own served and reference tuple, implies field equality in a common extension. Hence a single arbitrary change of target frame per stage, constant across tuples, costs nothing in this test. Only the reference contribution $\sum_j h_j$ and the valuations of the product of those normalized determinants on the union of supports then need comparison. This also allows identical finite terms with different normalized determinants when the differences are units at the serving places.

Jin identifies the full measured operator with $\theta^{\rm low}P_{\rm low}$ on each used tuple, by reading the normalized integral on the specified marked level. He also uses the actual level form in his signed indices and the negative measured metric on the target lattice. Consequently on the same marked input, with fixed target readings, the two presentations give the same finite determinant by (1). For transferring his estimate use the **intermediate** bounds on the image indices, pairing indices and negative covolume. They upper-bound their sum. The final upper bound on the quotient slope alone would not imply an upper bound on this sum. [Jin's full measured identity][jin-theta] · [Class form and target metric][jin-form] · [Pairing, two-run estimate and negative-member bound][jin-bound]

For the original family the sufficient remaining comparison is to exhibit such a $J$ where needed, or to bound (4) from above together with the difference $T_J-T$ of the prescribed signed main terms, uniformly by $O(mL(B_0+N_c))$. This follows by adding $Z_{0,J}^{(0)}-T_J$ to the two differences. Equality of the normalized main terms, finite determinants and averaged metric determinants is a sufficient special case. Jin's structural proposition takes comparisons on specified hosts and levels as hypotheses. Separate existence of linked selections does not identify their marked inputs, their target frames across tuples, or their metrics at the other embeddings. That comparison across the two selections is still open here. The direct route below avoids it. [Quantifiers of the structural construction][selection]

## The small-section estimate on the fitted component

The component-volume comparison in entry 7 can be carried out on the **normalization of the fitted projective image**, with the original Petersson Gram at infinity. The following verifies the section injection, its finite correction, and the precise arithmetic Hilbert–Samuel upper bound once the carrying section and the depth and frame estimates on that component are available. [Jin, Appendix J, normalized image][j-image] · [Roadmap, inequality (8.28f)][road-image]

Fix a component $Z$ and write $F$ for its field, $d$ for its generic dimension, $X$ for its integral projective image, $A=\mathcal O_X(1)$ and $\nu:X^\nu\to X$ for normalization. Use the projective generators given by the original integral series. At a tested valuation $v$ extending the coefficient-place valuation $v_w$, let $d_Z(v)$ be their minimal order in the measured frame. Use Jin's nonzero section $\Theta_Z$ of weight $8(u-u')$ and his constants $a_p,e_w$ and depth $\phi_C/e_0$. The two estimates needed *before* introducing the mixed-frame error are
$$
 v(\Theta_Z)/8\ge \phi_C/(2500e_0)-e_w,\qquad
 d_Z(v)\le \phi_C/(2500e_0)+v_w(p)a_p .
$$
The depth inequality can itself be transferred from the [prime-by-prime reference depth](PERIOD_REFERENCE_DEPTH_BUDGET.md) on a fitted cover. Use the original lattice of cusp sections characterized by integrality at its prescribed evaluation frames. The [component idempotent on the marked cover](PERIOD_SELECTED_MARKED_ORIGIN.md#identifying-components-flags-and-valuations) evaluates to $1$ on $Z$ and $0$ on the others. Multiplication by it preserves weight, cuspidality and all the defining integrality tests, so it preserves this lattice. For a frame on $Z$, the orders attainable from the component summand therefore have exactly the same infimum as those attainable from the whole integral lattice. In particular the infimum is read on any finite generating set, by the nonarchimedean triangle inequality. If the test trait is represented on the fitted cover with $v_V|_F=e_0v_w$, the reference-depth estimate there reads
$$
 \inf_s v_V(s)\le \phi_C/2500+v_V(p)a_p .
$$
Divide by $e_0$ to obtain the displayed inequality for $d_Z(v)$ with the original valuation $v=v_V/e_0$. The argument applies to the lattice defined by those frame evaluations and the given identification of test traits. It leaves the chart and jet estimates for that cover and the lifting of all required traits to it as precise hypotheses. [Jin's component and ramification convention][j-depth]

The parallel factor has a fixed order at a coefficient place on the component. Put $\delta_w=v_w(p)\mathbf1_{p\in S_B} e_p\|u'\|_1$. At $m=8k$, a mixed-integral section $f$ has $v(f)\ge-m\delta_w$. Adding the two estimates above first gives
$$
 v(\Theta_Z^k f)+m(e_w+v_w(p)a_p+\delta_w)\ \ge\ m d_Z(v).
$$
The loss $\delta_w$ occurs once. One must keep it available to offset $v(f)$ when passing from the multiplier estimate to the product. Choose a scalar $b_m$ in the fractional ideal whose $w$-exponent is $\lceil m(e_w+v_w(p)a_p+\delta_w)\rceil$. Dividing $b_m\Theta_Z^kf$ by the $m$th power of a generator attaining $d_Z(v)$ gives nonnegative order. Under the fitted trait comparison these tests detect the vertical orders on $X^\nu$. Horizontal regularity follows from the complex chart bound for $\Theta_Z^k f$ and boundedness of the fixed cusp section. The latter follows because positive boundary vanishing dominates the polynomial in the logarithms of local boundary equations. A normal noetherian domain is the intersection of its height-one valuation rings. The resulting sections consequently belong to $H^0(X^\nu,\nu^*A^m)$, and the map is injective at the generic point. [Multiplier, order and archimedean estimate][j-mult] · [Original cusp-depth and mixed-frame estimates][j-depth] · [Horizontal chart argument][j-horizontal]

Let $G_y$ be the evaluation Gram of the given weight-$u$ basis. The bound $\|\Theta_Z(y)\|^2\le e^{16t_\sigma}G_y^8$ produces $G_y^m$ in degree $m=8k$. This is exactly the denominator for the squared Fubini–Study norm on $A^m$. With $e'_w=e_w+v_w(p)a_p+\delta_w$ and $\ell_w=\log Nw/[F:\mathbb Q]$, Minkowski's theorem allows the balances
$$
 |\sigma b_m|\le C_Z\exp\{m(\textstyle\sum_w\ell_we'_w+\bar t-t_\sigma)\},
 \qquad \bar t=[F:\mathbb Q]^{-1}\sum_\sigma t_\sigma .
$$
Indeed the product of the archimedean radii counts each complex pair twice. Its exponential factor is the norm of the finite ideal up to the bounded rounding at its fixed support. The discriminant and rounding enter the constant $C_Z$. Thus for any $\eta>0$ the source small sections inject into the image sections with an increase of logarithmic norm per degree at most $\sum_w\ell_we'_w+\bar t+\eta$, once $m$ is large on this component. No uniform bound on $C_Z$ is needed in this fixed-component degree limit. [Jin's scalar and cost][j-scalar]

There is also a direct proof that normalization preserves the *specific subsequence* used here. Give $A_t$ the original Fubini–Study norm times $e^{-t}$, for a fixed real $t$ above the generator bound $c_s$. The norm is induced from a smooth metric on the ambient projective space, even for a singular image. The integral generating list gives relative nefness, nonnegative degrees on horizontal arithmetic curves, and the pulled-back Fubini–Study curvature is semipositive. Apply Moriwaki's birational invariance theorem to $\nu$ with the Hermitian bundle $8A_t$ and zero fixed twist. The two limsups of the logarithmic small-section counts, divided by $n^{d+1}$, are equal. Substituting $m=8n$ gives the same equality with denominator $m^{d+1}$ along precisely the required multiples of eight. Moriwaki's arithmetic Hilbert–Samuel formula for nef smooth Hermitian bundles now identifies this limit on $X$ with its arithmetic self-intersection divided by $(d+1)!$. With Jin's count normalization $d!/[F:\mathbb Q]$, the denominator of the self-intersection is $(d+1)[F:\mathbb Q]$. The component Chow comparison gives the upper bound $\deg(X)(h_Z+t)$. This argument proves the required upper bound on $X^\nu$ and avoids any same-degree descent claim on $X$. Establishing the displayed depth and multiplier estimates, the vertical trait comparison and the source lower volume on the actual selected level remains necessary when verifying the full geometric covolume theorem.
