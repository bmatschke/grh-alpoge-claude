# Character choices and integral theta comparison on the first pass

Let $L$ be the full coefficient field, $L_0$ its distinguished subfield and $W$ the set of places retained by the first saturated state. Fix the moduli-point and theta-series data at the preceding level. The next record chooses a coefficient ring and an embedding at each place of $L$, identifies the two descriptions of each active definite level and its character, and compares the old and new theta maps over those rings. The last clause is tested on every indicated source vector and every expression for its old theta image as a finite linear combination of rational forms. [Record][Crec] · [Scope of the stage assertion][Cst]

## The character and its two readings

On this pass $Q(w)=\varnothing$, $k(w)=0$ and the auxiliary Hecke character is $1$ at every $w$. The active places are exactly $w\in W$. At such a place the selected tuple is the distinguished twist of the reference tuple by the character $\delta_{\ell(w)}$, with the appropriate choice between the two distinguished characters. The equality $\xi(\mu_{\rm Tw}(w))=\delta_{\ell(w)}$ follows from the original column identification $\xi(\mu_{\rm Tw}(w))=\delta_{\ell(w)}\psi_w$. Its ramified distinguished character still enters the definite level. [Untwisted data][Cuntw] · [Active places][Cactive] · [Reference column](FIRST_PASS_COLUMN.md)

Choose one representative per conjugation orbit of coefficient places. The covering property gives an index $i$ with residue characteristic $\ell(w)$ whose positive embedding reads the restriction of that representative to $L_0$. Give its conjugate the same index and the opposite side if the two places are distinct. This reads the conjugate place since $v_{\bar w}(x)=v_w(\bar x)$ and conjugating the argument interchanges the two embeddings. To construct $e_w:L\hookrightarrow K_w$, extend the resulting embedding of the completion of $L_0$ to $L_w$ inside an algebraic closure of $\operatorname{Frac} O_{i,0}$, and form the compositum with $\operatorname{Frac} O_{i,0}$. Here $L_w$ in the construction denotes the completion of $L$, and $K_w$ the compositum. Its ring of integers $O_e(w)$ is a finite local DVR extension of $O_{i,0}$, since the latter is complete and the extension is finite separable. It has $e_w(x)\in O_e(w)\iff v_w(x)\ge0$, and the restriction of $e_w$ to $L_0$ is the prescribed side. [Conjugate orbits][Corbit] · [Local extension and restriction equations][Cext] · [Integral closure of a complete DVR](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/09E8)

Use the trivial character of the selected ring class quotient for every $w$. Its pullback has open kernel, is anticyclotomic, has image in $\mu_{\ell(w)^0}=\{1\}$, is unramified everywhere and is trivial on every decomposition group over $\ell(w)$. The empty support satisfies the splitting and avoidance requirements. The Frobenius reading and its inverse are both $1$. The comparison of two character packages with the same index holds with exponent $1$, prime to $\ell(w)$. These choices supply the character, admissibility, and reciprocity conditions, including at active places. [Trivial-character admissibility][Ctwok] · [Choice conditions, including the separate level test][Cside] · [Assembling the arithmetic choices][Cpack]

A simplification concerns the *entire* auxiliary twist used by the definite oscillator model. For either embedding $f$ into $\mathbf C$, its three entries are the chosen half of $Q$, the complex reciprocity pullback of the character, and the residue-character assignment constructed from that pullback. They are therefore $(\varnothing,1,\operatorname{resOf}(1))$ for every $f$. The old tuple already realizing this twist serves twice in the assertion asking for occurrences of the two twists in one realization. Take the original row, phase, frame and evaluation maps themselves. The compatibility maps are identities, and both occurrences are the given occurrence in that row. This removes the need to construct a second tuple for the conjugate embedding at this step. [Empty half and trivial pullback][Cequal] · [Original occurrence][Cocc] · [Identity realization with the two occurrences][Cself] · [Original geometric pins for this application][Cpins]

## The old and new definite levels at every active place

Fix an active coefficient place $w$, a side reading, and a row of rank $n=n_V$ with $2\le r<n$. Write $\eta=\mu_{0,j}\,\xi(\mu_{\rm Tw}(w))$ for the full row character. On the first pass its second factor is the distinguished character. Let $J,\chi$ be the old definite level and character and $J',\chi'$ the coefficient-valued level and character. For any complex embedding of the coefficient ring reading the required side of $L_0$, **the subgroups coincide and $\chi$ is the complex reading of $\chi'$**. In particular this applies to the restriction of an embedding of $K_w$ extending the old embedding of $L$. [What the level equality asks for][Cside] · [Local group and character equations][Cshapes]

Both levels are products. Away from the marked type places and the conductor of $\eta$ their local groups are the standard maximal compact group with trivial character. At a marked type place above $p=g_k$, the group reduces to the block upper parabolic whose leading $r$-by-$r$ block is upper unitriangular. The local character is
$$
 \psi_0\!\left(\sum_{a=1}^{r-1}h_{a,a+1}\right)\xi_v(\det h_{22}).
$$
Here $\xi_v$ is the local component of $\eta$ at the marked split half. It is unique by continuity and density of the global field. The new level uses this same component for every side reading because the prepared tame character is over $L_0$ and the distinguished twist is unramified here. At a conductor place away from the type places the group is the inverse image of a block parabolic and the character is $\xi_v(\det h_{22})$ in its split coordinates. For the new level this follows by reading the distinguished character on local units by Artin reciprocity, with the selected half specified by $P_q$. There are no variable conductor primes. At this point the half implicit in the old conductor group and the additive factor at a marked prime must still be identified. [Uniform character at a type place][Cuniform] · [Conductor-place formula][Cshapepara] · [Prepared tame characters](FIRST_PASS_SERIES_CHARACTERS.md)

The old evaluation and the new reading both use the principal frame for this definite row. Pull their two explicit oscillator levels back under this one frame and intersect them in a subgroup $J_1$. At each conductor place $J_1$ contains the local parahoric at the half selected by $P_q$. At a type place it contains the indicated unipotent-block level. Both assertions follow by taking a matrix supported at this single place and reducing it in the specified frame. All disjoint marked coordinates then reduce to $1$. On $J_1$ every old form transforms by the old oscillator character and every new form, read in the series embedding, by the new oscillator character. At the type place those scalars have equal $(p-1)$st powers. Indeed the factors from residue-unit determinants have order dividing $p-1$, and the common finite unipotent factor is read from the same rational row datum. The identification on finite adelic arguments in this construction is the identity. [Common subgroup, frame and both transformation laws][Cprincipal] · [Equality after taking powers][Cpow]

There is a nonzero form of either weight and character. To see this, choose a marked prime $p$ and the integral frame with first two columns $(1,\ldots,1)^t$ and $(0,1,\ldots,n-1)^t$, and the remaining columns the corresponding standard basis vectors. Its determinant is $1$. An integral rational isometry of the standard positive definite lattice is monomial with roots of unity as entries: each nonzero entry is an algebraic integer bounded by one at every embedding, hence a root of unity, and its row has norm one. A stabilizer in this class fixes the first column modulo $p$, so every such root reduces to $1$ and is $1$, as $p>2$ is unramified in the host. Its permutation sends each second-column entry to itself plus the same residue. The differences of these integer entries have absolute values at most $n-1$ and $p\ge2n$, so their pairwise congruences are integer equalities. The only constant shift preserving $\{0,\ldots,n-1\}$ is zero. Thus the stabilizer is trivial. Extend any nonzero coefficient vector on this class using the transformation laws and give it value zero on the other classes. [Frame and stabilizer calculation][Cfree] · [Prime size, unramifiedness and application to forms][Cfreest]

At a type place apply the equal-power identity to $h=1+yE_{12}$ with $y$ integral. Its lower determinant is $1$, so the two nonzero test forms detect the old and new additive values. Both values are $p$th roots of unity because the characters kill the maximal ideal. Their $(p-1)$st powers agree, so the values agree. This determines the additive factor on every entry required by the level. The type formula above is independent of the permitted side reading of the coefficient field. [The transvection test][Ctrans] · [Side-independent formula][Cuniform]

At a conductor place fix either of the nonzero test forms, and write $P$ for its parahoric in the split coordinates defining its level. The integral matrices acting by scalars on this form are exactly $P$. For the reverse inclusion suppose such a matrix $h$ has a unit entry $h_{ij}$ below the block diagonal, with $i>r$, $j\le r$ in one-based indices. Choose a unit $a$ with $\xi_v(a)\ne1$, possible by ramification, and put $t=(a-1)/h_{ij}$. The integral invertible matrices
$$
 G=1+t\,h e_j e_i^t,\qquad H=h^{-1}Gh=1+t\,e_j e_i^t h
$$
are both in $P$, since only a trailing column or a leading row has been changed. Both full determinants are $a$. Their lower-block determinants are $a$ and $1$. They must act by the same scalar because $h$ acts by an invertible scalar and conjugates $H$ to $G$. This contradicts the character formula. The common parahoric in $J_1$ therefore lies in the parahoric of both forms. The other split half gives the opposite block parabolic under inverse transpose. The selected parahoric has a transvection $1+E_{1,r+1}$ whose reduction belongs to it and not to that opposite parabolic. Hence both forms select the same half. Their local characters now agree by uniqueness of the component of $\eta$ there. [Rank-one conjugation test][Cdetect] · [The two split halves and the transvection][Copp]

The local equalities give the equality of the two product levels and their characters, since every element is a product of components at the finitely many exceptional places and an element supported in their hyperspecial complement. The two weight actions also match after the side reading. The same ordered CM embeddings and the same dual infinitesimal character give the same dominant highest weight, and the new cell is the scalar extension of that coefficient representation. On the finite set of definite double cosets the two spaces are now given by the same finite systems of stabilizer equations after complexification. Field extension preserves their kernels, giving equality of the old and new ranks. This proves the level and auxiliary rank inputs to the character choice at each active place independently of the readability of its class sums. [Weight comparison][Cweights] · [Rank equality][Cranks] · [Installation of the level equality][Cpack]

## Descent of the row scalar

The selected complex embedding $\epsilon:K_w\hookrightarrow\mathbf C$ extends the prescribed series reading. Its restriction along $e_w$ can differ from the old complex embedding of $L$. They agree on $L_0$. Extend the resulting isomorphism of embedded number fields to $\tau\in\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbf C)$ fixing the original image of $L_0$, and put $\iota=\tau^{-1}\epsilon$. Thus $\iota e_w$ is the old coefficient embedding. The simplification above identifies the auxiliary twists for $\iota$ and $\epsilon$. The conjugation of the old polynomial coordinates, the target evaluations and their common phase still uses $\tau$. [Construction of the two embeddings][Ctwo]

Here is the remaining linear-algebra argument. Fix an active $w$ and a row. Suppose the conjugate complex comparison gives a semilinear map $\Psi$ which preserves independence of an old complex basis, whose integral image spans the integral definite forms in the $\epsilon$-reading, and for which every old theta relation compares to the finite class sum by a single $z\in\mathbf C^\times$. Suppose some positive integer $N$ is a unit in $O_e(w)$, and both $N^d z$ and $N^d z^{-1}$ are algebraic integers for some $d$. Finally require that on these source vectors the installed theta map reads as that actual class sum after coefficient extension. [Complex comparison][Ccmp] · [Where class-sum rationality is used][Crat]

The old rational lattice spans the old row over $L$. For each member of a basis chosen in this lattice, lift its $\Psi$-image through the injective definite reading over $K_w$, then extend $e_w$-semilinearly. The lift $e$ agrees with $\Psi$ on the entire old row by injectivity of the reading. Equality of the integral spans follows by reading both spans and using injectivity again. The lifted basis is independent and spans after passing to $K_w$, since the target lattice is full. In any tested theta relation let $A,B$ be respectively the new theta value of $e(x)$ and the transported linear combination for the old value. Their target readings satisfy $r(A)=z\,r(B)$. The readings of a $K_w$-basis of the target are independent over $\mathbf C$. If some $B\ne0$, comparison of one nonzero coordinate gives $z=\epsilon(t)$ for $t\in K_w^\times$, and comparison in every relation now gives $A=tB$. The integrality of $N^dt^{\pm1}$ and invertibility of $N$ put $t,t^{-1}$ in the valuation ring. Rescaling $e$ by $t^{-1}$ gives exact equality in all relations and preserves the integral span. If every $B=0$, faithfulness gives every $A=0$ and the unit $1$ works. Choose the normalized map for each active place and row. [Lifting the map and its lattice][Clift] · [Descending the unit][Cunit] · [Installing the normalized maps][Cinstall]

## Remaining comparison on the active rows

The complex comparison still has to use conjugation of the flat theta series with their prescribed phase and identify the class sums in the target reading. On the first pass the simultaneous occurrence premise of this conjugation comparison has the single old tuple as its witness. Summation then uses the reciprocal stabilizer cardinalities, which are rational. The [rationality calculation](FIRST_PASS_SERIES_RATIONALITY.md) specifies a sufficient finite test on each actual row, the fixed determinant twist in every such test and the normalization of its arithmetic section. On the [adapted cover](FIRST_PASS_DISTINGUISHED_COVER.md) the base generators have rational readings with the geometric phase. The same chapter gives the finite coefficient comparison still needed for conjugation. The unit argument above applies once conjugation and membership in the installed reading hold there. The formal full-stage theorem packages the remaining analytic and arithmetic hypotheses. [Conjugate expansions and finite class sums][Cconj] · [Point at which the target reading is used][Crat] · [Full-stage implication][Cfull]
