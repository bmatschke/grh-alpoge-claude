## From two theta integrals to one real representation

Fix a definite bottom triangle $X=(A,A',W)$ for the square. Write $I_t(P)$ and $I'_t(P')$ for its two integrated theta kernels, viewed as functions on the target adelic unitary group. The test at the finite places is the prescribed one in each integral. The polynomial belongs to the corresponding archimedean Fock space. The following proof specifies which facts about these kernels enter the rationality argument.

1. The three frames of $X$ fix the real exponents on both sides. Twisting by a finite-order Hecke character preserves them since $\mathbf C^\times$ is connected. In the pinned matching construction the two polynomial spaces have surjections $p,p'$ onto one countable-dimensional irreducible complex module $\Pi_\infty$ for the real Lie algebra action, common to all the twists at these fixed exponents. To see what this buys, fix one tuple and a matched full quotient. Each finite test then gives an equivariant map from $\Pi_\infty$ to that quotient. Their images span by the spanning condition on polynomial tensors. Their sum admits a decomposition as a direct sum of copies of $\Pi_\infty$: a sum of simple submodules is semisimple. Its endomorphisms on one simple summand commuting with the action are scalars. Indeed, a nonscalar endomorphism $T$ would give invertible $T-z$ for every $z\in\mathbf C$ by irreducibility. The vectors $(T-z)^{-1}v$ for distinct $z$ and $v\ne0$ are linearly independent: a nontrivial finite relation gives a nonzero polynomial in $T$ killing $v$ after multiplication by the common denominator. Over $\mathbf C$ such a polynomial is a product of invertible linear factors, which is impossible. This contradicts countable dimension. Consequently the full quotient identifies with $\Pi_\infty\otimes M_t$ for its multiplicity space $M_t$. The two images of polynomial-pure tests are $p(P)\otimes r_t(f)$ and $p'(P')\otimes r'_t(f')$. Existence of the common real quotient with these actions is a local theta matching input. [Real exponents, scalar commutant and product decomposition](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/ThetaCorrespondence/ThetaPairLifts/LocalTypeDictionary/TwistedSquareLocalMatching.lean#L18-L80) · [Fixed frames before the tuple is chosen](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/ThetaCorrespondence/ThetaPairLifts/LocalTypeDictionary/BottomTripleIntegralFactorizationOfReal.lean#L21-L149)

2. The finite matching calculation for the prescribed pair gives a scalar $\kappa_t$ with $r_t(f_t)=\kappa_t r'_t(f'_t)$. This holds for every matched quotient since the intertwining isomorphism between two choices carries the two projections into one another up to a scalar. Matching the integrals supplies a nonzero scalar $a_t$ on the matched tests. The primed integral kills the finite coinvariant relations: integrating a translate of a test has the same value by invariance of the quotient measure. In the source the matched representative differs by such relations, and convergence permits subtraction under the integral. In particular $p'P'=0$ forces $I'_t(P')=0$. Thus $I'_t$ descends to a linear map $J_t$ on $\Pi_\infty$. The irreducible-defect argument gives $J_t=0$ or $J_t$ injective. Comparing both functions on $pP=p'P'$ and using the displayed finite relation gives
$$
 I'_t(P')=J_t(p'P'),\qquad I_t(P)=a_t\kappa_t\,J_t(pP).
$$
The assertions about the matched representatives and irreducibility are needed for these equations on the actual tests. 3. On this same $X$ and for each $t$, the prescribed finite-vector norm computation gives a polynomial $P_t$, a Haar measure on the target quotient, and $b_t\ne0$ such that
$$
 \int_{[U(W)]}|I_t(P_t)(h)|^2\,dh
      = b_t\prod_{s=1-e_0}^{h_0}L_t(s).
$$
The polynomial and the factor $b_t$ can depend on $t$. On the active grid $\nu_t=\nu_{t_0}\xi_t$ is unitary and has the same positive infinity exponents $m_\tau$ for every $t$. For $1-e_0\le s\le\mathrm{halfRank}$ the grid inequalities give $s+m_\tau\ge1$. ## A finite linear system for rational periods

Assume the common factorization just obtained, and use the **same** bottom triangle on which `SumsIn` gives nonzero complex periods $\Omega,\Omega'$ for all its monomials and all $t$. The field assumption first supplies such a triangle as soon as *some* bottom triangle exists. Apply the factorization to this arithmetic triangle. At each finite adelic point $h=(1,h_f)$, all monomial values of $I_t/\Omega$ and $I'_t/\Omega'$ belong to $K$. These kernels on definite quotients are continuous and automorphic. Values at such $h$ determine the function: the Cayley chart and weak approximation give density of $U(W)(F)$ in its real points. If the function vanishes at $(1,h_f)$ for every $h_f$, then left translation by any rational $\gamma$ gives its vanishing at $(\gamma_\infty,h_f)$ for every $h_f$. Continuity gives vanishing everywhere. The same reasoning applies to the difference of two such functions. [Arithmetic bottom-triangle quantifiers](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Definitions/TwistedInnerProducts/MemHalfCosetSelf.lean#L197-L261) · [Use of the same arithmetic triangle in the formal proof](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/TwistedOperators/RatioOfLocalMatchingTowers.lean#L27-L94)

There is a **single pair of $K$-coefficient polynomials** $Q,Q'$ such that
$$
 \frac{I_t(Q)(h)}{\Omega}
   = \frac{\lambda_t}{\lambda_{t_0}}\,
      \frac{I'_t(Q')(h)}{\Omega'}\qquad\text{for every }t,h,
 \qquad I'_t(Q')\ne0\ \text{for every }t.
$$
Here is a finite-dimensional proof, in place of choosing a linear functional on all of $\mathbf C$ as in [TT17](../../../research/modules/theta/theta_test_inputs/main.tex#L862-L926). Choose $P'$ with $u=p'P'\ne0$ and $P$ with $pP=u$. Let $V,V'$ be the spans over $K$ of the respective finite monomial supports. On $V\oplus V'$ impose the equations
$$
 \frac{I_{t_0}(Q)(1,h_f)}{\Omega}
    -\frac{I'_{t_0}(Q')(1,h_f)}{\Omega'}=0
 \qquad\text{for all }h_f .
$$
They are $K$-linear equations. Choose from their rows a basis of the row span. At most $\dim_K V+\dim_K V'-1$ rows are needed because the complexified system has the nonzero solution $(\Omega P/\lambda_{t_0},\Omega' P')$. At some finite $h_*$ the primed evaluation of that solution, divided by $\Omega'$, is nonzero by detection and injectivity of $J_{t_0}$. Finite Gaussian elimination over $K$ identifies the complex kernel with the scalar extension of the $K$-kernel. Therefore at least one $K$-kernel vector $(Q,Q')$ has nonzero primed evaluation at $h_*$. Its two normalized reference functions agree everywhere, so injectivity of $J_{t_0}$ gives $\lambda_{t_0}pQ/\Omega=p'Q'/\Omega'$ and $p'Q'\ne0$. Apply $J_t$ and use its injectivity and $\lambda_t\ne0$ to obtain the displayed identity.

For each $t$ choose a finite point $h_t$ with $I'_t(Q')(h_t)\ne0$. Both normalized values there are in $K^\times$, so their quotient shows $\lambda_t/\lambda_{t_0}\in K^\times$. Equivalently, writing $h_{t_0}=h_*$, the bottom ratio is the four-value expression
$$
  \frac{I_t(Q)(h_t)\, I'_{t_0}(Q')(h_*)}
       {I'_t(Q')(h_t)\, I_{t_0}(Q)(h_*)}.
$$
It is independent of the detecting points and of the polynomials satisfying these identities. The argument counts equations on fixed supports. It supplies no a priori bound on the polynomial degrees or an effective search for the points.

The normalized ratio also survives a change of the entire common quotient for the same triangle. Indeed, if another nonzero constant family $d_t$ is witnessed by fixed polynomials $A,B$, matching gives $\lambda_t pA=d_t p'B$ for every $t$ by injectivity of $J_t$. The primed nonvanishing in the constant witness implies $p'B\ne0$. Comparing this vector identity with the one at $t_0$ gives a single nonzero scalar $\gamma$ with $d_t=\gamma\lambda_t$ for all $t$. Each factorization gives such a constant witness by lifting any one nonzero $p'B$ through $p$. Thus two choices give identical normalized ratios. A *different triangle* can introduce tuple-dependent $K^\times$ factors under transfer, as the pinned change-of-frames statement specifies. ## The Gauss factors at the common base places

There is a sharper evaluation of the finite factors that applies to **every** admissible grid tuple. Let $v$ be a base bad place, let $\pi$ be the chosen local uniformizer, and write $k=\mathcal O_v/\pi$ and $q=|k|$. Put $\nu=\nu_{t_0,v}$, $\xi=\xi_{t,v}$ and let $e\in\{1,-1\}$ be the sign unit in the step. For conductor exponent $d>0$, the local factor in `stepFac` is
$$
 F(\nu)=\nu(e)\,q^{-dz}\nu(\pi)^d\,G(\nu),\qquad
 G(\nu)=\sum_{u\in(\mathcal O_v/\pi^d)^\times}\nu(u)^{-1}\psi(u/\pi^d).
$$
The character on units and the unramified additive character make the summands independent of the unit lifts at positive conductor. [Source definition, including the inverse on the multiplicative character](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/BlockTheory/DivisorBase/WeilTensorModels.lean#L101-L133)

There are precisely three conductor cases. At the distinguished base place $v_0$ the full character has conductor one and $\xi$ is unramified. At every other base place $v\in S_1$ the conductor is two and $\xi$ is tame. At a newly ramified place outside $S_0$ the full character has conductor one. The split chart identifies the chosen local unit and principal-unit filtrations with those of the Hecke character. Its chosen branch belongs to the prescribed support by the grid compatibility condition. [Exact conductor on all three parts](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/SquareNuCharConductorExponentsPosProdModulus.lean#L39-L87) · [Unramified twist at the distinguished place](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/SquareNuConductorExponentsPosProdEqModulus.lean#L57-L73) · [Local additive character and split branch](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/BaseFits.lean#L29-L39)

At $v\in S_1$, both $\nu$ and $\nu\xi$ have conductor exponent two. In fact $\nu$ is obtained from a depth-two character by a tame factor, so its restriction to $1+\pi\mathcal O_v$ remains nontrivial modulo $1+\pi^2\mathcal O_v$. Let $\bar\psi(y)=\psi(\tilde y/\pi)$ on $k$. There is a unique $b\in k^\times$ such that
$$
 \nu(1+\pi\tilde y)=\bar\psi(by)\qquad(y\in k).
$$
Indeed multiplication identifies this quotient of principal units with the additive group of $k$, and the nontrivial $\bar\psi$ identifies that group's characters with $k$ via multiplication. Choose a lift $\widehat a\in\mathcal O_v^\times$ of each $a\in k^\times$ and write each unit modulo $\pi^2$ uniquely as $\widehat a(1+\pi\tilde y)$. Its contribution at fixed $a$ is
$$
 \nu(\widehat a)^{-1}\psi(\widehat a/\pi^2)
               \sum_{y\in k}\bar\psi((a-b)y).
$$
The inner sum vanishes unless $a=b$, when it is $q$. Thus $G(\nu)=q\nu(\widehat b)^{-1}\psi(\widehat b/\pi^2)\ne0$. Tameness makes $\xi$ trivial on $1+\pi\mathcal O_v$, so the inner sums for $\nu\xi$ have the very same support and $G(\nu\xi)=\xi(\widehat b)^{-1}G(\nu)$. In particular,
$$
     \frac{F(\nu)}{F(\nu\xi)}
        =\frac{\xi(\widehat b)}{\xi(e)\,\xi(\pi)^2}.
$$
This is a root of unity in $K$, independent of the lift of $b$. The factor $q^{-2z}$ cancels for every rank parameter $z$. At the distinguished $v_0$ the twist $\xi$ is unramified, so $G(\nu\xi)=G(\nu)$ on residue-field units. The conductor-one calculation below gives $G(\nu)\ne0$, and the base quotient is exactly $\xi(\pi)^{-1}$. Thus **every base-place quotient is a root of unity**, including the places where the pinned proof bounds it by the quotient of two algebraic integers dividing $q^2$. ## Cancellation at successive ranks

At one removal of a hyperbolic plane the pinned `StepAlpha` and `StepBeta` each quantify the real maps on the two fixed geometries first. They then choose one $C\in\mathbf C^\times$ **before** the varying character data and finite maps. For an alpha step the relation reads $c=C\,\mathrm{stepFac}_t\, L^{S_0}_t(e)c_1$. For a beta step it reads $c\,L^{S_0}_t(e+1)=c_1/(C\,\mathrm{stepFac}_t)$, with the half-rank parameter $e$ of that step. Dividing a step at $t$ by the same step at $t_0$ cancels $C$ exactly. Consecutive removals can have different $C$. The remaining `stepFac` is a product of two signed ideal-character values and the listed finite local character and epsilon factors. Its ratio is calculated by distinguishing base bad places from newly ramified places and comparing the two ideal values by roots of unity. The passage to the finite Euler products and the final square equality includes the factors at the omitted places. The tower yields finitely many field-valued normalized step factors and a field multiplier. The other factors are the just-proved bottom ratio and products or reciprocals of $L_t(n)/L_{t_0}(n)$ at the designated positive integers. Once `NumbersIn.ratios` is available, `LNe` permits each of those divisions. This proves $c_t/c_{t_0}\in K^\times$ under the stated inputs.
