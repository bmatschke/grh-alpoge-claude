STATUS

This report proves the actual FramesOK construction from
tableLineDegreeFive.OrbitHyp, with all its stronger segment-span and L2-gap
clauses. It also proves the common-marked-tile and coverage clauses of the
stronger OrbitFrames/RestFrames conclusion. No abstract frame-existence
premise is retained. The construction does not prove abundance of subsets
TT satisfying OrbitHyp, nor the simultaneous global numerical conditions
used to supply such subsets.

The proof is finite combinatorics. It uses no representation-theoretic,
DVR, path-basis or previous local-rank theorem. The source's cyclic
enumeration is written with ordinary Euclidean division; the construction
is the literal bf_F used by bf_frames and bc_frames. The only stronger
observations are marked explicitly in FG8 and are not substituted into
downstream constants.

FG1. EXACT INPUTS AND DEFINITIONS

Write n=n'>0 for the even period, and identify TT subset Fin n with
T={t.val:t in TT} subset {0,...,n-1}. Let
  Ttilde={z in Z : z mod n belongs to T}.
All residues are the representatives in [0,n).

The source actual parameters, with delta=de, are
  u=ceil_+(1/delta),                  ell=(6r+1)u,
  M=ceil_+((h+3) log(x) / 10^(-(6r+1))),
  G'=(M+5)ell,
  N1=ceil_+(max(6 e G' delta, (h+3) log_2(x)))+1.
Here ceil_+ is the least nonnegative integer not below its argument;
the source calls these goodU, goodEll, orbitSmallMultiplicity, goodG'
and orbitWindowCount, respectively. The denominator in M can equivalently
be replaced by multiplication by 10^(6r+1), with no estimate.

For integers L>=0 and 0<=i<n, the cyclic window is
  Win_n(i,L)={(i+d) mod n : 0<=d<L}.
It is a set, not a list counting multiplicities. Put t=floor(n/ell).
The complete tiles are [s ell,(s+1)ell), 0<=s<t. The remaining interval
[t ell,n) is left over and must not be discarded in the cyclic spacing
calculation. Tile s has cells numbered c=1,...,6r+1,
  C(s,c)=[s ell+(c-1)u,s ell+c u).
Only integer points are considered. A tile is marked if:
  odd c: T intersect C(s,c) is empty;
  even c: T intersect C(s,c) is one point, which is even.
Thus every marked complete tile has exactly 3r selected even points.

The exact OrbitHyp, in addition to r>=2, n even and L1>=1 in frames_of,
says
  2L1<=L2<u,               3G'<=n,
and the following three finite conditions:
  A: for every i<n, |T intersect Win_n(i,L2)|<=r-1;
  Tiles: for every s0<t, some 0<=d<=M makes (s0+d) mod t marked;
  Intervals: for every i<n, |T intersect Win_n(i,G')|<=N1.
These are precisely CondA, CondTiles and CondIntervals; their conjunction
is IsGoodOrbit. Condition A counts all selected points, not just even ones.

The displayed inequalities imply u>=1. In the literal real-parameter
form they also imply delta>0: if delta<=0, then ceil_+(1/delta)=0
(with the source convention 1/0=0), contradicting L2<u. Hence ell>0,
G'>=ell, L2<n, and
  t>=3(M+5)>M+1.                                      (FG1.1)
No separate assumptions h>=0, delta<=1 or x>=3 are needed for this
finite construction once OrbitHyp is supplied. Those assumptions occur
in the source's separate asymptotic size/abundance uses.

In fact the raw construction only needs L1<=L2<u, r>=2, n positive
and even, G'=(M+5)ell, 3G'<=n and A/Tiles/Intervals. This is exactly
CornerGLContinuity.OrbitRaw. The stronger 2L1<=L2 belongs to the actual
OrbitHyp application and is retained there.

FG2. THE PERIODIC ENUMERATION AND ITS TRUE SUCCESSIVE GAP

Let S be the set of marked indices in {0,...,t-1}. Tiles at s0=0
and t>0 show S is nonempty. List it increasingly as
  s_0<...<s_(J-1),             J=|S|>=1.
For every integer j, write its Euclidean division uniquely as
  j=qJ+a,             q in Z, 0<=a<J,
and define
  sidx(j)=s_a,         B_j=qn+s_a ell.                 (FG2.1)
This is the source's explicit bf_seq; negative j use Euclidean
division with nonnegative remainder, not truncation toward zero.
It immediately gives B_(j+J)=B_j+n and enumerates every marked index.

The cyclic distance D_a from s_a to the next marked index is
  s_(a+1)-s_a                  if a+1<J,
  t+s_0-s_(J-1)               if a=J-1.
It satisfies
  1<=D_a<=M+1.                                      (FG2.2)
Indeed apply Tiles at (s_a+1) mod t. The supplied marked tile occurs
at a positive cyclic distance 1+d<=M+1<t. The next marked tile is
the first such tile, so it cannot be farther away. This proves both
the ordinary and wrap cases at once; no probabilistic independence
or choice of a sparse subsequence is involved.

Write n=t ell+R, 0<=R<ell. At a non-wrap transition,
B_(j+1)-B_j=D_a ell; at a wrap transition it is D_a ell+R.
Consequently
  ell<=B_(j+1)-B_j<(M+2)ell.                         (FG2.3)
The extra ell in the strict upper bound is the remainder R. Replacing
the physical period n by t ell at the wrap would be incorrect.
Each lifted interval [B_j,B_j+ell) is a marked tile for Ttilde.
This proves every bf_Seq clause, including the same marked index
for a whole lifted tile and surjectivity onto all marked indices.

FG3. THE THREE BLOCKS OF EVEN TOPS AND THEIR ISOLATION

For every j and 0<=v<3r, let a_(j,v) be the unique selected point
in the even cell c=2v+2 of [B_j,B_j+ell). Then
  B_j+(2v+1)u <= a_(j,v) < B_j+(2v+2)u.             (FG3.1)
The lifted marked condition follows directly from the original
complete tile: subtract qn to identify its cells with C(s_a,c).
Evenness is preserved because n is even, including negative q.
This is the only parity requirement on the period; u need not be even.

The 3r points are strictly increasing, and they are all selected
points in the lifted tile, since the cells partition it. They obey
  a_(j,v)+u<a_(j,v+1),                              (FG3.2)
as the intervening odd cell is empty. Their endpoint buffers are
  B_j+u<=a_(j,0),        a_(j,3r-1)+u<B_j+ell.      (FG3.3)

More strongly, every other selected point y<a_(j,v), even if y
is outside this tile, obeys
  y+u<a_(j,v).                                     (FG3.4)
For v>0 the greatest possible earlier selected point in this tile is
a_(j,v-1)<B_j+2vu, whereas a_(j,v)>=B_j+(2v+1)u.
For v=0 no earlier selected point lies in the tile, so y<B_j and
the first inequality of (FG3.3) applies. The same argument works
for earlier points outside the tile when v>0. A symmetric argument
gives a_(j,v)+u<y for every selected y>a_(j,v). Neither assertion
assumes the rest of the period is unselected.

The source function bf_tp chooses exactly these unique cell points.
The classification above is bf_class, the predecessor inequality is
bf_gap, and uniqueness gives
  a_(j+J,v)=a_(j,v)+n.                              (FG3.5)

FG4. THE ACTUAL FRAMES AND ONE COMMON TILE INDEX

Define the frame to be the middle r of those 3r points:
  f_(j,i)=a_(j,r+i),              0<=i<r.            (FG4.1)
Its cell number is 2r+2+2i. Set F.TT=TT and F.J=J.
For the source's total function fr(j,i) on all natural i, put
fr(j,i)=B_j when i>=r. This is exactly bf_F, not just an isomorphic
frame choice, and ensures its period identity for every natural i.

Equations (FG3.1–5) prove:
  f_(j+J,i)=f_(j,i)+n;
  f_(j,0)<...<f_(j,r-1);
  f_(j,r-1)<f_(j+1,0);
  every frame entry belongs to Ttilde and is even.
For the inter-frame inequality, use
f_(j,r-1)<B_j+4ru<B_j+ell<=B_(j+1)
and f_(j+1,0)>=B_(j+1)+(2r+1)u.
No selected point lies strictly between two consecutive entries
of one frame: these are consecutive members of the marked tile's
complete selected list.

For each j, one and the same marked index sidx(j) serves every
coordinate i<r:
  f_(j,i) mod n belongs to C(sidx(j),2r+2+2i).       (FG4.2)
It is not merely a separate existential choice of tile for each i.
Conversely every marked complete tile occurs, by taking j=a when
its index is s_a. In particular its cell 2r+2 contains f_(a,0) mod n.
These are the stronger common-index and coverage clauses of bc_frames
and OrbitFrames, beyond the weaker per-coordinate statement bf_frames.

FG5. SEGMENT SPAN, BOUNDARY LETTERS AND THE STRONG L2 GAPS

The source segment and its intermediate selected letters are
  W_j=Ttilde intersect [f_(j-1,0),f_(j,r-1)],
  LL_j=Ttilde intersect (f_(j-1,r-1),f_(j,0)).
The old-frame condition IsW(j,x) means x<=f_(j-1,r-1).
Because the frame entries are consecutive selected points, the selected
old part of W_j consists of exactly those r frame entries.

The exact first r members of LL_j are
  a_(j-1,2r),...,a_(j-1,3r-1),
and its exact last r members are
  a_(j,0),...,a_(j,r-1).                             (FG5.1)
Each displayed list lies in LL_j by the frame order and (FG2.3).
Within each boundary marked tile there are no other selected points
between its consecutive listed points. Anything outside the previous
marked tile lies after its last listed point; anything outside the
current marked tile lies before its first listed point. This proves
the assertions about the first and last r members, regardless of
the selected points in unmarked intervening tiles. The two lists
are disjoint, so |LL_j|>=2r, in particular the source requirement
|LL_j|>=r holds.

The literal endpoint predicate is
  |{y in LL_j:y<=x}|<=r
        or |LL_j|<|{y in LL_j:y<=x}|+r.
For an increasing finite list, these mean respectively that x is
among its first r or its last r members, including both endpoints.
Thus (FG5.1) covers the source predicate exactly. Every such x is
even. By (FG3.4), it has distance >u>L2 from every earlier selected
point; in particular its immediate predecessor in W_j has that gap.
The same holds for f_(j,0). This is the full GapGt L2 assertion,
not merely the weaker L1 gap in Valid. Passing to L1<=L2 preserves it.

The segment has the required strict span:
  f_(j,r-1)-f_(j-1,0)
    < B_j-B_(j-1)+(2r-1)u
    < (M+3)ell
    <=G'.                                           (FG5.2)
The first inequality uses the exact last and first frame cell bounds;
the second uses (FG2.3) and (2r-1)u<ell. The source retains the
looser G'=(M+5)ell. Neither equality at G' nor an extra inclusive
endpoint is introduced. In particular every point of W_j lies in
[f_(j-1,0),f_(j-1,0)+G').

FG6. WINDOW CARDINALITY AND THE OLDEST VECTOR

For any integer b and integer 0<=L<=n, reduction modulo n is injective
on the integer half-open interval [b,b+L). Indeed two congruent integers
differ by a multiple of n, whereas their difference has absolute value
<n. Its image is Win_n(b mod n,L). It follows from A and Intervals that
  |Ttilde intersect [b,b+L2)|<=r-1,
  |Ttilde intersect [b,b+G')|<=N1.                    (FG6.1)
Here L2<n and G'<=n/3, as already checked in FG1.
Using (FG5.2) in the second estimate gives
  |W_j|<=N1.                                         (FG6.2)
This checks the interval-cardinality clause of Valid with the precise
N1=orbitWindowCount from the actual input.

For any x in W_j outside its old part, the r old-frame entries are
distinct even selected points in W_j strictly before x. Hence the
source sorted list of even predecessors has at least r entries.
Its index length-r is valid: the default-zero branch in the source's
getD definition of oldest is never used. Let y be that r-th most recent
even predecessor. The r most recent even predecessors all lie in [y,x).

If x-y<=L1, then x<=y+L1<=y+L2, so these r distinct selected points
lie in [y,y+L2). This contradicts the first bound of (FG6.1).
Therefore
  x-oldest(j,x)>L1                                   (FG6.3)
for every required late x. This argument explains the strictness even
when L1=L2: the predecessor list is strictly below x, while the
counting interval is half-open. It proves bc_oldest_far directly;
the older bd_oldest_far assumes the unnecessary L1+1<=L2, which is
also available in the actual OrbitHyp application.

FG7. ASSEMBLY INTO FRAMESOK AND RESTFRAMES

Take F from FG4. We now account for every Valid L1 N1 clause:

  (i) J>=1: FG2.
  (ii) Periodicity for all natural frame indices: FG4, including i>=r.
  (iii) Increasing rows and ordered consecutive frames: FG4.
  (iv) Selected even frame entries: FG3–FG4.
  (v) No selected points between consecutive entries of a frame: FG4.
  (vi) At least r intermediate selected points, and the exact first/last-r
       endpoint parity and L1 gap, plus the gap before f_(j,0): FG5.
  (vii) The full segment has at most N1 selected letters: FG6.
  (viii) The r-th even predecessor has distance >L1 at every late x: FG6.

In addition, FG5 proves the strict segment span<G' and retains both
L2-gap assertions at the intermediate endpoints and f_(j,0). Along
with F.TT=TT, these are exactly Pf14.FramesOK TT L1 L2 N1 G' F.
Therefore frames_of is fully proved from its actual OrbitHyp premises.

Under the more general OrbitRaw inputs of FG1, the same argument proves
the full OrbitFrames conclusion: common marked-tile index for every
frame, coverage of every marked tile, the span and segment-cardinality
pair, and the stronger L2-gap clauses. This proves the actual content
of TopChoiceBounds.restFrames_proved. The wrappers are consequences of
the explicit construction above; they are not assumed as premises.

FG8. QUANTITATIVE FRAME COUNT AND PRECISE REMAINING SCOPE

For the constructed frame, different marked complete tiles are disjoint
subsets of [0,n), and each has exactly 3r selected points. Hence the
derived stronger count is
  3rJ<=|TT|.                                         (FG8.1)
In particular J*r<=|TT| and J<=|TT|. The exact weaker bound Jr<=|TT|
used downstream also holds for any Valid periodic frame: the Jr entries
f_(j,i), 0<=j<J,0<=i<r, are strictly ordered in one half-open period
[f_(0,0),f_(0,0)+n), so their reductions inject into TT. This is the
source's frame_count_mul_le_tops and does not require the special
marked-tile construction.

Likewise (FG5.2) and G'<n make reduction injective on W_j, so the
derived bound |W_j|<=min(N1,|TT|) holds for the constructed data.
The source standard choice Ns=min(N1,2|TT|) remains valid; we do not
change that choice or any downstream estimate.

No specialized frame-construction claim remains unexpanded in this
report. What is still supplied is the actual OrbitHyp: the three
finite selected-set conditions and their numerical separation. The
probability/abundance theorem ensuring enough good orbits, the global
choice of TT, the asymptotic inequalities for these parameters, and
compatibility with all later Type-II degree/chain hypotheses are outside
this finite construction. In particular a statement of CondTiles for a
given T is not a proof that an abundant family of such T exists.

This is a source-backed human proof. No Lean source was edited, no new
Lean formalization of the shorter enumeration was made, and no full Lean
build or kernel replay is asserted.
