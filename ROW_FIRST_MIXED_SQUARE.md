# The beta comparison on the first mixed square

Let $j=m-3$, $m\ge3$. The upper source ranks are $a,n$, the target rank is $d=a+n$, and $e=(a-n)/2>5$, $n>5$. The upper defects are $(1,1)$. Remove a rational hyperbolic plane $H$ from the small source and $H'$ from the target. The resulting triple has ranks $a,n-2,d-2$ and defects $(1,0)$. Fix the compatible oscillator splittings, analytic models, planes, evaluations, Haar measures and upper and lower real quotient maps. Normalize the finite maps by the usual spherical and marked zeta conditions. Use the [first alpha comparison](ROW_FIRST_ALPHA_STEP.md) at the lower triangle, including its definite nonvanishing input. Write $c_{1,t}$ for its nonzero scalar. With the standing local Howe, mixed-model covariance, Fock generating-type and arithmetic character inputs, the full mixed square identity on this model is
$$
 I_{b,t}=c_t I_{s,t}\quad\text{on matched tests},\qquad
 c_t=\frac{c_{1,t}}{C_\beta D^\beta_t\,L_t(e+1)},\qquad C_\beta\ne0.
$$
Here $C_\beta$ is independent of the twist on the fixed compatible models, the small test is primed as required in analytic matching, and $D^\beta_t$ has ideal exponents $(-1,-1)$ and local epsilon parameter $-e$ with sign $+1$. The proof needs a boundary comparison to an indefinite lower target and a cuspidality test on a rank-two target. ## Extracting beta on the two sides

At a target point $h$, the [mixed theta bounds](ROW_MIXED_INTEGRABILITY.md) give absolute source integration on both sides and target $L^2$. They are uniform on compact sets of $h$, including the unipotent representatives in the line average. Average over the center of the line radical, of probability volume one. Its additive character in the source-vector summand picks out exactly the rational null vectors. The zero vector on the big side has beta law $\beta(l)|l|_E^{a/2}$ and is ordinary evaluation $E_0$ onto the pair consisting of the **same** big source and the lower target $W_1$. The orbit of nonzero big null vectors now also contributes. Its horizontal unipotent average selects the zero Fourier index by nondegeneracy of $W_1$. Unfold the surviving sum to the vector stabilizer as in the [alpha orbit calculation](ROW_FIRST_ALPHA_STEP.md#rank-one-convergence-and-the-two-orbits). Its smaller source has rank $a-2$ and is definite. The scalar-line integral has weight $\nu(l)|l|_E^e$ and target law $\alpha(l)|l|_E^{n/2}$. It therefore contributes solely to alpha. These computations require only nondegeneracy of $W_1$ and compactness of the *source* after the nonzero-orbit reduction. At fixed $h_1$ the lower rational lattice sums are uniformly absolutely bounded over its compact lower source representatives by Schwartz decay. Absolute convergence of the big ordinary zero-orbit integral also follows by the rank-one estimate with target dimension $d-2$, since $d-2+2<2a$. [Big zero and nonzero orbit formulas][big]

On the small side apply the [projective-line Poisson calculation](ROW_FIRST_BETA_STEP.md#both-beta-coefficients). Orthogonality on the horizontal unipotent again uses only nondegeneracy of its pairing. After partial Fourier transformation, the omitted zero term for each rational line belongs to the alpha component along with the original zero orbit. The beta component is the unfolded cone $Q_B$ of exponent $e+1$ and character $\nu=\alpha/\beta$. Its lower source has rank $n-2$ and is definite. The Euler product of the absolute unramified scalar sums converges for $e+1>1$. At fixed $h_1$ the compact lower source, the finite exceptional widths and archimedean Schwartz bounds justify the same changes of variables and integrations. Thus on $m(1,h_1)$, also after a right translate of the input, the two beta components are
$$
 C_0 I_{b,1}(E_0\Phi)(h_1),\qquad C_1 I_{s,1}(Q_B\Phi')(h_1).
$$
Dilation by any idele of norm different from one separates the alpha and beta laws since $n<a$. This extraction commutes with right translation. [Construction of both components and their coefficients][key]

For the beta cone the primed local condition survives the removal of **both** planes. In the barred chart, if a lower matrix has a nonzero row relation, lift that relation from the orthogonal complement in the small source and adjoin the distinguished isotropic source vector. They are independent. On the fiber of the mixed evaluation, the Heisenberg translation laws force the associated two row functionals to vanish on the lower target and on $H'.x$. Only the single coordinate $H'.y$ remains. A nontrivial combination then vanishes there too, giving a left kernel vector for the upper matrix. To see the support implication, impose the vanishing conditions by Fourier orthogonality against source/target translations in the eliminated coordinates. A translated upper matrix with those constraints and nonzero value would violate full row rank. This gives full lower rank on every nonzero evaluated piece for each scalar shift in the cone. Compact source translates preserve the upper rank condition. Finite smooth averaging and the scalar-line integral preserve the lower support. Ordinary small zero evaluation for the *alpha* component has a different support property. The [row-operation replacement](ROW_BETA_PRIMED_REPLACEMENT.md) makes that evaluation primed while preserving the upper integral, since $n-1<(d-2)/2$. ## Boundary maps on an indefinite lower target

Both lower lifts are nonzero on the full restricted tensor of their prescribed irreducible local quotients. For the small lift, ordinary evaluation on the alpha branch reaches a nonzero definite integral. The lower alpha comparison then gives a nonzero big lift. Its unrestricted convergence and the compact small source permit individual local quotient descent on all polynomial inputs as in the [rank-one argument](ROW_FIRST_ALPHA_STEP.md). The simplicity argument in the [stage factorization](ROW_STAGE_STANDARD_TESTS.md#injectivity-and-a-polynomial-shared-by-the-twists) makes each lower map injective on its quotient tensor. This detection holds with arbitrary nonzero projected factors frozen. On the upper triple apply the [mixed $L^2$ bounds](ROW_MIXED_INTEGRABILITY.md) and local Howe duality to each slice. In the designated primed slot an auxiliary split place can supply full rank while that slot varies by the [two-place replacement](ROW_BETA_SQUARE_INTEGRABILITY.md#local-smoothness-semisimplicity-and-the-designated-place). This proves the individual upper kernel descents prior to comparing its two integrals.

At a real place the ordinary big evaluation is onto on polynomials, hence has nonzero projection to the lower module. The beta cone on the small side also has nonzero lower projection. Freeze a lower small finite test and a target point on a nonzero lower theta function. Evaluation of its integral at that point gives a nonzero continuous source-invariant linear functional on lower real Schwartz vectors, since the source is compact. For this functional the [real scalar-line Fourier localization](ROW_BETA_LOCAL_COMPARISON.md#nonvanishing-on-the-two-actual-oscillator-spaces) applies word for word. The scalar phases and the strict bound $\kappa=m_\alpha-m_\beta>d>2e$ are unchanged. The proof integrates a Schwartz slice against the angular weight, localizes at a nonzero dual frequency and uses constant phase and a positive density over its compact stabilizer fiber. It never integrates over the lower target. Thus a polynomial cone has a nonzero lower projection. Polynomiality follows from orthogonality of the two removed hyperbolic blocks to the surviving variables in the adapted positive oscillator metric, as in the [alpha calculation](ROW_FIRST_ALPHA_STEP.md#comparison-of-the-alpha-boundary-maps). [Use of a nonzero lower Schwartz functional][conept]

To descend the real boundary operators, freeze finite factors with nonzero lower projections. At marked places the standard pair has the nonzero Gauss quotient calculated in the [marked beta comparison](ROW_BETA_LOCAL_COMPARISON.md#the-marked-split-beta-factor-on-all-quotient-classes), and away from them the two vacuum factors are nonzero. At the designated primed place choose a full-rank representative of the vacuum class. Projecting the cone on lower source coinvariants preserves its value under this replacement. Indeed for each functional on the lower quotient the compact and multiplicative average is an integral over the upper source vector stabilizer quotient. Moving a source translate across this integral preserves it by invariant measure. This argument on the split chart is valid also on the unprimed vacuum, because the local cone integral of a compactly supported scalar slice converges at $e+1>0$. Thus all the selected lower finite vectors are nonzero. An upper real kernel vector has zero integrated upper function and hence zero beta coefficient. Lower detection then forces its projected boundary vector to vanish. Comparing the beta coefficient after a Levi or unipotent translate proves the parabolic covariance of the descended maps. Differentiate the real action on polynomial vectors, or use difference quotients in the smooth $L^2$ realization. [One-place kernel and covariance arguments](ROW_BETA_LOCAL_COMPARISON.md#finite-quotient-descent-for-the-beta-coefficient) · [Projected cone at the good place][localaug]

There is a short multiplicity calculation even though the lower target $U(d-3,1)$ is noncompact. At one real place write
$$
 s=(m_\beta+a-2)/2,\quad t_*=(m_\beta-a+2)/2,\quad
 u=(\kappa-d+4)/2,\quad v=(\kappa+d-4)/2.
$$
In the row Fock convention the generating compact type of the common upper module for $U(d-2)\times U(2)$ has weights
$$
 ((s+u)^{[a-1]},s^{[n-1]})\quad\text{and}\quad(t_*+v,t_*).
$$
These are the weights of the compact joint harmonic highest vector for the big source of signature $(a-1,1)$, including the target splitting shift. They follow by inserting $(a-1,1,d-2,2)$ in the two source and target weight formulas. The nonzero image of the generating type generates the simple upper module. At the lower level the target dimension has dropped by two and its negative index by one. Its signature difference is still $d-4$ and the big source is unchanged. Hence the lower generating type has the same entries with one $s$ and one $t_*$ deleted. [Real quotient for the prescribed maps][lam] · [Two polynomial realizations][fock]

The compact part of the line Levi retains $U(d-3)\times U(1)$ and acts by the same circle on the deleted positive and negative coordinates. The unitary interlacing rule says that the deleted positive weight is $s+i$, $0\le i\le u$, and the deleted negative weight is $t_*+j$, $0\le j\le v$. Each interlacing component occurs once. The beta character on their common circle has weight $m_\beta=s+t_*$. Thus only $i=j=0$ contributes. It leaves precisely the lower generating compact type. This type has multiplicity at most one in the lower quotient itself. Indeed the lower small source is compact of rank $n-2$. In its polynomial Fock model the block with $d-3$ target columns decomposes by the Cauchy identity into pairs of Schur modules with multiplicity one. The one-column block contributes a single symmetric power for each degree. A fixed target $U(d-3)\times U(1)$ type therefore selects one Schur factor and one symmetric power on the source, up to the fixed determinant twists and duals. Both are irreducible. The prescribed one-dimensional source character occurs in their tensor product with multiplicity at most one by Schur's lemma. Compact source projection and passage to the lower quotient preserve that bound. This proves the needed compact Hom bound in an infinite-dimensional lower module.

Finally $\mathfrak g_\mathbb C=\mathfrak p_\mathbb C+\mathfrak k_\mathbb C$. Ordering the enveloping algebra with parabolic terms on the left shows that a parabolic map is determined on the upper generating compact type. Products of real places use exterior tensor products of those types. The two nonzero real boundary maps must therefore be proportional by a nonzero scalar. Their values on polynomial lifts are independent of the finite characters for fixed real maps. The infinite Weil operators for the same real splitting exponents coincide, and evaluating the two real operators on a matched pair with nonzero lower image fixes their proportionality constant. [Parabolic characterization and uniqueness in the pinned proof][par] · [Independence of the real action][same]

At finite places the marked and spherical zeta calculations depend on the ranks, adapted columns, conductor and additive measure. They are unchanged by the real signature of the lower target. For a varying finite slot, freeze the two nonzero real factors just constructed and all the other nonzero finite factors. Individual upper descent, extraction of beta and lower injectivity give the finite descended maps and their parabolic law, exactly as for the real slot. At the primed place first use the stabilizer quotient argument above to descend the projected cone through the source-translation relations. Spherical generation and Iwasawa determine the unmarked scalar. Ramified multiplicity one on the unchanged big rectangular model determines the marked scalar on all quotient classes from its standard vector. This gives equality of the lower tensor images of $C_2D^\beta_t L_t(e+1)E_0\Phi$ and $Q_B\Phi'$ on any matched upper polynomial pair, with $C_2$ fixed across twists. Applying the lower alpha comparison on this pair establishes the asserted beta relation with $C_\beta=C_1C_2/C_0$. A nonzero lower big polynomial lifted by $E_0$ detects it. Surjectivity onto the common upper module, including full-rank representatives at the designated place, gives a matched small partner. ## The other line character and the plane parabolic

Subtract $c_t I_{s,t}$ from $I_{b,t}$. Applying the beta computation to every simultaneous compact translate and then parabolic Iwasawa shows that the difference has pure alpha line constant term. In the other plane removal, from the big source to ranks $a-2,n$, the alpha part on every translated Levi slice is a difference of lower theta functions. Its big summand is the convergent alpha cone from the big nonzero orbit above. Its small summand uses ordinary zero evaluation after the primed replacement above. The zero correction terms vanish for this representative by the lower full-rank condition. These are the lower membership and two-orbit hypotheses in the [two-step line argument](ROW_MIXED_LINE_REMAINDER.md). Opposite dilations on an isotropic flag eliminate the lower beta exponent, and the already established pure-alpha vanishing on $(0,1)$ gives zero for the entire upper line coefficient. At a fresh split place the [rank-two Whittaker calculation](ROW_MIXED_PLANE_TERM.md) then kills the isotropic-plane constant term. The [two reverse pairings](ROW_MIXED_CUSP_ORTHOGONALITY.md) make the resulting cuspidal difference orthogonal to both of its own summands. Its norm is zero. Continuity gives equality at every target point, and the mixed seminorm bounds pass the polynomial equality to the simultaneous approximants of matched Schwartz tests. The nonzero beta detector proves uniqueness as well as existence on these models. [Use of the other plane removal][other]

The [mixed grid calculation](ROW_MIXED_GRID_RATIOS.md) transports this identity to the prescribed endpoints, composes it with the lower alpha comparison, and identifies the surviving finite character.
