# Preserving every prescribed character grid in the two field requests

The field used for the list of layer characters and the field used for the Fourier evaluations have different positions in the construction. The first is the embedded fixed field $L_0$. It must be chosen before the untwisted member is exported. The second is the ambient coefficient field $K$. It can be enlarged after export, once the actual finite set $\Xi$ of retwist characters is known. On the two-pass construction one can arrange the following stronger choices.

1. For a fixed preliminary datum and positive auxiliary prime, at gap one and depth $C_F$, the *original* characters of every permitted grid have their unramified values in $L_0$, and $L_0$ contains the required primitive roots for the depths of that grid. The choice of $L_0$ is independent of the later row completion, level indices, grid and twist.
2. Under the analytic and arithmetic inputs of Roadmap B8.17 and the pointwise Hida and square-recursion conditions of B8.20, one ambient request after export suffices for an evaluation-field and square-field choice preserving the base prime, its chosen lift and the entire sequence of layer characters of **each prescribed grid**. This request fits inside the actual retwist step along with the caller's field request. It can therefore be used while constructing the selected class without changing its prime allowance or reacquiring its avoidance and all-powers bounds.

Here $L_0$ is the field in the native list condition, not the prescribed exact small subfield $K_s$. The theorem concerns the realization of field requests inside the common design. It does not assert that a supplied, unrelated coefficient field already meets either request. ## A fixed field for the values of every grid

Fix the preliminary data $P,f$, including the archimedean weights, host $E/F$, the five rational primes $g_0,g_1,p_\delta,p'_\delta,q_0$, and the constants $(1,C_F,b)$. Write $V$ for the set of places of $F$ above $q_0$ that occur in a grid. This finite set can be specified before choosing any later completion: take the union over all possible completions of $P$ and their grids. Every element divides the same rational prime; only its occurrence is in question. For each $v\in V$ and $1\le i\le m$ the grid midpoint at gap one fixes the algebraic infinity type. Let
$$
 \mathfrak m=\prod_{\mathfrak P\mid g_0g_1p_\delta p'_\delta q_0}
                  \mathfrak P^2,\qquad
 h=|\operatorname{Cl}_{\mathfrak m}(E)|,\qquad
 e=\exp \operatorname{Cl}_{\mathfrak m}(E).
$$
The product uses **all** primes of $E$ over the five rational primes. The actual character is unramified outside its support, trivial on the second principal-unit subgroup above $g_0,g_1,p_\delta,p'_\delta$, and trivial on the first above its chosen base place. These are bounds on its conductor, even if the base-place test depth is greater than one. Thus its finite conductor divides $\mathfrak m$. [Grid conductor, infinity types and depths][conductors]

For each realized $v\in V$, take one grid with base place $v$. Its layer characters $\chi_{v,i}^0$ have algebraic unramified values contained in a single number field $N_v$. Indeed on principal ray ideals the ideal character is evaluated by its infinity type. Each angular exponent is $2c$, so the archimedean factor is given by a product of integral powers of conjugates and their ratios in a normal closure of $E$. Use the **actual conductor** of $\chi_{v,i}^0$ in this argument, so it controls every unramified prime of that character. Choose representatives for its finitely many ray classes. A representative to the power given by its class order is principal on the ray, so its character value is algebraic. Adjoining the values on these representatives produces a number field containing the values at all unramified primes. Take the compositum for $1\le i\le m$. [One-grid field of values](FIRST_PASS_TWIST_SETTINGS.md#one-field-request-for-all-the-grids-that-can-occur) · [Unramified-value convention][values]

If $\chi_{v,i}$ is the same layer of **any** other grid with this base place, the quotient $\eta=\chi_{v,i}/\chi_{v,i}^0$ has trivial infinity type. The prescribed archimedean equality on $E^\times$ suffices by density and continuity. Its conductor divides $\mathfrak m$, so as an idele class character it factors through the finite ray class group $\operatorname{Cl}_{\mathfrak m}(E)$. In particular every value of $\eta$ lies in $\mu_e$, including at a chosen uniformizer. The two layer characters are ramified at exactly the primes over the four marked primes and the given $v$: the nontriviality on first principal units at the marked primes and on units above $v$ is part of the grid law. Hence at every prime at which $\chi_{v,i}$ is unramified the reference character is unramified too. Its uniformizer value times this root of unity lies in $N_v(\mu_e)$. This proves the field assertion for the original $\chi_{v,i}$ while retaining all its local characters. Also there are at most $h$ possibilities for the layer character for each $v,i$. [Level laws][conductors] · [Older replacement at fixed base place][replace]

The finite support $S_1\cup\{v\}$ and the required depths $d(q)$ depend only on the preliminary data, $v$ and the grid constants. Take the compositum
$$
 N_{\rm list}=
 \mathbb Q\!\left(\mu_e,\ (N_v)_{v\in V},
        \ \mu_{(Nq)^{d(q)}}:\ v\in V,\ q\in S_1\cup\{v\}\right).
$$
A finite algebraic generating set belongs in the first request on $L_0$. The construction leaves $K_s$ at its required exact value. If there are no realized grids the assertion is vacuous. Otherwise this gives the native field-of-values and depth-root conditions on **every** grid on a later permitted completion of this $P$ at these constants, including one already carrying the required abelian list. There is no need to replace its characters by the representative's. The list equality itself still comes from the stage recording the abelian layers. [Field condition][field] · [Preserving the exact small field](FIRST_PASS_FIELDS.md#meeting-the-requests-in-order)

## A single ambient request on the actual retwist

At the construction thresholds of B8.21, keep the requested $C_F$ fixed and take the first-pass list depth $c_{\rm dep}=C_F$. The fourth request in that theorem is a function of $(P,f,\Xi)$, with empty additional requests at the three earlier positions. To see its finiteness, for each character $\xi\in\Xi$ occurring in an admissible realization, its marked half $Q_t$ is a subset of the finite ramification set, and the latter is contained in $Q_t\cup\overline{Q_t}$. Every level key is a subset of the finite union of the possible pairs $(\xi,Q_t)$. This permits repeated tuples and arbitrary tuple multiplicities without adding a new key. Separately, the prescribed grid key
$$
 \gamma(G)=(v_0,w(v_0),\chi_1,\ldots,\chi_m)
$$
has at most $2|V|h^m$ possibilities by the preceding argument; the base place splits in $E/F$. There is no requirement to bound the size or degree of the finite request uniformly in $P$. [Actual level-key computation][keys] · [Extension on a prescribed grid and transport by keys][full]

For each realized pair of keys use the complete evaluation choice on that representative **with its prescribed grid** from B8.17/B8.21. Also use the square-field assertion at the representative. Its height-independent field step has two parts. The higher toric ratios are algebraic since their twelfth powers are ratios of algebraic normalized nonzero Hecke values. For the common bottom data, when a common period and two-torsion point and bottom witnesses exist simultaneously, choose such a family jointly and adjoin the two norms of each pair. Its coordinates are algebraic and the class norms are stabilizer-weighted Hermitian sums. Thus the field contains actual norms from a joint family, rather than incompatible witnesses chosen at different periods. These statements use the hypotheses on the CM toric evaluations and on the square-recursive row. Take generators of the compositum of these fields and the complete evaluation fields over the finite set of representatives. Denote them by $S_{\rm full}(P,f,\Xi)$, with empty request for infinite $\Xi$. The level-agreement and grid transport of B8.20 give a full grid on every subsequently permitted realization of that pair of keys with $\gamma(G)=\gamma(G_0)$ for the grid $G_0$ supplied there. This preserves those specified characters and the chosen lift. Other auxiliary choices need only satisfy the full-grid conditions for that row. [Evaluation-field theorem][evaluation] · [Algebraic toric ratios and joint bottom data][square] · [Full-field request in the ordered construction][fourth]

To install this particular request, begin at the fitted built untwisted origin with its all-powers export, as in the second-pass theorem. The character triples are chosen after the caller's avoidances and before the ambient enlargement. On the old base they define twist data $T_1$ with $\Xi=T_1.{\rm set}(f)$, including the frame characters. In the retwist step use
$$
 S_{\rm caller}(P,f,\Xi)\ \cup\ S_{\rm pin}(P,f,\Xi)\
                       \cup\ S_{\rm full}(P,f,\Xi).
$$
The enlarged field also contains the original required roots for the local coefficient indices. It can be taken normal, with $L_0$ and $K_s$ still given by their old embedded fields. Pull back each character triple along contraction of coefficient places. Lying over makes the restriction of places surjective, so the new tuple characters have **exactly** the same range $\Xi$. Conjugation commutes with restriction and the avoidance law is imposed on the host conductors, which are the previously chosen ones. Consequently the full-field request evaluated on the *new* column is the finite set actually adjoined. The coprime-power export and the disjointness of the chosen supports continue to concern the original triples. No alteration of the host frame or of the prescribed prime allowance is used. [Enlargement theorem with the literal ambient request][lift] · [Old seed and preserved column](SECOND_PASS_RETWIST.md#enlarge-once-and-retain-the-pin)

On a retwisted row produced with its list at this depth, take the recorded grid $G_0$. Under the foregoing Hida and square-recursion conditions the request supplies a full $G$ on **that realization**. The first part supplies its character values and roots in $L_0$, and $\gamma(G)=\gamma(G_0)$ transports the whole abelian-list equality: its distinguished place is $w(v_0)$, its critical weights use the same gap, and its layer functions use the actual $\chi_a$. In the native fitting predicate these are the fixed-field grid condition and the full list tie. The choice also satisfies the ambient evaluation and square-field requirements. Taking $C_F$ above the common constant, then the maximum of the host and the two-pass cutoffs before the member, preserves the order of quantifiers. [List through both spectral replacements](SECOND_PASS_GOOD_CORE.md#preserving-goodness-through-the-two-changes-of-spectral-realization) · [Place, depth and list equalities on the actual level][transport]

This supplies the field and prescribed-grid comparison on the forward two-pass construction under its stated supplier hypotheses. The point and its marked geometric model, initial/exported data and two-pass comparison are still required for existence on every host. To apply this proof to a family chosen independently, its members must lie in that construction with the same norm/list and coefficient requests or admit a separate comparison of the glued bottom lattice. The result leaves the coefficient and divisor-height bounds themselves to the period argument. [Scope of common design, B8.04 item 4][design]

## Which toric inputs enter the field and height proofs

For a positive critical integer on a level row, the two toric sums have the same infinity type. After unfolding, their ratio is the quotient of two values with the same factor $C_0/\Omega^M$. Clause 3 of B1.14k places both normalized values in $\overline{\mathbb Q}$ for a single period chosen before the twist. When the reference value is nonzero the quotient is algebraic. This suffices for the toric part of the field $N_1$. At a fitted square the two specified prime twists also meet clause 1. It gives algebraic $\beta(x),\beta(y)$, so $(y/x)^{12}=\beta(y)/\beta(x)$ gives the ratios required for $N_2$. The two bottom norms entering $N_2$ still come from the joint bottom witness described above. [Positive critical ratios in B8.17][positive] · [Prime twists of the fitted square][square]

B1.15a supplies all five toric assertions under the elliptic main theorem of complex multiplication, the Hilbert class field theorem for $j$, Siegel–Klingen rationality and Deligne–Ribet integrality, and the partial-zeta and Hecke–Tate functional equations. Its proof also identifies which estimates need more than algebraicity. [Statement of B1.15a][toricsupply]

| Clause of B1.14k | Proof and use |
|---|---|
| 1. Denominators of class terms and Picard-twisted sums | The CM derivative estimates in B1.14c, the class-order identity at representatives that are units over $\ell$, and the ultrametric inequality give the explicit denominator bound for $\beta$. B8.21a applies this bound at every finite prime for the positive-ratio height. [Proof of the bound][denom] · [Application on the fitted host][finheight] |
| 2. Ordinary transport and ideal classes | Elliptic CM and the Hilbert class field of $j$ transport the sum and count the embeddings above each ideal class. B8.21a uses this count with Chowla–Selberg to bound the archimedean contribution. Its use on the selected host still requires $\lvert\Re(L'(1,\chi_d)/L(1,\chi_d))\rvert\le x^\varepsilon$. [Ordinary transport][ordinary] · [Archimedean height][archheight] |
| 3. Twisted transport with common periods | Transport of the marked elliptic lattices fixes the two period scales before the CM extension, weights and Picard twist. Translation by the finite idèle permutes the order classes. Common-period algebraicity already suffices for the field choice above. [CM and period scales][cmperiod] · [Summation over classes][ordinary] |
| 4. Partial-zeta denominators | Deligne–Ribet applies to the class indicator with the Euler factors at $\ell$ removed. These factors have integral inverses on the class functions. The prime-by-prime minimum then gives the summed bound on $\sum_\ell e_\ell\log\ell$ used alongside clause 1 in the finite height. [Deligne–Ribet, original paper](https://math.berkeley.edu/~ribet/Articles/deligne-ribet.pdf) · [Derivation in B1.15a][classdenom] · [Finite height][finheight] |
| 5. Reflection on the grid | The local epsilon-factor quotient is a unit away from the twist conductor. At newly ramified places its Gauss sum is a unit away from the residue prime, and the reference uniformizer factors cancel in conjugate pairs. Cross-multiplying the two Hecke–Tate equations gives the reflection identity under the four twist/weight conditions and the coefficient-field condition in B1.14k(5). Dividing the identity later requires nonzero values. [Local calculation][refproof] · [Original cross-multiplied proof][refnative] |

B8.21a requires the fitted sizes and the field and conductor comparisons at the given host. The uniform height and the realization of these hypotheses on the selected member are separate steps.
