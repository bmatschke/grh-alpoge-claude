Reciprocity and the split-CM injection
------------------------------------

CIP1 uses the correct inverse arithmetic normalization.  Its open-map
argument is valid: the idele group is locally compact and sigma-compact,
and the compact Hausdorff target is a Baire space.  Images of countably
many translates of a compact neighborhood cover the target; one image
has interior, giving an identity neighborhood inside the image of the
chosen open set.  The inertia and inverse-Frobenius clauses are checked
in both directions; decomposition images are closed because they are
compact.  Thus the exact five-field source predicate is obtained.

CIP2's uniqueness proof has the required scope.  It compares finite
continuous quotients, first kills the full product of outside-S units,
then uses the same inverse-Frobenius element and the reverse direction
of the source frob clause.  Weak approximation handles the finitely
many remaining places.  For CM fields all infinite ideles are in the
connected component, as used in the proof.  No extra Chebotarev or
global-map uniqueness assertion is silently introduced.

CIP3 is the key new local-unit argument and is sound.  Projecting the
global kernel closure to finite ideles puts the supported unit in the
closure of E*.  Because the full finite-unit subgroup is open, it lies
in the closure of global units.  For a CM unit e, e/c(e) is an algebraic
integer unit with every conjugate of modulus one; bounded coefficients
of the minimal polynomials of its powers prove it is a root of unity.
The corresponding root-of-unity group of the fixed number field is
finite.  The map y -> y/c(y) is continuous on finite units.  An untouched
finite coordinate forces its diagonal root-of-unity value to be 1,
and w != c(w) then forces the original supported unit to be 1.  This
proves exactly the unit injection needed here, without a generic
Chevalley-units assertion.  HidaSetting.split supplies w != c(w) at
every required ell-adic place.

CIP4 uses this injection only after ord_eq has made the ratio of two
admissible Artin values a unit.  The universal glob field, together with
CIP2, therefore proves equality on the whole original Weil group.
The Hilbert-choice artOf is activated after nonemptiness is established.
For the factor field the usual Weil topology, with open inertia, may
be used to factor smooth characters; the literal source only asks for
continuity on inertia, which is explicitly verified.  There is no
need to identify the finer usual Weil topology with a subgroup
topology inherited from the profinite Galois group.

CIP5's sign check passes.  Arithmetic reciprocity acts on ell-power
roots at a rational local unit by its inverse.  The positive-integer
approximation argument, principal-idèle relation and away-from-ell
arithmetic Frobenius action give that inverse directly.  Passing to
geometric reciprocity therefore gives the positive local norm.
The source's continuous embeddings are exactly the Q_ell embeddings
of the same finite local field.  Consequently the product in IsWtPart
has the stated positive sign, and weight -1 is the correct convention.
The conclusion preserves the every-IsLocalArtin quantifier and does
not claim to produce separating ordinary arithmetic fibers.

Integral NiceI normalization
---------------------------

CIP6 correctly uses the integral invertible symmetric Point.P.
Over the odd-characteristic power-series DVR, a primitive vector
with unit self-pairing exists by reduction and polarization.  Its
orthogonal complement remains unimodular, giving integral
diagonalization.  A finite constant extension supplies the finitely
many constant square roots; coefficient recursion supplies the
power-series roots.  The resulting T and inverse are both integral.
Coefficientwise extension is injective, local and finite free,
preserves t, and preserves the exact Z_ell and O coefficient maps.

CIP7's density argument is valid in every characteristic.  The
finite-dimensional algebraic proof uses scalar endomorphisms of
an irreducible module and induction on independent vectors, with
the graph case contradicting linear independence.  It yields full
matrix span after the actual constant extension and conjugation.
Clearing finitely many Laurent-series denominators gives the
required integral t-power span.  For a=n the source Ev block is
indeed the full matrix space.

CIP8 proves the missing character continuity consequence rather
than assuming it: a rational flag diagonal entry is a finite
rational linear combination of continuous representation entries;
clearing denominators and reducing modulo every t-power gives a
closed kernel.  That kernel contains the closed commutator subgroup.
The ell-primary topological-abelianization torsion is then killed
because A has characteristic ell and no nilpotents.  Intersections
of the rational full flag with the integral lattice are saturated;
their successive quotients are free of rank one.  An adapted basis
gives the source's exact w^{-1} rho w convention and original
character order.  Injectivity preserves both N1 clauses.

CIP9 checks determinant equality as an entire polynomial law on
every coefficient algebra.  N3 survives by a Bezout identity.
For N4, finitely many coefficient functionals of the original
twisted-commutant equations span the full dual.  Their nonzero
determinant remains nonzero after field extension.  Conjugation
therefore gives the requested vanishing for the old F.D0.

CIP10 accounts for every NP field, including adic continuity,
normalized matrix-one form, full-block span, prescribed tame
subgroups, torsion, flags and N1.  CIP11 retains full determinant
matching, the ordered characters, constant-field structural maps,
the original ideal J and the unchanged point kernel.  The h=0
source branch uses only NiceI; it does not require the NiceE
normalization.  The proof works at n=4, ell=3 and introduces no
factorial or trace denominator.

CIP12 correctly leaves the original ordinary system, four Hida
endpoints, same-J all-level HasAut and the five remaining support
producers explicit.  The fixed iota precedes the common threshold;
that threshold precedes all later coefficient objects and spaces.
The two original Links retain their common kappa and distinct
auxiliary primes.  Ideals are matched within each original ring,
not identified across the two rings.
