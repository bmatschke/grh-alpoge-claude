# Rational short sections at the distinguished conductor

Fix a row and side as in the [first-pass rationality comparison](FIRST_PASS_SERIES_RATIONALITY.md). Put $M_0=g_0g_1$, $p_d=p_{\delta_{\ell_i}}$, $M_2=M_0p_d$ and let $L$ be the coefficient field constructed there. We may choose the auxiliary cover $C$ of the original moduli space in proving rationality of the base generators. We specify its marking below. There are two different level changes in the argument. Passing from the original level to $M_2$ retains the fixed character. To construct sections on $C$, use $C$ itself as the base of an identity cover. Its *relative* auxiliary conductor is $1$ and its full level is still $M_2$. We prove the arithmetic section construction at this level and identify its exact geometric normalization. [Upper marking, coefficient field and uniformization][cover] · [Identity cover on the given base][own]

## Sections with prescribed residue coefficients

Write $n$ for the definite source rank, $r$ for the target rank, $S=\mathcal O_E^n$ and $\mathcal Q$ for the $n$-tuples in the finite level module of $C$. Use the theta line, connection and marking on $C$. The identity cover has constant weight-zero factor $1$. A change of marking in its stabilizer induces the identity on the quotient defining $\mathcal Q$. Consequently each point mass $w_t(q)=\mathbf 1_{q=t}$ satisfies the descent condition. Its use need not be restricted to the single additive character of an adapted residue. [Identity data and residue permutation][own]

Let $\Lambda$ be an allowed unimodular lattice, $N$ an admissible odd integer with $M_2\mid N$ and $NS\subseteq I=\Lambda\cap S$. Take a harmonic polynomial $P$ with coefficients in $L$ and the prescribed weight. For residue $q$ denote by $J(q,u;P)$ the contraction of $P$ with the covariant jets of the translated theta germs at the enumerated isotropic torsion lift $u$. At a framed point the section for $w_t,P$, with the algebraic lattice normalization set to $1$, has value
$$
 \frac{1}{[I:NS]^r|\mathcal Q|\,N^{\deg P}}
       \sum_{q\in\mathcal Q}\mathbf 1_{q=t}\sum_u J(q,u;P).
$$
To use the earlier formula with normalization $\beta_H(\Lambda)$, multiply its section by $\beta_H(\Lambda)\in L^\times$. This gives exactly the displayed value over the upper coefficient field. The integers in the denominator are invertible in $L$.

Indeed, over a faithfully flat extension of the test algebra one may choose all division points and theta-group lifts at once. Changing a division point permutes the full inner sum. The commutator on the enumerated subgroup is trivial by isotropy. The product of the lift multipliers has square one and odd order dividing $N^2$, so it is one. Changing a formal exponential frame contributes only the mixed contractions killed by harmonicity. Thus the two pullbacks of the displayed value agree, and faithfully flat descent gives its unique value on the original algebra. Weight covariance gives a section of the required bundle. At an isotropic boundary the Fourier terms which could survive in the constant term have deficient rank in the relevant block. Positivity of the last weight supplies a full-height minor of that block in every highest-weight term, which makes those coefficients zero. This condition is checked at each good embedding. If there is no rational isotropic line there is no such boundary condition. We obtain an $L$-rational cusp section. For any finite coefficient table $a(t)$ in $L$ take the sum of the point-mass sections weighted by $a(t)$. The same proof allows a different harmonic polynomial of the given weight at each $t$. ## Phase of the section reading

Choose a finite-adelic argument on a reached component of $C$. Use the symplectic marking from its uniformization and the lift of that marking to the identity cover. Relative to the same standard vacuum, write $\kappa'$ for the scalar of the tensor of theta generators at that frame. The analytic torus calculation of the section above gives its residue contribution with factor
$$
 \frac{z_\Lambda}{m_\Lambda|\mathcal Q|}\,\kappa',
 \qquad m_\Lambda=[I:NS]^r,\qquad z_\Lambda=[\Lambda:NS]^r .
$$
The factor $N^{\deg P}$ in the arithmetic formula cancels the dilation of the derivative on the torus. The finite Abelian character sum in the normalized kernel uses the same marking, torsion points and polynomial. The vacuum comparison on this component evaluates its generator by the same $\kappa'$, with the component multiplier $cc$ and the determinant multiplier $\mu(g)$. The dominant normalization of the good cover identifies $cc$ on each reached component with the pinned $C.cc_2$ times the single reference vacuum phase. This equality follows by transporting the reference scalar and using uniqueness of the dominant normalization on dictionary components. [Torus calculation with its scalar][torus] · [Vacuum comparison at the common frame][kernel] · [Normalization on reached components][phase]

Put $D_\Lambda=[\Lambda:I]$. Index multiplicativity gives $z_\Lambda/m_\Lambda=D_\Lambda^r$. Thus after the geometric and adelic factors are put on the same side, the class multiplier is $\chi_W(y)^{-1}D_\Lambda^{-r/2}$, where $y$ is the determinant idele specified by the split translate. The remaining factors are the harmonic sign $(-1)^{\deg P}$, the prescribed Lie-algebra factor, $cc$ and $\mu(g)$. This computation only cancels nonzero numerical factors. It gives the same identity at a zero of the section. In particular the component comparison for these residue terms is exact under the given dictionary and dominant vacuum pins. [Index and scalar calculation][cancel] · [Putting the residue terms together][sum]

## From the given finite test to these residues

The given flat test $\Phi$ lives on the additive half-lattice selected by the definite type and the fixed character. Its support and period descend to a finite additive quotient $A$ killed by $M_2$. The coefficient field of the full level contains the required $M_2$th roots of unity. Choose an identification of the character group of $A$ with the character functions from the opposite half by the self-dual Heisenberg pairing. Equivalently one may index those characters with repetitions by the lattice generators used in the source. For each $\lambda\in\widehat A$ put
$$
 a_\lambda=|A|^{-1}\sum_{x\in A}\Phi(x)\lambda(-x).
$$
These coefficients belong to $L$. Orthogonality gives $\Phi(y)=\sum_\lambda a_\lambda\lambda(y)$. Acting on the vacuum, this expresses the level test as an $L$-linear combination of finite Heisenberg translates in **its own** polarization. In the redundant parametrization the normalizing denominator is $M_2^b$ for $b$ lattice generators. The two expansions agree after collecting equal characters. This establishes the passage from the whole finite table to individual translates on this half, including when some values vanish. [Pairing and roots of unity][four1] · [Orthogonality with the source's denominator and vacuum][four2]

For the base generators the variable conductor is empty. The fixed finite test is on the half specified by $\overline{P_g}\cup D_\delta$. Here $P_g$ consists of the primes fixed by the old geometric marking and $D_\delta$ consists of the primes selected for the distinguished character. Hence its Fourier translates use $P_g\cup\overline{D_\delta}$. Above $p_d$ the selector chooses one prime of $E$ in each conjugate pair, and all its choices lie above a single prime $\mathfrak p$ of the distinguished imaginary quadratic field. It follows that it selects every prime above $\mathfrak p$. Indeed conjugation takes the other members to primes above $\bar{\mathfrak p}$. [Actual finite test][halfdatum] · [One prime in each conjugate pair][halfsel] · [Common contraction to the quadratic field][halfpair]

Choose the auxiliary half ideal $I_d=\bar{\mathfrak p}\mathcal O_E$. Then $I_d\bar I_d=p_d\mathcal O_E$ and $I_d+\bar I_d=\mathcal O_E$. At this good split prime the two quadratic factors correspond to the two distinct roots of the polynomial of $\omega_0$ modulo $p_d$. In particular $I_d=(p_d,\omega_0-a)\mathcal O_E$ for the root $a$ corresponding to $\bar{\mathfrak p}$. Both generators belong to $\mathbf Z[\omega_0]$, as required for the higher marking. The cover construction accepts this ideal with the same field $L$, root of unity and ray condition. The old half ideal has support $P_g$. Its product with $I_d$ therefore selects exactly the translating half at every prime of $M_2$. Every Fourier translate above is now a marked level point on $C$. A single such point contributes $|\mathcal Q|$ times its indicator to the normalized residue mean, cancelling the factor $1/|\mathcal Q|$ in that mean. [Old marking][halfold] · [Cover with the chosen ideal][halfnew] · [Field and ray conditions][halfray] · [Level points and their exact weights][levelpoint]

For a permitted source argument $h$, choose an auxiliary rational prime $p$ away from $2M_2$ and all primes below its finite split support. Such an $h$ is standard away from its split support, which already avoids the primes over $2M_2$. The conjugate of the lattice moved by $h$ is unimodular and standard at $M_2$. Clearing its remaining finite denominators gives an allowed odd $N$ divisible by $M_2$ and prime to $p$, with $NS\subseteq\Lambda\cap S$. Thus the level points just found have the arithmetic sections of the first part on this same cover. [Split lattice and denominator][splitlat]

It remains to place the moved-lattice multiplier itself in $L$. Let $\mathfrak c$ be the integral ideal formed from the positive determinant valuations of $h$ on the split pairs. At a pair the determinant valuations are opposite, and the reference splitting character is unramified there. Its restriction used in the determinant calculation gives $\chi_W(y)=\chi(\mathfrak c)$. The local elementary divisors on opposite sides of a unimodular lattice are negatives of each other, up to reordering. If the exponents on one side are $e_i$, their contribution to the index has exponent $\sum_i |e_i|$, whereas the ideal norm has exponent $|\sum_i e_i|$. The difference is a nonnegative even integer. Multiplication over split pairs gives $D_\Lambda=(N\mathfrak c)k^2$ for a positive integer $k$. The half-normalized unramified values $\chi_v(\varpi_v)\sqrt{Nv}$ are in $L$ by the small-field condition for the reference character. For $r=2t+1$ this gives
$$
 \chi_W(y)^{-1}D_\Lambda^{-r/2}
 =(\chi(\mathfrak c)\sqrt{N\mathfrak c})^{-1}
     (N\mathfrak c)^{-t}k^{-(2t+1)}\in L .
$$
All square roots here are the positive real ones at the chosen embedding. [Index and determinant ideal][classidx] · [Arithmetic of the class factor][classarith] · [Earlier one-class formula](continuation_v74/join.tex#L3-L47)

Consequently each base generator with a small-field harmonic polynomial has a short-section expansion on $C$ with the common geometric phase $(\alpha\beta)^{-1}$ of the [rationality comparison](FIRST_PASS_SERIES_RATIONALITY.md). Linearity extends the calculation on the row by scalar extension from the small-field harmonic model. To restore the distinguished twist, multiply by $d_V(h)^{-1}d_W(g)$. Its source value is in $L$ and its target value is a rational component function on $C$. The resulting section has the required type at the marked level. Invariance under lifts of the identity there gives descent to the original cusp row under the standing rank bound. The coefficients lie in $L$, which is contained in the prescribed embedded local field $K$. Summing over the definite classes with their rational stabilizer weights therefore puts their sums in the target space with this geometric normalization. This argument uses the freedom to choose the auxiliary marking for the empty-variable-conductor generators. A universal assertion for an arbitrary prescribed cover or for nontrivial variable twists has additional changes of half. [Small-field harmonic generators and descent][baseread] · [General changes of half][two]

## Comparing with the installed row normalization

The row constructed with the theta map includes a scale $\nu$. Its rationality condition is conditional on the existence of a scale fulfilling the full row rationality predicate, including the nonempty twists and the universal coefficients. Proving the base-generator assertion alone does not supply this premise. The exponent in the power condition on an admissible scale is existential. Knowing that a ratio is a root of unity gives no bound here on its order relative to the residue characteristic. To use the calculation just made for the stored row it suffices to compare its scale with the geometric one up to $K^\times$, or to check directly that the tested class sums belong to that reading. A zero test passes in every normalization. On a nonzero family the scalars defining the two rational subspaces matter. [Condition on the installed scale][stored] · [Use of row rationality at a place][storedplace]

If constructing this row afresh, there is a more flexible sufficient condition. Exhibit one geometric scale $\nu_g$ for the whole triple $(i,j,b)$ that reads all of its base tests and satisfies the original power condition
$$
 \nu_g^{\,n}=(\operatorname{cj}(\mathrm{phs}_j)c)^n,\qquad n>0,
$$
for an allowed coefficient scale $c$. Here $\operatorname{cj}$ is the conjugation specified by the reference embedding. When the full rationality predicate has a witness, take that witness as the row scale. It reads all the base tests by definition. Otherwise take $\nu_g$. The full-rationality implication in the scale specification is then vacuous and its power condition holds by hypothesis. The series construction chooses one scale independently at each triple before defining the theta maps. Their Hecke comparison is homogeneous and the moduli-point step retains the resulting reading. Thus the proposed choice would suffice for a newly constructed first pass. The [reference-phase proof](FIRST_PASS_REFERENCE_PHASE.md) checks the displayed identity after a small-field adjustment of the geometric normalization. [Scale quantifiers and original choice][choices] · [Building the series with the selected scales][install]

If the original cusp space is zero, the geometric expansion makes every tested base series zero. This proves membership for any installed scale in this case. On a nonzero cusp row the power test for a fresh choice can be reduced further. Let $\phi_0$ be the geometric phase in the convention where the candidate reading scale is $\phi_0c$, and set $u_0=\operatorname{cj}(\mathrm{phs}_j)$. Suppose that one **untwisted reference** series $F$ is nonzero at $g_0$, that $\phi_0 F$ has an expansion in rational cusp sections over the fixed small field $L_0$, and that the reference conjugation law holds with a single root of unity $\zeta$ for $\zeta u_0 F$. These are tests at the reference tuple $\mu_0$, with rational polynomial coefficients. Put $a=\zeta u_0/\phi_0$. For any coefficient automorphism $\sigma$ fixing $L_0$, conjugation of the expansion fixes its coefficients. Comparing the two expansions of the reference lift gives
$$
 a\,(\phi_0F)(g_0)=\sigma(a)\,(\phi_0F)(g_0).
$$
Nonvanishing yields $a=\sigma(a)$, hence $a\in L_0^\times$ by the fixed-field criterion in $\mathbf C$. Multiplication of the reading scale by $a$ preserves its image over $K$. The adjusted scale $a\phi_0c=\zeta u_0c$ fulfills the power condition with the order of $\zeta$. This reduces the fresh-choice issue to the indicated reference expansion and conjugation on a nonzero series. The [nonzero reference calculation](FIRST_PASS_REFERENCE_PHASE.md) supplies these tests on the original tuple and retains the support of its source representative. [Fixed-field test at one nonzero value][fix] · [Reference tuple, power and choice of scale][fixnorm] · [Zero cusp space][zero]

For the comparison across coefficient embeddings there is a further finite test. With the same active tuple on both sides, identify the definite classes by the identity and use their common normal cover. For a coefficient automorphism $\sigma$, suppose the weight-zero cover section transforms by $a$, the finite Fourier tables for every class by a common $b$, and the class factors and volume have their specified fixed values. Conjugating the algebraic section formula term by term transforms the normalized class section by $(ab)^{-1}\operatorname{gal}_\sigma$. Its stabilizer weights are rational. Writing $\gamma$ for the analytic normalization root in the conjugation formula, it contributes $\gamma/(\sigma(\gamma)ab)$ in the comparison of expansions, with the extra determinant-character value for a flat translation. This gives a test on the cover section and finite tables independent of the projection used to install the theta map. The finite Fourier tables still contain the distinguished character. Checking their common factor and the transformation of the normalized cover section on these tests is needed for the active-row comparison across embeddings.
