# The theta image and good points on a first-pass row

Fix a fitted first-pass row $1\le j\le m$, an active coefficient place $w$, and its tuple $t$. Write $V=M_j^{\mathrm t}(\mu(w))$ for the complex definite row, $W=M_j$ for its indefinite partner, $\Theta_P:V\to W$ for the prescribed map and $\Theta_T=\Theta_P^*$. All local indices considered below belong to the same fibre $I_{j,w}$ and hence use these same two spaces. Begin with the prescribed rational readings, the primitive and level identities of the fitted state and the actual definite class-sum identification. In the common eventual range, impose the [finite request for the pointwise comparison](FIRST_PASS_POINT_COMPARISON.md) together with the [integral-model request](FIRST_PASS_GALOIS_COEFFICIENT_MODELS.md). The resulting assertion is that the image of the adjoint equals the sum of the full good isotypic spaces on this fibre. [First-pass readings][active] · [Local class sums on that state][actclass]

## Rank and orthogonality before labelling

Let $J_V,J_W$ be the row frames, $p$ the rational matrix called lPrj, $s$ the corresponding inclusion called lZ0, $B$ the rational matrix of the lift between the cut coordinates and the indefinite coordinates, and $e=e^*$ the finite-type operator on $V$. If $E$ represents $e$ in the definite frame, the fitted reading and the finite-level block identities give
$$
 \Theta_P J_V(x)=J_W(Bpx),\qquad ps=1,\qquad pE=p.
$$
Indeed $p$ splits the orthogonal inclusion $s$, and the central type idempotent acts as the identity on the image of this inclusion. The primitive base-level norm, lift and rationality identities give that $B$ is bijective. The $J_W(e_a)$ form a complex basis by the fixed-level realization and spanning of the chosen forms. Each $J_W(e_a)$ therefore has the explicit preimage $J_V(sB^{-1}e_a)$. The columns of $J_V$ span $V$, so $pE=p$ also proves $\Theta_Pe=\Theta_P$. Taking adjoints now gives
$$
 e\Theta_T=\Theta_T,\qquad Z_0=(\ker\Theta_P)^\perp\cap\operatorname{im}e
       =\operatorname{im}\Theta_T,\qquad
 \dim_\mathbf C Z_0=\dim_\mathbf C W=d_W.
$$
The last equality uses surjectivity of $\Theta_P$, hence injectivity of its adjoint. These identities already hold on the original row, independently of either of the two multiplicity propositions and of the later congruence lengths. They supply nonvanishing on every nonzero source subspace. [Original cut][original] · [Right inverse for $p$][right] · [Bijectivity and original rational reading][bij] · [Basis on $W$][frame] · [Finite idempotent and adjoint][cut]

For the orthogonality of the definite finite-adelic isotypic spaces $V_P$, write $G$ for the definite unitary group and $\beta$ for the positive invariant coefficient form. Choose a common compact open level $H$ fixing the two smooth forms $f,g$, and set
$$
 {\cal B}(f,g)=\operatorname{vol}(H)
   \sum_{t\in G(F)\backslash G(\mathbb A_f)/H}
        \frac{\beta(f(t),g(t))}{|\Gamma_t(H)|},
 \qquad \Gamma_t(H)=G(F)\cap tHt^{-1}.
$$
There are finitely many classes and each stabilizer is finite. On refinement to $H'\subset H$, $\Gamma_t(H)$ acts on $H/H'$ by conjugating into $H$. The sum of inverse stabilizer orders on the resulting orbits is $[H:H']/|\Gamma_t(H)|$. Multiplication by $\operatorname{vol}(H')$ recovers the original term. Simultaneous right translation conjugates the level and preserves its Haar volume. Thus ${\cal B}$ is an invariant positive inner product, and the given row inner product is a positive multiple of its restriction. For a character level use a principal open subgroup in the kernel of the character; the common character has modulus one. Orthogonal projection onto an irreducible summand intertwines translations. On a fixed compact open level this projection acts between finite dimensional fixed spaces, so the projected smooth vectors still belong to the corresponding smooth summand. Its restriction to an inequivalent irreducible summand vanishes by irreducibility. Taking sums of copies gives $V_P\perp V_Q$ for distinct $P,Q$, allowing multiplicities within each $V_P$. This is the full assertion of the pinned theorem whose name mentions a Gram class and a vacuum orbit. [Invariant form and normalization][sum] · [Application to the actual isotypic spaces][orth] · [Smooth projection argument][type] · [Gross, *Algebraic modular forms*](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02780173)

## From a nonzero pairing to a labelled point

Assume the normalized integral split-Hecke realization, its compatible coefficient embeddings, the finite-adelic isotypic realization, and the source decomposition $W=\bigoplus_\sigma W_\sigma$. A nonzero $V_P$ receives a point at some index of the same fibre. Choose $0\ne y\in V_P$. Each defining split spherical generator acts on the whole type as a scalar $\kappa_{v,k}$. Its matrix on the given split coefficient realization has characteristic polynomial with roots in the coefficient DVR $O$, giving $\kappa_{v,k}\in O$ under the fixed embedding. The nonzero joint kernel of the equations $T_{v,k}-\kappa_{v,k}$ descends to $\operatorname{Frac}(O)$. Indeed, in a finite dimensional ambient space a finite subfamily already cuts out the common kernel, and kernel dimensions of that finite matrix are preserved by field extension. Acting on a nonzero joint vector defines an $O$-valued character of the generated integral algebra. Its reduction specifies one maximal ideal. The normalized cover lists that ideal at a unique index $i'$, and the character with its nonzero eigenspace specifies $x'\in X_{i'}$. Transport the same finite-adelic isotypic space to $i'$; its eigenvalues under every defining generator are precisely those of $x'$. This is the condition that the transported type is pointed at $x'$. The source lift hypothesis in the pinned proof enters only when transporting its nonzero image and containment, after the index and character have been constructed. Distinct finite-adelic types can a priori share the same character. [Integral scalars and joint descent][p1] · [Block, character and transport][p2]

For the forward theta inclusion it suffices to supply the following pairing and parameter inputs on the actual source types. Whenever the pairing between $\Theta_T(W_\sigma)$ and $V_P$ is nonzero, the split component of $P$ is the local theta lift of $\sigma$, with the pinned character twist. With existence and split determination of base change and the determination of a target type by its local components on the admitted levels, two such $P$'s for the same $\sigma$ must coincide. Indeed their split parameters agree, hence their base-change lists agree up to permutation. Their split parameters and their nonsplit unramified base-change parameters then agree everywhere, and the stated determination property identifies the two target types. [Exact local and global determination hypotheses][hdef] · [Uniqueness for a pairing][hc] · [Realization of these premises from the paired split readings][co]

For $0\ne f\in W_\sigma$, injectivity gives $\Theta_T f\ne0$. At least one orthogonal projection onto a target $V_P$ is nonzero because the full isotypic spaces span $V$. Its pairing with $\Theta_T f$ is its own positive squared norm. By the preceding uniqueness all $Q\ne P$ have zero pairing with the lift of every vector in $W_\sigma$, so $\Theta_T(W_\sigma)\subset V_P$. This proves the whole-space incidence called thetaOf. It does not identify the dimension of that target type. [Isotypic projection and incidence][howe] · [Definition of incidence][mode]

Now assume the matching parameter $\Sigma$ of the source and the base-change formula for its target $P$ in the occurrence proposition. Precisely, the target list is the twist by $\eta_T^{-1}$ of $\Sigma$ with the single prescribed part $\mu_i$ appended. The preceding character construction preserves this containment on transport. At its pointed index $i'$ one may equivalently apply the parameter clause there to the transported lift. The appended part witnesses membership in $Z_N$. For a good source label the split parameters at the marked places agree with the labels of $\Sigma$, hence $\Sigma$ meets the two goodness conditions and $x'\in Z^{\rm good}_{i'}$. The split polynomial comparison also supplies the residual matching condition AtM there. On the standing large-host selection, source goodness is deduced using the marked vector fixed by the selected depth-zero projector and the Green and Zelevinsky inputs. The pointwise comparison supplies these parameter premises on the common coefficient request. ## Equality on full isotypic spaces

Let $U_{\mathrm{good}}$ be the span in $V$ of the full $V_P$'s pointed at good points somewhere on this fibre, transporting the isotypic spaces to the common row. The decomposition of $W$ and the preceding incidence give
$$
             Z_0=\operatorname{im}\Theta_T\subset U_{\mathrm{good}}.
$$
This direction uses no reduced multiplicity statement. The reverse inclusion is now equivalent to the single numerical equality
$$
                    \dim_\mathbf C U_{\mathrm{good}}=d_W .
$$
It also suffices to prove the upper bound $\dim U_{\mathrm{good}}\le d_W$. The pinned route obtains the reverse inclusion from the middle clause of ConclusionZ: at each index with a good point, the sum of the full good target eigenspaces equals the image of the good source part. Every type in that sum lies in $\operatorname{im}\Theta_T$. Its other two clauses are unnecessary for this projector comparison. The source provides a route to the middle clause from the parameter-wise upward and downward compatibility, local nonvanishing and identification of the good source part. A single occurrence in a target eigenspace of multiplicity greater than one only proves that a subspace there has been hit. [Meaning of eigC and goodPartM][mode] · [Reduced multiplicity clauses][cz] · [Transport of the good types][one] · [Specified reverse-direction inputs][reverse] · [Row conditions][rows-c]

There is a second way to prove the converse that isolates the required fixed-vector nonvanishing. For each target type $P$, let $U_P\subset V_P$ be the sum of the images of the source types assigned to $P$. The proven single-type incidence and the source decomposition give $Z_0=\bigoplus_P U_P$. These summands are orthogonal because the enclosing $V_P$'s are. For any $F\in V_P$ we have
$$
  F\perp U_P
   \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad F\perp\operatorname{im}\Theta_T
   \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\Theta_P F=0 .
$$
Consequently $U_P=V_P$ exactly when $\Theta_P|_{V_P}$ is injective. It suffices to check this on the types pointed at good characters. At such a point the condition called TypeNZRec asserts injectivity on the sum of all the corresponding types, so it suffices on its own after the forward incidence. The other parameter-wise upward, downward and source-part equalities used to produce ConclusionZ can be omitted in this argument. For a matrix $C_P$ giving a basis of $V_P$ in the complexified definite frame, the test is precisely $\operatorname{rank}(pC_P)=\dim V_P$, since $B$ is invertible and the indefinite frame is independent. For a line this requires just one nonzero column $pC_P$. This test applies to each good type even when several occur over one character. [Injectivity on the labelled eigenspace][nz] · [Stronger parameter-wise approach][dim]

The pinned transfer of TypeNZRec starts from a good eigenvector at the fixed level. The row reading realizes it as an automorphic vector $y$. Injectivity of the level evaluation and the theta reading carry $\Theta_P F=0$ to vanishing of the prescribed projected theta function of $y$. The two additional conditions EigString and EigGood identify its required split eigenvalue string and its good-vector property. The input called TypeNZ on automorphic vectors then forces $y=0$. Its proof separates the analytic issue of a nonzero theta lift for some test from the issue of detection by the prescribed test: the good-vector identity restores the unprojected fixed-test value, archimedean generation extends its vanishing to tests satisfying the finite-vacuum relations, and finite generation together with the excluded level characters forces every lift of this vector to vanish. This contradicts the separate nonvanishing input for the eigenvalue string. One upstream proof uses doubling, a regularized first-term formula and control of the partial Euler factor. The [lower-row assembly](LOWER_ROW_ASSEMBLY.md) already uses a second route from the full lower-row laws and the given theta datum, together with local and unramified doubling, unramified Howe, Hecke continuation and cuspidal decay. That route supplies the automorphic nonvanishing used here. These generation and good-string hypotheses enter the [linked point conditions on the fitted realization](FIRST_PASS_POINT_COMPARISON.md). [Transport on the actual row][nzcast] · [Evaluation and detection][nzinj] · [Precise test classes and good vectors][nzconditions] · [Five-input implication][nzproof] · [Production from doubling and generation][nzglobal] · [Gan–Qiu–Takeda on global nonvanishing](https://arxiv.org/abs/1207.4709)

For example, suppose the **whole row-place fibre** has two distinct simple joint characters and each eigenspace is a line, with exactly one good. The good-character space has dimension one: every good pointed type is in its corresponding eigenline, and the good point has a type by the definition of $Z_N$. If $d_W>0$, the preceding rank and containment force $d_W=1$ and equality. If $d_W=0$, the image is zero and any realized good target would require its own exclusion. With extra blocks on the same fibre, the argument can be applied blockwise only after a separate comparison of the corresponding projected theta ranks. Once $Z_0=U_{\mathrm{good}}$, distinct isotypic spaces are orthogonal and type labels cannot give opposite signs at two indices: the separating split Hecke generator would act on the same nonzero type with two eigenvalues differing by a unit. This proves the asserted isotypic dichotomy and its pointed signs. The [projection onto the local Hecke blocks](FIRST_PASS_TYPE_PROJECTION.md) carries this oriented decomposition to the rational eigenspaces and gives the integral length formula.
