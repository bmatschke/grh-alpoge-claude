# The two flag-pair determinant cutoffs on the represented labels

Fix an original tied row, with base flag $F$, a nonsingular moving flag $F'$ and cross Gram matrix $Z$. Use $T=T_u$, $T'=T_{u'}$, $N=T^f$, $g=g_0g_1$, $r=r_S$, $m=2r+2$, and $\rho=\mathrm{rd}(E)$. The two actual arithmetic cutoffs can be evaluated using only their reduced Gram labels. In particular the putative singular denominator in the *degenerate counting cutoff* cancels exactly at every archimedean place. This yields the finite-fibre bound below for both classes in B8.10ac. The real integral still has its own angular normalization.

## Gram product of the actual row lattice

Let $p_\ell$ denote the leading $\ell$-row minor of $Z$, with $p_0=1$. Put $a_\ell=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}(r+1-i)$ and $a=a_r=a_{r+1}=r(r+1)/2$. At each of the $f$ CM-type embeddings put
$$
 x_\tau=\rho^{-2a}|\tau p_{r+1}|,\qquad
 y_\tau=\rho^{-2a}|\tau p_r|,\qquad t=\sqrt{TT'},\qquad
 J_Z=\prod_\tau(x_\tau+t y_\tau).
$$
The normalized Gram product is
$$
 G_Z=g^{-mf}\prod_\tau(x_\tau^2-t^2y_\tau^2)
     =g^{-mf}\det U,                                            \tag{1}
$$
where $U$ is the integral lattice generated by the two isotropic lattices and the two last vectors in $\Lambda$. Here $\det U$ is the positive square root of the order of its Hermitian discriminant module, also for the nondegenerate indefinite span.

Indeed in the ordered, unscaled frame bases the Gram matrix has off-diagonal blocks $Z,Z^*$ and diagonal blocks supported just on their last entries, $-T,-T'$. In its determinant a nonzero term chooses either both distinguished diagonal entries or neither, by the equal sizes of the off-diagonal blocks. This gives
$$
 (-1)^{r+1}\det \operatorname{Gram}
       =|p_{r+1}|^2-TT'|p_r|^2
$$
at each embedding. The right side is strictly positive. The span is nonsingular by the term condition. Its negative index is at least $r+1$ because it contains the $r$-dimensional isotropic span together with its orthogonal vector of negative norm. Projection from that nonpositive subspace to a maximal negative subspace in the nonsingular span is injective. The ambient negative index is $r+1$. This fixes the sign at every place, even when $p_r=0$.

For each flag the coefficient ideals for its isotropic basis vectors, in order, are $\mathfrak d^{-r},\ldots,\mathfrak d^{-1}$ and the last vector has coefficient ring $\mathcal O_E$. These are imposed by the original frame and row conditions. The generated sum is direct as a sum of coefficient ideals since this full Gram determinant is nonzero. For any Hermitian lattice in a rational pseudo-basis with coefficient ideals $I_i$, its determinant is $|\mathrm N_{E/\mathbb Q}\det\operatorname{Gram}|^{1/2}\prod_i\mathrm NI_i$. This follows by dualizing locally, then taking norms of the discriminant ideal. Here $\mathfrak d$ is the extended different of $F$, with norm $\rho^{2f}$ by finite unramifiedness of $E/F$. The two flags thus contribute $\rho^{-4af}$. Finally the native $G_0$ divides this determinant by $\mathrm N(g\mathcal O_E)^{r+1}=g^{mf}$. This proves (1) with the exact row normalization. [Flag norms and minor weights][minors] · [Two integral frame conditions][frame] · [Lattice Gram product][lat] · [Reduced label definition][label] · [Different norm on this host][norm]

This supplies the two arithmetical lower bounds on a represented label as well. Its product $\prod_\tau(x_\tau^2-t^2y_\tau^2)=\det U$ is at least one by integrality. Each cross pairing at positions $i,j$ belongs to $\mathfrak d^{w_i+w_j}$, where $(w_i)=(r,\ldots,1,0)$. The ideal of a nonzero leading minor is therefore divisible by $\mathfrak d^{2a_\ell}$. Its normalized squared product is the absolute norm of the integral ideal $(p_\ell)\mathfrak d^{-2a_\ell}$ and is at least one. These assertions require the original integral frame conditions. Integral triangular changes preserve the leading minors used here. [Norm input to the original row][lower]

## The congruence ranks in the determinant

Write $A_i$ for the level lattice of the $i$-th moving flag in its definite perpendicular $H_i$, and $I_i$ for the index of its theta lattice in $A_i$. Write $U_i$ for the integral two-frame lattice above and $\mathcal B_i=U_i^{\rm sat}/U_i$ for its saturation quotient in $\Lambda$. The single-flag bound is
$$
 \det A_i\,I_i^2\le G_i.
$$
To see the determinant, unimodular gluing gives the same discriminant order for $U_i^{\rm sat}$ and its primitive perpendicular, the theta lattice. Hence $\det A_i\,I_i=\det U_i/|\mathcal B_i|$. For the index, the two lifts of a level vector differ by a vector whose class in $\mathcal B_i$ lies in $g\mathcal B_i$; its kernel is exactly the theta lattice. The fixed level conditions give $|\mathcal B_i[g]|\ge \mathrm N(g\mathcal O_E)^{r+1}$ by reducing the two marked frames locally at $g$. Thus $I_i\le|g\mathcal B_i|\le|\mathcal B_i|/g^{mf}$. Combining with (1) proves the displayed inequality. [Difference map][quot] · [Local level order and determinant][glue]

Let $\Theta_i$ be the two projections of the common pair space and $M$ the pair lattice. The isometry identifying the two realizations of $M$ identifies their Hermitian determinants. Orthogonal projection off $\Theta_i$ gives lattices $P_i$ of rank $s-1$ with
$$
 \(\det M)^2=\frac{\det A_1\,\det A_2}{\det P_1\,\det P_2}.        \tag{2}
$$
Indeed in each exact projection sequence the determinant of the original lattice is the product of the determinant of its kernel and that of its projected lattice, by orthogonal decomposition of volume. The pair-space identification uses the compatible marked lifts on both sides. [Exact projection identity][projection]

The form $b_\beta(v,w)=h(v,w)-h(\beta v,w)$ vanishes on the common spaces and takes values in $g\mathcal O_E$ on the two level lattices. To check divisibility one computes the two pairings on integral representatives in the base and moving flag perpendiculars. The level marking identifies the representatives up to $g\Lambda$ and the respective integral isotropic spans. Terms involving a perpendicular and its isotropic span vanish. Expanding the difference, the mixed isotropic terms cancel, leaving $g$ times integral pairings. At every embedding Cauchy–Schwarz for the two definite quotient metrics gives $|b_\beta(v,w)|\le2\|v\|\|w\|$. If the induced pairing is nonsingular of rank $e$, these two facts on the projected lattices give
$$
 \det P_1\,\det P_2\ge(g/2)^{2ef}.                              \tag{3}
$$
In fact $b_\beta/g$ maps the first lattice injectively into the Hermitian dual of the second. The real determinant of this map on the product of the $f$ complex spaces is at most $(2/g)^{2ef}$. Comparison of additive covolumes with the dual lattice proves (3), with the common field covolume normalization cancelling. In the nondegenerate class $e=s-1$. Multiplying (2) by $I_1^2 I_2^2$ and taking square roots gives
$$
 \det M\,I_1I_2\le(2/g)^{(s-1)f}\sqrt{G_1G_2}=\Delta_1.         \tag{4}
$$
[Level divisibility][div] · [Definite quotient norm and determinant][bound]

For clarity, the degenerate case uses two further flag facts from the pinned lattice proof. On each side the radical modulo $\Theta_i$ has dimension exactly one. On the left radical line there is an injective last-coordinate functional $\alpha_1$ satisfying $T|\tau\alpha_1(v)|^2\le\kappa_{2,\tau}^2\|v\|_\tau^2$ with $\kappa_{2,\tau}$ from the **other** moving flag, and conversely on the right. This comes from taking the coefficient of the fixed last vector in the difference of its two flag lifts. Its kernel on the radical is the common space. The two projections in the plane of the last vectors have coefficients bounded by $c_\tau/\sqrt{c_\tau^2-1}$ and $1/\sqrt{c_\tau^2-1}$. Their sum gives $\kappa_\tau=(1+c_\tau)/\sqrt{c_\tau^2-1}$ with $c_\tau=x_\tau/(t y_\tau)$, or $\kappa_\tau=1$ when $p_r=0$. Write $K_i=\prod_\tau\kappa_{i,\tau}$. [Radical rank and kernel][rad] · [Last-coordinate inequality in the flag normal form][angle]

Let $C_i$ be the radical line lattice in $P_i$, $Q_i$ its perpendicular projection, and $n_i=[C_i:C_i\cap\alpha_i^{-1}(\mathcal O_E)]$. Then $\det P_i=\det C_i\det Q_i$. Integrating the last-coordinate bound by taking all embedding products gives $\det C_1\ge N/(n_1 K_2^2)$ and $\det C_2\ge N/(n_2 K_1^2)$. Indeed the sublattice on which the coordinate is integral maps to a nonzero integral ideal, whose norm is at least one. The covolume comparison on the line gives the bound there, and passage back to $C_i$ divides by $n_i$. The last-coordinate map on $g\mathcal B_2$ modulo integers kills the image of $A_2$ modulo its theta lattice. Its image contains the coordinate classes from $C_1$, by the marked representative condition on the radical. Thus $I_2 n_1\le |g\mathcal B_2|$ and $I_1 n_2\le |g\mathcal B_1|$. This improves the two single-flag bounds to $G_1/n_2,G_2/n_1$, respectively. The two indices cancel in (2). Finally $b_\beta$ induces a nonsingular pairing between the $Q_i$ of rank $s-2$ with the same divisibility and norm bounds. Applying (3) there yields
$$
 \det M\,I_1I_2\le (2/g)^{(s-2)f}\sqrt{G_1G_2}\,
                  K_1K_2/N=\Delta_2.                            \tag{5}
$$
This uses the one-dimensional radical estimate on the actual flag configuration, including both cross indices. A degenerate pairing on abstract lattices of the same dimensions need not have this property. [Coordinate classes and integral rank-one determinant][line] · [Application in both directions][both]

## Cancellation and the height range

For every represented nonsingular label,
$$
              \sqrt{G_Z}\,K(Z)=g^{-mf/2}J_Z.                    \tag{6}
$$
For $p_r\ne0$ this is the identity $\sqrt{x_\tau^2-t^2y_\tau^2}\,(1+c_\tau)/\sqrt{c_\tau^2-1}=x_\tau+t y_\tau$ at each place. Positivity in (1) justifies taking the square roots. If $p_r=0$, the prescribed $K$ is one and $J_Z=\prod_\tau x_\tau$. In particular the exact degenerate cutoff is
$$
        \Delta_2=(2/g)^{(s-2)f} g^{-mf}J_ZJ_{Z'}/N.             \tag{7}
$$
If the right side is less than one then that pair fibre is empty. [Original angular factor on the represented support][angular]

Let $b\ge2$ be the size parameter and choose $c\ge2$ large enough for the single-frame estimates. The source height is $h_Z=b^{c^2m^3f}S_c(Z)^4$, where $S_c$ is the product over places and minors of $\max(1,\rho^{-2a_\ell}|\tau p_\ell|/b^{cm^2})$ for $1\le\ell\le r+1$. Positivity in (1) gives $t y_\tau<x_\tau$ at every place, hence
$$
 J_Z\le 2^f\prod_\tau x_\tau
      \le (2 b^{cm^2})^f S_c(Z)\le\sqrt{h_Z}.
$$
The last inequality uses $1+cm^2\le c^2m^3/2$ for $m\ge2$ and $c\ge2$. This step needs no upper bound on $T'$ beyond the positivity of the actual Gram form. Equations (4) and (7) give $\Delta_i\le\sqrt{h_Zh_{Z'}}$ in both classes, including at a very small Gram determinant. They also give $K(Z)^2\le h_Z$ since $\det U\ge1$ in (1). [Original label height][labelheight]

In the [actual finite-fibre count](SPECTRAL_FLAG_PAIR_FIBRES.md) one may now use $\Delta=\Delta_i$ and $H=h_Zh_{Z'}$. For a nonempty fibre, $1\le\Delta_i\le H^{1/2}$. In the single-frame calculation this gives $\Delta_i^2 N^2Q\le H^4$, using $N^2\le H$, $Q\le H^2$. In the final index sum $(\Delta_i N)^{A_s}\le H^{A_s}$ and the Euler bracket is at most $4H^{1/2}$. Therefore for both classes on the original tied row,
$$
 \mathcal W_{s,i}
 \le 4\,81^{sf} X_s(\Delta_i/N)^k
        H^{3r+8s(1+r)+8r^2+A_s+1/2}.
$$
This supplies the arithmetic finite-fibre estimate of B8.10ac at every corank $s\ge2$. To pass to the spectral assertion, the real squared-integral estimate and the estimate with angular normalization outside the [ordinary-amplitude range](SPECTRAL_HIGHER_CORANK_MASSES.md) still have to hold on the actual operator. Equation (6) cancels the angle in the counting cutoff.
