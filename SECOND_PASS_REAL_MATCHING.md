# The common real quotient at every square

On the restricted retwisted datum the two oscillator representations at a square come from different source groups. A finite twist changes neither the real signatures nor the exponents of the two splitting characters. We construct the **same** nonzero simple target module for both real polynomial models, before choosing any of the finite twists. The argument applies at the original square and after each allowed removal of a hyperbolic plane. The compact case is included. [Actual square hypotheses][triple] · [Two real quotient maps in the pinned source][pin]

## The generating compact type

Fix one real place. Write the signatures of the two sources as $(P,Q)=(a-p,p)$ and $(P',Q')=(q,a'-q)$, and those of the common target as $(R,S)=(P+Q',Q+P')$. Put $D=R+S$ and
$$
 c=\tfrac12(m_\alpha-m_\beta),\quad
 n=c-(R-S)/2,\quad m=c+(R-S)/2,\quad
 d=(P-Q)/2,\quad d'=(P'-Q')/2 .
$$
The parity of the character restrictions and the rank hypotheses make $n,m$ integers. Since $2c>D$, they satisfy $n>S$ and $m>R$. In the polynomial Fock model quotient by the source Lie relations for the characters $c\operatorname{tr}$ and $-c\operatorname{tr}$ on the two sides. On the second side add $c\operatorname{tr}$ to the target action. This accounts for the difference of the two target splittings. Their common overall twist can be put back after constructing the quotient.

The following determinant polynomials survive the source relations. Use the signature ordering in each target column block. On the first side take the $n$-th power of the determinant on the positive rows and first $P$ positive columns, times the $m$-th power of the determinant on the negative rows and first $Q$ negative columns. On the second side use the last $Q'$ positive columns on the negative rows for the $n$-th power, and the last $P'$ negative columns on the positive rows for the $m$-th power. Empty determinants equal one.

To check survival, write the positive- and negative-column factors as $x,y$. Let $D_+,D_-$ be the differential source actions on the respective polynomial spaces and $J$ the diagonal signature matrix of that source. With $h=c$ or $-c$, give the second polynomial space the auxiliary action $Z\mapsto h\operatorname{tr}Z-D_-(JZ^tJ)$. Under these two actions $x,y$ have the same block-diagonal character. Indeed the positive-column vacuum shifts on the positive and negative rows are $R/2,-R/2$, and the negative-column shifts are $-S/2,S/2$. The determinant powers and $h$ give equality on both blocks. The lowering block kills both vectors: its contractions differentiate opposite-sign rows, while each determinant uses only one row sign. Normalize the two cyclic vectors to unit Fischer norm. For two words in the source Lie algebra, commute the adjoint of the first times the second into raising, diagonal, lowering order. Any remaining lowering term on the right, or raising term on the left, has zero pairing; the diagonal terms use only their common character. This proves equality of their Gram matrices. Rescale the resulting cyclic-space intertwiner to send $x$ to $y$. It extends by zero on the orthogonal complement, which is invariant by adjointness and can be taken degree by degree. Denote this map by $L$. The bilinear Fischer pairing $[Lu,v]$ transforms by the required source character and takes a positive value at $x\otimes y$, since $y$ has real coefficients. It descends through the source relations and detects the determinant class. [Fock actions and determinant calculation][det]

Paul's joint-harmonic formula applies with $P,Q'\le R$ and $Q,P'\le S$. After removing the target vacuum shifts, the first target weight is $(n^{[P]},0^{[Q']};m^{[Q]},0^{[P']})$ and the second is $(0^{[P]},(-n)^{[Q']};0^{[Q]},(-m)^{[P']})$. Superscripts in brackets give multiplicities. Adding the corresponding vacuum shifts and the second target twist makes both equal to
$$
 \lambda=\big((d+n)^{[P]},d^{[Q']};(m-d)^{[Q]},(-d)^{[P']}\big),
$$
since $d-d'=(R-S)/2$. Howe's generation identity (4.5) applies to the source character, which has just one source compact type. Consequently its paired harmonic type, now known to survive in each coinvariant, generates the whole corresponding target module. [Paul, Lemma 2.5][paul] · [Howe, §4, (4.5)][howe]

## Why the two generated modules have a common simple quotient

Let $C_K=\sum E_{ij}E_{ji}$, summing over ordered indices in the same compact block, and let $b_0$ be its eigenvalue on the compact type $L_\lambda$. The first off-block layer has a gap of at least $D+2\gamma$ above $b_0$, where
$$
 \gamma=c-|d+d'|>0 .
$$
Indeed $|d+d'|\le D/2$. When $S=0$ there are no off-block operators, and the two modules are already the same compact type. Otherwise an off-block operator on a minimum-degree representative has only its degree-raising term left in the quotient: the lowering term is below the least source-character degree. At degree two higher, the images of the raising parts of the off-block source operators vanish as well, since their accompanying lowering terms are below the minimum degree. Take their Fischer orthogonal complement, the kernel of their adjoint contractions. There, on each column block, the sum of absolute shifted weights is its harmonic degree. This is Paul's compact-member harmonic formula. On the two sides the minimum source degrees are $Pn+Qm$ and $Q'n+P'm$ respectively. [Harmonic degree and first operators][first]

The compact tensor product with an off-block operator changes a dominant weight by adding one entry in one block and subtracting one in the other. To increase the sum of absolute shifted entries by two, both changes must increase an absolute value. On the first side the permitted positive entries are already filled and all other shifted entries are zero. The increase must be in the first positive plateau and the decrease at the end of a zero plateau in the opposite block. On the second side the negative entries are filled; one adds to a zero plateau and subtracts from the final negative plateau. Dominance forces addition in the first position and subtraction in the last position of the respective blocks. An empty required plateau simply forbids that move. The possible differences between these two entries in the actual weight are $n+2d=c+d+d'$ and $m-2d=c-d-d'$. The compact Casimir on a block of length $t$ has eigenvalue $\sum_i\lambda_i(\lambda_i+t+1-2i)$. Hence its change is $D+2(\lambda_i-\lambda_j)\ge D+2\gamma$ for either surviving move.

Take the graph of an identification of the two copies of $L_\lambda$ inside the direct sum of the two modules. Let $M$ be the target module it generates. Filter by the number $k$ of off-block operators, writing $\mathfrak p$ for their space. The layer $M_k/M_{k-1}$ is an image of $\mathfrak p^{\otimes k}\otimes L_\lambda$ symmetric in the $k$ slots, since the commutator of two off-block operators is block diagonal. Identify that image with the orthogonal complement of its kernel for compact-invariant tensor metrics. If $B$ is the kernel of the first action $\mathfrak p\otimes L_\lambda\to\mathfrak pL_\lambda$, the layer is in $B^\perp$ on each slot paired with $L_\lambda$: it is true for the last slot by composition and for the others by symmetry. The Casimir on $\mathfrak p$ is $D$. Writing $C_{jL}$ for the diagonal Casimir on slot $j$ and $L_\lambda$, expansion of the quadratic Casimir gives
$$
 C_{\mathrm{total}}-b_0=C_{\mathfrak p^{\otimes k}}
                 +\sum_{j=1}^k(C_{jL}-D-b_0).
$$
The first term is nonnegative for the compact metric. Each summand contributes at least $2\gamma$ as a quadratic form on the layer. Thus the layer has spectrum at least $b_0+2k\gamma$ for $k\ge1$. In particular the original graph is the entire $b_0$-eigenspace in $M$. [Filtered graph argument][graph]

A proper submodule misses the generating graph, and its intersection with each finite filtration step has zero projection to that graph. The projection there is a polynomial in $C_K$, so it preserves the submodule. Even the sum of all proper submodules has zero projection and is proper. Dividing by this sum gives a nonzero simple module. Both coordinate projections of $M$ are onto by generation, and their kernels are proper. The simple quotient therefore receives a surjection from each original module, with the correct action of every matrix unit. Making the same choices at the finitely many real places gives their external tensor product. It remains simple for the direct-sum Lie algebra: each simple factor is countable dimensional with scalar endomorphisms over $\mathbb C$, and the algebraic density theorem acts arbitrarily on any finite independent list. Applied to successive tensor factors this puts a nonzero pure tensor in every nonzero submodule, and that tensor generates. Here scalar endomorphisms also follow directly because the generating compact type is a single copy. [Local assembly in the original proof][pin] · [Order of real and finite maps][b816]

Finite-order changes of the Hecke characters leave $c$, the signatures and the polynomial models fixed at infinity. The rank inequalities persist at every required plane removal, and the same computation supplies its local quotient there. Consequently no additional real matching assumption or conductor-dependent cutoff is needed for the second pass on these grids. Choosing the normalized finite transforms, proving nonzero global proportionality, and producing a bottom-row detector also involve finite and global arguments.
