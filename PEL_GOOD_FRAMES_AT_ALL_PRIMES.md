# Good-reduction frames detect all marked cusp evaluations at every prime

Fix a supplied generic marked row of the project. Use its graph theta line, its original dominant nonnegative weights, the bounded complex cusp norms of the selected uniformization, and its original integral tangent and primitive theta frames. The original marked level $M=g_0g_1$ has prime-to-residue-characteristic part at least three at every prime, as required by the native marked-row setup. At a coefficient prime $\lambda$, normalize an additive valuation by dividing by its value on a uniformizer of $F_\lambda$. For every integral frame $x$ with finite residue field and every finite list of algebraic cusp rules $f_i$ on this row there exists a **good-reduction** algebraic frame $y$ above this same prime such that
$$
                         v_y(f_i(y))\le v_x(f_i(x))\quad\text{for all }i.       \tag{1}
$$
The values here and below are normalized when comparing different traits, and $v(0)=+\infty$. In particular a scalar sufficient on good-reduction frames works on the entire original test class at every prime, including those dividing the CM discriminant or the marked level. This is the transfer of B8.43n for these rows. The proof uses the qualitative [all-frame denominator](PEL_ALL_FRAME_DENOMINATORS.md) at one step. It requires no estimate for that denominator. The resulting implication preserves the **specified** scalar exactly.

## A marked family over a two-dimensional local ring

If $x$ has good reduction, apply [good-frame specialization](PEL_DIRECT_FRAME_SPECIALIZATION.md) to its readings. Otherwise write $V$ for its complete DVR. After finite extension split the torus and period lattice of the Raynaud extension, choose the coefficient embeddings, rationalize the lifts of the finitely many marking points and replace the period orders by multiples of $2N$, where $N$ is the lcm of the two marking orders. A finite totally ramified extension multiplies all orders simultaneously. These operations preserve original normalized valuations. Use $\pi$ for the resulting uniformizer. Denote the constant abelian part by $A/V$, its semiabelian extension by $G^\natural$, the character lattice by $X$ and the period lattice by $Y$. The principal polarization identifies $Y\simeq X$ and induces a principal polarization on $A$. Indeed the degree of the generic theta polarization is the square of the index of $Y$ in $X$ times the degree of the abelian-part polarization. Both factors equal one. In the formulas identify $Y$ and $X$ by this map. [Lan, §§4.2–4.5, in particular Lemma 4.3.2.9 and Theorem 4.4.16](https://www.kwlan.org/articles/cpt-PEL-type-thesis-revision-dense.pdf)

The bilinear periods are trivializations of the indicated Poincaré fibres. Their orders $B(y,z)$ form the symmetric, positive definite monodromy form, balanced for the CM involution. The graph theta line is the pullback of the rigidified Poincaré line along the polarization and the endomorphism $w_0$ of trace one. Thus in its Raynaud data the quadratic trivialization at $y$ is the bilinear trivialization evaluated on $(y,w_0y)$, with the same rigidifications. This follows by pulling the biextension multiplication law through the graph, including on the abelian part. Its order is
$$
             Q(y)=B(y,w_0 y)=\tfrac12B(y,y).
$$
Indeed symmetry and balance give the same value at $w_0$ and $\bar w_0$ and these sum to the identity. In particular $Q(y)>0$ for $y\ne0$. This fixes the integral quadratic orders even at residue characteristic two. [Theta-line identification](PEL_THETA_LINE.md) · [Marked integral monodromy and roots][arc]

Use $R=V[[t]]$, complete for its maximal ideal $(\pi,t)$. Keep $A,G^\natural$, the constant CM maps and abelian line over $R$. In each bilinear period replace its factor $\pi^{B(y,z)}$ by $t^{B(y,z)}$, and in the quadratic trivialization replace $\pi^{Q(y)}$ by $t^{Q(y)}$. The remaining factors are integral generators of the corresponding lines over $V$. This prescription is independent of the bases used to calculate their orders. Bilinearity, balance and the identity $Q(y+z)-Q(y)-Q(z)=B(y,z)$ preserve the descent and CM identities. Positivity in the complete local ring holds because $t^{Q(y)}$ tends to zero in the maximal-ideal topology as $y$ tends to infinity. The Mumford equivalence gives a semiabelian group $G/R$ with rigidified theta line and a principally polarized abelian generic fibre. The prescribed graph identity follows on this family from the same biextension trivializations. Specialization $t=\pi$ gives the original degeneration by functoriality. Specialization $t=\pi^n$ for each integer $n\ge1$ gives the analogous degeneration with bilinear orders $nB$ over $V$. Reduction modulo $\pi$ gives the Mumford degeneration over $k[[t]]$ with the same positive form and unit coefficients. Consequently its generic fibre is abelian: at the height-one prime $(\pi)$ of $R$ the group has good reduction. These are quotient base changes carrying the closed point to the closed point, with the positive degeneration data indicated in each case. [Lan, Theorem 4.4.16, Corollary 4.5.3.9 and Proposition 4.5.6.5](https://www.kwlan.org/articles/cpt-PEL-type-thesis-revision-dense.pdf)

Here the original two markings matter. In the split uniformization an $N$-torsion lift is represented by $(y,h)$ with $Nh=\iota(y)$ in $G^\natural$, modulo the corresponding shifts by periods. In the varying family multiply $h$ by the torus element whose $z$-coordinate is
$$
                        (t/\pi)^{B(y,z)/N}.
$$
The exponents are integers. Its $N$th power is exactly the change in the period $\iota(y)$. Additivity makes this substitution respect sums and changes of representatives by periods. Balance makes it CM-linear. The generic Weil pairing is a root of unity in $\operatorname{Frac}(V[[t]])\subset \operatorname{Frac}(V)((t))$. Every such root is constant (differentiate its defining equation in characteristic zero). At the characteristic-zero trait with parameter $t-\pi$ the group is abelian and its torsion extends by properness and finite étaleness. Specialization there identifies the constant with the original Weil pairing. It is invertible over the generic field, specializes to the identity at $t=\pi$, and commutes with passage to the two prescribed marking orders. The Mumford torsion description identifies this calculation with *algebraic* generic torsion on $G$. Hence the full compatible level, and therefore the prescribed partial marking, varies with the group. It need only be a marking on the generic abelian object of the integral frame. The original signature is preserved by the constant Lie-module comparison below. On the connected open $D(\pi t)$ any prescribed open-and-closed generic component condition holds everywhere because it holds at $t=\pi$. The argument uses étaleness only where the level is invertible. [Lan, Theorem 4.5.3.10](https://www.kwlan.org/articles/cpt-PEL-type-thesis-revision-dense.pdf) · [Native comparison of torsion lifts and the two markings][level]

## The same primitive theta generator on all specializations

The formal completion of $G$ along the closed fibre has its identity group chart in the Raynaud extension. The canonical comparison with $G^\natural$ identifies the Lie modules over every Artinian quotient of $R$. Passing to inverse limits identifies the finite free Lie module over $R$ with the scalar extension from the original constant extension. All identifications commute with the CM action. Transport the given integral eigenbases on $V$ by this identification. Over the fraction field these are the required tangent frames. Base change from $V$ to any of the DVRs used below is flat, so the kernels of the eigenvalue equations, and hence their saturated bases, are preserved. At $t=\pi$ these are the prescribed vectors.

For the theta generator use the Fourier coefficient indexed by $0\in X$. Choose any nonzero algebraic generic theta section and clear base denominators to extend it to the smooth group $G/R$. This is possible on a finite affine cover because restriction to the generic fibre inverts the nonzero elements of $R$. Its Fourier coefficients on the formal Raynaud extension are sections of the abelian-part line and its integral translates. The principally polarizing line on the abelian part has one-dimensional space of sections, with direct image a line over $V$ and formation commuting with base change. Choose an integral basis $s_0$ there. The zero coefficient of our section is $a s_0$ for some nonzero $a\in R$. It cannot vanish: the Fourier translation relation determines every coefficient from this one since $Y\to X$ is onto, and completion detects a nonzero global section on the integral group. Divide the rational section by $a$ and call the result $s$. More explicitly the translation relation at frequency zero expresses its $y$-coefficient as the integral translate of $s_0$ multiplied by the quadratic trivialization. This is $t^{Q(y)}$ times an integral generator times that translate. All terms are integral and the terms at $y\ne0$ tend to zero in the maximal-ideal topology. The resulting formal section reduces on the closed fibre to the pullback of $s_0\bmod\pi$, which is nonzero. [Lan, §4.3.1, especially (4.3.1.3), and §§4.3.2–4.3.3](https://www.kwlan.org/articles/cpt-PEL-type-thesis-revision-dense.pdf)

This also proves that the rational section $s$ is regular near the closed fibre. In a local trivialization of the line at a point of that fibre the numerator of $s$ belongs to $a$ times the completed local ring, hence to $a$ times the local ring by faithful flatness. The restricted Fourier series above supplies this membership in the completion. On any base divisor, the group fibre is geometrically integral. Its generic point specializes to the closed fibre locally along a smooth group chart. If $s$ had a pole along such a divisor, the pole locus would meet the closed fibre because it contains the base divisor’s entire group fibre closure (the group over that divisor is flat with integral fibres). This contradicts the local regularity just proved. Over the generic base fibre the section was regular already. A flat smooth group over the regular base is regular, and a vertical prime divisor lies over a height-one base point by flatness. Thus $s$ extends over $G$. Its reduction is nonzero on *every* base fibre: the nonvanishing locus of $s$ is open and contains a point of the closed fibre, so its image under the smooth morphism $G\to\operatorname{Spec}R$ is an open containing the closed point, hence the entire local base.

On specializing to any test DVR, this section is therefore primitive. Indeed a section of the generic principal theta line is a scalar multiple of $s$, and such a multiple is integral only if the scalar has nonnegative valuation, by restriction at the generic point of the special fibre. At $t=\pi$ its difference from the original generator is a unit of $V$. This does not affect any valuation, since each cusp reading has the prescribed integer theta degree. This argument uses a nonzero *section* on the closed fibre and permits its value at the identity to vanish. The same Lie and theta frames work on the completed Gauss DVR at $(\pi)$.

## Removing all horizontal poles using the qualitative denominator

Read a cusp rule on the generic framed abelian object just constructed, obtaining $F(t)\in\operatorname{Frac}(R)$. It belongs to $R[1/(\pi t)]$. To see this, a height-one prime different from $(\pi),(t)$ has a one-dimensional complete local quotient with finite normalization over that quotient, a DVR after passing to a branch. The images of both $t$ and $\pi$ there are nonzero nonunits. The monomial periods still have positive quadratic orders and the Mumford construction gives an abelian generic fibre on this branch. Its fraction field is the residue field at that height-one prime (or a finite extension). Thus the moduli point on that base fibre is in the ordinary abelian interior. Near the prime the group is an abelian scheme by openness of the abelian locus. The characteristic is zero there, the generic marking extends over the height-one DVR by properness of torsion in an abelian scheme and remains a marking since the level is invertible. The frame is integral and invertible there. Hence algebraicity and regularity of the cusp rule on the framed interior exclude a pole. The asserted inclusion follows by normality of $R$. Specialization $t=\pi^n$ computes the actual cusp reading in the corresponding generic framed fibre, by the same regularity and functoriality.

Let $c\ne0$ be an integer from the qualitative [denominator theorem for all rows](PEL_ALL_FRAME_DENOMINATORS.md), for this finite list and the fixed row. All $t=\pi^n$ are original integral tests there, so $v_\pi(F(\pi^n))\ge-v_\pi(c)$, uniformly in $n$. If $F$ has a $t$-pole of order $h>0$, write $F=t^{-h}\pi^{-u}g(t)$ with $g\in V[[t]]$ and $g(0)\ne0$. For $n>v_\pi(g(0))$ the value $g(\pi^n)$ has exactly the order of $g(0)$. Then $v_\pi(F(\pi^n))=-nh-u+v_\pi(g(0))$ tends to $-\infty$, a contradiction. It follows that $F\in R[1/\pi]$. This deduction uses no embedding of a valued-field power series in a holomorphic disk.

Let $W$ be the completion of $R_{(\pi)}$. At this good-reduction frame the order of $F$ is the minimum of the coefficient orders in $R[1/\pi]$. In particular
$$
                         v_W(F)\le v_\pi(F(\pi)).
$$
This remains true for zero evaluations. The valuations have the same ramification on $F_\lambda$. Apply the [good-reduction specialization theorem](PEL_DIRECT_FRAME_SPECIALIZATION.md) once to the entire finite list at this single Gauss frame. It gives an algebraic good-reduction frame over a number field with a specified place above $\lambda$, preserving every normalized valuation at $W$. This proves (1). The theorem of the [RH Lean Roadmap, B8.43n, and its original proof](https://github.com/rh-lean-roadmap/rh-lean-roadmap/blob/02a95b4c62db610642af5f278ee5d0d6df87b348/docs/src/book/main.tex#L57987-L58015) gives the same original scalar implication using a complex arc and a Gauss specialization.

## What this gives the period and common constructions

The infimum of the normalized valuation of a single cusp rule on the whole integral class equals its infimum on algebraic good-reduction frames. Whenever the former infimum is finite it is attained there: take a minimizing frame from the [divisorial minimum theorem](PEL_ALL_FRAME_DENOMINATORS.md) and apply (1). For a $d$-dimensional cusp space, the lattice tested by all good algebraic frames thus equals the original lattice $M_\lambda$. Their conditions on $\varpi_\lambda^{-1}M_\lambda/M_\lambda$ have zero common intersection, so at most $d$ of them suffice by successive strict drops of dimension. Away from the finite bad support of the global interpolation frames, those frames already have good reduction. Using the new good frames at the remaining primes gives the global finite integrality test entirely on good-reduction frames at their specified places. This does not bound their arithmetic heights or the number of exceptional places required by global interpolation.

In particular a good-reduction valuation inequality with the prescribed local different and short-theta normalizer transfers with that same inequality to all original integral frames whenever its reading is one of these algebraic bounded cusp rules. The [existing good-reduction jet estimate](RETWISTED_JET_DIFFERENT.md) and the prescribed coefficient and chart hypotheses are still needed for its numerical right side. In the [frame-trace determinant](PERIOD_FRAME_TRACE_DETERMINANTS.md) the local dual tests may now be chosen on abelian reduction alone. The estimate for the arithmetic height of those mixed determinants and the eventual production of the original marked family remain separate.

## A sharper denominator for descent from a ramified coefficient field

Take a tower cover with coefficient field $L$ over the base field $K$ satisfying the original rational level-descent comparison. Write $C$ for the base cusp space in the indicated weight and $M$ for its integral evaluation lattice. A cover cusp reading $u$ invariant under the base compact has a rational expression in $C\otimes_K L$ under that comparison. Fix a rational prime $\ell$, and any nonempty set $S$ of places $w$ of $L$ above $\ell$ where the cover reading is integral. Every good integral base frame lifts to a good integral cover frame after a finite extension, also for every coefficient embedding of $L_w$ over $K_\lambda$. Indeed choose the extra level marking on the generic abelian scheme over a finite extension, keep the same abelian model and frame after base change, and use the tower's pullback identity for its readings. This uses the original tower with generic finite-level markings. [Rational level descent and cover pullback](FIRST_PASS_UNRAMIFIED_DESCENT.md)

Localize $M$ at the finite set of places of $K$ below $S$ (that is, take the intersection of their local lattices inside $C$). This is a free module over the corresponding semilocal Dedekind ring. Take one basis $b_1,\ldots,b_d$ and write $u=\sum_i z_i b_i$, $z_i\in L$. Then the **same coefficients** satisfy at every $w\in S$, $\lambda=w|_K$,
$$
                    z_i\in\mathfrak D_{L_w/K_\lambda}^{-1}.                \tag{2}
$$
To prove this, fix $a\in O_{L_w}$. The section with completed base coefficients $v_a=\sum_i\operatorname{Tr}_{L_w/K_\lambda}(a z_i)b_i$ is integral on every good algebraic framed test above $\lambda$. In fact, after extending that frame to a common normal coefficient field, its value is the sum over all local embeddings $\sigma$ of $\sigma(a)\sum_i\sigma(z_i)b_i(x)$. Each inner reading is the invariant cover reading at the corresponding lifted good frame and is integral there. This works even if the fields of the sections and the test intersect, since one can form the scalar-extended marked objects for each $\sigma$ over the same larger field. The resulting trace value is integral. The completed integral base lattice is precisely the unit ball for these tests. To check this last statement for a vector with $K_\lambda$-coefficients, approximate each of its coordinates in the $b_i$ by an element of $K$ with integral error at $\lambda$. Since each $b_i$ is integral, membership for the vector is equivalent to membership for that rational approximation. Equality (1) and definition of $M_\lambda$ handle the rational approximation. Hence every $\operatorname{Tr}_{L_w/K_\lambda}(az_i)$ is in $O_{K_\lambda}$. Vary $a$ to get (2) by the trace-dual characterization. [The different and the trace pairing](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0BW0)

Here is its cost using rational prime powers. Let $e_w=e(L_w/K_\lambda)$ and let $e_\lambda=v_{K_\lambda}(\ell)$, with the local valuations taking value one on the respective field uniformizers. For $e_w=1$ the ideal in (2) is integral. For $e_w>1$ the normalized loss is at most
$$
  \frac{v_{L_w}(\mathfrak D_{L_w/K_\lambda})}{v_{L_w}(\ell)}
         \le v_\ell(e_w)+\frac{1-e_w^{-1}}{e_\lambda}.                  \tag{3}
$$
Pass first to the maximal unramified subextension of $L_w/K_\lambda$, whose different is trivial. Over it any uniformizer $z$ of $L_w$ has Eisenstein polynomial $P$ of degree $e_w$, generates the integer ring, and the different is generated by $P'(z)$. The nonzero derivative terms have pairwise distinct orders modulo $e_w$, from their respective powers $0,\ldots,e_w-1$ of $z$. The smallest order is therefore no larger than the order of $e_w z^{e_w-1}$. Divide this bound by $e_\lambda e_w$ to obtain (3). [Serre, *Local Fields*, Chapters I and III](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4757-5673-9)

Consequently $\ell^{1+v_\ell(e_w)}$ clears the coefficients at a ramified $w$, with no power of $\ell$ needed anywhere unramified. For any set $S$ as above take the maximum of these integer exponents, using zero for the unramified places. This proves the invariant-cover descent simultaneously over $S$, including wild ramification. It sharpens the single-place bound $\ell^{e^2}$ of [B8.43p](https://github.com/rh-lean-roadmap/rh-lean-roadmap/blob/02a95b4c62db610642af5f278ee5d0d6df87b348/docs/src/book/main.tex#L23755-L23759), where $e$ was the absolute ramification index of a common containing DVR. Inequality (2) is a sufficient bound and does not claim its sharpness for particular cusp spaces.
