REVIEW FINDINGS
1. PA5: the odd-prime norm/trace argument separates a=r+sqrt(-d) from its conjugate. At p=2, a=(1+sqrt(-d))/2 has odd trace and even norm because d=7 mod16. The selected prime is nonzero of height one. The exact SplitK disjunction is preserved.
2. PA6: local freeness follows over the prime-local DVR. Multiplying each basis vector by a unit of that localization places it inside the original lattice; finitely many generating coefficients then admit one denominator outside the selected prime. This gives both source containments without asserting global freeness.
3. PA7: q=alpha/conj(alpha) has positive valuation at the selected prime, with conjugate q=q^-1. Thus e_N tends to 1 while its conjugate tends to 0. The skew matrix is exactly skew-Hermitian; the Cayley transform is unitary and tends to the required matrix on its open domain. Factoring an arbitrary g into a scalar and a matrix avoiding eigenvalue -1 closes the domain issue. This argument does not assume conjugation is continuous at a split prime. Inversion is used only at a nonsingular limit, and finitely many rational entries give one denominator outside that prime.
4. PA8: transporting the trace-self-dual A^4 through the same gamma0 produces the exact full O_K lattice. Coordinate tests give self-duality in both directions. Self-duality supplies the conjugate-denominator containments. The integral fixed element a=conj(alpha)t+alpha conj(t) belongs to Q, hence Z, and is nonzero modulo the selected prime. Therefore s=|a|>0 is prime to p and works in both directions for every vector. This is stronger than, and implies, the two literal pointwise AtP clauses. No independent model or scalar-extension identification is substituted.
5. PA9: the prime-ideal class has order dividing h_K, so its h_K power has a nonzero generator; the 2h_K root of its conjugate ratio exists and is nonzero. Choice is made on the prime/split subtype; the other indices can use the explicit fallback. The 8 KapIn, 8 VacIn, 3 AmbIn and 2 Ok fields match the pinned definitions. The derivative scalars use the independent finite principalization and flat base-change proof in TL17, for the same host and embeddings. PA9 does not claim StdOK or later theta stages.
6. PA10: the fixed gamma0 and its inverse have a single global integer denominator N. For split p not dividing N, gamma0 and the AtP gammap identify the same localized lattice with the standard one at every prime over p; the relative determinant is therefore a unit. This proves genuine finite support among the split rational primes, and the determinant ratio of any two AtP choices proves the exact YIndep statement even when those choices only have pointwise denominators. The product is explicitly over the source prime/split subtype and its root choices are kept fixed; the argument does not infer finiteness from totalized finprod and does not assert independence from root choices.
