BS1. FINITE BERNOULLI LAWS AND ONE VARIANCE BUDGET

Let A be any finite set. For each a in A fix p_a in [0,1], and give a subset
T of A probability

  w(T) = product_(a in T) p_a * product_(a not in T) (1-p_a).

These weights are nonnegative and sum to product_a(p_a+(1-p_a))=1.
For X_a(T)=1_(a in T), direct summation over every coordinate except a
gives E X_a=p_a; summation except a,b gives E X_a X_b=p_a p_b for a≠b.
Also X_a²=X_a. Consequently, for arbitrary real numbers b,g_a,

  E(b+Σ_a g_a X_a)²
    = b²+2bΣ_a g_a p_a+Σ_a g_a²p_a
         +2Σ_(a<b)g_a g_b p_a p_b
    = (b+Σ_a g_a p_a)²+Σ_a g_a²p_a(1-p_a).

The displayed pair sum may be written using any ordering of the finite
set; the identity is order independent. This is exactly sel_moment and
sel_moment', expressed using finite probability.

Let c:A→C classify A into finitely many possibly empty classes. Put
S_j=|T∩c^(-1)(j)| and μ_j=Σ_(c(a)=j)p_a. Applying the identity to the
indicator of that class gives E(S_j−μ_j)²=Σ_(c(a)=j)p_a(1-p_a).
Each a appears in precisely one class. Hence, if p_a≤δ,

  E Σ_j(S_j−μ_j)² = Σ_a p_a(1-p_a) ≤ δ|A|.

This is the important normalization. Estimating each class separately
and then applying the same worst-case variance to all of them would
introduce an unnecessary factor |C|. Empty classes contribute zero.

BS2. SIMULTANEOUS FINITE SELECTION

Fix a finite index set I, nonnegative weights ω_i, and arbitrary predicates
Bad(i,a,T). They can depend on the whole selected subset and on a, and
can be highly correlated for different i.

Suppose, for every i in I and a in A,

  P(a in T and Bad(i,a,T)) ≤ βδ,

where β≥0. Define

  B(T)=Σ_(i in I)ω_i |{a in T:Bad(i,a,T)}|,
  M=βδ|A|Σ_(i in I)ω_i.

Since all sums are finite, exchanging them gives E B≤M. The set-cardinality
identity used here is |{a:P(a)}|=Σ_a1_P(a), the source's sel_ncard.

Let D>0 and D²≥2δ|A|. If M>0, form

  F(T)=D^(-2)Σ_j(S_j−μ_j)²+B(T)/(4M).

BS1 and E B≤M imply E F≤1/2+1/4=3/4. A positive-probability subset T
with F(T)≤E F exists: otherwise every positive-weight value would
strictly exceed the average, contradicting its defining weighted sum.
This is the elementary sel_exists principle. Nonnegativity of both
summands gives |S_j−μ_j|≤D for every j and B(T)≤4M. In fact this choice
has the stronger bounds Σ_j(S_j−μ_j)²≤3D²/4 and B(T)≤3M, but only the
source-compatible weaker conclusions are used downstream.

If M=0, the finite nonnegative sum E B≤0 forces B(T)=0 at every
positive-weight T. Choose a positive-weight T at which the squared-error
sum is at most its average, hence at most D²/2. This gives the same
conclusion without dividing by zero. It covers δ=0, β=0, an empty prime
list, or zero total weight. If some p_a is 0 or 1, probability-zero
subsets are simply absent from the support; the proof is unchanged.

Thus there is one T for which

  every class-count error is at most D,
  B(T)≤4βδ|A|Σ_iω_i.

The literal source select theorem assumes 0≤p_a≤δ≤1, D>0 and the
same selected-and-bad joint bound. The argument above proves that
theorem, with a slightly stronger positive-M intermediate estimate.

BS3. COSET COUNTS NORMALIZE TO THE REQUIRED SHARES

Let A now be a finite abelian group and A0 a subgroup of index o.
Write n0=|A0| and N=|A|=on0. Translation is a bijection from A0
onto any of its cosets, so every coset has n0 elements.

Use p_a=δ on A0 and p_a=3δ/4 outside A0, with δ>0. The zero
coset has expected count δn0; every other coset has expected count
3δn0/4. This is tg_class_sum. Let C_j denote the actual coset counts
of a selected set T, and suppose every count differs from its mean
μ_j by at most D.

Writing S=|T| and M0=(3o+1)δn0/4, sum once over the o distinct cosets:

  |S−M0|≤oD.

The source sums over A and divides by n0, which is the same argument.
Let 0≤η≤1 and suppose 4oD≤ηδn0. Then oD≤δn0/4, and therefore

  S≥3oδn0/4=3δN/4,
  S≤(3o+2)δn0/4≤5δN/4.

These improve the literal source's δN/2≤S≤2δN, and in particular S>0.
The hypothesis η≥0 is implicit in the literal shares_of_counts statement:
δn0>0, D≥0 and 4oD≤ηδn0 already force η≥0.

Define λ_j=4/(3o+1) on the zero coset and 3/(3o+1) on the others.
Then μ_j=λ_j M0. It follows that

  |C_j−λ_j S| ≤ |C_j−μ_j|+λ_j|S−M0|
               ≤ D+λ_j oD
               ≤ D+4oD/(3o+1)
               ≤3D.

The scale condition gives

  3D≤3ηδn0/(4o)≤3ηoδn0/4≤ηS,

using o≥1. Thus |C_j−λ_j S|≤ηS for all cosets. Replacing a class
j by any representative γ is exactly the source's expression
|{t in T:t−γ in A0}|. This proves shares_of_counts with the same
relative error and the same T as BS2.

BS4. THE UNIFORM NUMERICAL THRESHOLD

Fix ε>0 and real bounds Co,Lb. The group data satisfy

  h=2+8ε, δ=x^(−ε/8), x≥1,
  x^h/2≤N≤Co x^h, o≤Co.

The existence of such group data already implies Co≥1. If Co<1,
the conditional theorem is vacuous; no invalid division by Co is used.

Set D=sqrt(2δN), η=x^(-1), and require x^ε≥64Co^4. Since ε>0,
this is imposed by a threshold independent of the eventual group.
We have

  δN≥x^(2+63ε/8)/2≥x² x^ε/2≥32Co^4x²≥32o^4x².

Because all quantities being squared are positive, this last inequality
is equivalent to

  sqrt(2δN)≤δN/(4o²x).

Multiplying by 4o and using N=on0 gives

  4oD≤δn0/x.

Therefore BS3 applies with η=x^(-1). Since x≥1 and ε>0,
x^(-1)≤x^(-1+ε), so it gives the literal, slightly weaker Shares
tolerance used in topsGroup. This is an explicit proof of tg_num.

BS5. ASSEMBLY OF THE COMPLETE BIASED TOPS THEOREM

Quantifier order:

  Fix ε>0, Co,Lb.
  Choose Cb>0 and the BO5 uniform lower threshold x_BO.
  Choose x0=max(x_BO,1,(64Co^4)^(1/ε)).
  Only then choose x≥x0, the finite abelian group A and subgroup A0,
  L2, the selector b, the finite list P, the steps h_p and weights ω_p.

The group bounds are those of BS4; L2≤Lb. For every p in P assume
ord(h_p)≥x^(ε/4). The upper order bound follows from Lagrange:
ord(h_p)≤|A|≤Co x^h. Fix a selector b:A×A→A satisfying, for every
h,t, b(h,t)+i h=t for some natural i. This selector is part of the input
and is fixed before the random subset is sampled.

For p,t, put n_p=ord(h_p) and pull the subset T back to the cyclic
orbit using

  i↦b(h_p,t)+i h_p,  i in Z/n_p Z.

The event Bad(p,t,T) is exactly failure of IsGoodOrbit with that
pullback, the supplied L2 and BO's K,r,δ,h,x parameters. Crucially, the
base can depend on t. The even-index condition is not invariant under
an arbitrary cyclic translation. Nothing in this proof identifies badness
for two distinct t, even if they lie on the same unbased orbit.

Under the biased point probabilities, BO5 gives

  P(t selected and Bad(p,t,T))≤δCb x^-2.

Apply BS2 with the classes A/A0, β=Cb x^-2 and D=sqrt(2δN).
Every coset count is within D of its biased expectation, and

  Σ_(p in P)ω_p |{t in T:Bad(p,t,T)}|
      ≤4Cb x^-2 δN Σ_(p in P)ω_p.

BS3 and BS4 give, for this same T,

  δN/2≤|T|≤2δN,
  | |{t in T:t−γ in A0}|−λ_γ|T| |≤x^(-1+ε)|T| for all γ.

This is exactly topsGroup. Our proof additionally retains 3δN/4≤|T|≤
5δN/4 and the relative share error x^-1. Those stronger inequalities
are optional consequences; the downstream construction can use the
source's original constants without alteration.

There is no requirement that P consist of all primes below a bound.
At this stage it is any finite list with the required order property,
and nonnegative weights are arbitrary. The exact Lean statement uses
a finite subset of natural numbers; primality is not used in BS5.

BS6. PRIME-WEIGHTED BADNESS WITH THE SAME SELECTED SET

For z≥2 set n=floor(z). Unique factorization of n! gives

  log(n!)=Σ_(p≤n) v_p(n!)log p
         ≥Σ_(p≤n)floor(n/p)log p.

For 1≤y, floor(y)≥y/2. Since p≤n, it follows that

  (n/2)Σ_(p≤n)(log p)/p≤log(n!)≤n log n,

and hence Σ_(p<z)(log p)/p≤2log z. For 1<z<2 there are no
such primes. This is an elementary factorial estimate; it does not
invoke the prime number theorem or a sharp Mertens theorem.

Let the finite candidate primes P lie below X'=exp(x^(ε/3)).
Substitute ω_p=(log p)/p in BS5. Its bad mass is bounded by

  4Cb x^-2 δN * 2x^(ε/3)
    ≤16Cb|T|x^(-2+ε/3)

because δN≤2|T|. Requiring x^(2ε/3)≥16Cb gives the source-style
allowance |T|x^(-2+ε). This may be enforced by increasing x0 to at
least (16Cb)^(3/(2ε)), before choosing the group or prime list.
All previous properties of this same T are retained. For an empty
candidate list the assertion is immediate.

BS7. FOURIER EXPLANATION AND ORDINARY-RH SPECIALIZATION

The probability mass on the finite quotient Q=A/A0 prescribed by
the shares condition is

  μ(q)=4/(3o+1) if q=0, 3/(3o+1) otherwise,

where |Q|=o. Equivalently,

  μ=(3o/(3o+1))u_Q+(1/(3o+1))δ_0.

Let χ:Q→C^× be a group character. Every value is a root of unity
since Q is finite, so |χ(q)|=1. If χ is nontrivial, choose q0 with
χ(q0)≠1. Translation permutes Q and gives

  Σ_qχ(q)=Σ_qχ(q+q0)=χ(q0)Σ_qχ(q),

forcing its sum to zero. Thus the μ-average of χ equals 1 for
the trivial character and 1/(3o+1) for every nontrivial character.

If T has shares with error η and S=|T|>0, its empirical quotient
mass ν(q)=|T∩q|/S satisfies |ν(q)−μ(q)|≤η. Therefore

  | S^(-1)Σ_(a in T)χ(a mod A0) − Σ_qμ(q)χ(q) |
     ≤Σ_q|ν(q)−μ(q)|≤oη.

For the actual BS5 subset one can use η=x^-1, or retain the source's
x^(-1+ε). The finite Fourier statement is a derived simplification
of the bias, not a new source axiom or an independent explicit formula.

The source's cyclic level is Level.cyc(ψ)=Level.ofSub(<ψ>). Its
Level.ofSub record has psK=none and o equal to the finite character
subgroup size, so Level.wt is 1/(3o+1) at each nontrivial retained
primitive character; the trivial term occurs separately with coefficient
one in Level.Gm. When the quotient and character subgroup are the actual
paired objects of the host, the Fourier values above match these weights.
This algebra explains why the selected bias is appropriate. It does not
by itself construct that host identification, control Euler factors, or
prove the centered-prime-sum explicit formula.

If o=1, the finite subgroup index identity implies A0=A. The
probabilities are then δ at every point, λ is identically one, and
the shares error is exactly zero for every T. The size and badness
selection still has to be performed. Thus the ordinary-RH specialization
removes the extra coset balance while retaining the common orbit and
arithmetic construction duties.
