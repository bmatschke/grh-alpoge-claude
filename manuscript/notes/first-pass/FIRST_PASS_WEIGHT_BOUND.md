# The weight bound and intrinsic lattice on the fourth-stage column

Fix the [returned first-pass fourth stage](FIRST_PASS_FOURTH_STAGE.md) $D=w.D(Z)$, its split-prime column $t_0$, and the original depth and character data. Write $k,m,t$ for its rank parameters and put $\Delta=\lceil x^{c_D}\rceil$. One integer
$$
                         c=16k\Delta
$$
works for every word, real position, and served coefficient place. From the actual column one can now choose the entire family of local orbit labels and weight fillings. It works for **every subsequent choice** of local levels, chains, ramified submodules and compatible size data. In particular it works at the divisor chosen on this fourth stage. [Order of choices][order] · [Use at the actual divisor][use]

**Quantifiers and numerical bound.** In the eventual range $0<\epsilon<1/52$, increase the lower bound on $c_D$ to ensure $c_D\ge0$. After the earlier parameters are fixed the rank estimates give $1\le n\le x^3$ and $k\le x^5$. With $b=\exp(x^\epsilon)$, the actual scale is
$$
    w''(n)=\frac{\exp(x^\epsilon-x^{\epsilon/3})}{n}.
$$
For all sufficiently large $x$ this is between $x^{c_D+6}$ and $b$, uniformly in $n$ in the stated interval. For instance, in addition to the rank threshold take $x\ge35$, $x^{2\epsilon/3}\ge2$ and $\log x\ge4(c_D+9)/\epsilon^2$. Then $x^\epsilon-x^{\epsilon/3}\ge x^\epsilon/2\ge\epsilon^2(\log x)^2/4$. Since $\Delta\le 2x^{c_D}$,
$$
              16k\Delta+k+2\le35x^{c_D+5}\le w''(n).
$$
Take maxima with the earlier $c_D,c_h,x$ thresholds at their respective places in the quantifier order. There is a single range before the base, column, stage, word, divisor or coefficient place is selected. For any size record $L$ on this stage with the prescribed scale and valuation data, the stage parameter is $D.s=1$, so its own definitions give $S_c(L)=4k\Delta$ and $c_G(L)=4S_c(L)=c$. This proves the required upper bound on the **same** $c$ for every $L$. [Scale definitions][scale] · [Rank and scale estimates][rank] · [Nested ranges and explicit bound][range] · [Size normalization][sz] · [Value of the stage parameter][val]

At a real position $\tau$ write $U_i$ for the entries of the word's upper Harish-Chandra parameter and $\lambda_i=U_i-(k-1)/2+i$ for its integral highest weight. For $1\le i\le k-2$ the stored bit $\xi_{i-1}\in\{0,1\}$ gives
$$
       U_i=4(k-1-i)\Delta-\tfrac12-\Delta\xi_{i-1}.
$$
Thus $3\Delta-\tfrac12\le U_i\le4k\Delta$. At a non-top position $U_0=8(k-1)\Delta+\tfrac12,\ U_{k-1}=-\tfrac12$. Here the actual endpoints are $\lambda_0=8(k-1)\Delta-(k-2)/2$ and $\lambda_{k-1}=(k-2)/2$. Consequently every interior $|\lambda_i|$ is bounded by $4k\Delta+(k-1)/2$, and every entry required by the cloud inequality is at most $8k\Delta$ in absolute value. At a top position the two distinguished endpoints are $(a_T,-b_T)$. The cloud bound there applies only to the interior coordinates. [Word code and top endpoints][code] · [Off-top formula and place windows][val] · [Integral shift and signs][shift] · [Three exact requirements][bound]

For the other direction of the bound, let $B_\tau$ be the first lower entry at $\tau$. At a top place $B_\tau=b_T+\lfloor b'/x\rfloor$, at a thick place $B_\tau\ge\lceil b\rceil$, and at a thin place $B_\tau=\lceil w''(n)\rceil$. Thus $B_\tau\ge w''(n)-1$ everywhere. The gap above $B_\tau$ gives $e_{\tau,0}\ge B_\tau+(t+3)/2$. For $1\le j\le m$, the weight of the added block, with the sign used by the local lattice, is
$$
   w^{\rm rec}_{j}(\tau)=w_j(\tau)-\kappa_j,\qquad
   w_j(\tau)=e_{\tau,j-1},\qquad
   \kappa_j=\frac{k+(2j-1)(t+1)}2 .
$$
It follows that $w^{\rm rec}_{1}(\tau)\ge c+1$. All these are integers because $w^{\rm rec}_j$ is the negative of the corresponding coordinate in the *given* full highest weight. Dominance of that weight gives both $w^{\rm rec}_1\ge-\lambda_{k-1}$ and monotonicity as $j$ increases. A lift appends $t+1$ copies of $-w^{\rm rec}_j$ at block $j$, so the lifted weight is dominant. This proves the three cloud clauses from the actual coordinates. [Lower-entry estimate][fill] · [Block weight identity][shift] · [Monotonicity and last coordinate][mono] · [Definition of the lifted weight][lift]

**Frobenius orbits and lattice compatibility.** For each word and each served coefficient place $v$, put $p=\operatorname{char}k(v)$. These are primes $p\ge5$ of the specified inert class satisfying the host's even-order Frobenius condition, away from the level primes and the word's integer $T$. The actual split-prime column identifies the pair consisting of $D$ and its position map with the reconstructed pair on its own levels, local identifications and characters. Use this identification once, before selecting a compatible size realization. On this class the frame step is $h_p$. In the frame's cyclic orbit $a+\langle h_p\rangle$, choose the member of least source label. The forward-orbit and backward-orbit conventions give the same minimum because $h_p$ has finite order. The resulting label-to-orbit map is injective on the chosen representatives. If $a_0$ is one of them, its $s$-th position is $\tau_1(a_0+sh_p)$ and the length is $\operatorname{ord}(h_p)$. [Served primes][served] · [Frobenius condition][frob] · [Intrinsic column identification][intrinsic] · [Canonical orbit selector](../../../research/modules/construction/canonical_refill/main.tex#L30-L47) · [Orbit comparison and injectivity][orbit]

Mark the $s$-th position exactly when it is top. Use $(\lambda_0,-\lambda_{k-1})$ there for the two stored degrees and $(0,0)$ elsewhere. At every position put $w^{\rm rec}_j$ in the $j$-th filling slot, with $c$ as above, and keep $t,m$ from the original column. The preceding inequalities give a valid filling. The construction also gives the correct local level parameter $r_G$ below $p_0(k)$ and $1$ at and above it. For every admissible parameter realization on the same scale, its canonical orbit selector agrees with the minimum just chosen and its unshifted fillings on $1\le j\le m$ read the actual $w_j(\tau)$. Subtracting $\kappa_j$ proves the local agreement in both families of real positions. The $\tau_2$-positions are never top. Along $\tau_1(a_0+sh_p)$, top membership means $a_0+sh_p\in TT$, and the two stored degrees there are exactly $a_T,b_T$. These are all the lattice comparison clauses. Their definition depends only on the position map, orbit indices and fillings, so the assertion survives **every** later choice of local levels, maps and ramified submodules. [Filling on the column][bound] · [All lattice clauses][laws] · [Comparison in the two families][compare] · [Quantifiers after the choice][intrinsic]

For the kernel construction, the fourth-stage row supplies the prescribed active-set comparison, and the chosen divisor supplies $Q_p$. The jet and served-place constructions use these data to choose adapted bases, actual levels and local kernel maps. Install the first comparison maps and the prescribed ramified submodule. This keeps the displayed orbit indices, fillings and levels, hence the universal lattice comparison just proved. The kernel entry identity additionally uses those adapted bases and local kernel maps on this same divisor. The cloud and lattice argument requires only the frame, the explicit weight and scale estimates and the true column identification already present. Its bounds use integer absolute values of the true weights. The adelic volume and Petersson comparisons continue to be carried by the fourth-stage real and row data.
