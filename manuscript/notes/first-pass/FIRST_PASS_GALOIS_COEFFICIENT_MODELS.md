# One coefficient request for integral Galois models and absolute irreducibility

Fix the constructed first pass, its character level and the original finite set $W$ of active places. There is a finite request at each $w\in W$ with the following two consequences for **every** automorphic realization meeting it.

* Every continuous semisimple representation over $\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell$ matching a localized Hecke point on the prescribed arithmetic Frobenius elements is conjugate to a representation over the given valuation ring $O_i$.
* For every integral Hecke–Galois representation at a point, each irreducible subrepresentation over $K_i=\operatorname{Frac}(O_i)$ is absolutely irreducible.

Here $\ell$ is the residue characteristic at $w$, and each $O_i$ and its embedding are those of the realization under test. The two conclusions are the first two clauses of the source's large-coefficient condition. In the second clause many irreducible constituents and repeated constituents are allowed. ## A finite list before choosing the new fields

At $w$ fix the isomorphism $\iota_w:\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell\simeq\mathbf C$ and the original complex spaces of definite forms, one for each allowed row $j$. Remove the finitely many bad primes, including those over $\ell$, the marked primes and the conductors. The set can be chosen from the old rows before imposing a further coefficient request. On the remaining split primes a joint Hecke character on each original row specifies its normalized Frobenius polynomial. The commuting operators act on a finite-dimensional space. Their generated algebra is finite-dimensional, hence has only finitely many characters. Taking the union over the finitely many rows gives finitely many systems of polynomials at $w$. Each localized point in every admissible specialization occurs on one of these original rows and has that row's normalized eigenvalues on the common domain. This last fact uses the specified Hecke action and complex embedding of the localization. It is what makes the finite list independent of the future coefficient field. [Hecke action on the fitted row](FIRST_PASS_HECKE_LATTICE.md) · [Finite systems before specialization][systems] · [Matching local points on the common domain][matching]

Pull these polynomial systems back to $\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell$ by $\iota_w^{-1}$. For a fixed system $c$, consider the continuous semisimple representations of $G_E$ of the prescribed rank having these polynomials at the allowed split Frobenius elements. If there are any, they form one conjugacy class. Indeed the Frobenius elements in question meet every conjugacy class in each finite Galois quotient. To see this, pass to its normal closure over $E^+$ and use Chebotarev on a lift fixing $E$. The resulting prime splits in $E/E^+$ and the residue degree in that quadratic extension is one. Characteristic polynomials are continuous. Their equality therefore extends to the whole group. Brauer–Nesbitt in characteristic zero identifies the two semisimple representations. This argument is carried out inside the fixed $\ell$-adic closure. The abstract isomorphism $\iota_w$ only transports polynomial coefficients. ## A single field per realized system

Choose one representative for each nonempty class and decompose it over $\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell$ as a direct sum of irreducibles. Each is continuous. Its image is a compact subgroup of $\mathrm{GL}_a(\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell)$, so it is contained in $\mathrm{GL}_a(F)$ for a finite extension $F/\mathbf Q_\ell$. Inside $F^a$ there is a stable full lattice. For instance, compactness bounds all translates of $O_F^a$ inside $\pi^{-t}O_F^a$ for some $t$. Their $O_F$-span contains $O_F^a$, is finitely generated, and is stable under the group and its inverses. These two steps are exactly Lemmas 4.1 and 4.2 of Bary-Soroker, Fehm and Wiese. Choose lattice bases in the irreducibles and take the compositum $F_c$ of their fields. This gives the whole representative as a direct sum of integral models of its absolutely irreducible summands over $F_c$. [Bary-Soroker–Fehm–Wiese, §4.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1203.4217#page=11) · [The source's integral-model request][field] · [The source's splitting request][summands]

For each nonempty class put the $\iota_w$-image of a primitive generator of $F_c/\mathbf Q_\ell$ into the request $b_w$. Empty classes need no generators. Outside $W$ take the empty request. For any realization of this request, the specified image of $K_i$ in $\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell$ contains each $F_c$ at its place. Its valuation ring intersects $F_c$ in $O_{F_c}$ since the scalar embedding is compatible with $\mathbf Q_\ell$ and its valuation. Let $\rho$ over $\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell$ match a point $x$ in that realization. The polynomial comparison identifies $\rho$ with the chosen representative for $c$. Its basis thus conjugates $\rho$ into $\mathrm{GL}_n(O_i)$. The basis change is allowed over the algebraic closure, exactly as required by the integral-model clause.

Now take an integral point representation over $O_i$. Its fraction-field representation is semisimple by the point condition. It remains semisimple over the algebraic closure in characteristic zero. Over the closure it is isomorphic to the same representative. It is already isomorphic over $K_i$ to the direct sum of the chosen models extended to $K_i$. To see the descent, the intertwining matrices solve homogeneous linear equations over $K_i$. A finite subset spans their equation space since the matrix space is finite-dimensional. Extension of scalars therefore commutes with this space of intertwiners. The determinant polynomial on it is nonzero because of the invertible intertwiner over the closure. Since $K_i$ is infinite, the determinant is nonzero at some $K_i$-point. Each constituent of the resulting direct sum remains irreducible over the closure. Hence every simple subrepresentation over $K_i$ is absolutely irreducible. This argument puts no cyclicity or residual irreducibility condition on the original lattice. A bound on the degrees of the resulting fields as the first-pass state varies would require a separate estimate.
