# One regularized value for all translates of a small square kernel

Fix the Hida datum, grid, frame, tuple, matched tests, measures and both exterior endpoints of a square. On the finite-volume quotient by the rational points of its small intermediate group write $f(u)$ for the small kernel. For a finitely supported function $z$ on the adelic group write $R_z f(u)=\sum_g z(g)f(ug)$ and $d(z)=\sum_g z(g)$. When $R_z f$ is integrable put $I(z)=\int R_z f$. The square law quantifies over **every** such $z$, including combinations of degree zero. It is useful to separate the analytic input for their integrability from the proof that they give consistent values. [Actual kernel and square law][law]

## Independence of the regularizing place

Suppose $f$ is fixed on the right by the product of its lattice stabilizers outside a finite set of finite places. Suppose also that outside each prescribed finite set one can find a new place $v$ and a combination $w$ supported on elements acting only at $v$, such that $d(w)\ne0$ and $R_w f$ is integrable. Then there is a unique complex number $J$ with
$$ I(z)=d(z)J \qquad\text{whenever }R_z f\text{ is integrable}. $$

To see this, fix one successful $w_0$ at $v_0$ and set $J=I(w_0)/d(w_0)$. For a given $z$, every group element in its finite support belongs to the stabilizer at almost all finite places. Replace its components there by the identity, retaining its real component. Right invariance gives exactly the same translate of $f$ at every point. Adding coefficients for coincident representatives gives a combination $z'$ of the same degree, all of whose elements have identity components away from a single finite set and the infinite places. Choose $w$ at a place outside that set and different from $v_0$. Its support commutes pointwise both with that of $z'$ and with that of $w_0$. [Truncation on the actual square kernel][strip]

If the supports of $u$ and $v$ commute, with $R_u f,R_v f$ integrable, right invariance of quotient Haar measure gives
$$
 d(u)I(v)=\int R_u(R_v f)=\int R_v(R_u f)=d(v)I(u).
$$
Both iterated integrals are finite sums of translates of an integrable function. This avoids integrating the individual translates of the possibly nonintegrable $f$. Apply the identity to $(w,z')$ and $(w,w_0)$ and cancel $d(w)d(w_0)$. This gives $I(z)=d(z)J$, including $I(z)=0$ for every integrable degree-zero combination. The test $w_0$ proves uniqueness. In particular adding two local regularizers in different orders introduces no extra scalar. If $f$ itself is integrable then $J=\int f$, by taking the identity translate. This applies on every square in the last column because its small intermediate group is definite. ## Absolute convergence at a fresh split place

Fix one square, a tuple, admissible measures, matched tests and exterior endpoints. Write $n=a'$, $d=a+a'$ for the small and ambient ranks, with $a'<a$. Outside any prescribed finite set choose a completely split good place $v$ where the chosen small test has its lattice vacuum. The realized square triple supplies its small unitary Weil pair. In the barred split polarization all pure summands of this test have the same $v$-component $c\,1_{\Lambda^n}(PY)$, with $P$ invertible and $\Lambda$ the specified row lattice. [Choice of the fresh place][dc-place] · [Small Weil pair][dc-pair] · [Actual vacuum and local action][dc-vac]

Put $q=\#k_v$ for the residue cardinality, $\pi$ for a uniformizer, and $\nu$ for the unitary character in the left split action. There is a finite combination $z$ of translations at $v$ which replaces this lattice indicator by the indicator of rows independent modulo $\pi\Lambda$, with the same $c,P$. Indeed, after $i$ rows are independent modulo $\pi\Lambda$, subtract from the indicator that the next row lies in $\Lambda$ the sum of the $q^i$ indicators that it lies in a residue class in their span. Each subtraction is a shear followed by division of the new row by $\pi$. Undoing the Weil determinant multiplier of this operation gives $\nu(\pi)q^{-d/2}$. Thus on adjoining row $i+1$ the total coefficient sum is multiplied by $1-\nu(\pi)q^{i-d/2}$. Conjugating by the transpose of $P$ gives the prescribed basis change and preserves the coefficients. In particular
$$
 d(z)=\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}(1-\nu(\pi)q^{i-d/2})\ne0,
$$
since $i<d/2$ and $|\nu(\pi)|=1$. The result belongs to the primed Schwartz space, whose local definition is full row rank in this barred model. [Row subtraction][dc-row] · [Normalized degree][dc-deg] · [Conjugation][dc-conj] · [Definition of the primed space][dc-so]

For a primed Schwartz vector $\Phi$ and fixed exterior block $h$, the theta function $u\mapsto\theta(\Phi;u,h)$ is bounded on the whole intermediate quotient. To see the role of full rank, first split its local component into finitely many pieces supported, in the barred coordinates, on small compact open cosets $Y_0+L$. Compactness of the support in the full-rank locus allows one to choose $L$ so that every $2Y_0+L$ still consists of full-rank matrices. Pair a piece with its reflection under $x\mapsto-x$, which has the same theta sum. For any singular local index $Z$, a character of the local matrix space trivial on $L$ separates $Z-2Y_0$ from $L$. The resulting Heisenberg modulation multiplies the piece by a scalar. Covariance of the matrix coefficient forces the coefficient at $Z$ to vanish, since the two scalar factors differ. Poisson summation of the product of the two theta sums and the Schwartz bounds on its matrix coefficients now majorize $|\theta|^2$ by a constant times a lattice sum over **full-row-rank matrices in $M_{n,d}(E)$**. Finite sums of pieces satisfy the same bound by the triangle inequality, enlarging the compact finite support and constants. [Local separation][dc-cut] · [Vanishing by modulation][dc-van] · [Poisson bound][dc-poi] · [Full-rank majorant][dc-sq]

Fix $h$. On a unitary Siegel set write the intermediate representative as $A(s)\kappa$, where $s_1\ge\cdots\ge s_r\ge0$ are logarithmic Witt coordinates, and put $S=\sum s_i$. Left rational changes reindex the summation. The compact factor $\kappa$ and the singleton $\{h\}$ enlarge the finite support and the comparison constant, so the remaining torus parameters are $s$ and $0$. The finite support gives a common denominator for the entries in rational Witt bases. Summing the rapid archimedean majorant one coordinate at a time over this fractional lattice bounds the unrestricted sum by $C\exp([E:\mathbf Q]dS)$. For a rank-$n$ matrix the $r$ rows in the expanding directions of $A(s)^{-1}$ have a nonzero $r\times r$ minor. After clearing its denominator the determinant has norm at least one at some embedding. Under the diagonal action that minor gains $e^S$. Expanding the determinant bounds its archimedean size by a constant times $(1+\|A(s)^{-1}Y\|_\infty)^{n+1}$, using the sum of entry norms over the infinite places. Consequently $M(n+1)$ extra powers of Schwartz decay gain $e^{-MS}$ on every full-rank term, for any integer $M\ge0$. Take $M=[E:\mathbf Q]d$. The resulting bound is independent of $s$. The empty Witt frame gives $S=0$. This proves exactly the exponent-zero bound needed with these endpoints. The two-variable source argument also gives polynomial dependence on the exterior height. [Adelic reduction][dc-red] · [Compact factors and lattice sums][dc-lat] · [One-coordinate count][dc-one] · [Integral minor][dc-min] · [Taking extra Schwartz powers][dc-full] · [Two-variable estimate][dc-est]

In the square model the translated kernel has exactly the norm of this primed theta function. The endpoint character has absolute value one and is independent of the integration variable. Continuity and finite quotient volume therefore make $R_z f$ absolutely integrable. This supplies the fresh-place hypothesis for every realized input in the square law, independently of the proportionality of the two integrated kernels. The latter still requires their comparison on matched real and finite tests. [Norm comparison on the actual routes][dc-norm] · [Continuity and finite volume][dc-vol]

## One relation on the real polynomials for every tuple

The existence condition **ConstEx** requires a *single* real match for the given grid and frames, and permits a scalar $c_{ij,t}$ for each square and tuple. At each fixed square let $U_b,U_s$ be its real route polynomial spaces. The local comparison on the prescribed exponents supplies a nonzero common quotient $P$ and surjections $q_b:U_b\to P$, $q_s:U_s\to P$. The exponent calculation is independent of the finite twist. Use the relation
$$
       \mathcal R_{ij}=\{(a,b):q_b(a)=q_s(b)\}.
$$
Both coordinate projections are onto, and the relation is proper since $q_b$ is onto a nonzero space. It can therefore be installed as the real match on this square simultaneously for all tuples. Do this on the finite grid, keeping the given real vacua. This produces one match over the prescribed datum. [Construction before the twist and nonzero quotient][rel]

For this one relation, the further fixed-tuple comparison must supply a scalar $c$ working for every admissible measure, matched tensors and exterior endpoints. The comparison may initially deliver the equality for just one convergent combination $z_*$ of nonzero degree for **each** such input. It may choose the combination after seeing that input. Indeed, if $I_b$ denotes the big integral and $d(z_*)I_b=cI(z_*)$, the first part gives $I_b=cJ$. Hence
$$
             d(z)I_b=cI(z)
$$
for every convergent pair in the square law. This argument does not divide by $J$ and works when $J=0$. On the pinned local quotient the finite tensors supply an additional proportionality $\lambda$; the constant is the product of $\lambda$ and the comparison constant of the auxiliary global distributions. Nonvanishing of that product on the actual square is proved separately using a [nonzero matched witness](ROW_MATCHED_SQUARE_TESTS.md). Taking the maximum of the two lower thresholds at each stage gives the original large-scale order. [Tuple comparison on the fixed relation][tuple] · [Common match, scalar and all translates][paste] · [Use of ConstEx in choosing the coefficient field](ROW_GRID_CHOICE.md)

The remaining global work for the choice is to establish the real and finite quotient identifications and the one-witness proportionality for the integrated kernels. The explicit fresh-place operator above supplies the required absolute bound for each fixed pair of exterior endpoints. This proof extends that comparison to the full translate quantifier and makes the real choice simultaneously for the tuples. The rank recursions used to evaluate ratios of the resulting constants belong to the [next comparison](ROW_SQUARE_RATIOS.md).
