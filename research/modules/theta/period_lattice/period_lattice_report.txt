WHAT THIS PASS ACTUALLY PROVES

The section fragment period_lattice_section.tex proves a complete algebraic
piece of the intended RH argument. Given finite class models, rational common
theta quotients, the period comparison identity, local divisibility bounds,
and archimedean operator bounds, it constructs the K-bilinear maps B_u, proves
that they take all shifted source lattices into the dual of ONE row lattice,
and derives the precise quotient/chain norm bound. The lattice is constructed
explicitly by an intersection of localizations. The B_u are explicitly finite
algebraic class sums followed by canonical quotient descent.

This is stronger than naming a “common integral target” as an interface: the
descent and integrality mechanism is completely proved, and every object is
traced to concrete definitions. It does not prove the theta identity, the
rationality of the theta comparison matrices, or the local gains themselves.
The corresponding remaining inputs are PL6, PL13, PL15, and PL16 in the ledger.
No Lean compilation, kernel replay, or formal checking of the reconstruction
was performed. “Faithful translation” means checked against the cited source,
not independently verified from the Lean kernel in this session.

The fragment uses only v0.1 macros/environments and is sized for a 3–4 page
section before later integration edits. The provenance JSON records exact source
declarations, ranges, hashes, dependencies, and the distinction between
translated statements and our derived simplifications.

1. THE COEFFICIENT MODULES ARE CONCRETE POLYNOMIAL MODULES [PL1]

Let lambda_1 >= ... >= lambda_n be an integral dominant weight. Put
c=lambda_n and nu_i=lambda_i-c >= 0. For a matrix of variables Z=(z_ij), the
module nabla_nu over a coefficient ring A consists of polynomials which have
column multidegree nu and are unchanged by replacing column j by column j+t
times column l for every l<j, with t an indeterminate. This is an exact
translation of dualWeyl in GradedWeylDivisibility/StatementA.lean:24–29.

Write D_s(Z) for the determinant of the leading s by s submatrix and nu_{n+1}=0.
The highest-weight polynomial is

  h_nu(Z) = product_{s=1}^n D_s(Z)^(nu_s-nu_{s+1}).

For two row indices a,b, substitute row_b -> row_b+t row_a and take the
coefficient of t^k. This coefficient operator is the divided row operator
rowDiv(a,b,k). The integral Weyl module Delta_nu is the A-span of all finite
compositions of these operators applied to h_nu. This is literally the
definition of weyl, not an appeal to an unspecified representation-theoretic
construction. The determinant twist c is carried in the action separately.

For the CM-type embeddings tau:E->K, VV(K,lambda) is the tensor product of the
nabla_{nu_tau} over K, realized as polynomials in separate tau-blocks. The
standard integral companions NabStd and DelStd are the corresponding tensor
products over O_K of the dual-Weyl and Weyl modules. The polynomial action is
the product of determinant twists times block substitutions by transposed
matrices (CoefMod.actP). Source coefficients use the contragredient action,
implemented by inverse transpose; row coefficients use the direct action.

For a lattice L in E^n, tauLat(tau,L) is the O_K-span of its coordinatewise
image under tau. To transport NabStd or DelStd to a family of K-lattices M_tau,
the definition imposes the following at every finite place w and every
tuple B_tau of local basis matrices of M_tau:

  rho(B^{-1}) f belongs to the localization of NabStd (respectively DelStd).

The intersection of these conditions is transLat. Thus these coefficient
lattices are defined even when there is no global free O_K-basis. Independence
of the local basis and lattice/fullness properties are proved in the source;
their quantitative denominator and duality estimates are not reconstructed
in this pass [PL16].

For a decorated lattice d, define L(d) by the residue-functional kernel
conditions and Lsharp(d) as its hermitian dual. The column coefficient lattice
is DelAtr(lambda,Lsharp(d)); DelAtr applies Del to the ordinary bilinear duals
of tauLat(tau,Lsharp(d)). The row coefficient lattice is NabAt(sigma,N(d)).
This specifies precisely which of the two integral modules occurs on which
side. Replacing every lattice by a generic “integral automorphic form” would
lose information needed for divisibility.

2. THE FINITE CLASS MODEL AND THE PERIOD SUM [PL2, PL3, PL17]

The decorated point set is generally infinite. A point consists of a full
unimodular O_E-lattice N in E^n, a nonzero residue functional at each prime in
S_T, and a residue frame at each prime in S_G. The functionals/frames are
linear on the corresponding localizations; the formal definitions extend
them by zero off that localization. The rational isometry group of
sum_i conjugate(x_i)y_i acts, together with a finite residue level group.
The source proves finiteness of the orbit set and of each stabilizer, then
uses a choice of representative in each orbit. The finite-class assertion is
a real arithmetic fact; it is not implied merely by having written a quotient.

For any such G-set D with coefficient representation rho on V, define

  A = { f:D->V : f(gd)=rho(g)f(d) }.

Evaluation at orbit representatives d_c identifies A with the direct sum of
V^{Gamma_c}, where Gamma_c is the stabilizer. The integral function lattice
is specified pointwise using Nab(d) or Del(d). Fullness has a short independent
proof once the coefficient lattices are full: intersect each coefficient
lattice with V^{Gamma_c}; clearing denominators of a K-basis of this invariant
space proves it spans the invariant space. It is finitely generated because
O_K is Noetherian. Taking the finite direct sum proves the assertion. This
does not require averaging by 1/|Gamma_c| and introduces no new denominator.

A covariant kernel k(d,d'):V->V' acts by

  (T_k f)(d') = sum_c |Gamma_c|^{-1} k(d_c,d') f(d_c).

Its covariance is k(gd,g'd')=rho'(g') k(d,d') rho(g^{-1}). If d_c is replaced
by g d_c, equivariance of f cancels rho(g^{-1}), and the stabilizer order is
unchanged under conjugation. Thus the expression is independent of the
representatives and is equivariant at the target. Linearity is immediate.
The source uses matrices of these kernels for finite families of models.
The final coupled kernel has one further finite sum over an auxiliary set
Y(d_c), and the same intermediate stabilizer factor. All these sums are over
finite sets after the stated finiteness results; they are not infinite theta
series at this algebraic stage.

For the final period the coefficient pairing is

  b_alg(f,g) = c_lambda^{-1} sum_alpha alpha! f_alpha g_alpha,
  c_lambda = product_tau sum_alpha alpha! (h_{nu_tau})_alpha^2.

It is K-bilinear, with NO conjugation of f_alpha. The hermitian Fock pairing
used in the metrics is a different pairing. This distinction is necessary:
the rational period is to define an element of the algebraic dual, while a
complex conjugate-linear row realization later reconciles it with a Hilbert
inner product.

The branching map br_u in the final pairing is brAt evaluated at
tau(conjugate(pi_T(u))). Its domain is the terminal highest-weight module and
its codomain is the row highest-weight module. The finite period sums the
pairing of br_u applied to the terminal column value with the row value,
weighted by inverse stabilizer orders. DivisionHeightBoundStatement.colPer_eq
identifies this concrete period with the abstract chain-plus-final-pairing
factorization used by the norm theorem. The individual arithmetic theta
kernels inside the chain remain a separate reconstruction task [PL15].

3. SOURCE LATTICES: WHAT IS IDENTIFIED AND WHAT IS NOT [PL4, PL12–PL14]

The initial ramified source C_u is the intersection, inside the stage-zero
equivariant function space, of the kernels of Nm_P=sum_{a in kappa(P)^x}<a>
for P in Pr.vT(u). This set is defined as the prime divisors of the chosen
element piT(u); it is not automatically all primes above the rational T_u.
The underlying initial full lattice is MramL=ColL intersect C_u, interpreted
as a lattice in C_u. The coordinate basis ramBas is a K-basis of this subspace,
and the coordinate dimension ramDim is exactly dim_K C_u.

The coordinate lattice Nu in the final divisibility bridge is

  Cons.nuOf(L,u,eNu) = eNu^{-1}(L.NuLat(u)).

The record field L.NuLat is NOT definitionally MramL. NuDef says that, away
from the word's own type prime T_u, it is locally contained in MramL, and it
is locally equal to MramL at the specified discarded/non-inert places. It
also records the normalized local length. The theorem's main norm/rank
space is still C_u. Local inclusion at T_u is a separate hypothesis; one
must not silently deduce it from NuDef.

NprimeDef says that the final HLat source lattice D.div.Nu(u).L is
I_u L.NuLat(u), where

  I_u = product_w p_w^{-floor(e_w s_u(w))}.

The coordinate pullback commutes with ideal multiplication. This is proved
by shift_nuOf in DivisibilityConstruction/ShiftOfColTie.lean:53–79. The floor
is taken after multiplying by e_w. A positive shift enlarges the lattice at
that place; a negative shift shrinks it. Both cases are covered by the
common-integrality theorem.

There is a concrete global source-lattice constructor in NZColD.lean:
nuLat is the intersection of cond(u,w), where cond is

  at type prime T_u:    {x : nuIn(x) lies in the local scaled ramified lattice};
  at chosen gate primes: {x : cmpN(x) lies in the local gate lattice NPf};
  elsewhere:             the localization of MramL.

The scaled ramified lattice in the column model is the image of MramL under

  T_u^{expTc(u,w)} ( product_{P seen at w} U_P )^m,
  expTc(u,w)=m sum_{tau in topsP(u,w)} bTop(u,tau).

The U_P are finite neighbor-sum operators. The constructor uses finite-support,
local fullness/absorption, and saturation hypotheses. HOWEVER, the present
symbol search found sizeDataNu only in its defining file, and did not establish
that this constructor is the particular witness supplied along the headline
RH path. It is therefore reported as a concrete constructor available in the
repository [PL14], not asserted to be the chosen final witness. The exact
bridge witness L.NuLat and the transport of its gate conditions remain PL13.

Rank caution for integration with the local-cost section: duS is the full
complex dimension of Mu0, while du is the dimension of its ramified part.
The source's RamRank formula is

  du = duS (1 - 1/(T_u-1))^f.

The local length bound uses duS and the slope denominator uses the retained
ramified rank. Do not set their ratio to 1. Parent has independently detected
and corrected this issue in the local-cost reconstruction. Exact references
are included under PL4 and PL13.

4. THE ROW QUOTIENT, ITS LATTICE, AND THE COMMON K-SPACE [PL5, PL6]

Let G_0 be the finite residue group, X a finite set of selected irreducible
characters, P a level subgroup, and psi its one-dimensional level character.
The selected row space is the image of the product of two group-algebra
projectors:

  e_X = sum_{chi in X} (chi(1)/|G_0|) sum_{g in G_0} chi(g^{-1})g,
  e_{P,psi} = |P|^{-1} sum_{h in P} psi(h^{-1})h.

The source calls their product goodAt. These are auxiliary finite-group and
level characters; taking the target Dirichlet character to be trivial does
not by itself make these projectors trivial.

Let W_t be this selected space. Its lattice is the intersection of W_t with
the pointwise Nab lattice, in a chosen basis. Its hermitian form has the
additional 1/|P| normalization displayed in goodPartHermitianForm. Let Z_t be
the rational part of the range of the complex theta adjoint, and then take
its part inside W_t. The map pi_t is orthogonal projection to that part,
expressed in its own coordinates. If H is the metric matrix and Z a column
basis matrix, then

  pi_t = (Z^* H Z)^{-1} Z^* H.

The row quotient lattice is pi_t(M_t). It is NOT M_t intersect Z_t. The source
defines the latter separately (cutZ) and uses it to track denominator/index
costs. Confusing image and intersection would erase real arithmetic losses.

At the distinguished embedding the theta map on the selected quotient is
J_t=iC(t). Hypothesis H2 gives injectivity, equality of the images for all t,
and identification of that common image with the selected Hilbert row space.
RATmu says the comparison matrix J_0^{-1}J_t is K-rational. Then rowMat is that
matrix over K and furnishes iota_t. Its base value is the identity. The formal
definition kMat can select a fallback if rationality is absent, so reading
rowMat alone is NOT a proof of rationality. The H2 and RATmu hypotheses must
be carried explicitly.

5. CANONICAL B_u AND THE COMPLEX SCALAR BRANCH [PL7, PL8, PL15, PL18]

The actual rational definition is

  B_u(phi,y) = P_{u,t0}(phi, r y),  pi_{t0} r = 1.

The source chooses some right inverse using existence; uniqueness of descent
shows that the result is independent of that choice. The kernel-annihilation
proof reduces to orthogonality to theta-adjoint images, as proved in the main
fragment. This is a genuine simplification of the source's coordinate proof.

The input comparison identity MIid is more precise than an equality of
unscaled operators. In its notation,

  xi_{u,t} sigma(P_{u,t}(phi,F))
    = a_u < sEE(V Theta_t(jR(eV_t F))), Pu_u(U_u sigma(phi)) >.

Here U_u and V are norm-preserving on the required spaces, sEE is
conjugate-linear with a specified common normalization, and a_u is nonzero.
There is no assertion that a_u has absolute value 1. The conjugate-linearity
of sEE is what makes the right hand side bilinear in phi and F, under the
source's inner-product convention. The common-image/rationality facts and
orthogonal quotient compatibility turn this into equation (pl-complex) in
the main fragment.

MIUnit2 is weaker than the earlier root-of-unity condition MIUnit. It requires
nonzero complex xi and, when xi is the image of an element of K at the selected
finite-place parameter, that element must have valuation zero. It does not
require rationality of every xi as a separate hypothesis. The formal proof
uses exactly the zero-period branch reconstructed in Proposition pl-descent.
If a period value is nonzero, xi is forced to be a quotient of two K-values.
If no such value exists, the factorization holds with xi replaced by 1.

The scalar in PairingFactorsThroughPeriods is eta=xi^{-1}, rather than xi.
Both orientations have valuation zero, but the inversion matters in an exact
formula. The main fragment uses eta consistently.

6. THE COMMON ROW LATTICE AND INTEGRAL PAIRING [PL9, PL10]

The source's RR is exactly

  R = intersection_w ( iota_{mu(w)} pi_{mu(w)} M_{mu(w)} )_w.

All localizations are inside the same rational K-vector space, over the DVR
O_{K,w}; these are not completions. The map mu equals the base parameter away
from a finite set. Surjectivity of pi and invertibility of iota imply fullness
of all candidate lattices. The complete gluing proof in the main fragment
uses the primary decomposition of d^{-1}L_0/dL_0 and proves both the exact
localization identities and uniqueness. It is a basis-free reconstruction of
the source's LatticeGlue/RR_spec arguments.

The global integrality proof uses only:

  (i)   eta_{u,mu(w)} is a w-unit;
  (ii)  the local period valuation is >=sigma_u(w), unless it vanishes;
  (iii) N'_u=I_u N_u and ord_w(I_u)=-floor(sigma_u(w));
  (iv)  R_w is the selected projected row lattice at w.

After localization, (i) transfers the period bound to B_u. Valuations are
integer-valued, so the bound is actually ceil(sigma_u(w)). The shift in (iii)
leaves a nonnegative valuation. Intersecting all valuation rings gives O_K.
The vanishing case is essential because the formal valuation function is
not being interpreted as +infinity at zero in all source interfaces.

The same theorem works for arbitrary finitely supported integer shifts
k_u(w)<=ceil(sigma_u(w)), including ceil itself. This is already allowed by
the formal DivIntoDualShift statement. The use of floor in the actual family
must therefore be attributed to its chosen gain/rounding bookkeeping, not
to a limitation of the integrality argument. This pass does not propose
changing the global source construction to ceiling shifts; other tied records
and quantitative estimates would need to be checked first.

7. ARCHIMEDEAN QUOTIENT NORM AND HEIGHT [PL11, PL20]

For a surjective linear map pi:W->Q with the quotient metric, every q has a
unique lift in (ker pi)^perp of the same norm. If a functional kills ker pi,
its descended functional has exactly the same operator norm: one inequality
uses the contraction pi, the other this minimal lift. This is the exact norm
descent in the source, not an extra estimate hiding a dimension factor.

For a chain T_r...T_1 and final bilinear pairing P_end, the main fragment
therefore gives

  ||B_u||_v^2 <= p_v^2 product_j a_{j,v}^2 / s_v,

where p_v bounds the final pairing norm, a_{j,v} bounds each chain operator
norm, and iota_0 expands squared quotient norm by at least s_v. Although
iota_0 is the identity in coordinates, its two metric spaces differ.

The source obtains s_v=kappa_v min_p product_j lambda_{j,p,v} from its spectral
scalar comparisons. Here p ranges over a finite nonempty set, kappa_v>0, and
every lambda_{j,p,v}>0. To use this with an orthogonal decomposition, the pieces
must span the quotient; the source's broader
IsScalarOn formulation supplies the same lower singular-value conclusion.
In either formulation one must establish the lower norm comparison. It is
not justified merely by listing positive numbers called eigenvalues.

Taking the mean over ALL infinite places of the logarithm of this inequality
is precisely the pointwise chain-height bound in DivisionHeightBoundStatement.
The average of the minimum over p occurs; interchanging mean and minimum
would give an unjustified stronger bound. Our formula keeps the correct order.
For B_u=0, the pointwise norm inequality remains valid but the ordinary
logarithmic height is not defined via log 0. The source handles this by choosing
positive majorants cu(v), with a specified mean logarithm, in DivClauses.

8. THE EXACT OUTSTANDING RECONSTRUCTION TASKS

A. [PL6, PL15] Expand the proofs that the selected complex theta-adjoint images
are rational, that the J_t images coincide, and that J_0^{-1}J_t lies in K.
Then derive MIid and its finite-place unit comparison property from the actual
theta/period construction. The present work follows them to their exact
interfaces and gives the complete consequences; it has not proved them.

B. [PL13] Identify the concrete witness L.NuLat supplied by the final family
construction and transport its local gate lattices to the ramified source
coordinates. The available NZColD constructor is useful, but its connection
to the headline route is unproved in this reconstruction. Do not substitute
it silently. Once tied, its global gluing requires no new conceptual theorem.

C. [PL15, PL16] Expand the individual kernel formulas and prove their local
division and archimedean norm estimates. Stabilizer orders, Weyl versus
dual-Weyl lattices, the row projected-lattice denominators, and type-prime
scaled U-operators are all concrete locations where losses can arise. Parent
owns the local accounting/valuation reconstruction, so this report does not
duplicate those estimates or presume they vanish in ordinary RH.

D. [PL19, PL20] Check the final record ties and uniform parameter quantifiers.
DIVRecBridgeSel is a compatibility/package bridge, not a new explicit kernel
formula. Its DivisionKernelConstantBounds requires a package for every late
family member and supplies rationality/fullness fields; linkDIVt_of_bridge_kernel
then transports these to LinkDIVt. DivisionHeightBoundStatement.DivClauses
exports common integrality, pointwise positive norm majorants with the required
mean logarithm, the numerical row/divisibility inequality, and the exact shift
slope identity. Those four conclusions should remain distinct in the final
provenance, rather than being summarized as a single unexamined “theta bound.”

9. ORDINARY RH SPECIALIZATION

The present gluing/descent proof is independent of the order o of the target
Dirichlet character. Setting o=1 reduces the outer analytic target to zeta,
but does not itself delete the auxiliary type primes, residue-unit averaging,
the finite-group good-part projector, the local row choice mu(w), or the
theta chain. No new claim that these inputs disappear has been made. This
section is useful for ordinary RH because it isolates a short reusable algebraic
argument whose hypotheses can now be checked in that specialization.
