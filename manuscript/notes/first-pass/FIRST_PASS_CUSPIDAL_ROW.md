# The cuspidal parameter of an occurring good row

Fix the [first labelled state](FIRST_PASS_LABELLED_STATE.md) on the pinned column, with the standing hypotheses of its [geometric starting state](FIRST_PASS_STARTING_STATE.md) and the published theta, local correspondence and definite base-change inputs specified below. Let $F=E^+$ and take a finite type $\sigma$ for which $\operatorname{isot}(j,\sigma)\ne0$ in some row $1\le j\le m$. If its type is good, there is a unique isomorphism class of regular algebraic conjugate-self-dual cuspidal representations $\Pi_\sigma$ of $\mathrm{GL}_r(\mathbb A_E)$ with the prescribed split parameters. It is unramified at all nonsplit places and at split places away from the two type primes. Writing $v_i$ and $b_h$ for the prescribed row weights, at each chosen CM embedding its infinitesimal character is $\{v_i\}_i\sqcup\{-b_h\}_h$, and at the conjugate embedding all signs reverse. The conclusion concerns every such occurrence, even when the list of auxiliary local records is empty. [Row quantifiers and normalizations][claim]

## One definite constituent on all split places

Take a nonzero $y$ in the isotypic space of $\sigma$ in row $j$. Let $\Theta$ denote the theta map at the reference tuple. In the concrete reading the target is spanned by the pinned label spaces, and $\Theta\Theta^*$ acts on each by its strictly positive reference scalar. Each label space consequently lies in the image of $\Theta$. A read vector killed by $\Theta^*$ is orthogonal to that image and hence zero. The positive norm factor of the row reading then gives injectivity on this space. Thus $x=\Theta^*y$ is nonzero. The realization of the multiplicity space has image the actual fixed-type space. Its intersections with the full isotypic spaces of irreducible automorphic representations span it. Because $\langle x,x\rangle\ne0$, some such intersection contains a vector $f$ with $\langle f,x\rangle\ne0$. Fix its irreducible class $S$. This selection occurs before fixing any local place. **Split places.** Fix a split $w$ and let $\tau_w$ and $S_w$ be the local components of the row and of the selected definite constituent. The reference trilinear theta form reads the nonzero pairing with $f,y$ on its distinguished test. The distinguished test is spanned by images of the split matrix Schwartz space. The two isotypic readings are spanned by local intertwiners from $S_w$ and $\tau_w$. By multilinearity we can choose one image in each variable on which the form remains nonzero. Acting on these variables by adeles supported only at $w$ gives the local invariant form with the prescribed splitting characters. Smooth admissibility and unitarisability on the row side turn it into the local theta pairing of $\tau_w$ with $S_w\otimes(\eta_w\circ\det)$. This argument applies also to the marked split places. Write $\mu_j$ for the prescribed reference character $\mu_{0,j-1}$ at row $j$, and let $\mu$ be its image under the rank-one correspondence. In the range $r<t$, the general-linear theta calculation gives the normalized induced product $\tau_w\times(\mu_w\circ\det_t)$ for the twisted $S_w$. Here $\tau_w$ is unitarisable and $\mu_w$ is unitary. The actual isotypic occurrence gives a vector fixed by the maximal compact off the marked primes and by the first principal congruence subgroup at the marked split primes. These fixed vectors give the eligibility needed to twist the restricted local correspondence back by $\eta_w^{-1}$. Its product and determinant laws and the pinned split parameter readings now give
$$
 \operatorname{par}(S_w)=
 \left(\operatorname{par}(\sigma_w)\ \oplus\

    \otimes\eta_w^{-1}.
$$
The summand for the character of the determinant has zero monodromy. **Nonsplit places and infinity.** The character $\mu$ is conjugate self-dual because its restriction to the real subfield's ideles is trivial. Both $\mu$ and $\eta^{-1}$ are unramified at nonsplit places by the character frame and the splitting of the marked primes. At each such place the fixed reference type gives a nonzero hyperspecial-fixed vector in $S$ by projection from its nonzero isotypic intersection. The exponents of $\eta^{-1}$ are $-1/2,+1/2$ at the two CM embeddings. To compute the infinitesimal character take the dual coefficient weight of $S$ in the actual multiplicity family. At the chosen embedding its multiset is the row multiset shifted by $-1/2$, plus the centered $t$-string for $\mu\eta^{-1}$. At the conjugate embedding the row multiset is negated and shifted by $+1/2$, with the corresponding character string there. These computations use the weight comparison on the selected reference space. They complete the definite-lift assertion under the pinned theta model, level and multiplicity readings, restricted local correspondence and the stated smooth representation theory. Extracting the general-linear cuspidal row parameter is a subsequent use of this witness. ## The spherical bounds away from the marked primes

Let $E/E^+$ be the CM extension of the [labelled first-pass state](FIRST_PASS_LABELLED_STATE.md), $g_0,g_1$ its two type primes, and $R_{\sigma,w}$ the installed rank-$r$ row parameter at the finite place $w$ of $E$. For **every** row type $\sigma$ and **every** $w$ over a split place of $E^+$ with $w\nmid g_0g_1$, put $q=\#k_w$. Then $R_{\sigma,w}$ is unramified and its arithmetic Frobenius roots satisfy
$$
 q^{-(r-1)/2}\ \le\ |\alpha|\ \le\ q^{(r-1)/2}
 \ \le\ q^{-3}q^{(t-1)/2}.
$$
The strict upper bound $|\alpha|<q^{(t-1)/2}$ is the local input to the cuspidal parameter extraction. Both primes in each eligible split pair are covered. The statement has no coefficient prime, auxiliary lower bound on $q$, or goodness hypothesis on $\sigma$. [Definition of the required bound][sph-row] · [Application to the chosen first-pass state][sph-use]

**The actual fixed vector.** Write $v=w|_{E^+}$. The choice of $w$ identifies the split unitary group at $v$ with $\mathrm{GL}_r(E_w)$. In the prescribed finite frame its local lattice stabilizer is conjugate to $K_w=\mathrm{GL}_r(\mathcal O_{E_w})$. An element supported at $v$ in this conjugate stabilizer preserves the framed lattice at both $w$ and $\bar w$. In the conjugate component this follows by taking its inverse and using the Hermitian form. At every principal-level prime its reduction is the identity, since those primes lie over $g_0$ or $g_1$ and lie outside the fibre of $v$. These elements therefore belong to **both** candidate row levels and are in the kernel of each level character. Each type $\sigma$ occurring in the row reading occurs at one of these two levels. Projecting a nonzero vector of the occurring type to a nonzero irreducible copy gives a vector in the row space fixed by the conjugate stabilizer. Translate by a finite adele supported at $v$ to obtain a nonzero vector fixed by $K_w$. [Lattice at $w$, its conjugate and the level-character calculation][sph-lev] · [Occurrence and fixed vector][sph-vec] · [Translation on the actual row][sph-tr]

The cuspidal row realization has a positive definite Hermitian pairing invariant under finite right translations. The pinned construction restricts its automorphic pairing to a copy of the irreducible row and transports it to the chosen model. Here it uses the Harish-Chandra finiteness and cuspidal rapid-decay inputs together with the row's Haar measure and fundamental domain. Its split local component $\tau_w$ exists by the local decomposition of the row. The fixed-vector calculation makes $\tau_w$ spherical. Its equivariant local embedding into the row space pulls the global pairing back to a positive invariant Hermitian form on $\tau_w$. Thus $\tau_w$ is unitarizable. The first-pass row identification reads $R_{\sigma,w}$ under the installed Weil–Deligne identification as the local Langlands parameter of this very $\tau_w$. [Local decomposition][sph-decomp] · [Invariant form and its restriction][sph-form] · [Identification inherited from the labelled state][sph-id]

**The bound from the spherical unitary dual.** In the spherical correspondence $\tau_w$ has an unramified parameter, and arithmetic Frobenius reads the inverses of the normalized Satake roots. Tadić's classification gives a short proof of the size bound. A unitary unramified determinant character on a block of rank $d$ has Satake absolute values $q^{(d-1)/2},q^{(d-3)/2},\ldots,q^{-(d-1)/2}$. A complementary pair of rank $2d$ consists of two such strings shifted by exponents $s$ and $-s$ for $0<s<1/2$. Its exponents have magnitude at most $(d-1)/2+s<d/2\le(2d-1)/2$. The classification supplies every irreducible spherical unitary $\mathrm{GL}_r$ representation by normalized parabolic induction from these blocks. The Satake multiset is their union, so every exponent has magnitude at most $(r-1)/2$. Inversion preserves that interval. Finally, $r=k+m-1$ and $t$ are odd, and the original rank hypothesis gives $t\ge r+5$. Thus $t-r\ge6$, which gives the displayed factor $q^{-3}$. This proof allows non-tempered spherical constituents. The pin obtains the same bound from the spherical Hecke operators acting on lattice translates of a unit vector. [Tadić, theorem on p. 49][sph-tadic] · [Arithmetic normalization on the row][sph-rec] · [Pinned lattice argument][sph-lattice] · [Rank hypotheses][sph-rank]

One can also see the bound directly in the lattice model of the pin. Let $H_m$ sum the translates of the spherical vector over sublattices of colength $m$, and let $T_i$ sum over sublattices with quotient $k_w^i$. The lattice identity
$$
 \left(\sum_{i=0}^{r}(-1)^i q^{\binom i2}T_i X^i\right)
 \left(\sum_{m\ge0}H_m X^m\right)=1
$$
follows by fixing the terminal sublattice. For a proper sublattice the intermediate elementary quotients run over the quotients of a nonzero residue vector space of dimension $d$. Their signed sum is $\prod_{j=0}^{d-1}(1-q^j)=0$ by the finite $q$-binomial identity. On the spherical line $T_i$ acts by $q^{i(r-i)/2}e_i(\beta)$ for the Satake roots $\beta$. The scalar series of $H_m$ is therefore $\prod_{\beta}(1-q^{(r-1)/2}\beta X)^{-1}$. Its $m$th coefficient is bounded in absolute value by the number of index-$q^m$ sublattices because every translate preserves the norm of the vector. Triangular lattice bases bound this number by $\binom{m+r-1}{r-1}q^{m(r-1)}$. The series thus converges for $|X|<q^{-(r-1)}$. Its numerator is $1$, so no reciprocal root is cancelled and $|\beta|\le q^{(r-1)/2}$. Applying the same argument to the unitary contragredient bounds the inverse roots as well. [Lattice recurrence and coefficient growth][sph-recurrence]

## The definite transfer and the rank-one block

Apply strong definite base change to $S$. On this host $E/F$ is unramified at finite places and $[F:\mathbf Q]$ is even. The definite group is in the identity-form frame. The transfer is a list $\boxplus_i\rho_i[d_i]$ of conjugate-self-dual unitary cuspidal blocks. Their ranks $a_i$ satisfy $\sum_i a_i d_i=r+t$. The theorem compares all split local components, the spherical nonsplit places and the infinitesimal characters. Its use with the restricted local dictionary of the labelled state is part of the third-place comparison. ### Removing the long character string

At a common unramified split prime $w$ away from the type primes, put $Q=Nw$. The moduli of the row roots are at most $Q^{(r-1)/2}$ by spherical unitarity, strictly below $Q^{(t-1)/2}$ since $r<t$. The reference string has a unique root of that largest modulus, with value $Q^{(t-1)/2}\alpha_w(\mu_j\eta^{-1})$, where $\alpha_w$ denotes the unramified character root. For the unitary cuspidal base of each block, the Luo–Rudnick–Sarnak bound gives the strict upper bound $Q^{1/2}$ for the modulus of each unramified root. The same bound applies to inverse roots by passing to the contragredient. Thus if every $d_i<t$, every root on the transfer side has modulus strictly below the observed maximum. There is a block of length at least $t$. Its underlying rank must be one and it is unique, since $r+t<2t$. Its underlying character is unitary, so its length is exactly $t$ and equality of the extremal root identifies its character at $w$. This holds at almost all split primes. Quadratic split-prime multiplicity one for characters identifies the global block as $(\mu_j\eta^{-1})[t]$. [Row bound][rowbound] · [Luo–Rudnick–Sarnak, *On the generalized Ramanujan conjecture for GL(n)*](https://doi.org/10.1090/pspum/066.2/1703764) · [Extremal comparison][openbound] · [Global character identification][char] · [Ramakrishnan, Corollary B](https://www.its.caltech.edu/~dinakar/papers/mildTcheb-JNT.pdf#page=2)

Delete that block and twist the remainder by $\eta$. Direct-sum cancellation in the split Weil–Deligne parameters identifies the resulting rank-$r$ list with $R_{\sigma,w}$ at every split $w$. To see why the list is cuspidal, take the first type prime $g_0$. At each marked place $v$ over it the specified tame polynomial has the factorization $f_v=\prod_i f_{i,v}^{d_i}$ of the remaining list, with $\deg f_{i,v}\le a_i$. Since $\deg f_v=r=\sum_i a_i d_i$, all these inequalities are equalities. Goodness gives squarefreeness and hence $d_i=1$ for each $i$. If two or more blocks remained, the smallest rank $a$ would be at most $r/2$ and would give a degree-$a$ divisor of $f_v$ at **every** marked $v$ over $g_0$. The good-type condition supplies a place where no such divisor exists. The single remaining block is therefore a cusp of rank $r$. At infinity subtract the same known string and undo the two opposite half-shifts of $\eta^{-1}$ to obtain the displayed row weights, which are regular algebraic. Unramifiedness at nonsplit places descends to this direct summand and survives the unramified twist. [Good-polynomial argument][good] · [Split cancellation, infinity and unramifiedness][remainder]

The fitted dictionary realizes this cusp as an eligible automorphic class. Two cuspidal candidates, even if obtained from different $j$ for the same $\sigma$, agree at all but finitely many relative degree-one places of the quadratic extension $E/F$ by their prescribed split components. Ramakrishnan's Corollary B identifies them globally. This proves the existence input in the [coefficient-field calculation](FIRST_PASS_CUSPIDAL_COEFFICIENTS.md) on the stated fitted geometry. Existence uses the actual nonzero theta image, its matching at every split place, and strong definite transfer. Uniqueness already follows from the spherical split entries. The source assembles the geometric comparisons from the column pin, row realization and existence/intertwining data, the local and global group identifications, and the labelled state's row and parameter dictionaries, under its stated published inputs.
