# Filtered dimensions and Weil–Deligne parameters on the first-pass state

Let $E$ be the CM field of the [starting state](FIRST_PASS_STARTING_STATE.md), $\Omega$ its specified algebraic closure, and $A_w\subset\Omega$ the specified valuation ring above each finite place $w$. The [row-label construction](FIRST_PASS_ROW_LABELS.md) identifies the new local parameter set at $w$ with the isomorphism classes of Frobenius-semisimple Weil–Deligne representations at $A_w$. Write $T_w$ for this identification. We construct the Galois-side parameters and prove the filtered-dimension identity on this same state. For every prime $\ell$, algebraic isomorphism $\iota:\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell\simeq\mathbb C$, continuous representation $r:G_E=\operatorname{Gal}(\Omega/E)\to\mathrm{GL}_a(\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell)$, and embedding $\tau:E\hookrightarrow\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell$ inducing $w$, the identity is
$$
 r|_{G_{E_w}}\text{ de Rham}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \#J_\tau(r)=a.
$$
Cardinality counts multiplicities. The first-pass interface later quantifies over abstract homomorphisms as well. Its choice of de Rham predicate is specified below. [Exact cardinality statement][card-st] · [The original valuation in the parameter identification][wd-tie]

## The prescribed place, embedding and finite coefficient field

The test that $\tau$ induces $w$ is $x\in w\ \Longleftrightarrow\ |\tau(x)|_\ell<1$ for $x\in\mathcal O_E$. Applying this to $\ell$ gives $w\mid\ell$. Extend $\tau$ algebraically to $j_0:\Omega\to\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell$. The valuation ring pulled back by $j_0$ and $A_w$ have the same restriction to $E$, namely $\mathcal O_{E,w}$. Transitivity of $G_E$ on extensions of this valuation lets us precompose $j_0$ with an element of $G_E$ so that the resulting $j$ satisfies *both*
$$
 j|_E=\tau,\qquad A_w=\{x\in\Omega:\ |j(x)|_\ell\le1\}.
$$
The closure $K$ of $\tau(E)$ in $\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell$ is its completion $E_w$ in this embedding. Indeed $\mathbb Q_\ell\tau(E)$ is a finite complete extension of $\mathbb Q_\ell$ and $\tau(E)$ is dense in it. The usual local decomposition-group isomorphism carries the stabilizer of this very $A_w$ to $G_K$. In this presentation the local embedding extending $\tau$ is the inclusion $K\subset\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell$. [Construction with prescribed $\tau$, $A_w$ and decomposition group][cj-emb]

There is a finite extension $L/\mathbb Q_\ell$, inside this algebraic closure, containing the normal closure of $K$ and every matrix entry of $r(G_E)$ in the given basis. Here the group structure matters. Take a countable cofinal family of finite subextensions for finite sets of elements of $\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell$. For instance, by density and Krasner's lemma each element belongs to an extension generated by an element of a fixed embedded $\overline{\mathbb Q}$; take finite composita of these extensions. The inverse images of $\mathrm{GL}_a(L_i)$ are closed subgroups covering the compact Hausdorff group $G_E$. Baire's theorem gives one with interior, hence an open subgroup of finite index. Adjoin the matrix entries of finitely many coset representatives and the normal closure of $K$. This gives $L$ and a continuous representation on $L^a$ whose scalar extension is exactly $r$. In particular the original $G_E$, its matrices and $A_w$ make sense throughout the comparison. [Finite coefficient model][cj-model] · [Countability and Baire argument][comp-mod]

For every fixed $\ell$ the construction of the parameter side may choose valuation-compatible bridges for all $w\mid\ell$ at once. A finite compositum contains their required local splitting fields because there are only finitely many such $w$. The coefficient field for a particular representation can be enlarged further. Two such models have identical matrices after passing to their compositum. Filtered invariants commute with this finite coefficient extension, as is seen by expanding a vector in a coefficient-field basis. Two embeddings realizing $A_w$ identify the same decomposition group with conjugate local presentations. Conjugation gives the isomorphism of period rings with their Galois actions and filtrations, and transports the labelled local embedding by its restriction to $E$. Thus a calculation with the special bridge realizing $\tau$ gives the jump multiplicities for the side already installed. This compatibility is part of what must be verified in constructing a side. The abstract cardinality lemma subsequently applies it to *any* side satisfying that compatibility. [Choice of simultaneous bridges and comparison with a second model][bridges] · [Coefficient and integral model clauses][wd-loc]

## One labelled component and its jumps

Use the finite coefficient model $V=L^a$, restricted to $G_K$, and the period data $D.\mathrm{loc}(\ell)$ tied to Fontaine's period rings. Put
$$
 D_{\mathrm{dR}}(V)=(B_{\mathrm{dR}}\otimes_{\mathbb Q_\ell}V)^{G_K},
 \qquad
 D_{\sigma}=\{x\in D_{\mathrm{dR}}(V): kx=\sigma(k)x\ \ (k\in K)\}
$$
for each $\sigma:K\hookrightarrow L$. The two actions in the last equality come respectively from the period ring and from the coefficient field. The filtration on $D_\sigma$ is induced from the filtration on $B_{\mathrm{dR}}$. The actual definition of de Rham imposes the *total* dimension
$$
 \dim_L D_{\mathrm{dR}}(V)=[K:\mathbb Q_\ell]a.
$$
Since $K\otimes_{\mathbb Q_\ell}L=\prod_{\sigma:K\hookrightarrow L}L$, the total space is the direct sum of the $D_\sigma$. Each summand has dimension at most $a$. To see the bound, use Fontaine's injective period comparison $B_{\mathrm{dR}}\otimes_K D_{\mathrm{dR}}(V)\to B_{\mathrm{dR}}\otimes_{\mathbb Q_\ell}V$. Choose an embedding $h:L\hookrightarrow B_{\mathrm{dR}}$ such that $h\sigma$ is the structural embedding of $K$. Project in $B_{\mathrm{dR}}\otimes_{\mathbb Q_\ell}L=\prod_hB_{\mathrm{dR}}$ to the idempotent for $h$. This gives an injection of $B_{\mathrm{dR}}\otimes_{L,h}D_\sigma$ into $B_{\mathrm{dR}}^a$. There are $[K:\mathbb Q_\ell]$ summands. Under the de Rham hypothesis their dimensions sum to the sum of their upper bounds, so $\dim_L D_\sigma=a$ for each $\sigma$. [Total dimension][total] · [Labelled eigenspace and filtration][fil] · [Component dimension theorem in the tied period data][components] · [Fontaine, Exposé III, §§1.4–1.5 and 3.7–3.8][font-dr]

Take $\sigma$ to be the inclusion of $K$ into $L$ on the bridge constructed for $\tau$. In the source convention, the multiplicity of $n\in\mathbb Z$ in $J_\tau(r)$ is
$$
 m_\tau(n)=\dim_L\operatorname{Fil}^{n}D_\sigma-
           \dim_L\operatorname{Fil}^{n+1}D_\sigma .
$$
The integer is the decreasing filtration index. The induced filtration is exhaustive and separated. Finite dimensionality gives integers $u<v$ with $\operatorname{Fil}^{u}D_\sigma=D_\sigma$ and $\operatorname{Fil}^{v}D_\sigma=0$. Thus
$$
 |J_\tau(r)|=\sum_{u\le n<v}m_\tau(n)=\dim_LD_\sigma=a .
$$
This also covers a repeated jump and a rank-zero representation. Only the equality with these actual local filtration multiplicities and the component dimension enter this argument. No particular list of automorphic weights is required. [Multiplicity convention][mult] · [Telescoping and return to the prescribed global representation][cj-card]

## The local parameter on either side of $\ell$

For $w\nmid\ell$, restrict $r$ to the decomposition group of $A_w$. The residue field of the local base field is finite. Grothendieck's local monodromy theorem gives unipotent action on an open inertia subgroup. In a tame $\ell$-adic coordinate $t_\ell$ this action is $\exp(t_\ell(s)N)$. Removing that exponential gives a smooth Weil action. Use $\iota$ on its coefficients and take Frobenius semisimplification, retaining $N$. Inertia acts on the resulting smooth Weil representation with finite image. Consequently $N=0$ exactly when the original inertia image is finite. Unramifiedness requires $N=0$ and trivial smooth inertia, exactly trivial original inertia. Frobenius semisimplification preserves the inertia action and the Frobenius eigenvalues. For any inertia element the exponential factor is commuting and unipotent, so the characteristic polynomial equals that of its smooth action. At an unramified place the resulting polynomial is $\iota(\det(X-r(\mathrm{Frob}_w)))$, with the source's arithmetic Frobenius $x\mapsto x^{\#k_w}$ on the residue field. Changing the extension of the valuation conjugates the Frobenius lift up to inertia and gives the same polynomial. [Grothendieck's proposition in Serre–Tate, appendix, p. 515][groth] · [Away-from-$\ell$ parameter identities][away] · [Frobenius convention][frob]

For $w\mid\ell$ and continuous de Rham $r|_{G_K}$, Berger's $p$-adic monodromy theorem makes $V$ semistable over a finite extension of $K$. It applies to the underlying $\mathbb Q_\ell$-representation. The commuting $L$-action on the periods recovers the coefficient-field construction. Fontaine's $D_{\mathrm{pst}}(V)$ with its Frobenius, monodromy and descent action gives a Weil–Deligne representation of dimension $a$ with these coefficients. Use $\iota$ and Frobenius-semisimplify. In this construction $N=0$ is equivalent to $V$ being potentially crystalline. Indeed over a field of semistability $\ker N$ in the semistable periods gives the crystalline periods there. Descent to $K$ gives a crystalline representation over $K$ precisely when $N=0$ and the descent inertia on $D_{\mathrm{pst}}(V)$ acts trivially. This is exactly the unramified condition on its Weil–Deligne parameter. In the second criterion the inertia action is on the period module. [Berger, Theorem 0.5][berger] · [Fontaine, Exposé VIII, §§2.3.3 and 2.3.7][font-wd] · [Coefficient-field version][coeff-berger] · [The two crystalline criteria][above]

In both constructions apply $T_w^{-1}$ to put the result in the installed local parameter set. The interface assigns a parameter of dimension $a$ even when its usual construction has no input. In particular above $\ell$ it uses a trivial parameter of dimension $a$ when there is no potentially semistable model. Its definition of the jump multiset returns the empty multiset when there is no eligible filtered model. The local laws above have their continuity and admissibility hypotheses. This convention therefore supplies total functions without giving a de Rham or automorphic origin to arbitrary abstract homomorphisms. ## Why the primed first-pass statement holds

For the original fixed embedding $j_E:E\to\overline{\mathbb Q}$, an isomorphism $\Omega\simeq\overline{\mathbb Q}$ extending $j_E$ exists by uniqueness of algebraic closures. This is the global frame. It is distinct from the local embedding $j$ chosen for $\tau,A_w$, and from the algebraic relabelling $\iota$ of coefficients. The first-pass construction takes this frame on the newly labelled data and forms a side $W'$ from its constructed side $W$ by the rule
$$
 \mathrm{dR}_{W'}(\ell,\iota,r,w)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 r\text{ is continuous on }G_E\ \text{ and }\ \mathrm{dR}_{W}(\ell,\iota,r,w).
$$
It uses the same $\mathrm{WD}$, crystalline predicates and jump functions. The compatibility clause for integral models tests continuous representations and the given continuous coefficient map from the integer ring. The finite coefficient clause likewise tests continuous models and the continuous field inclusion. In both clauses the added conjunct therefore holds. On the primed side the cardinality assertion can quantify over every homomorphism because its de Rham hypothesis supplies the continuity required for the calculation above. This proves exactly the $\mathrm{PhtOn}$ required when the side and the chosen frame are installed in the first-pass record. The ambient local period family and the original $(\Omega,A_w)$ are the ones identified by the record's final equalities. Any further identification of a Galois representation attached to an automorphic constituent, or determination of *which* jumps occur from its highest weight, requires the corresponding automorphic input.
