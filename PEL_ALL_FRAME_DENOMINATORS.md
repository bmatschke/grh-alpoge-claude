# An integral denominator on all marked semiabelian traits

Fix one row of the generic small-field marked PEL moduli scheme over a number field $F$, with the CM action, principal polarization, graph theta line and the period description at one designated embedding. Use the actual nonnegative tangent and theta exponents and suppose the given algebraic cusp rules are bounded on the original complex unit frames. **For every finite list of these rules one integer clears their evaluations on all the original integral framed traits, including positive torus rank, in every residue characteristic.** No bound for the size of the integer is asserted. In particular the full small-field section lattice on this row follows from the project's opposite finite-generation bound. This supplies the qualitative denominator part of the common geometric construction on every supplied row. Existence of the upstream marked data and the numerical height bounds are separate inputs. The original [invariance of the rigidified theta norm](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/CubicalLineBundles/ThetaSectionNormAndHermitianFormIndepUniformisatStatement.lean#L17-L19) fixes the Gaussian mean, and the [supremum bound for cusp forms](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/CubicalLineBundles/CuspFormBoundedBySupNormAtGoodEmbeddingAllEmbedd.lean#L25-L44) supplies the boundedness under the source’s uniformization, fixed-parameter and dominance hypotheses.

The extra construction is a model of the *primitive theta generator* that works simultaneously on all traits. It suffices to compactify an affine neighbourhood of the identity in a Siegel semiabelian family. The complex calculation needed on this model involves one-variable disks in characteristic zero.

## A common model for the theta divisor

Use auxiliary symplectic levels $N=3,4$ separately, over $O_{F(\zeta_N)}[1/N]$. Choose a projective admissible Siegel toroidal compactification carrying the smooth universal semiabelian group $G$. Only Siegel data at primes away from the auxiliary $N$ enter this theorem. Take the reduced closure of the image of each component of the generic marked space with the auxiliary level, and its normalization in that component's function field. This is finite over the closure, since the base is excellent and the generic map to the Siegel space is finite onto its image as in [the interior calculation](PEL_GOOD_REDUCTION_DENOMINATOR.md). The result $Y$ is proper over the indicated ring. It contains the marked component on the generic abelian locus. The pulled-back $G/Y$ remains smooth and semiabelian. Homomorphisms of the generic abelian schemes extend uniquely to semiabelian schemes over a normal base, so the CM action extends as well. One may do the construction on each of finitely many affine charts of $Y$. [Faltings and Chai, *Degeneration of Abelian Varieties*, IV](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-662-02632-8) · [Lan, Theorem 1.41, including the degenerating family](https://www.kwlan.org/articles/cpt-fam.pdf) · [Lan, Proposition 3.3.1.5, extension of homomorphisms](https://doi.org/10.1515/9781400846016)

Shrink a chart $S$ so that there is an affine neighbourhood $U\subset G_S$ of the identity section $e(S)$. Such charts cover $Y$ by taking an affine neighbourhood of $e(y)$ and shrinking about $y$. Take $S$ affine principal after shrinking so $U$ may also be kept affine. Embed $U$ as a locally closed subscheme of projective space over $S$, and let $\bar U$ be its projective closure. On the dense characteristic-zero interior the generic theta divisor is canonically defined: the principal theta line $L$ has invertible pushforward $H$, and the universal evaluation $p^*H\to L$ cuts out a relative effective Cartier divisor. This works also when the theta function vanishes identically at the identity section; the divisor lives on the group.

Close the generic divisor of $U$ in $\bar U_{F(S)}$. Its Hilbert polynomial determines one projective relative Hilbert scheme over $S$. Let $Z$ be the normalization of the closure of this function-field point in that scheme. The map $Z\to S$ is proper, surjective and birational. Restrict the universal flat subscheme to $U_Z$ and denote it by $D$. It is flat over $Z$. Over the whole moduli interior it is the true theta divisor on $U_Z$: both have flat structure sheaf over the integral base and agree over its function field. On affine opens flatness implies that the ideals are saturated with respect to the nonzero base elements. In particular this comparison covers interior points over which taking the projective closure could have changed the Hilbert polynomial at infinity. [Grothendieck, *Les schémas de Hilbert*](https://www.numdam.org/item/SB_1960-1961__6__249_0/)

In fact $D$ is a relative effective Cartier divisor throughout $U_Z$. For $z\in Z$ choose a DVR centred at $z$ with generic point in the moduli interior, allowing an extension of the residue field. For example, at positive codimension normalize the blowup of the maximal ideal of $\mathcal O_{Z,z}$ and use the local ring of a generic exceptional divisor; at codimension one take the normal local ring itself. Its function field is the original one. The pullback $U_V$ is regular, smooth over the DVR, with geometrically integral fibres, each a nonempty open of a connected semiabelian group. By flatness $D_V$ is the schematic closure of its generic Cartier divisor there. On this regular space the closures of its horizontal prime components with their original multiplicities form a Cartier divisor with no vertical component. That divisor has flat structure sheaf over the DVR because its local equation is not divisible by the uniformizer; its agreement generically identifies it with the schematic closure. Its reduction is again an effective Cartier divisor, allowing the empty divisor. Faithful field extension descends this claim to $U_z$. The fibrewise Cartier criterion applied to $D\subset U_Z$ now proves the assertion. [Stacks Project, Lemma 31.19.9](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/062Y)

Put $H_Z=(e^*\mathcal O(D))^{-1}$. The line $\mathcal O(D)\otimes p^*H_Z$ has its tautological rigidification at the identity. On the moduli interior it is precisely the original rigidified theta line on $U$, with $H_Z$ the line of theta sections. Indeed the evaluation above gives $\mathcal O(D)=L\otimes p^*H^{-1}$, and $e^*L$ is canonically trivial. The canonical divisor section times a local basis of $H_Z$ is a primitive section on each trait.

To check the last statement in the actual integral frames, take any original DVR test and auxiliary level invertible on it. After finite extension one may choose that level, lift the generic point through $Z$ on a chart containing the closed centre, and extend by properness. This includes generic marked points in the exceptional locus of the modification. Its image is closed and contains the generic point of the chart, and every nonempty finite-type fibre has a closed point defined over a finite extension. Extend the valuation to that extension and use the valuative criterion on the chosen lift. The two semiabelian groups over this extended DVR agree by full faithfulness and the marked generic comparison. The original primitive theta section is regular on the entire group, with nonzero restriction to the special fibre by primitivity and integral connectedness. Its divisor restricted to $U$ is the flat closure of its generic divisor, since $U$ meets the generic point of that special fibre. It equals $D_V$. Sending the canonical divisor section to the actual section gives an isomorphism of their divisor lines; evaluating this isomorphism at $e$, using the actual rigidification, identifies the actual primitive vector with a basis of $H_Z(V)$. This comparison is valid even if $e$ lies in $D_V$. Thus the theta coordinate for the original frame lifts to the finite-type $\mathbb G_m$-bundle of generators of $H_Z$.

Conversely, on a DVR of the framed construction whose generic point is in the moduli interior, the line comparison on $U$ produces an actual witness on the entire semiabelian group. Take the closure of the generic theta divisor on all of $G_V$. This is Cartier on the regular total space, has no vertical component, and restricts on $U_V$ to $D_V$ by flatness. Rigidify its divisor line by the inverse of its restriction at the identity. Its canonical section times the chosen basis of the inverse identity line now generates the global section module: every regular section is a scalar multiple on the generic abelian fibre, and regularity at the generic point of the special fibre forces that scalar to be integral. On any power of $G_V$ each normalized generic comparison of two rigidified lines extends uniquely: the divisor of the comparison would be a multiple of the sole vertical prime, and division by that uniformizer power gives an invertible comparison whose restriction to the identity shows the power was zero. This extends the generic cube, symmetry, adjoint comparisons for every CM endomorphism and the graph-line comparison; equality of the cubical diagrams follows from density. The original marking and polarized Tate type belong to the original generic moduli point, and the two generic cartesian comparisons are supplied by its identification with the generic fibre of $G_V$. This gives the full line and generator data in the original valuation test. The complex metric enters the horizontal regularity proof below. For the tangent frames adjoin their column coordinates $A$, the eigenvector equations, and a matrix $A'$ with $AA'=A'A=cI$, where the nonzero integer $c$ clears all CM spectral idempotents. Also require an invertible maximal minor in each individual eigenblock, taking the finite union over choices (with empty minor one). This expresses saturation of the block lattice on each DVR, including at a ramified prime. The actual eigenlattice basis on a DVR gives such $A'$ because the sum of the eigenlattices contains $c\operatorname{Lie}(G)$. On the generic fibre this is exactly the frame bundle for the CM eigenspaces. Work on the reduced closure of its generic interior with the theta generator as above and normalize; the valuation points lift after extension, by finiteness, dominance and the actual normal generic frame bundle over the interior. All charts involved are finite in number. No smoothness at a prime dividing the CM discriminant or the original level is required. ## Horizontal regularity of the cusp reading

On the resulting normal finite-type framed chart $P$ the algebraic cusp rule is rational and regular over the interior. It suffices to exclude poles on divisors of $P_F$. After the designated complex embedding, choose a transverse analytic disk through a general smooth point of any such divisor with punctured disk in the interior. Any pole at that divisor would be a pole on this disk. Finite base change of the disk is harmless. Its image in the *Siegel* toroidal base has a semiabelian period chart. If the torus has rank $a>0$, choose a symplectic basis adapted to its saturated vanishing lattice $I$. Write $g$ for the full abelian dimension. The period matrix on the punctured disk is of the following form after clearing finite monodromy:
$$
 Z(t)={\log t\over 2\pi i}
 \begin{pmatrix}B&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix}+H(t),\qquad B=B^{\mathsf t}>0,
 \qquad \Im H_{22}(0)>0 .
$$
Here $B$ is integral of size $a$, $H$ is holomorphic at zero, and an empty lower block is allowed. In toroidal coordinates this follows by writing each nonzero boundary monomial as $t^k$ times a holomorphic unit, taking logarithms of the units and using the period and extension coordinates on the boundary stratum. The positive definite block is obtained by restricting the monodromy form to its rank-$a$ span. On the smooth semiabelian open the local fibre coordinates are $\exp(2\pi i z_i)$ for $i\le a$ and the remaining additive $z_i$ in a local abelian uniformization. The translation-invariant Lie frame $\partial_{z_i}$ extends across zero; for toric $i$ it is a nonzero multiple of the logarithmic torus vector. These are the comparison charts for the Siegel family; no arbitrary embedding of a series with coefficients in a local field into a convergent complex series is used. [Lan, *Comparison between analytic and algebraic constructions of toroidal compactifications*, §§3.2, 3.7, 4.1–4.3](https://www.kwlan.org/articles/cpt-ana-alg.pdf)

The characteristic of the actual theta line matters in this calculation. Let $A(x,y)$ be the integral alternating form of the principal polarization. For the graph line $L(x)=\mathcal P(x,\lambda w_0x)$, its Appell–Humbert semicharacter on integral periods is
$$
                    \chi(x)=(-1)^{A(x,w_0x)} .
$$
Indeed the normalized biextension is the second difference of any theta line with polarization $A$. In Appell–Humbert data the semicharacter of this second difference at $(x,y)$ is the quotient $\alpha(x+y)/(\alpha(x)\alpha(y))=(-1)^{A(x,y)}$. Pull back by the graph. The CM action commutes with monodromy and preserves $I$, the saturation of its logarithmic image. Hence $A(I,w_0I)=0$ by isotropy. With the first $a$ real periods spanning $I$, the shift in the torus Fourier indices of the ordinary theta series with characteristic is therefore zero modulo $\mathbb Z^a$. This conclusion would require a separate check for a translated theta line. [The graph identification on the actual marked row](PEL_THETA_LINE.md)

Write the remaining two half-characteristic vectors as $\alpha=(0,\alpha_2)$ and $\beta$. In the standard rigidified period frame the section is represented by
$$
 \Theta(z,t)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z^g+\alpha}
       \exp\{\pi i n^{\mathsf t} Z(t)n+2\pi i n^{\mathsf t}(z+\beta)\}.
$$
Finite base change clears the exponents and multiplier monodromy. Every torus term has nonnegative $t$-order $n_1^{\mathsf t}Bn_1/2$. On compact sets of the smooth semiabelian coordinate chart this series is normally convergent for $|t|$ small: the positive definite lower block controls the $n_2$ sum, the mixed terms cost at most a constant times $\|n_1\|^2$ after completing squares, and the growing toric quadratic controls that loss. Its reduction at $t=0$ is the nonzero theta function of the lower-dimensional principally polarized abelian part (the constant one if its dimension is zero). It may vanish at the origin, but it is not identically zero on any open neighbourhood there. Thus in a coordinate neighbourhood of the identity the analytic theta function defines the horizontal closure of the generic divisor, with no vertical component. It gives exactly the divisor $D$ pulled back to that neighbourhood: $D$ is relative Cartier, and both relative Cartier divisors are closures of the same generic one. Taking the standard local period trivialization with identity value one, the induced isomorphism from $\mathcal O(D)$ to this local theta line is regular and invertible, even where the theta value at the origin is zero. Its restriction to the identity gives a holomorphic unit comparing the analytic theta generator with the chosen algebraic generator of $H_Z$ on the disk. More explicitly the canonical divisor section is sent to $\Theta$ by this isomorphism, so its evaluation as an isomorphism of *line fibres* at the identity fixes the scalar in the rigidified comparison over the punctured disk. Vanishing of a theta **value** there has no effect on this scalar.

Put $Y(t)=\Im Z(t)$. In the canonical cubical theta metric with translation-invariant curvature and rigidification of length one at the identity the squared norm of this theta generator, integrated against the probability measure of the period torus, equals $2^{-g/2}\det(Y(t))^{-1/2}$. Integrate first over the real period variables to remove unequal Fourier indices and then integrate the resulting Gaussian over all translates of the other period cube. In particular the norm is bounded as $t\to0$. The integral tangent columns are holomorphic in the preceding Lie frame. Their Hodge norms are bounded since $Y(t)^{-1}$ stays bounded, by the positive abelian block and the growing positive toric block. The factors on the diagonal of the triangular change from an orthonormal flag basis are bounded by the original column norms, by Gram–Schmidt. When the image stays in the abelian locus the same bounds hold directly by smoothness and positivity. The cusp rule is bounded on the unit frames and all exponents on the diagonal factors and the theta generator are nonnegative. The value on the framed disk is therefore bounded. A pole is impossible. [Project cusp and weight hypotheses, RH Lean Roadmap B8.43n](https://github.com/rh-lean-roadmap/rh-lean-roadmap/blob/02a95b4c62db610642af5f278ee5d0d6df87b348/docs/src/book/main.tex#L23739-L23745) · [Original tangent and theta norm convention](https://github.com/rh-lean-roadmap/rh-lean-roadmap/blob/02a95b4c62db610642af5f278ee5d0d6df87b348/docs/src/book/main.tex#L57997-L58005)

## Clearing the vertical denominators

By the codimension-one criterion on normal varieties the cusp reading is regular on each characteristic-zero chart. Its coordinate ring there is the localization of the integral chart algebra by the nonzero coefficient integers. A nonzero rational integer therefore clears the reading on the entire affine chart. Take the product for finitely many charts, components, embeddings as required, sections and the two auxiliary levels. For each trait one of $3,4$ is a unit; the lifted integral frame is a point of one of these charts. Integrality descends to the original valuation ring. The same proof works for all positive torus ranks and all test residue characteristics, retaining the hypotheses on the generic marked row, the bounded complex cusp rule, and its exact weight. Applying this to a basis and the opposite finite-generation argument gives the full $O_F$-lattice, and the original coefficient extension transports it to the retwisted copy. ## Preserving the same denominator away from a finite set of primes

There is also a finite exceptional set of rational primes depending only on the fixed marked model and its framed charts, independent of the cusp rule and its nonnegative weight. Away from it, integrality of any such rule on all good integral finite-residue tests at a place implies integrality with the **same scalar** on all the original semistable tests there.

Indeed the normal framed charts are of finite type. On their generic fibres the ordinary interior frame loci are smooth and dense in every component. After first extending the number field to separate the finitely many geometric components one may assume their generic fibres geometrically integral, or apply the argument component by component on that extension. By generic flatness and spreading geometric integrality, after excluding finitely many rational primes the special fibres of these charts are geometrically integral, and their smooth interior good loci are fibrewise dense. One may use a dense affine open of the interior over which the Hilbert modification is an isomorphism, the original marked model is good and all frame idempotents are integral. Its closed complement has dimension less than the generic fibre there, and hence, after shrinking the number ring, less than the fibre dimension in every fibre. This establishes the required density even on an auxiliary affine chart meeting the boundary. On splitting into geometric components one uses the normalization in the finite coefficient extension and its dense opens; these charts still cover the original valuation tests after finite extension.

Suppose the rational cusp reading (including the proposed scalar) had a pole over such a place. The horizontal calculation removes all horizontal poles. Normality gives a vertical prime with a pole, hence a generic component of the special fibre. In a nonempty smooth open of that component within the good locus the reading is a negative power of the base uniformizer times a unit. Here the uniformizer defines the reduced fibre, by smoothness. Choose a closed point of that open and lift it, by smoothness and completeness, over the unramified extension of the coefficient DVR having its finite residue field. This is an actual good integral framed point, and its evaluation has negative valuation. This contradicts the hypothesis on good tests. The codimension-one criterion and lifting of traits now imply the asserted integrality at every test. Restricting a good or general test over the finite coefficient extension to the original coefficient field preserves the notion of test at the place and the inequality descends. Thus the assertion is valid over the original number field after excluding the underlying finitely many rational primes. In particular the qualitative theorem above makes no restriction on residue characteristic, while this sharper implication introduces no loss at any place *outside* the fixed exceptional set. The size and prime support of the exceptional set have not been bounded along the family.

## An exact finite valuation test at each prescribed prime

At any fixed coefficient place $\lambda$, use one auxiliary level $N$ prime to its residue characteristic. Keep every component and place over $\lambda$ needed for the finite coefficient extension. Let $\mathscr V_\lambda$ be the union, over the normalized framed charts above, of the finitely many prime divisors over $\lambda$. Ignore a chart with empty special fibre, and use the value $+\infty$ when a section is identically zero on a component. Writing $\varpi_\lambda$ for a base uniformizer and $e_D=\operatorname{ord}_D(\varpi_\lambda)$, the actual optimal bound is
$$
 \inf_y\frac{v_y(f)}{v_y(\varpi_\lambda)}
   =\min_{D\in\mathscr V_\lambda}\frac{\operatorname{ord}_D(f)}{e_D}.
$$
The infimum uses the original integral framed tests with finite residue field. Repetitions of valuations on overlapping charts cause no change. For the lower bound take a rational number $u/v$ below or equal to the right-hand side, with $v>0$. At each chart above $O_{F,\lambda}$ the rational function $f^v\varpi_\lambda^{-u}$ has nonnegative order on every vertical divisor by hypothesis and on every horizontal divisor by the complex argument. It is regular by normality. Evaluating on a lifted frame and dividing by $v$ proves the bound.

Each finite ratio on the right is attained by an original finite-residue test. Choose a closed point on $D$ in the regular locus of the total space and the smooth locus of its reduction. Avoid other components of the fibre, the closures of generic boundary loci, and the zeros and poles of the unit in $f=\xi^{\operatorname{ord}_D(f)}u_0$, where $\xi$ cuts out $D$ near its generic point. These exclusions are proper on $D$. Its residue field at the chosen point is finite. In the regular local ring there, the reduced $D$ is regular and $\xi$ is part of a regular system of parameters; moreover $\varpi_\lambda=u_1\xi^{e_D}$ with $u_1$ a unit. Mod out by all complementary parameters and complete. This yields a horizontal complete DVR with uniformizer $\xi$ and finite residue field. Its generic point lies in the framed interior, and the pulled-back tangent columns form saturated bases by the unit block minors. The Hilbert construction identifies the chosen generator with the primitive section of the actual rigidified line: equivalently first take the actual global rigidified line and generator on the DVR's semiabelian group by [the extension and vertical-order argument](PEL_SECTION_LAWS.md), and compare their restriction on $U$ as above. A further finite extension used to realize markings preserves the ratio. Thus this test has the displayed normalized value. Choosing a point avoiding the finitely many corresponding horizontal zeros and poles realizes the orders of an entire finite list simultaneously on each $D$. This result gives an exact finite description of each local integral lattice. It gives no family bound for the number or multiplicities of the vertical divisors at the exceptional primes.

The ordinary tests can even be chosen independently of $f$ in the entire fixed finite-dimensional cusp space $C$. For each $D$ fix the parameter $\xi$ used above and, for $0\le r<e_D$, take the residue space
$$
 W_r=\{(\xi^{-r}f)\bmod D : f\in C,\ \operatorname{ord}_D(f)\ge r\}\subset k(D).
$$
These are vector spaces over the finite residue field $k_\lambda$. Their dimensions sum to at most $\dim_F C$. To see this, lift an independent residue basis in each $W_r$ to elements of $C$ of order $r$. In a nontrivial $F$-linear dependence consider terms of minimal $D$-order. Their $r$'s agree modulo $e_D$, hence agree, and the corresponding coefficients all have the same $\lambda$-order. After dividing by that power of $\varpi_\lambda$ and the common $\xi^r$, reduction contradicts independence in $W_r$. Thus there are only finitely many nonzero leading residues altogether. Choose the one closed point on $D$ clear of the zeros and poles of all of them. For arbitrary $f$ of finite order write $\operatorname{ord}_D(f)=a e_D+r$. The function $f/(\varpi_\lambda^a\xi^r)$ is regular near the chosen point by normality: it has no horizontal pole there and $D$ is the sole local vertical component. Its reduction is one of the nonzero residues above, hence its value on the transverse DVR is a unit. Thus *one ordinary framed point per divisor* computes the whole local minimum for every $f\in C$.

For the weaker integral-membership test one can keep at most $\dim_F C$ such points. Let $M_\lambda$ be the lattice of elements integral on all tests above $\lambda$. This is the localization of the previously obtained full global lattice: each element has a denominator at only finitely many other primes and one may clear it there with a scalar which is a unit at $\lambda$. Thus $M_\lambda$ is a free lattice over $O_{F,\lambda}$. On $\varpi_\lambda^{-1}M_\lambda/M_\lambda$, each point's integrality condition is a $k_\lambda$-linear subspace, and the intersection over the sample is zero. Successively choosing a point which strictly lowers the dimension keeps at most $\dim_F C$ points. An element outside $M_\lambda$ can be multiplied by a nonnegative power of $\varpi_\lambda$ to land in $\varpi_\lambda^{-1}M_\lambda\setminus M_\lambda$, so these retained tests suffice on all of $C$. This bound is for integrality; the full family of sampled divisors retains the exact normalized valuations.

## Finite algebraic frames for the whole arithmetic lattice

Fix the finite-dimensional $F$-space $C$ of rules in a fixed row and weight, and write $d=\dim_F C$. The transverse sampling in the preceding argument actually gives *number-field* points with specified places. Before completion the horizontal one-dimensional regular local ring is essentially of finite type over the ring of integers, and its residue field is finite. Its fraction field $L$ satisfies $\operatorname{trdeg}_F L=0$ by the dimension formula at the closed point of this horizontal curve. Thus $L/F$ is finite, and the DVR is the local integer ring of $L$ at its centre (it is a DVR with this fraction field dominating that local integer ring). Its completion is $O_{L,w}^{\wedge}$ for the induced place $w$. The generic marked object and its frame are defined over $L$ by the chart map. All the data for its integral witness just constructed pull back over this DVR. Consequently the $\le d$ local integrality tests at each specified $\lambda$ can be taken at algebraic framed points with a distinguished $w\mid\lambda$.

There is one **finite list of algebraic framed points** testing the full global lattice $M$ of this fixed $C$, using the following sets of places.

1. Choose algebraic generic framed points $x_i$ over finite fields of definition $L_i/F$ such that $J:C\to\bigoplus_i L_i$, $f\mapsto(f(x_i))_i$, is injective. At most $d$ points are needed: any nonzero rule is nonzero somewhere on the reduced generic framed space, hence at a closed algebraic point, and adjoining this evaluation decreases the dimension of the common kernel. Each of these abelian objects and its complete original frame has good integral reduction at all places of $L_i$ away from a finite set. Indeed the abelian scheme, CM action and level extend over an open of its ring of integers; the eigenbasis minors, characteristic idempotents and the generator of the invertible theta direct image remain integral and invertible after removing finitely many more places.
2. Choose a finite set $S$ of places of $F$ containing the places just removed, by contraction. Enlarge it so both $J$ on $M$ and an $F$-linear left inverse of $J$ on $\bigoplus_i L_i$ preserve the respective lattices $M$ and $\bigoplus_i O_{L_i}$ over $O_F[S^{-1}]$. This costs finitely many primes by clearing the coordinates of the images of finitely many module generators in both directions. For every $\lambda\notin S$ the condition $f\in M_\lambda$ is now equivalent to $v_w(f(x_i))\ge0$ for every $i$ and **every** $w$ of $L_i$ over $\lambda$. Integrality at all such $w$ means precisely that $f(x_i)$ belongs to $O_{L_i}\otimes_{O_F}O_{F,\lambda}$.
3. For each $\lambda\in S$ keep the at most $d$ algebraic frames $x_{\lambda,j}$ and their particular places $w_{\lambda,j}\mid\lambda$ from the local test. Require $v_{w_{\lambda,j}}(f(x_{\lambda,j}))\ge0$ at the **listed** place of each such frame. These extra frames need not be tested at their other places. Together with step 2 this is equivalent to $f\in M$ because a full lattice is the intersection of its localizations.

Thus one may use at most $d(1+|S|)$ number-field framed points with the specified place sets. Equivalently one may test at every place of every member of this finite list *at which that particular frame is integral*: the additional checks are necessary for members of $M$ anyway. The global assertion is for integrality on $C$; it does not assert that the same interpolation points recover each fractional valuation optimum at the infinitely many ordinary places. Neither an effective bound on $S$ nor a bound for the degrees of the sampling fields or their residue fields in terms of the original family parameters is obtained. Choosing closed points in the good open is qualitative and the construction permits finite residue extensions of unrestricted degree.
