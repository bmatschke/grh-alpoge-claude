\paragraph{J90P1. Both rational row identifications preserve the integral metrics and lattices.}
Fix a selected bundle with its original witness as in X87S1.
The rows can be described by integral forms at a finite level, or by
their images as automorphic forms. At the sharp and the base levels
the given rational identifications $e_{RR}$ carry the model row
lattice onto the native one, with equality of the Hermitian
majorants at every infinite place.

Here is the integral assertion. Keep the notation $V_t,P_t,M_t$
of R88L1 at either level. Write $L_t$ for the integral lattice in
the level space and $N_t=\{z: V_tz\in L_t\}$ for its cut in
the rational coordinates of the good part. The latter coordinates
form a basis of the range of the good projector. By the integral
reading, the native integral level forms are exactly the images
under $j_{A,t}$ of $L_t$. The native carrier uses a coefficient ring whose image consists of
algebraic integers by the integrality axiom. It is therefore contained
in the span of its generators over the full ring of integers. The
reverse inclusion follows since the carrier contains these generators
and the supplied reading makes it stable under that full ring and
under addition. Consequently its intersection with the good subspace is
exactly $j_{A,t}(V_tN_t)$. To see the reverse inclusion implicit
in this equality, write a good integral form as $j_{A,t}(l)$
with $l\in L_t$. Its coordinates belong to the complex span of
the columns of $V_t$. An invertible minor of that rational basis
solves for $z$ over the coefficient field, and $V_tz=l$ gives
$z\in N_t$. This intersection uses the original lattice before
applying the row map.

On this intersection projection away from the kernel of $\iota$
does not change its image under $\iota$. The equation of R88L1
therefore identifies its row images with
$j_{EE}e_{RR}(M_tP_tN_t)$. Injectivity of $j_{EE}$ identifies
the rational preimages too. In particular taking integral spans
agrees on the two sides. At each finite prime the selected readings
give the same tuple $t$ for the two descriptions. Localization and
intersection of the resulting conditions in the rational row space
commute with the fixed isomorphism $e_{RR}$. This proves the
assertion for the glued row lattices at both levels, including the
primes where the selected tuple differs from the base tuple.

At the distinguished infinite place, let $z$ be the automorphic
row represented by the coordinate vector $y$. In the sharp
realization its native squared norm is $n_P^{-1}\|z\|^2$.
Indeed the multiplicity inner product in the native reading
integrates the same matrix coefficients as the model with Haar
measure scaled by the inverse finite-level factor $n_P^{-1}$.
The integral, hence its multiplicity scalar, scales by that factor.
For $z=0$ both sides vanish. The model majorant on $y$ is
$n_P^{-1}\|z\|^2$ by the normalized row Gram matrix. At the
base level the stored base norm and base Gram matrix give the
same equality with the corresponding level factor. Polarizing on
$x+y$ recovers the real part, and polarizing with $y$ multiplied
by an element of the totally imaginary field with nonreal image in the
distinguished embedding recovers the imaginary part. At each other infinite
place the two metric identities are explicit in the readings.
All choices here are those of the same twisted input $x$.
Its sharp and base division records already give the required
row comparisons, as observed in R88L1.

\paragraph{J90P2. Schur's lemma for the adelic multiplicity pairing.}
Fix one real place, fix the entries of the two multilinear maps at
all other places, and put $D=H^\infty$ at this place. Let
$a,a':D\to C_b(X)$ be the resulting maps on the finite-measure space
$(X,\nu)$. Here the source gives a Sobolev bound for each functional
$w\mapsto a(w)(x)$, with the order and constant allowed to depend on
$x$. It also gives $\sup_x|a(w)(x)|<\infty$ for every $w\in D$.
Since $D$ is Fr\'echet, Banach--Steinhaus makes this family of
functionals equicontinuous. Thus $a:D\to C_b(X)$ is continuous in
the supremum norm. The same reasoning applies to $a'$. The estimate
$\|f\|_2\le\nu(X)^{1/2}\|f\|_\infty$ shows that
\[
 B(u,v)=\int_X\overline{a(u)(x)}\,a'(v)(x)\,d\nu(x)
\]
is jointly continuous on $D\times D$. In the application $X$ is
the adelic group and $\nu$ is Haar measure restricted to a
finite-volume fundamental domain. Cusp decay gives the required
boundedness of each function. Right translation preserves the
quotient integral of the product because the forms are left
rationally invariant and Haar measure is right invariant. This
gives diagonal $G$-invariance of $B$ even though the chosen
fundamental domain itself may move.

When $H\ne0$, its compact-finite smooth vectors $V$ form an
admissible irreducible Harish-Chandra module by W90G1. The integral
restricts to a $K$-invariant form on $V$, and differentiating
diagonal invariance in the smooth-vector topology gives
$B(d\sigma(Y)u,v)+B(u,d\sigma(Y)v)=0$ for $Y\in\mathfrak g$.
These are the two invariance conditions in (8.7c) of Adams,
van Leeuwen, Trapa and Vogan, \emph{Unitary representations of real
reductive groups}. Their Proposition~8.9(3) says that such a
sesquilinear form on an admissible irreducible Harish-Chandra
module is unique up to complex scalar whenever a nonzero form
exists. The Hilbert inner product supplies one. Equivalently one may
exchange the two arguments to match the authors' convention of
linearity in the first. Thus $B(u,v)=c\langle u,v\rangle$ on
$V$, and continuity and density (W90G2) extend the formula to $D$.
If $H=0$ take $c=0$. Only smooth-topology continuity was needed.

For the finite product of real places choose a smooth unit vector at
each place (a zero factor makes all multilinear values vanish).
Each application of the one-place identity extracts a factor
$\langle u_\tau,v_\tau\rangle$. Its coefficient is recovered by
putting the chosen unit vector in both slots at that place, so the
identity can be iterated without division, including for orthogonal
inputs. The remaining coefficient is the Petersson pairing evaluated
on the two fixed unit tuples. The bounded functions are integrable
in product on the finite-volume domain. This proves precisely the
multilinear pairing required by $\mathrm{SchurMult}$, for each pair
of multiplicity maps.

\paragraph{J90P3. One compatible realization on the selected bundle.}
At every sufficiently late index of the original selected class,
the bundle realizing R89C1 has the full comparison
$\mathrm{JoinAt}$ of R89C2. In particular (C89.1) and
(C89.2) may be used on that bundle. There is no further existence
hypothesis for its row and column coordinates.

To construct them, use the sharp coordinate map of J90P1 at the
base tuple and the native column bases $j_{N,u}e_{\mathrm{Nu},u}e_i$.
Let $\eta$ be identity or complex conjugation according as the
model embedding agrees with the native embedding or its conjugate.
Extend the rational row identification to a semilinear map $J$
by sending $j_{EE}e_{RR}e_i$ to the model row with the same
coordinate vector. These vectors are bases on their respective
row spaces by R88L1 and the native basis reading. On their whole
spans the Gram equality in J90P1 gives a norm similitude with
the common positive sharp-level factor. R88L1 identifies the
range with the good model row space. The model base-tuple input
coordinate map to native level forms extends $j_{A,t_0}V_{t_0}$.
Its image is the native good subspace by the projector identity.
The equation of R88L1 at $t_0$ intertwines its row operator with
$J$ after applying $\eta$ to coordinates. For the columns,
expand in the displayed native basis and use the stored column
Gram matrix. This gives a coordinate isomorphism for every word
with its prescribed positive norm factor. On the rational basis
pairs the recorded $\mathrm{Pt}$ evaluations are exactly
$\mathrm{perCol}$ by the period reading of the same selected
point. These identities give the chain and coordinate conditions
in the definition of the join.

It remains to compare the two ways of embedding the rows in the
spectral Hilbert space. Transfer Haar measure and the rational fundamental
domain along the selected adelic group isomorphism, which identifies
the rational subgroups. The right translate by the selected point
preserves the quotient integral. Write $j'$ for the model evaluation
isomorphism on its level multiplicity space, given by evaluation
on a smooth unit tuple $v_1$, and $e$ for the reading of the
native row into that space. The selected realization gives $jF$
by evaluation on another tuple $v_0$ at the same translated
point, times a fixed scalar $a$. By J90P2, testing both vectors
against $j'T$ for every level multiplicity vector $T$ gives
\[
 \langle j'T,jF\rangle
   =a\!\prod_\tau\langle v_{1,\tau},v_{0,\tau}\rangle\,
                         \langle j'T,j'(eF)\rangle .
\]
The common scalar is independent of $F$ and $T$. Both vectors
belong to the good spectral row space, and $j'$ maps onto it. Hence the two vectors agree up to this scalar. If it is zero,
injectivity of $j$ forces the native row space to be zero and any
nonzero comparison scalar works there. Otherwise conjugating and
inverting it gives the nonzero scalar for the conjugate-linear
row realization. Multiply by the prescribed row normalization
$u_S\kappa_S$, which is nonzero since $|u_S|=1$ and
$\kappa_S>0$. For a native column, expansion in its recorded basis
now gives precisely the spectral period operator in these column
coordinates. Pairing with the preceding row identity proves the
inner-product condition of the join. This step identifies the
spectral pairing in two coordinate systems. The comparison with
the arithmetic period values is the separate equation (C89.1).

Finally the witness of X87S1 identifies its realization with the
bundle's, its translated evaluation point with the selected one,
and its column representation with the original column family.
Thus the above choices give a join for the very spectral model
having $\mathrm{FinX}$ in R89C1. Taking that same eventual range
works for every index. The three representation inputs D90S3,
M90H4 and W90G2, together with R89D2--3, also discharge the five
general analytic hypotheses used by the original construction.
