STATUS AND PURPOSE

This component supplies the actual QH.thin and UpperTheory.Top.ThinH
implications, with their real-zero exclusion and host RealBox assumptions
retained. It proves the analytic inputs used in those implications,
including the primitive functional equation, the zero window and zero-free
estimates, the explicit formula, and the exact principal and nonprincipal
centerings. It does not prove RH or GRH, and it does not infer the source's
simultaneous PairEx or relative progression theorem from thinning.

The full mathematical arguments are in main.tex, paragraphs CC1–CC20.
Each paragraph has its own exact SHA-256 and source declaration ranges in
provenance.json and source_locators.txt. This report explains the choices,
quantifier discipline, and current connection to the larger reconstruction.

1. THE ACTUAL FUNCTIONS

For a character chi modulo N, extend chi by zero at nonunits and put
  delta_chi = 1 if chi is principal, 0 otherwise;
  psi0(chi,u) = sum_{1<=n<=floor(u)} Lambda(n) chi(n) - delta_chi u.
The primitive principal case has N=1. This distinction matters: one cannot
use the zeta principal centering for an imprimitive principal character
without accounting for its deleted Euler factors.

For nonprincipal chi the centered function is
  G_chi(y) = sum_{n<=floor(y)} Lambda(n)chi(n)(y/n-1)
             + y L'_chi(1)/L_chi(1).
For the primitive principal character it is
  G_1(y) = sum_{n<=floor(y)} Lambda(n)(y/n-1)
             - y(log y-1-gamma).
The source G0 chooses between these cases. On the actual summation range
the max(y/n-1,0) in An.gChi equals y/n-1; therefore these are the same
functions, with exactly the same logarithmic-derivative sign and constant.

2. ANALYTIC INPUTS: CC1–CC8

CC1 derives the primitive completion directly. The finite character Fourier
transform is tau(chi) conjugate(chi(k)); primitivity forces vanishing at
nonunit k, and finite Parseval gives |tau(chi)|=sqrt(N). Gaussian
periodization gives the theta transformation and its factor
tau(chi)/(i^a sqrt(N)), where chi(-1)=(-1)^a. Mellin integration then proves
the functional equation. The principal case separates the theta constant
and has the two usual completed poles. The DLMF reference records the
normalization; it is not used in place of the derivation.

CC2 does not assume a global completed Hadamard product. Periodic bounded
character sums, four mean-zero periodic primitives, and integration by
parts give polynomial growth on a strip wide enough for the disks in CC3.
Euler products give a fixed nonzero lower bound on the line Re(s)=2 and
the exact von Mangoldt logarithmic derivative.

CC3 applies the finite disk lemma already proved in QR15. Jensen controls
the number of zeros in a fixed smaller disk; a finite Blaschke product
removes those zeros, and the logarithm of the remaining zero-free function
has bounded derivative on a still smaller disk. This proves both the
height-radius-one count (|Im rho-t|<=1) and the local logarithmic-derivative formula.
Every disk remains inside the strip proved in CC2, and every radius gap
is a fixed positive number.

CC4 separates boundary issues from the later zero-free strip. QW14 gives
nonprincipal nonvanishing at one. The alternating zeta series excludes
principal real zeros in (0,1). The elementary trigonometric polynomial
3+4 cos(theta)+cos(2theta) proves nonvanishing on Re(s)=1 at nonzero height.
The functional equation then identifies the simple zero at the origin for
a nonprincipal even primitive character; no other zero occurs in the
left strip used for the contour.

CC5 reuses the numerical positivity contradictions of QR17–QR18 after
checking their analytic premises for actual Dirichlet characters. This
does not assume a Dirichlet LFam instance. The output is the usual narrow
zero-free region with at most one simple real exception at a fixed
modulus. If a real nonprincipal character has no real zero in
(1-c0,1), the exception is excluded and
  beta <= 1 - c/(lambda+log(|gamma|+3))
for lambda>=max(1,log N). The constant c depends only on c0. Uniform
comparability of the two logarithmic denominators and a fixed shrinkage
of b give the literal closed-strip zFRDirichlet statement. Taking c<=1
also covers Re(rho)<=0 automatically; CC4 excludes Re(rho)>=1. Thus the
actual every-zero pt_zero_free interface is obtained.

CC6 reflects rho for chi to 1-rho for the inverse primitive character.
The same exceptional-zero exclusion applies to that inverse. Hence low
zeros also stay a controlled distance from zero. This is essential:
estimating u^rho alone would not control the denominator rho in the
explicit formula.

CC7 sums all reciprocal zero weights, with multiplicity:
  sum_{|Im rho|<T} m_rho/|rho| <= C2(lambda+log T)^2.
Low zeros use the reflected lower bound on Re(rho); higher zeros are
grouped in unit-height intervals and summed with weights 1/j. C2 is
allowed to depend on the fixed exclusion c0.

CC8 proves the literal ExplicitFormulaWith bound:
  |psi0(chi,u)+sum_{|Im rho|<T} m_rho u^rho/rho|
  <= C7 [u log^2(NuT)/T + log(Nu)
          + sum_{|Im rho|<1} m_rho/|rho|],  2<=T<=u.

The proof keeps full floor endpoints, including integer u. It chooses
upper and lower contour heights separately in [T,T+1], avoiding zero
ordinates by a reciprocal-logarithmic distance. The local logarithmic
derivative controls the horizontal sides. Reflection to Re(s)=3/2 and
a direct gamma logarithmic-derivative bound control the left side
Re(s)=-1/2. The pole at one gives the main term; nontrivial zeros have the
minus sign. The residue at zero is b0-e0 log u, and b0 is bounded by the
same displayed low-zero reciprocal sum and O(log(N+2)). Finally the good
heights are changed back to the source's strict |Im rho|<T convention.
No unbounded constant at zero is silently dropped.

3. CONDUCTOR-SENSITIVE THINNING: CC9–CC10

Choose T=exp(sqrt(log u)), before making any conductor-dependent choice.
For u>=e, this lies between 2 and u. The zero-free region, the reciprocal
zero sum, and the explicit formula then give
  |psi0(chi,u)|
  <= C u (log u+lambda)^2
       exp[-c log u/(lambda+sqrt(log u))].
C and c are chosen from c0 before N, chi, lambda and u. The displayed
real-zero exclusion is still a hypothesis. Nonreal and principal
characters require no such extra condition. This is the precise
QH.psi_thin quantifier order.

4. THE EXACT CENTERINGS: CC11–CC14

A fixed modulus has finitely many characters. Nonvanishing at one supplies
a positive real-zero margin for that finite collection, so the previous
estimate yields psi0=O_chi(u/log^2 u) for each fixed character.
Imprimitive Euler-factor deletion contributes only O_N(log u).
Consequently psi0/u^2 is absolutely integrable on [1,infinity).

For a nonprincipal character, finite Abel summation and this integrability
show that sum Lambda(n)chi(n)/n converges. Dominated convergence in the
Euler logarithmic derivative identifies its sum as -L'(1)/L(1).
This is convergence of the series, not absolute convergence of the series.
Abel summation at finite y then gives its exact centered tail integral.

For the primitive principal character, the Laurent expansion
zeta(s)=1/(s-1)+gamma+O(s-1) gives
  integral_1^infinity (psi(u)-u)/u^2 du = -1-gamma.
The finite integral identity gives exactly the principal centered function
above, including the otherwise easy-to-miss "-1" in the constant.

Thus in both primitive cases,
  G0(chi,y) = -y integral_y^infinity psi0(chi,u)/u^2 du, y>=1.
This proves QH.g_integral and identifies An.gChi on the same actual
arguments. The proof uses only fixed-character convergence and does not
borrow a uniform Siegel–Walfisz theorem to justify the centerings.

5. A SHORT UNIFORM TAIL ARGUMENT: CC15–CC16

Assume
  lambda <= A x^(eps/5) log^2 x,
  y >= exp(x^(eps/3))/3,
with positive fixed eps,A and x sufficiently large.
Writing V=log y and u=exp(v), we have V>=x^(eps/3)/2. Uniformly for v>=V,
  lambda <= v^(61/100).
The reason is the strict exponent inequality 61/300>1/5; the spare power
x^(eps/300) absorbs log^2 x and the fixed constants. Therefore
  (v+lambda)^2 exp[-c v/(lambda+sqrt(v))]
  <= 4v^2 exp[-(c/2) v^(39/100)].
Factor half the exponential at V. The remaining integral is a fixed finite
constant, while the extracted exponent is at least a fixed multiple of
x^(13eps/100). The strict gap
  13/100 - 1/8 = 1/200
absorbs every prescribed multiplier c' and the fixed integral constant.

Combining this estimate with the exact tail identity gives
  |G0(chi,y)| <= y exp[-c' x^(eps/8)]
simultaneously for every y above the cutoff and every primitive character
satisfying log N<=A x^(eps/5) log^2 x and NoExc(c0).
There is one threshold depending only on eps,A,c',c0. This is QH.thin.

6. INSTALLING THE SAME-HOST STATEMENT: CC17–CC19

A primitive real character has odd conductor exponents at most 1, and
2-adic exponent at most 3. For odd p the principal-unit kernel is a
p-group, so its image in {+1,-1} is trivial. At 2, every unit congruent to
1 mod 8 is a square; the proof lifts an odd square root one binary digit
at a time. The Chinese remainder theorem and primitivity give the
source's actual real_cond divisibility conclusion.

For a fixed bound B0, continuity and nonprincipal nonvanishing at one
give one common positive zero-free interval for all characters modulo
N<=B0. Neither reality nor primitivity is needed in this bounded-modulus
small_zeros statement.

Now take the actual supplied host with condN=QS*q*d and bounded odd part
of QS. Every primitive host character has conductor dividing condN.
If it is real and involves q or d, the supplied RealBox gives its zero
exclusion with width 1/400. Otherwise its conductor divides 8*oddPart(QS)
and is bounded by 8*floor(B); the bounded-modulus result supplies its
exclusion. Choose the smaller of these two fixed widths and apply QH.thin
with A=2 and c'=1. The exact function identification then gives ThinH with
outer constant 1.

This supplies the thinning premise in HC9. It does not construct a host
with RealBox, nor does it weaken the original 0<eps<1/52 requirement of
ThinH, even though the analytic thinning argument itself allows eps>0.

7. A FURTHER CONSEQUENCE: CC20

The exact integral identity and the finite ramp formula give local
absolute continuity and, away from integers,
  G0'(y)=psi0(chi,y)/y+G0(chi,y)/y.
The right derivative uses the full floor convention at integers.
The same pointwise majorant and thinning estimate, with a slightly
stronger prescribed exponential, show that for any b>0,
  |G0(chi,y2)-G0(chi,y1)|
  <= (y2-y1) exp[-b x^(eps/8)]
above the same exponential cutoff. This is an additional human
consequence, distinguished in the ledger from literal source endpoints.

It must not be substituted for the source's square-root estimates on
the earlier polynomial-height interval [Y,Y^A]. The cutoff scales and
quantifiers are different.
