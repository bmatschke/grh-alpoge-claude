4. WHY THE SIMPLIFICATIONS HELP

Finite descent through actual frame bundles. A framed point consists of finite algebraic data in vector-bundle frames and a theta-section frame. Once those objects represent the literal source functor, descent to a finitely generated subalgebra becomes a finite-presentation argument. A second direct local construction makes the same descent explicit. The proof does not smuggle a flatness or Noetherian hypothesis onto the coefficient algebra; it uses the original section-base-change statement only where its hypotheses hold. This separation is essential for the full reduced-test conclusion.

A Fourier calculation for the complex section line. The theta transformation law gives a recurrence among Fourier coefficients. Every coefficient is determined by one initial coefficient, and the classical theta series supplies a nonzero solution. This proves the required complex-fiber section space is one-dimensional directly. It avoids introducing a stronger general section-twisting premise into the normalized-frame construction. It does not remove the separate SecBaseChangeSt used to descend arbitrary coefficient-algebra frames.

Normalization is calculated rather than hidden in a positive scalar. The theta integral over the actual fundamental domain is 1/sqrt(2^g det(Im tau)). The normalized-period identity gives det(H) det(Im tau)=|det h|^2. Unit-minor frames fix det(H) as the source product over the CM type, and the normConst multiplier contributes the reciprocal factor required for cancellation. Consequently the final Gaussian mean is exactly 1, for either sign of the chosen square root. This verifies the literal normalization field needed later.

Elementary auxiliary arithmetic. The character-seed argument needs primes in the residue class 1 for an explicit modulus; a Euclid-cyclotomic construction supplies precisely that. The auxiliary real quadratic field needs congruence and nonsquare conditions, rather than a new prime in a prescribed progression. CRT imposes n congruent to -1 modulo 4M and valuation one at a fresh odd prime. This makes n nonsquare while retaining the desired local conditions. These simplifications are proved only at their actual source scope; the later automorphic representations are still separate.

A single product majorant for the last Bessel interchange. Let A=rho(y), B=rho(y^-1g), G0=rho(g), with rho the Frobenius norm. On the unitary group, A and B are at least 1 and G0<=AB. Therefore A^(-beta)B^(-beta)<=G0^(-beta/4)A^(-beta/2). The first factor is integrable over the compact/unipotent period variables and the second over the full group at the original StepGap thresholds. This establishes the required absolute Bochner integrability with a shorter estimate, preserving the actual character, measures and both free translates.

Asymptotic comparisons in the denominator bound. The source conclusion is eventual and uniform in the specified order, so its explicit numerical Large threshold can be replaced by proved uniform scale comparisons. The rapidly growing scale b=exp(x^epsilon) dominates the smaller exponential and polynomial factors; the remaining band scale has an additional exp(-(x^epsilon)^(1/3)) factor. The argument still chooses K=2000(|Ca|+1)^3 before the family, and later chooses the threshold only from the allowed fixed parameters and family cutoffs. It proves the entire original export conclusion, with no claim to the upstream existence of ConclF.
