# One cyclotomic specialization for all coprime powers

Fix a nonzero twist index $i$, write $\ell=\ell_i$, and use the local injections of coefficient DVRs $O_0\longrightarrow O\longrightarrow O_k$ on the column. Write $v_A$ for the additive valuation of a DVR $A$, with uniformizer of value one. Normalize by $\bar v_A=v_A/v_A(\ell)$. There are $R$ variables $X_q$ in the first block and $R$ variables $Y_q$ in the second. For all $M\ge M_0$ the level-selection argument receives two finite lists of polynomials. The polynomials themselves may change at every $M$. [Actual coefficient rings, quantifiers and tests][level]

* Each $F_{M,s}$ over $O_0$ has a coefficient of total degree at most $D$ and valuation at most $\mu_s$.
* Each $f_{M,y}$ over $O$ satisfies
  $$
  f_{M,y}\equiv H g\pmod{(Y_q,\ (1+X_q)^{\ell^M}-1:\ q=1,\ldots,R)},\qquad
  v_O(g(0))\le b_y .
  $$
  Modulo the maximal ideal, $H$ is congruent to $u\prod_q X_q^{n_q}$ modulo the $\ell^M$-th powers of *all* variables, with $u(0)\ne0$ and $n_q\le n_{\max}$. The factors, exponents and unit at the origin may depend on $M,y$. [Factor hypotheses][factor]

For every $\epsilon>0$ there exist $M\ge M_0$, $1\le k\le M$ and exponents $e_q<k$ on the $2R$ variables, with $\ell^{e_q}/\varphi(\ell^k)\le\epsilon$. Substitute $\zeta^{\ell^{e_q}}-1$ in each variable. **For every primitive $\ell^k$-th root $\zeta$**, all evaluations are nonzero and satisfy
$$
 \bar v(F_{M,s})\le\frac{\mu_s}{v_{O_0}(\ell)}+\epsilon,\qquad
 \bar v(f_{M,y})\le\frac{b_y}{v_O(\ell)}+\epsilon.
$$

**A bounded-degree coefficient for the second list.** Temporarily send every $X_q$ to $T$ and every $Y_q$ to zero. Put $d=Rn_{\max}$, $V=\max_y b_y$ (or zero for an empty list) and $W=(V+1)d$. For all sufficiently large $M$, the image of $H$ modulo a uniformizer $\pi$ has first nonzero coefficient at $h=\sum_q n_q\le d$, since $\ell^M>d$. The image of the error $f-Hg$ vanishes through degree $W$ modulo $\pi^{V+1}$. Indeed the generators from $Y_q$ vanish identically, and $\binom{\ell^M}{j}=(\ell^M/j)\binom{\ell^M-1}{j-1}$ for $1\le j\le W$ is divisible by $\ell^{V+1}$ when $M$ is large enough.

Work now over $(O/\pi^{b_y+1})[[T]]$. Write the image of $H$ as $T^h U+\pi B$, where $U(0)$ is a unit. If all coefficients of $f$ through degree $W$ vanished there, $Hg$ would be divisible by $T^{W+1}$. Multiply by $\sum_{j=0}^{b_y}(T^h U)^{b_y-j}(-\pi B)^j$. The result gives $T^{(b_y+1)h}U^{b_y+1}g\in (T^{W+1})$. Since $(b_y+1)h\le W$, this forces $g(0)=0$ in that quotient, a contradiction. Thus the one-variable image of $f$ has a coefficient of valuation at most $b_y$ in degree at most $W$. That coefficient is the sum of the original coefficients of the same total degree having zero $Y$-exponents. At least one has valuation at most $b_y$. Both lists now have bounded-degree witnesses in their respective DVRs. [Truncation and the original low-coefficient estimate][truncate] · [The corresponding product lemma][product]

**A common substitution.** Denote any polynomial from either list by $P_{M,t}$, its coefficient DVR by $A_t$ and its valuation bound by $b_t$. Pass to an increasing subsequence of levels along which the divisibility of every coefficient by each $\pi_t^j$, $0\le j\le b_t+1$, eventually stabilizes. There are countably many monomials, finitely many tests at each monomial and only finitely many divisibility patterns. A diagonal subsequence suffices. Each index $t$ retains a bounded-degree witness. Among the limiting coefficient valuations at most $b_t$, let $d_t$ be the least. Among exponent vectors with that limiting valuation choose $m_t$ lexicographically least when coordinates are read last to first. Choose $L\ge1$ with $\ell^L>2R\max_{t,q}m_{t,q}$, using zero for the empty maximum. Number the variables from zero and take $e_q=Lq$. Each $m_t$ has strictly smaller weight $w_t=\sum_q m_{t,q}\ell^{e_q}$ than every other vector at the same limiting valuation. At the last different coordinate the increase dominates the sum of all smaller-coordinate contributions of $m_t$. [Limiting divisibility patterns and weights][limit]

Set $W_*=\max_t w_t$ with zero available. On all sufficiently late members of the subsequence the divisibility patterns have settled through total degree $W_*$. Substitute $(1+T)^{\ell^{e_q}}-1$ for each variable. Terms of total degree greater than $w_t$ cannot affect degree $w_t$. In the remaining truncation divide the coefficients by $\pi_t^{d_t}$ and reduce modulo $\pi_t$. The substitution becomes $T^{\ell^{e_q}}$ in residue characteristic $\ell$. The coefficient in degree $w_t$ comes solely from $m_t$ because of its strict minimum weight. Thus the resulting polynomial $P_t(T)$ has a coefficient in degree $w_t$ of valuation $d_t\le b_t$, on arbitrarily large common levels. [Coefficient after substitution][weight]

**Choice of a cyclotomic order.** For any polynomial $\sum_j c_j T^j$ with $c_w\ne0$, set $a_j=v_A(c_j)$ on its support and $E=v_A(\ell)$. For $k\ge1$ consider the lines $a_j+j E/\varphi(\ell^k)$. At most $w+a_w$ levels have a tie for the minimum. Indeed, at a tie let $u<v$ be the extreme minimizing indices. As $k$ increases, $E/\varphi(\ell^k)$ strictly decreases, so the next $u$ is at least the previous $v$, by comparing the two minimum inequalities. Ties with $u<w$ use distinct indices in $0,\ldots,w-1$. At each remaining tie, $v>w$ and $a_v<a_w$. The successive values $a_v$ strictly decrease by comparison at the later positive slope. There are at most $a_w$ such ties. [Exceptional-level bound][ties]

For a primitive root in any local DVR extension, $\bar v(\zeta-1)=1/\varphi(\ell^k)$. Indeed $\Phi_{\ell^k}(1)=\ell$ and all factors $1-\zeta^a$ for $(a,\ell)=1$ have the same valuation, since their ratios with $1-\zeta$ reduce to $a$ modulo the maximal ideal. Thus at a level without a tie the evaluated polynomial has a unique least-valuation term. Its normalized valuation equals the minimum and is bounded by $a_w/E+w/\varphi(\ell^k)$ for every such root. [Evaluation for every primitive root][roots]

Choose $k_0>\max_q e_q$ large enough that both $\max_q\ell^{e_q}$ and $W_*$ divided by $\varphi(\ell^{k_0})$ are at most $\epsilon$, with $k_0\ge1$ and vacuous coordinate bounds when $R=0$. Let $B=\sum_t(w_t+b_t)$. Choose a stabilized level $M\ge\max(M_0,k_0+B)$. After fixing these actual polynomials, at most $B$ levels are exceptional for their union. The $B+1$ choices in $[k_0,k_0+B]$ supply a common $k$. This proves the assertion. In particular $M$ and $k$ need not be chosen separately for different powers. [Choice on the actual level][joint]

For the column, the first list consists of the good Galois labels and the second list consists of the abelian labels and the pairs of row and side. The argument assumes their bounded coefficients and factor congruences at every sufficiently high level. The [joint choice on the fitted first pass](FIRST_PASS_COMMON_PRIMES.md) supplies these arithmetic inputs on one conductor at each sufficiently high level. Once the coordinate characters have been realized on the ray quotient, choose $\psi$ using the designated root. Raising $\psi$ to any integer $a$ coprime to $\ell$ simply replaces this root by $\zeta^a$ in the same formula. This is again primitive, as is its inverse on the opposite side. The same $M,k,Q$ and valuation bounds apply to all these powers, including negative ones. [Origin of the two lists][lists] · [Ray factor and powers on both sides][powers]

## One choice for powers with bounded $\ell$-adic order

The finite-window specialization in [Nelson’s revised workbook](https://github.com/ultronozm/grh-workbook/blob/637a0de87e55071e53ce48a1c59b9f2aaadf4b73/20261126T040300--roots-of-unity-for-many-polynomials__workbook.tex#L118-L172) suggests allowing powers divisible by $\ell$ as well. On the original fitted first pass the bounded-coefficient Galois, toric and row-determinant lists above yield this simultaneously, even though their polynomials vary with $M$.

Fix a nonnegative integer $a$. In the construction above keep the substituted polynomials and common $k_0$. Here $B=\sum_t(w_t+b_t)$ bounds the total number of exceptional cyclotomic orders for these **particular** polynomials, independently of the sufficiently late level $M$ on the stabilized subsequence. Choose such an $M$ with
$$
                   M\ge k_0-1+(a+1)(B+1).
$$
Partition the first $(a+1)(B+1)$ integers starting at $k_0$ into $B+1$ consecutive disjoint blocks of length $a+1$. At least one block contains no tie for any polynomial. Write $k$ for its largest member. If $h\ne0$ and $0\le v_\ell(h)\le a$, and $\zeta$ is primitive of order $\ell^k$, then $\zeta^h$ is primitive of order $\ell^{k-v_\ell(h)}$. That order's exponent lies in the chosen block. Applying the unique-minimum formula there to each polynomial gives exactly the two normalized upper bounds stated at the beginning of this note, with the same $M,k$, the same coordinate exponents and the same error $\epsilon$. The normalized valuation of each coordinate difference is $\ell^{e_q}/\varphi(\ell^{k-v_\ell(h)})\le\epsilon$ since $k-v_\ell(h)\ge k_0>e_q$. Negative $h$ and arbitrary unit parts are included.

For example $a=\lfloor\log_\ell m\rfloor$ covers all $1\le h\le m$. One can even take $a=\lfloor(M-k_0+1)/(B+1)\rfloor-1$ on sufficiently late stabilized levels. It tends to infinity. This is a statement about the valuations of the original specialized polynomials. Nonunit powers can reduce the conductor or change a local character type. Transport to Selmer lengths, theta indices or the prescribed arithmetic retwist at those smaller conductors requires the corresponding arithmetic identification. The source's existing all-coprime-powers export uses exactly the inputs in the preceding paragraph.

## What the Galois determinant measures

For a coefficient DVR $A$ with uniformizer $\pi$ and fraction field $K$, let a specialized square matrix act on $(K/A)^h$. If its determinant is nonzero, Smith normal form gives elementary divisors $\pi^{t_1},\ldots,\pi^{t_h}$ up to units. Invertible changes of basis act invertibly on $(K/A)^h$. The kernel of each diagonal entry is $\pi^{-t_j}A/A$, of length $t_j$. Thus the kernel has length $v_A(\det)$. For a singular matrix a copy of $K/A$ survives and the length is infinite. The Galois-side comparison transfers this equality to the Selmer group when the universal presentation is natural on torsion coefficients and the selected character is trivial above $\ell$. The latter condition identifies the specified local conditions at $\ell$ after specialization. Here the determinant measures the total length of this twisted Selmer group. [Alpöge’s item 17](ALPOGE_REMARK_GUIDE.md) motivates keeping track of cohomological length elsewhere in the construction as well. The [square presentation](FIRST_PASS_SELMER_PRESENTATION.md) and [bounded determinant coefficient](FIRST_PASS_GALOIS_PRIMES.md) give this equality on the prime family used for export.
