# When do the arithmetic and spectral periods agree?

**Edition 0.90 · 25 November 2026**

The period-height argument attaches two bilinear forms to each word in a chain of theta lifts. One comes from rational arithmetic data, the other from an automorphic period integral. They may have different normalizations and different column coordinates for each word. All words, however, must use **one and the same change of row coordinates**. Edition 0.90 supplies the common automorphic realization for an already selected family member and gives an exact test of the remaining period identity using the ranks of three matrices. ## Where the two realizations meet

For a member already supplied by the original selection, consider an integral form in the good subspace at its finite level. In model coordinates it is a vector in the intersection of the rational good subspace and the integral level lattice. Its image under the row operator is exactly the native integral row image. This works prime by prime at the tuple chosen at that prime. The metrics agree as well. At the distinguished infinite place both squared norms are the same automorphic norm divided by the same finite-level factor. The argument works at both the base and the sharp level.

The remaining analytic point is that two evaluations of a row land in the same automorphic Hilbert space up to the prescribed normalization. Cuspidal decay and the pointwise Sobolev bounds give continuity on smooth vectors. The invariant Petersson pairing on each irreducible infinitesimal module is then a scalar multiple of the invariant inner product. Evaluating at a smooth unit vector at each real place computes the scalar without dividing by a matrix coefficient which might vanish. Transporting the resulting row identity and the column bases gives the required comparison on the selected bundle itself. This proves the hypothesis called `JoinAt` in edition 0.89. ## The three ranks

Write $E$ for the common row space and $M_u$ for the column space for word $u$. Regard the two bilinear periods as maps $T_u,S_u:E\to M_u^\vee$. The rational period map already recorded by the arithmetic construction computes the entries of $T_u$. One distinguished base-tuple lift per basis vector of $E$ suffices. The entries of $S_u$ are native spectral period integrals. Taking adjoints uses the given Hilbert metrics, including the dual column metric if the rational bases are not orthonormal. [Quotient lifts and their Gram matrices](continuation_v90/base.tex)

Set $A_u=T_u^*T_u/\operatorname{tr}(T_u^*T_u)$, and define $C_u$ by the same rule from $S_u$. Assign zero to a zero map. The wordwise scales disappear, and equality of row Grams is exactly the condition for a wordwise column isometry. Thus the complex period comparison is equivalent to simultaneous unitary equivalence of $A$ and $C$. Edition 0.87 proved the mixed-trace criterion for that condition. The new rank formulation needs three linear systems, each with $(\dim E)^2$ unknowns. [Earlier trace test](continuation_v87/periodbessel.tex) · [Published simultaneous-equivalence theorem](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1701.08826#page=8)

For two lists $P,Q$, let $h(P,Q)$ be the dimension of the solution space of $Q_uX=XP_u$ for every $u$. Decompose each row space into irreducible pieces for its list of self-adjoint operators. If a given piece occurs $m_j$ times on the arithmetic side and $n_j$ times on the spectral side, Schur's lemma gives

$$h(A,A)+h(C,C)-2h(A,C)=\sum_j(m_j-n_j)^2.$$

The left side is computable by linear equations in the original row coordinates. Its vanishing matches the pieces with all their word labels and gives the common unitary. The three dimensions also detect a zero period on one side with a nonzero partner on the other. In dimension one this matching of zero periods is the entire comparison. **The outstanding assertion is that this integer vanishes for every sufficiently late selected member.** [Three-system theorem](continuation_v90/intertwiners.tex)

For example, take two words on a two-dimensional row space. Suppose the arithmetic periods see two orthogonal lines and the spectral periods both see the first line. Each separate word gives a rank-one orthogonal projection of trace one. For the arithmetic list and the spectral list separately, the commuting matrices are the diagonal matrices, so $h(A,A)=h(C,C)=2$. A cross-intertwiner must send the first arithmetic line into both the first spectral line and its orthogonal complement, and likewise for the second arithmetic line. Hence $h(A,C)=0$ and the defect is four. This elementary example illustrates why a word-by-word norm calculation leaves a genuine issue for the theta periods.
