# The functional inequality before family selection

The functional hypothesis of the common construction holds on the original extension that remembers the signed character list, its origin and the extra grid condition. The conclusion is conditional on the division estimate of the family to which it is applied. This implication can be proved before choosing a family. It is one of the two inputs singled out in [the construction of the design window](../arithmetic/COMMON_DESIGN_WINDOW_TRANSFER.md). The other input is the geometric covolume comparison. We use the standing unitary-group and local theta hypotheses, agreement of the theta integrals, local matching, and the allowed local Langlands system. The original assembly derives the functional implication from these inputs before using the covolume bound. [Native assembly][assembly] · [Roadmap, the common construction][required] · [Precise functional conclusion][fun]

## What must hold before the family is chosen

Write $Q$ for constructed membership, $Q'$ for good membership on the augmented extension, and $L_k$ for its linkage of points to bundles. A linked row gives a strengthening $R$ of $Q$ on eligible bases and a property $E$ of bundles. Beyond the construction thresholds, every linked bundle over a $Q'\land R$ member satisfies $E$ at the permitted indices. Its last assertion is universal over a construction class $\mathcal A$ and a selector $\sigma$ satisfying the linkage and $E$. For the functional row, at every eligible $F_m\in W_3(\mathcal A)$ and height $C_H$, it has the following form. If for some $a,X$ every scale $x\ge X$ in the family and every index and layer satisfies
$$
                 \eta_j\le aD_0 L(B_0+N_c),
$$
then eventually at all its selected indices
$$
           \mu_{\max}(RR)+\langle\log\kappa\rangle_\infty
                         \le x^{C_H}b.
$$
Here $L=x^\varepsilon$ in the family division bound. $RR$ is the glued base-level row lattice of the bundle, and the frame factor $\kappa$ uses squared norms. The row contract asks for a lower bound on a separate comparison parameter $C$ followed by a point cutoff for each $C$ above it. The proof gives lower bound zero and even a common cutoff for all these $C$. This structure is what allows the row to enter the intersection before the selector exists. ## The marked grid and the four rows

First choose a threshold $C_{F0}$ for the fitted functional and, independently, a threshold $C_{F1}$ for the full-grid extension. Set $C_F=\max(C_{F0},C_{F1})$ and use the same value for the dependence parameter. To see why the field extension is available at this stage, fix the preliminary frame and its finite twist set. All conductor sets are contained in the union of its ramification sets and their conjugates. The distinguished place and its lift each range over a finite set. At fixed infinity type, any two allowable characters differ by a character of a fixed finite ray class group. There are therefore finitely many marked keys. Take the compositum of the fields for the finite tests and square values for these keys in the prescribed embedding. This is the fourth field request, whose dependence on the finite twist set is allowed. It is imposed before the supported realization. The auxiliary split prime $q_0$ on this grid is already determined by the host (the first prime in the specified progression avoiding the conductor norm). Pullback retains it. [Roadmap v41, host and grid](https://github.com/rh-lean-roadmap/rh-lean-roadmap/blob/c7755c1086f05d0918ee8496d95912e4f35e22f9/docs/src/book/main.tex#L15289-L15306) The resulting full grid preserves the ordered characters, conductor sets, distinguished place and its lift. Its coefficient values are read in the field actually supplied by the subsequent realization. [Jin, finite keys][keys] · [Field request and its order][request] · [Conclusion of marked transport][transport]

An augmented witness has the form $((n,o),g)$, with $n$ the list-extension datum, $o$ the origin and $g$ the grid property. The projection $((n,o),g)\mapsto n$ fixes the base, frame, twist, two levels and bundle indices. It transfers the twelve open rows from the list extension. In particular the divisor row needed here has a construction on this earlier extension. The mixed period identity for a witnessed bundle kills its definite base kernel. Together with its rational pairing law and real realization, this gives its divisor comparison. The mixed identity used here follows from the unitary theta and doubling inputs on this extension. This provides the divisor row without taking a Gram inequality or a nonvanishing conclusion from a subsequently selected family. [Construction on the list extension][open] · [Mixed identity and divisor comparison][divisor] · [Pullback of the twelve rows][pullback]

Intersect the primitive, divisor and good-type rows with the geometry row, retaining their strengthenings and bundle predicates at identical indices. The geometry row is obtained as follows. Choose a compatible recursion $t$ using the tie of this bundle. The class-sum and divisor assertion holds for each such $t$, so it supplies the point compatibility for this very $t$. The grid assertion holds for every $t$ with that compatibility and hence supplies a fitted grid on the same point. On an arbitrary selector meeting these predicates choose these recursions pointwise. Its family-membership hypotheses give the seven family duties. Its primitive and divisor data, good local types, root and frame compatibility, and real globalization give the eventual admissible point hypotheses. The real package and reference row in each new summand are exactly those of its original bundle. All four rows and the implication can consequently be prepared before any common class is chosen. ## Why the slope bound is uniform

At a fitted point the surjective reference quotient $\iota_0 P_0$ and the separating tests give $\ker V_0=\ker P_0$. The rational map $\Psi$ induced by $V_0$ on the quotient is injective. At the tuple $t=\mu(w)$ used over a finite place $w$, the square comparison identifies the same $\Psi\iota_tP_t$ with $r_t^{-1}\zeta_t^{-1}V_t$, where $r_t\ne0$ and $\zeta_t$ is a root of unity. Thus $\Psi(RR_w)\subseteq\mathfrak p_w^{-d_F(w)-v_w(r_t)}M_w$ for the test lattice $M$. At infinity, the level-zero and level-one base eigenvalues agree. The level-one lower bound follows on the whole admissible point class from the unitary inputs. Its sixth entry has zero coefficient of $C$, and its cutoff is uniform in the index, layer and $C$. Applying it to the quotient norm bounds the mean logarithmic squared operator norm of $\Psi$. Taking exterior determinants on every positive-rank sublattice gives the fitted inequality
$$
 \mu_{\max}(RR)+\langle\log\kappa\rangle_\infty
               \le E_M+H_I+2G_*+B_F .
$$
Here $E_M$ bounds the maximal slope of $M$, $H_I$ bounds the map height, and $2G_*+B_F$ bounds the finite ideal cost in the squared-norm convention. The family division antecedent is used in the estimates for the fitted datum. [Fitted rational comparison][comparison] · [Level-one floor at all members][floor] · [Jin, slope of the injection][height] · [Fitted calculation in this repository](../periods/PERIOD_SELECTED_BASE_FUNCTIONAL.md)

The target slope in this argument can be checked on nonsaturated sublattices as well. Use the coordinate dual of the direct sum of the test Weyl lattices, with metric Gram matrix $G=(H^{-1})^{\mathsf T}$. The integer $s$ given by the product of squared Fock norms of the integral extremal vectors clears the Hermitian pairing on this dual lattice. For any submodule of rank $j>0$, choose a basis $B$ of its rational span and let $\mathfrak a$ be the fractional ideal of its top exterior power in that basis. Put $\Delta=\det(B^*GB)$, with $*$ using the specified field involution. Positivity at the distinguished conjugation embedding gives $\Delta\ne0$. Taking determinants of the cleared pairing gives
$$
             s^j\Delta\,\mathfrak a^*\mathfrak a
                       \subseteq\mathcal O_K .
$$
At another embedding $\sigma$, exterior Cauchy–Schwarz bounds $|\sigma(\Delta)|^2$ by the product of the two metric Gram determinants at $\sigma$ and $\sigma^\#=\overline{\sigma\circ *}$. This permutation preserves the embedding average. The two determinant ideals have equal norm. The product formula now bounds the submodule's squared-norm slope by $\log s$, and the extremal-vector bound gives $\log s\le4f n_pR_p\log(n_pR_p)=E_M$. Retaining $\mathfrak a$ makes the argument valid even when the determinant module has no global integral basis. Roadmap v40 makes the determinant-ideal argument explicit, and Jin gives its paired-embedding proof. [Roadmap v40, the dual test lattice][target] · [Jin, the fractional ideal and both embeddings][ideal]

For the order of thresholds put $T=\lceil |C_t|\rceil+1$ for the outer rank exponent and $Q_x=x^{T+9}$. Fix the closed size thresholds independently of $F_m$. The middle threshold can require $C_H\ge16(T+9)+1$. Once the family and $C_H$ are supplied, choose one constant $C_1$ for its fitted estimates, level sizes and base eigenvalue floor. Jin's and the Roadmap's computation gives an upper bound $13{,}000{,}000{,}000\, C_1^4 Q_x^{16}b$ for the preceding sum. Take the family cutoff above $13{,}000{,}000{,}000\, C_1^4$ and all its fitted, division and admissibility cutoffs. This proves the desired inequality at every index beyond that cutoff for all $C\ge0$. This $C_1$ is a proof constant fixed for the family, distinct from the quantified parameter $C$. Applying the bound to any selector satisfying the four rows proves the implication required by B8.06 on the augmented extension. The [common-family argument](../geometry/COMMON_FAMILY_AND_PERIOD_HEIGHT.md) supplies the geometric comparison B8.34 on this same extension. ## The two archimedean averages on the same CM curve

Fix a curve datum with period lattice $\Lambda$, an unkilled two-torsion point $t_0$, and a number field $M\subset\mathbb C$ containing $g_2(\Lambda),g_3(\Lambda),\wp_\Lambda(t_0)$. Write $R=\mathrm{rd}(E)$ and $\lambda_d=\Re(L'(1,\chi_d)/L(1,\chi_d))$. Assume $L\ge1$, $\log R\le L$ and $|\lambda_d|\le L$. For each embedding $\rho$ let $A_\rho$ be the area of its conjugate lattice and choose any unkilled two-torsion point $t_\rho$ there. Conjugation preserves the curve datum conditions, including discriminant one, integral $j$, and stability under the integers of $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-d})$. The unkilled condition makes $t_\rho$ a nonzero class modulo the lattice. Write $\langle\cdot\rangle$ for the average over the $[M:\mathbb Q]$ embeddings and $\log^+s=\max(0,\log s)$ for $s>0$. [Roadmap B8.21ma](https://github.com/rh-lean-roadmap/rh-lean-roadmap/blob/c7755c1086f05d0918ee8496d95912e4f35e22f9/docs/src/book/main.tex#L19399-L19417) · [Original conjugate-lattice proof](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/FoundationProofs/ConjugateLatticeMeansPosLogAreaThetaBounded.lean#L16-L67)

**The inverse area.** For $\Lambda=a(\mathbb Z+\tau\mathbb Z)$, $y=\Im\tau$, its area $A=|a|^2y$ satisfies $A^6=(2\pi)^{12}y^6|\Delta(\tau)|$ by the discriminant-one normalization. The right side is bounded on a modular fundamental domain by continuity on compact truncations and exponential decay at the cusp. Fix the resulting absolute bound $A\le C_A$, with $C_A\ge1$. The CM period identity gives $\langle\log(A_\rho/\pi)\rangle=\gamma+\log(2\pi)-\tfrac12\log d-\lambda_d$. Since $\sqrt d\le R$ and $\log^+(1/A)=\log^+A-\log A$, we obtain
$$
 \langle\log^+(1/A_\rho)\rangle\le\log^+C_A+\tfrac12\log d+\lambda_d
 \le (\log^+C_A+2)L.
$$
This uses the signed period identity before taking the positive part. [Period identity and the equal multiplicities of its conjugates](REMARK_NORMS.md#the-area-identity) · [Discriminant bound](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/FoundationProofs/AreaBoundedOfDiscrEqOne.lean#L49-L82)

**The normalized theta value.** On any one conjugate lattice, let $\sigma_*$ be the odd theta function with $\sigma_*'(0)=1$. Its Gaussian mean square is $N=A^{-1}\int_{\mathbb C/\Lambda}|\sigma_*(z)|^2e^{-\pi|z|^2/A}\,dz$, with planar area measure. Put $w_u=|\sigma_*(u)|^2e^{-\pi|u|^2/A}$. Gaussian translation preserves the mean, so the theta function translated by $t$ and divided by its value at zero has mean square $\nu_t=N/w_t$. The genus-one norm identity gives $N^2=2\pi^2/A$. In period coordinates the canonical odd theta has mean square $(2y)^{-1/2}$ by integrating its Fourier series. Its derivative in the lattice coordinate has absolute value $2\pi|\eta(\tau)|^3/|a|$ by Jacobi's derivative formula. Dividing by this derivative and using $|a|^{12}=(2\pi)^{12}|\eta(\tau)|^{24}$ gives the stated identity. [Gaussian integral and derivative normalization](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/FoundationProofs/OddThetaNormUnimodularLattice.lean#L243-L325)

For every nonzero period $z$, the sublattice spanned by $z,\sqrt{-d}\,z$ has area $\sqrt d\,|z|^2\ge A$. Thus the open disk of radius $r=\sqrt{A/(4R)}$ embeds in the torus. For any $u$, the entire function $f_u(z)=\sigma_*(z+u)\exp\bigl(-\pi(z\bar u+|u|^2/2)/A\bigr)$ satisfies $|f_u(0)|^2=w_u$. The circular mean of $|f_u|^2$ is at least $w_u$. Integrating this inequality on the disk against its radial Gaussian gives
$$
 A(1-e^{-\pi r^2/A})w_u
 \le\int_{|z|<r}|f_u(z)|^2e^{-\pi|z|^2/A}\,dz \le AN.
$$
The second inequality uses periodicity of the integrand and injectivity of the disk. As $(1-e^{-s})^{-1}\le1+1/s$ for $s>0$, we have $w_u\le(1+4R/\pi)N\le BN$ with $B=3(2R+1)$. This disk argument supplies the constant used in the original genus-one estimate. [Original pointwise bound and shortest-period estimate](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/FoundationProofs/SigmaStarValueLeMeanSquareGenusOne.lean#L195-L242)

To bound the reciprocal of $w_t$, take all three nonzero half-periods $t_i$ and write $x_i=\wp(t_i)$. These are the distinct roots of $4x^3-g_2x-g_3$. For one such $t$, put $x=\wp(t)$, $u=f_t(0)$ and $k=\pi\bar t/A$. The sigma addition formula and $\sigma_*(z+2t)=-e^{2k(z+t)}\sigma_*(z)$ give $(\wp(z+t)-x)\sigma_*(z+t)^2\sigma_*(t)^2=e^{2k(z+t)}\sigma_*(z)^2$. Comparing the $z^2$ coefficients yields $\wp''(t)u^4=2$. The differential equation gives $(12x^2-g_2)u^4=4$. Substituting $g_3=4x^3-g_2x$ in $g_2^3-27g_3^2=1$ gives $(12x^2-g_2)^2(g_2-3x^2)=1$, so $u^8=16(g_2-3x^2)$. By Viète's formulas $g_2-3x_i^2=(x_j-x_k)^2$ for the other two roots. The discriminant of the monic cubic is $(g_2^3-27g_3^2)/16=1/16$. Multiplying and taking absolute values therefore gives $(w_{t_1}w_{t_2}w_{t_3})^4=16^3/16=256$. The other two values are each at most $BN$, so $1/w_t\le (BN)^2/4$ at **each** nonzero half-period. Thus
$$
 \log^+\nu_t\le 2\log^+ B+
 \tfrac32\bigl(\log^+(2\pi^2)+\log^+(1/A)\bigr).
$$
The exponent $3/2$ comes from the three factors of $N$ in $N/w_t\le B^2N^3/4$. [Half-period identity](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/FoundationProofs/OddThetaHalfPeriodValueIsIntegral.lean#L49-L119) · [Original cubic product and reciprocal estimate](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/FoundationProofs/ExistsBoundMeansPosLogAreaNormalisedTheta.lean#L22-L127)

Finally $R\ge1$ and $B\le9R$, hence $\log^+B\le9L$. Averaging over $\rho$ and applying the inverse-area estimate yields $\langle\log^+\nu_{t_\rho}\rangle\le C_{\rm gal}L$ with $C_{\rm gal}=18+\tfrac32(\log^+(2\pi^2)+\log^+C_A+2)$. The choices of half-period may vary independently. In the functional estimate one averages first over embeddings extending a given coefficient embedding, then over coefficient embeddings. Each such fiber has cardinality equal to the relative field degree, so this is the same global average. For a mean over complex places represented by one embedding per pair, use the displayed upper bound for $\log^+\nu_t$. It depends on the lattice only through its area, which is invariant under complex conjugation. Pairing the embeddings therefore preserves the bound even with independently chosen half-periods. The coefficient-field involution used for the row factors permutes the infinite places.
