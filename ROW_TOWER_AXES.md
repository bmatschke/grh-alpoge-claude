# Vanishing on the two axes of the signature diagram

Let $a,n,d=a+n$ be the ranks and let $e=(a-n)/2$. At a fixed tuple put $\kappa_w=m_{\alpha,w}-m_{\beta,w}$. Assume the actual row inequalities $\kappa_w>d$ at every archimedean place and $n,e>2(p+q)+1$. In the signed oscillator convention the source signatures are $(a-p,p)$, $(q,n-q)$ and the target signature is $(d-p-q,p+q)$. Write $J_b,J_s$ for the two theta integrals. Take arbitrary polynomial tests on the big side and polynomial tests primed at the prescribed split place on the small side. Their finite parts can be any allowed Schwartz–Bruhat data. The two inputs need not match. Scalars can be absorbed into the polynomials. Write $F=J_b(\Phi)-J_s(\Phi')$.

* At $(0,q)$ for every $q\ge1$, the alpha component of the line constant term vanishes, even for each summand separately. Consequently a pure-alpha $F$ is zero.
* At $(p,0)$ for every $p\ge1$, the beta component vanishes for each summand separately. Consequently a pure-beta $F$ is zero.

“Pure” means that the entire line constant term along one rational line satisfies the indicated law for every right argument. For the first assertion the two distinct laws are $\alpha(l)|l|_E^{n/2}$ and $\beta(l)|l|_E^{a/2}$, valid on both axes. The vanishing of a component is local at infinity combined with an unfolded identity. The conclusion $F=0$ also uses cuspidal orthogonality, including a nonzero definite-bottom lift in the correct Witt tower. The hypotheses for this last use are given below. ## The ordinary evaluation on the small side

At $(0,q)$ the big source is definite. Its only rational null vector is zero, and its line constant term has beta law. For the small source remove one target plane, with rational isotropic generators $x',y'$ and complement $W_1$. The **ordinary** evaluation $E'_0$ leaves the small source of rank $n$ unchanged. It takes values in the oscillator for the real pair
$$
 U(q,n-q)\ \times\ U(R,S),\qquad R=d-q-1,\quad S=q-1 .
$$
Thus for $q=2$ the lower target is $U(d-3,1)$, and the lower small source is $U(2,n-2)$. The cone from the nonzero null vectors, which removes two source rows as well, belongs to the *other* coefficient. [Small two-law decomposition with the precise support condition][pu-any]

At the designated split place, in the barred chart an upper matrix has columns $(x,Z,y)$ with $Z$ of size $n\times(d-2)$. Up to the fixed scalar $E'_0$ integrates one of $x,y$ at zero in the other. A full-rank upper test can have a singular $Z$ in its evaluated support. Apply the [primed replacement](ROW_BETA_PRIMED_REPLACEMENT.md) to each finite tensor coefficient, after any compact target translation under consideration. On a matrix ball for $Z$, the row sieve gives a combination of source translates whose sum of coefficients, after normalizing the translate operators on the lower oscillator, is nonzero since $n-1<(d-2)/2$ and the splitting character is unitary. The upper and lower restricted source actions intertwine under $E'_0$ in the compatible charts. The normalized replacement is a sum of source-translation differences modulo the original upper test. Each summand stays primed on the upper side and the new evaluation is primed on the lower side. The earlier full-rank kernel majorant gives absolute integration, including after each such translation. Thus the upper integrated function is unchanged. [Exact simultaneous replacement][pu-move] · [Source invariance on convergent vectors][pu-conv]

In the mixed orbit formula the alpha part contains the ordinary zero term and a projective-line correction from switching the polarization in the nonzero-vector sum. This correction vanishes after the preceding replacement. The further evaluation factors through $E'_0$, followed by source-plane evaluation on $W_1$, up to a nonzero scalar by Heisenberg uniqueness. Its local invariance forces a nonzero rational source vector to be a left kernel vector for a contributing $Z$, by the character/modulation relation. This is excluded on the lower primed support for every such line. The resulting alpha value on a unit-scalar Levi slice is a fixed multiple of $J_{V',W_1}(E'_0\widetilde\Phi')(h_1)$. This is an *ordinary absolutely convergent* lower integral. For fixed finite factors and $h_1$, it is a continuous, source-invariant functional of the archimedean Schwartz test by the full-rank estimate and quotient Haar invariance. The lower finite support, including its preserved priming under target translation, suffices without a nonzero matching map for this lowered pair. [Projective correction][pu-corr] · [Its local vanishing][pu-corr-loc] · [Passage to the lower integral][pu-small]

## The missing compact weight

Fix one real place, one lower target argument and all other data. In the positive $U(q)$ block of the source, the lower polynomial Fock space is the symmetric algebra on $R$ copies of the defining representation $V=\mathbb C^q$, $S$ copies of its dual, and variables from the other source rows on which this $U(q)$ acts trivially. This is the action on the variables after the scalar oscillator-vacuum shift has been removed. For a rank-one projection $P$ in that block, the unsplit infinitesimal action is the defining/dual derivation plus $(R-S)/2$ times the identity. The actual small-source splitting adds $+\kappa_w/2$. Source invariance therefore requires the functional $\ell$ on the polynomial variables to satisfy
$$
 \ell(D_P f)=-h_w\ell(f),\qquad h_w=(\kappa_w+R-S)/2>0.
$$
These equations follow by differentiating the action on the finite-dimensional compact orbit of each polynomial. Equivalently, they are Fourier weight identities for a compact circle in the actual split oscillator. The splitting sign uses $\alpha/\beta$. [Oscillator determinant shift][pu-der] · [Source splitting and invariant functional][pu-real]

For each linear polynomial $L$, choose a unit vector $v\in V$ annihilated by all $S=q-1$ dual components of $L$. Such a $v$ exists because $\dim V=q$. Use $P$ equal to the orthogonal projection onto $\mathbb Cv$. Its circle rotates the defining variables along that line with weight $+1$, and the dual components of this particular $L$ are fixed. Thus $L=L_0+L_1$ with $D_PL_0=0$, $D_PL_1=L_1$. Every term of $L^N$ has a nonnegative integer weight $j$. Its evaluation by $\ell$ is killed by $j+h_w$, so $\ell(L^N)=0$, including $N=0$. Powers of linear forms span each homogeneous degree by polarization over $\mathbb C$. It follows that $\ell=0$ on every polynomial, and density and continuity give the same conclusion on Schwartz vectors. For $q=2$ this chooses a line killed by the *one* dual column for each linear form. The choice of the line separately for each $L$ handles all lower tests. [Polarization][pu-pol] · [Rank deficiency and trace-one action][pu-rank] · [Passage to actual real Schwartz functionals][pu-density]

Apply this to the Levi slice after every compact target translation. Compact translation preserves polynomiality and full-rank support, and the replacement can be chosen anew for each translated test. Unipotent invariance, the alpha law and parabolic Iwasawa then give zero on the entire target. The two exponents are distinct since $a>n$, so a norm-changing idele separates them. Uniqueness of this decomposition gives the same vanishing for any difference $F$. [Real application on the small side][pu-end-a]

## The large source when the small defect is zero

At $(p,0)$ the small source is definite, so contributes only the alpha law. For the big beta term the lower real pair is
$$
 U(a-p,p)\ \times\ U(R,S),\qquad R=d-p-1,\quad S=p-1 .
$$
The usual mixed orbit expression identifies the beta component of the big integral on the Levi with its ordinary zero evaluation on this lower pair. The source splitting exponent is $-\kappa_w$. Use the negative $U(p)$ block. Its defining copies now belong to the $S=p-1$ negative target columns, its dual copies to the $R$ positive columns, and its vacuum shift is $(S-R)/2$. Source invariance requires $\ell(D_P f)=+h_w\ell(f)$ with $h_w=(\kappa_w+R-S)/2>0$. For each linear polynomial choose a line orthogonal to all its $S$ defining vectors and let $P$ project onto that line. All defining components of that polynomial are fixed. Its remaining dual components have weights $0,-1$. Its powers have nonpositive weights, and hence pair to zero. The same polarization argument proves vanishing. Unrestricted convergence holds on the lower target because $(d-2)+2p<2a$ follows from $e>p-1$. The lower big integral with an arbitrary fixed finite factor is a continuous functional by the ordinary Weil majorant. No primed replacement for this large-source evaluation is needed. ## From the vanishing line term to cuspidality

If the whole line constant term has the missing character, that term vanishes by the two distinct norm exponents. Witt extension carries the vanishing to every rational line. On a target of Witt index at least two the [higher-parabolic argument](ROW_TOWER_PARABOLICS.md) applies to this same difference at every right argument. It gives cuspidality, also when the inputs are unrelated.

## Orthogonality on an axis of any length

There is one indefinite source and one definite source. Against a cuspidal difference $F$ the indefinite-source pairing is zero by the [reverse-kernel argument](ROW_MIXED_CUSP_ORTHOGONALITY.md). The proof uses a fresh completely split place, where the two rectangular quotients agree. This is a local property of the two Weil actions with the actual reciprocal splitting characters. Finite source-translation differences are in the kernel of the absolute integral (on the primed side keep its designated place free). At that fresh place the reverse pairing is consequently represented through the *opposite* rectangular model, for a difference of arbitrary tests on the two sides. Stacking rows gives the square distribution and makes the reverse integral invariant under the local source group. Use one rational source hyperbolic plane and the torus growing on it, fixing the other planes. Full rank at a spectator place gives decay along this torus and a polynomial target-height majorant uniform in the source. Rapid decay of $F$ gives dominated convergence and Fubini. On the indefinite big side unrestricted convergence allows its test to be replaced first by a full-rank representative at another good place. All these steps work with more than one negative coordinate because escape along this one torus suffices. [Both orientations of orthogonality][pu-rev] · [One escaping source torus with the general estimate][pu-escape]

For the definite source $Y$ let $r=q$ or $p$ and remove all $r$ target planes, giving a definite $X$ in the same rational Witt tower with $W=X\oplus\mathbb H^r$. At $(0,q)$ keep the positive big source and shorten the other source by $2q$. At $(p,0)$ keep the negative small source and shorten the other source by $2p$. Repeated rational splittings on the unchanged-source side of this tower and the complementary source give a definite triangle at ranks $(a,n-2q)$ or $(a-2p,n)$. The lower ranks stay in the definite range. Use the [definite doubling nonvanishing](ROW_MATCHED_SQUARE_TESTS.md#nonzero-integrated-kernels-at-the-definite-bottom) on this triangle with the same additive character and splitting characters. Their restriction parities are unchanged, since all rank changes are even. This supplies a polynomial/Schwartz test with $\theta^X_{Y,\psi}(\mathbf1)\ne0$ in the *actual target tower*. If an equivalent coordinate realization was used for the bottom seed, transport it by the Schwartz intertwiner for the same rational spaces and Weil splitting. Nonvanishing survives both invertible operations. This is a statement about the existence of a vector, sufficient for the whole-space theorem. It requires no relation between the arbitrarily chosen tests in $F$.

The constant representation on $U(Y)$ is irreducible cuspidal. The lift $\pi=\theta^X_{Y,\psi}(\mathbf1)$ is cuspidal by anisotropy of $X$ and irreducible by Wu's Theorem 5.5. With $\chi_1,\chi_2$ Wu's two determinant splitting characters, Theorem 5.4(1) with additive character inverted gives $\theta^Y_{X,\psi^{-1}}(\chi_1^{-1}\pi)=\chi_2\mathbf1$. Apply Theorem 5.1(2) to the irreducible cuspidal $\chi_1^{-1}\pi$ and this nonzero cuspidal return lift. It gives orthogonality of $\theta^{X\oplus\mathbb H^r}_{Y,\psi}(\chi_2^{-1}\chi_2\mathbf1)$ to **every** cusp form for **every** $r>0$. This is precisely the fixed definite-source lift to $W$, including all its test vectors. Wu’s theorem applies directly from the definite bottom, regardless of cuspidality at intermediate stages. [Wu, §5.2, Theorems 5.1, 5.4 and 5.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1409.0770#page=15)

Both terms in $\langle J_b(\Phi)-J_s(\Phi'),F\rangle$ therefore vanish. Cuspidality with uniform moderate growth gives rapid decay and justifies this pairing, and the kernel estimates justify source integration. Hence $\|F\|_2^2=0$, and continuity gives $F=0$ for every target argument. This completes the pure-function result on the actual axes, including $(0,2)$. For an abstract triple not already identified with the arithmetic tower, the line-component proof still applies on its stated theta and convergence inputs. The full-function argument additionally needs the actual opposite split realization, the lower definite nonvanishing in that same tower, and the published cuspidal and doubling inputs. Local Howe duality by itself does not supply that global seed. No boundary proportionality or common upper test on the two sides was used.
