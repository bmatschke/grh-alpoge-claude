# Geometric Hecke operators and the moduli points of the third intermediate level

Fix a second intermediate level of the first pass. Its rational forms, their realization as cuspidal sections, its series and its character list have already been chosen. After imposing the two eventual host-frame and standard-size conditions, the following construction works for every such level on the common range, every twist index $i$, both coefficient embeddings $b$, and every row $1\le j\le m$. Fix a real depth $c_{\rm dep}$. From the state $x$ it produces a state $x'$ on the same underlying parameter $P$ with third intermediate level, and carries any given list at depth $c_{\rm dep}$ on $x$ to a list at that depth on $x'$. [Quantifiers][statement] · [Geometric inputs on the common range][actual] · [Application to the state and list][apply]

## Which points act on the forms?

Let $F$ be the coefficient field of the geometric core, identified with $K_s$, and $K_i=\operatorname{Frac}(O_i)$. Write $\phi_b:F\hookrightarrow K_i\hookrightarrow\mathbf C_{\ell_i}$ for the sided reading. A test point is a framed point of the row's actual PEL moduli scheme over $\mathbf C_{\ell_i}$ obtained from an algebraic integral framed object over a complete discrete valuation ring. The reading of its fraction field extends $\phi_b$ and pulls the unit ball back to its valuation ring. The frame can then be changed by an upper triangular matrix whose diagonal entries have norm one. Weight covariance makes this change multiply the evaluation by a scalar of norm one. On a simple tensor $a\otimes f$ the evaluation is $\iota_i(a)$ times the value of the cuspidal section corresponding to $f$ on that frame. This defines evaluation on the entire ambient form space. [Framed points and readings][pts] · [Section, field and evaluation][eval] · [Change of frame][weight]

Write $U_b$ for the inverse image in $K_i$ of the closure in $\mathbf C_{\ell_i}$ of the compositum of $\phi_b(F)$ and the indicated maximal unramified field. A section is rational over $U_b$ if it is a finite sum of tensors $a\otimes f$ with $a\in U_b$ and $f$ in the rational geometric form. The seven geometric assertions are separation of forms by the test points, generation of the rational full lattice by bounded $U_b$-rational forms, identification of that lattice with the given full lattice, the geometric Hecke formula, stability of the test points under Hecke quotients, constancy of the number of kernels, and the zero ambient space off the row range. [Completed unramified field][unr] · [Exact statements][laws] · [Their seven suppliers][seven]

For separation, take independent rational sections in the finite expansion of a putative kernel vector. Evaluations of these sections at geometric framed points over finite extensions of $F$, allowing every coefficient-field embedding, span the dual space after extension to an algebraic closure. Indeed a relation over the algebraic closure descends to a finite extension. Applying all embeddings to the evaluations and separating the coordinates of a basis of that extension gives relations over $F$. A rational section vanishing at all finite-extension framed points is zero, by detection on the reduced moduli scheme. A finite subfamily of evaluation rows already has full rank, so the same is true over $\mathbf C_{\ell_i}$. Every such evaluation is realized by an integral test point up to a common nonzero multiplier for the fixed weight. A relation on all integral test points therefore has zero coefficients. [Detection on finite extensions and scalar descent][detect] · [Geometric production of detecting points](../geometry/PEL_SECTION_LAWS.md)

For the lattice assertion, the given integral rational sections have norm at most one at all test points. Conversely the rational forms bounded at the selected place form the localization of the rational integral lattice there: clear the finitely many other denominators by a multiplier which is a unit at the selected place. Choose a basis of this local lattice. Its elements have evaluation norm at most one. The finite unramified descent used here says that a bounded form over a finite extension unramified at the chosen place is a sum of bounded rational forms with locally integral coefficients. One proves this with lifts of a residue-field basis. Integrality of all conjugate evaluations separates its coefficients, after base change of the framed tests. [Rational local lattice][localball] · [Finite unramified descent][findes]

For the closure, write a bounded $U_b$-rational form in that basis. If some coefficient has maximum absolute value $M>1$, divide all coefficients by an element of the base field of that same absolute value. The completed unramified extension has the same value group. Now there is a unit coefficient and all evaluations have norm at most $|\varpi|$, where $\varpi$ is a base uniformizer. Approximate all the coefficients simultaneously, with error strictly less than $|\varpi|$, inside a finite unramified extension at the chosen place. The basis vectors are bounded, so the new evaluations still have norm at most $|\varpi|$. Division by $\varpi$ and finite unramified descent put all the approximating coefficients in $\varpi$ times the unit ball, contradicting the unit coefficient. Thus the original coefficients are integral in $O_i$ by the valuation compatibility of $\iota_i$. This proves that the full geometric lattice equals the $O_i$-span of the bounded $U_b$-rational forms. The second-level series identifies this full lattice under the existing isomorphism of form spaces; the same series makes the off-range spaces zero. [Value group and normalization][max] · [Approximation, contradiction and lattice equality][descent] · [Use of the second-level series][seven]

## The scalar in the Hecke formula

Take a split place $P\notin S_{0,i}$ and $1\le k\le r$. Put $q=N P$, $p=\operatorname{char}k(P)$, $n=n_V$. Here $r$ is the upper bound on the Hecke index from the level data. Those data imply that $p$ is prime to the marked level and $p\ne\ell_i$. The auxiliary half-sets and the old exceptional set are avoided: the old set is exactly the marked places, and the new frame includes all primes above the marked rational primes. Let $d_P\in F$ have designated complex value $\delta_{\ell_i,P}(\varpi_P)$, for the prescribed finite-order **automorphic** frame character at $P$. The character is unramified at $P$ and the state contains its value in the designated field. For every form $f$, every admissible test point $z$ and **any** representatives $z_C$ of its framed quotient isogenies one has
$$
   \operatorname{ev}_z(H_{k,P} f)=
   \phi_b\!\left(d_P^{-k}q^{-kn}\right)
     \sum_{C\in\mathcal K_{k,P}(z)} \operatorname{ev}_{z_C}(f).
$$
Here the quotient representatives satisfy the specified transport of frames. The ordinary character value $d_P$ is read on the indicated side, so the displayed inverse does not require a further sign change for $b=-$. The multiplier belongs to the geometric coefficient field and has $\ell_i$-adic norm one on both sides. [Split, level and prime checks][domain] · [Coefficient-field witness and side reading][scalar] · [Scalar convention for the geometric operator][scaldef]

To check the normalization, start with a rational cuspidal section $f_0$. The geometric transform is another cuspidal section $g$ and its value is the sum over the framed quotients multiplied by the geometric normalization. Its input scalar is $q^{-kn/2}$. The definition of the geometric Hecke transform multiplies this once more by $(N P)^{-kn/2}$. The integer $n$ is even in the prescribed stage, so the result is $q^{-kn}$. Evaluate $g$ in the pinned automorphic model. The reading of the transformed cuspidal section is the corresponding double-coset sum. In the series' Hecke relation the base character contributes $\mu_{0,j}(\varpi_P)^k$ on every representative. The finite-order determinant factor also has a common value, namely $\delta_{\ell_i,P}(\varpi_P)^k$. Comparing the geometric coset reading with the series reading cancels the base character and leaves the inverse of the latter factor. Injectivity of the complex reading, obtained from uniformization, the fixed highest-weight vector, and the rational form, proves the equality of forms. Evaluating on each test point and extending from simple tensors by linearity gives the displayed formula. The same scalar works simultaneously for all $f,z,C$. [Construction of the transformed cusp and the common character values][transform] · [Comparison under the faithful reading][compare] · [Extension from simple tensors][extend]

This step uses three assertions from the standing series input. The cuspidal transform and its complex double-coset reading yield the identity just proved, using rapid decay and the already fitted real data for the latter. The integral Hecke-point assertion shows that the quotients are again test points. More precisely, take a representative over a complete discrete valuation ring for $z$, and carry its kernel through the frame change. The prime-to-$\ell_i$ integral Hecke assertion supplies an integral quotient frame after a finite extension. Extend the reading to that extension; its units and their inverses have absolute value at most one, so the composite frame change has diagonal of norm one. This proves point stability for every kernel. The three Hecke inputs have a joint producer from the pinned Hecke-point, theta-transport and split-model results. [Reading of cuspidal sections][cusp] · [Integral quotient and frame change][stable] · [Joint supplier][supplier]

## A count independent of the point

The count can be expressed as a Gaussian binomial. Let $\Lambda$ denote the row's defining $\mathcal O_E$-lattice, of rank $d$. The Tate-module identification at a geometric fiber sends a kernel to its $p$-multiple and identifies the resulting datum with a subgroup $N/p\Lambda$ of $\Lambda/p\Lambda$. The conditions on $N$ are stability under $\mathcal O_E$, annihilation of $N/p\Lambda$ by $\bar P$, and $|N/p\Lambda|=q^k$. Thus all kernels are counted by the $k$-planes of the residue-field vector space $(\Lambda/p\Lambda)[\bar P]$. At primes over $p$ other than $\bar P$ the ideal $\bar P$ is a unit, so its annihilator is zero. At $\bar P$, write $p\mathcal O_{E,\bar P}=(\varpi^e)$. The lattice is finite projective of rank $d$, hence free after localization. The annihilator is $(\varpi^{e-1}/\varpi^e)^d$, a $d$-dimensional space over the residue field of cardinality $q$. This argument also allows ramification over $\mathbf Q$. Counting ordered independent $k$-tuples and dividing by $|\mathrm{GL}_k(\mathbf F_q)|$ gives
$$
  |\mathcal K_{k,P}(z)|=
  \prod_{a=0}^{k-1}\frac{q^d-q^a}{q^k-q^a}
  \quad(0\le k\le d),
$$
and zero for $k>d$. The formal result uses the common cardinality, independently of $z$, the side, or its field of definition. [Projective lattice and its rank][datum] · [Subgroup condition][countdef] · [Identification in the fiber][fiber] · [Transfer from the universal fiber][count]

## The new state and its list

For each $i,j$, take as the common point set the disjoint union of the two sets of sided test points. A form on side $b$ is evaluated there by its geometric value on its side and by zero on the other. Each point on its own side is covered literally with the identity frame change. Label the kernels at each point by a set of the common cardinality just proved and choose an integral quotient for each label. These moves preserve the side. The Hecke identity and scalar of norm one now give the Hecke evaluation relation, also at points on the other side where both evaluations vanish. Separation gives injectivity. The two-sided unramified field in the new data is the intersection $U_+\cap U_-$; the sided spaces retain their own $U_b$-rational forms. The lattice result above is exactly the bounded-evaluation specification. [Joint point set and moves][moves] · [Full geometric evaluation data][new] · [Coverage and identity frame][cover]

Replace the previous abstract evaluation data by these geometric data. In the pin this operation retains the existing series, transports the level data and their normalized reading, and identifies the row, prime selector, rational form, Hecke maps, scalar eigenvalues, generic character, and row characters on the transported state. Consequently the new third intermediate level carries the prescribed moduli-point record together with the series for that same state. Finally transport the old grid along the induced equality of the level and character data. Its value-field condition, its roots-of-unity condition and its identification with the auxiliary character list all transport. This establishes the implication from any old list at the prescribed depth to a new list at the same depth. The conditional inputs throughout are those of the second intermediate level and the named geometric and automorphic suppliers.
