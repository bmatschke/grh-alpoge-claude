IP1. EXACT SIGNED PATH POLYNOMIALS

Let R be a commutative ring, n>=1, and let N letters x_i=(x_{i,1},...,x_{i,n}),
0<=i<N, be labelled either vector (+) or covector (-). Fix their nonnegative
degrees d_i. A path is a sequence of dominant integer tuples

  lambda^0=0, lambda^1,...,lambda^N in Z^n,

with a horizontal-strip increase of size d_i at a vector letter, and a
horizontal-strip decrease at a covector letter. "Horizontal strip increase"
from a to b means

  b_1>=a_1>=b_2>=a_2>=...>=b_n>=a_n,
  sum_j(b_j-a_j)=d_i.

All tuples may have negative entries. Put h_i(c)=#{a:c<=lambda^i_a}.
The strip inequalities give h_{i+1}(c)-h_i(c) in {0,1} at vector letters
and in {0,-1} at covector letters.

For an array A indexed by subsets J of {1,...,n}, use the source's
right-wedge sign convention:

  W_x(A)(J)=sum_{m in J}(-1)^{#{j in J:j>m}} x_m A(J\{m}),
  C_x(A)(J)=sum_{m notin J}(-1)^{#{j in J:j>m}} x_m A(J union {m}).

Start every column c at the array 1_empty. For c>0, a rise of h applies W,
a fall applies C, and no change does nothing. For c<=0 interchange W and C.
Call the resulting array after N letters K_c. This is exactly Kcol.
It has exterior degree h_N(c) for c>0 and n-h_N(c) for c<=0.

Choose subsets I_c of those respective cardinalities, and put

  hat I_c=I_c (c>0),        hat I_c=I_c^complement (c<=0).

The source standard condition says that, for every t,

  #(hat I_{c+1} intersect {1,...,t})
       <= #(hat I_c intersect {1,...,t}).

Equivalently the increasing lists in consecutive columns are weakly
increasing along rows. Outside a finite interval all I_c are empty.
The path polynomial is

  Psi_{lambda,I}=product_c K_c(I_c).

Only finitely many factors differ from 1. The source uses the larger
finite interval colRange(lambda)=[-T,T], T=sum_{i,a}|lambda^i_a|; the
extra factors are exactly 1. Thus our finite product is byte-definition
equivalent to the source polynomial, not a new normalization.

IP2. THE UNIVERSAL BASIS: TWO INDEPENDENCE ARGUMENTS

We first work over an arbitrary field k; no characteristic assumption
enters.

(a) Canonical path products.
For an endpoint lambda, define I_c^can={1,...,h_N(c)} for c>0 and its
complement for c<=0. Denote Psi_{lambda,Ican} by H_lambda, retaining the
whole path in the subscript.

Order exponents of the last letter lexicographically, giving coordinate n
first priority at a vector letter and coordinate 1 first priority at a
covector letter. The highest component of H_lambda for this order is

  epsilon * H_{truncated lambda}
       * product_a x_{N-1,a}^{|lambda^N_a-lambda^{N-1}_a|},
       epsilon in {1,-1}.                                      (IP2.1)

Here "highest component" retains the entire polynomial in the earlier
letters. To verify (IP2.1), examine one changing column. For c>0, a rise
h->h+1 has output {1,...,h+1}, and the highest term of W uses m=h+1.
A fall h->h-1 has output {1,...,h-1}, and the highest term of C uses m=h.
For c<=0, the output is complementary: a rise uses C with m=h+1,
and a fall uses W with m=h. In every case the remaining subset is the
previous canonical subset and the coefficient is a sign. Other summands
use a strictly less preferred coordinate of the last letter. Products
preserve this lexicographic leading component. The number of changing
columns assigned to a is exactly |lambda^N_a-lambda^{N-1}_a|, proving
the displayed exponent.

For a fixed final endpoint, that exponent vector determines the preceding
endpoint, since the sign of the step is prescribed. In a relation among
canonical products, take the largest last-letter exponent appearing,
and use induction on N for the preceding paths. This proves independence
of the canonical products with fixed endpoint over every field, and
equally over any field extension. Their leading coefficients are units
even in characteristic 2.

(b) The final-index functions.
Let g be a generic invertible n by n matrix. For a fixed dominant lambda,
define the rational function

  b_I(g)=product_c det(g_{I_c^can,I_c})                  (c>0)
                  det((g^{-1})^t_{I_c^can,I_c})        (c<=0).

Choose integers lo<=0<=hi with lo<=lambda_a<=hi. Jacobi's complementary
minor identity gives

  b_I(g)=epsilon_I det(g)^lo
           product_{c=lo+1}^hi det(g_{[1,h(c)],hat I_c}).       (IP2.2)

The signs epsilon_I are units. Jacobi's identity follows by applying the
perfect exterior pairing between complementary subsets to g and g^{-1};
equivalently it follows by expanding the adjugate identity. It holds
over Z[entries(g),det(g)^{-1}], so it remains valid in every field.

Give generic matrix variables lexicographic row-major order, with a
smaller column index preferred within a row. Each flag minor has leading
monomial its diagonal product, with coefficient 1. The leading monomial
of the product in (IP2.2) records, for each tableau row, the multiset of
entries in that row. Standardness orders that multiset weakly increasingly,
so it reconstructs the row and hence all hat I_c. These leading monomials
are distinct. Therefore the b_I(g), for standard I, are linearly
independent over k. The common nonzero det(g)^lo and signs do not affect
this conclusion.

(c) All path polynomials.
Apply the generic GL_n change of variables, vectors by g and covectors
by inverse transpose. Exterior covariance and Cauchy–Binet expand the
transform of Psi_{lambda,I} as a sum over all terminal subset choices J.
Its torus weights are idxWeight(J), where each positive column contributes
1_J and each negative column contributes -1_J. For fixed subset sizes,

  idxWeight(J) is dominated by lambda^N,

and equality occurs only for J=Ican. Indeed, for each initial coordinate
set {1,...,t}, a positive subset contributes at most min(t,h), and a
negative subset at least max(0,t-h); equality for all t forces the
canonical choices. Summing the column inequalities proves dominance.
Consequently the lambda^N-weight component of the generic transform is

  b_I(g) H_lambda.                                      (IP2.3)

In a nonzero relation, choose a maximal endpoint for dominance, using the
sum of initial partial sums as a strictly increasing height function.
Projecting the generic transform to that endpoint kills every term with
a different surviving endpoint. The independence in (a), now over k(g),
then gives a relation among the b_I for each fixed path; (b) makes all
its coefficients zero. This contradiction proves independence of all
standard path polynomials in every field.

This is a triangular argument with generic weights. It does not identify
the individual path polynomials with rational irreducible summands.

IP3. COUNTING AND THE INTEGRAL CONCLUSION

Let s_n(lambda) be the number of standard terminal choices for lambda.
After choosing lo<=min(0,lambda_n), the columns hat I_{lo+1},...,hat I_hi
are precisely semistandard tableaux of the partition lambda-lo*(1,...,1)
on the alphabet {1,...,n}. Adding a complete height-n column changes no
count: that column is forced to be 1,...,n and its row comparisons with
the next column are automatic.

The elementary row-insertion bijection gives

  sum_{mu/lambda horizontal strip of size b} s_n(mu)
       = s_n(lambda) * binom(n+b-1,b).                    (IP3.1)

For clarity, insert the weakly increasing word a_1<=...<=a_b into a
semistandard tableau by replacing the first entry strictly larger than
the inserted entry in a row and passing that entry to the next row.
If there is no such entry, append the inserted entry. Comparison of two
consecutive insertion routes shows that the later new box is strictly
to the right of the earlier one; the added boxes therefore form a
horizontal strip. Reverse insertion removes added boxes from right to
left, moving the displaced entry upward by replacing the rightmost
strictly smaller entry. These rules undo each other; reversing the order
of the recovered letters gives the original weakly increasing word.
They preserve the row and column inequalities. There are
binom(n+b-1,b) possible input words. A sufficiently large determinant
shift makes every shape in this finite identity a partition, so the
same identity holds for arbitrary dominant integer lambda.

For downward strips use lambda^vee=(-lambda_n,...,-lambda_1). The map

  I_c -> J_c=I_{1-c}

is a bijection of standard choices for lambda and lambda^vee: its hatted
columns are the complements of the old columns in reversed order.
Complementation reverses the prefix-count inequalities. Horizontal
increases correspond to decreases under lambda->lambda^vee.
Thus (IP3.1) also holds with downward strips.

Iterating (IP3.1) along the signed letters, from lambda^0=0, gives

  # {(path,standard I)}
       = product_i binom(n+d_i-1,d_i),                   (IP3.2)

the number of ordinary monomials of the prescribed letter degrees.
There are finitely many paths, since every coordinate remains between
-sum d_i and sum d_i. Every column operation is linear in the current
letter and a horizontal strip uses exactly d_i columns, so all Psi have
those prescribed degrees.

Form the square integer coefficient matrix of these Psi against the
ordinary monomials. IP2 shows that its reduction is nonsingular over
F_p for every prime p. Its integer determinant consequently has no prime
divisor and equals +1 or -1. Its inverse is an integer matrix. Therefore
the Psi are a basis over Z and, by scalar extension, over every
commutative ring R. This establishes the precise integral basis needed
here without assuming integral semisimplicity.

IP4. PREFIX MINIMUM AND MAXIMUM FILTRATIONS

For the first i letters, let v_i(F) and M_i(F) be the minimum and maximum
Laurent exponents from the source generic conjugated rank-one torus
substitution (last coordinate for v_i, first coordinate for M_i).
For a zero polynomial set v_i=+infinity and M_i=-infinity.
For arbitrary integer cutoffs theta_i and U_i, the integral basis in IP3
has the exact property

  v_i(F)>=theta_i and M_i(F)<=U_i for every i
    iff F is an R-linear combination of the Psi for which
         lambda^i_n>=theta_i and lambda^i_1<=U_i for every i,   (IP4.1)

when R is an integral domain. The implication to the right retains the
coefficients in R.

Here is a proof, including the point where cancellation must be excluded.

First work over a field. Conjugating a diagonal rank-one torus by a
generic matrix, then undoing the outer change of variables, shows that
its Laurent exponents are exactly the corresponding coordinate weights
of the generic GL transform of F. The outer substitution is invertible,
so a Laurent coefficient vanishes before that substitution exactly when
it vanishes afterward. Thus the definitions used here coincide with the
source valS and lam1S, rather than with diagonal weights in one fixed
basis.

For the full prefix i=N, all weights in the generic transform of
Psi_{lambda,I} have every coordinate between lambda_n and lambda_1:
each positive column of height n contributes 1 in every coordinate;
other positive columns contribute 0 or 1; the complementary assertion
holds for negative columns. This proves containment for good endpoints.

Conversely expand F in the basis. If a bad endpoint occurs, choose one
maximal in dominance among the bad endpoints. A good endpoint cannot
dominate it: the condition theta<=lambda_n and lambda_1<=U is downward
closed among dominant weights of fixed total sum. Another bad endpoint
can contribute to its torus-weight component only if it dominates it,
so maximality eliminates all different such endpoints. Formula (IP2.3)
and the two independence statements of IP2 show that this forbidden
weight component cannot vanish. This contradicts the Laurent bounds.
Thus the full-prefix filtration is exactly the good-endpoint span.

For i<N, expand F by its monomials in the tail letters. Those monomials
are fixed by the prefix substitution and are linearly independent.
Consequently the Laurent bounds hold separately for every coefficient
polynomial in the first i letters. The constrained space therefore has
dimension

  (# standard prefix path polynomials with good endpoint)
      * product_{j=i}^{N-1} binom(n+d_j-1,d_j).            (IP4.2)

The full path polynomials with good lambda^i lie in this space. Indeed,
cut each exterior column recurrence immediately after letter i. The
remaining operations are linear in that intermediate exterior array;
expanding its coordinates writes the full Psi as a sum of prefix path
products with arbitrary terminal subset choices, times tail polynomials.
Every such prefix product has its generic weights between lambda^i_n
and lambda^i_1, by the preceding column count.

These full path polynomials are independent. Their number equals
(IP4.2), because iterating the Pieri count starting from a fixed
lambda^i gives s_n(lambda^i) times the tail monomial count. They therefore
span the whole prefix-constrained space. This proof uses dimensions of
explicit polynomial spaces and a combinatorial count, not a decomposition
into irreducible representations.

There is one basis for all prefixes. Uniqueness of its expansion makes
intersection of the individual prefix spans the span of the intersection
of their index sets. This proves the simultaneous version over a field.
For an integral domain R, first expand F in the R-basis from IP3, and map
to Frac(R). The field result makes every forbidden coefficient zero in
Frac(R), hence zero in R. This proves (IP4.1) integrally.

For minimum cutoffs alone, use the same proof with U_i=sum_j d_j, which
is a universal upper bound; no extra restriction results.

A useful sanity check is rank 2 with two vector letters of degree 1.
The family consists of three ordered monomial products and their
2 by 2 determinant. Its coefficient matrix is unimodular, including
modulo 2. The three monomials are not all symmetric tensors. No false
integral symmetric/alternating decomposition has been used.

IP5. THE EXACT SHARED PlaneGram.SPAN COLUMN DATA

For each basis element, take the actual factors
C_c=Kcol(lambda,c,N,I_c), with c in the source colRange. Put

  occ_c={i:h_{i+1}(c)!=h_i(c)}.

The recurrence proves that C_c is homogeneous of degree 1 in every
letter in occ_c and degree 0 in every other letter. Each strip has
size d_i, so exactly d_i of the occ_c contain i. For c>0, h_0(c)=0,
and height rises only at a used vector letter; hence

  h_i(c)<=#{j<i:j in occ_c and j is a vector letter}.

Cutting the column recurrence at prefix i and applying exterior
covariance gives

  v_i(C_c) >= q(c,h_i(c)),
  q(c,h)=1_{c>0 and h=n}-1_{c<=0 and h<n}.                 (IP5.1)

For a positive column, the intermediate coordinates transform in
exterior degree h: every rank-one torus weight is 0 or 1, and is 1
when h=n. For a negative column the intermediate coordinates transform
in exterior degree n-h of the dual: weights are 0 or -1 and all are 0
when h=n. Tail coefficients are fixed by the prefix action, which proves
the stated lower bound even if terms cancel. Finally

  lambda^i_n=sum_c q(c,h_i(c)),                           (IP5.2)

by counting positive columns c<=lambda^i_n and negative columns
lambda^i_n<c<=0. Equations (IP4.1), (IP5.1), (IP5.2), the factorization,
and the occurrence counts are precisely the SPAN clauses in
MonomialOrderSegments/LiftBoundOfHypotheses.lean, with n there called r.
For a valuation ring, coefficients in R are exactly coefficients with
nonnegative valuation. The assertion therefore applies to that interface
without any change of the path basis.

IP6. THE ACTUAL MGen0-TO-PGen INCLUSION

Return to a source frame segment. Enumerate its lifted selected positions
s_0<...<s_{N-1}, and use vector letters at even s_i, covectors at odd s_i.
Write k=r+K. A literal MGen0 generator is A times G in disjoint coordinate
blocks of dimensions r and K, after renaming the lifted positions. Both
are integral and homogeneous separately at every letter. The tangential
degree of G is zero on the preceding-frame block W. That block is an
initial interval of letters consisting entirely of vector letters.

If A or G is zero the conclusion is immediate. Otherwise apply IP4 to
A and G with their actual finite prefix lower and upper weights.
They are integral combinations of path polynomials with paths nu and
kappa satisfying all the source weight bounds.

Outside W, write v_<i and M_<i for the weights of letters with index <i,
and v_<=i and M_<=i for those with index <=i. The zero-gap conditions
are exactly

  vector i:   M_<=i(G)<=m_i<=v_<i(A),
  covector i: M_<i(G)<=m_i<=v_<=i(A).

Therefore every resulting pair satisfies, in the path indexing of IP1,

  vector i:   kappa^{i+1}_1 <= nu^i_r,
  covector i: kappa^i_1 <= nu^{i+1}_r.                    (IP6.1)

On W the kappa path stays zero, since its letter degrees are zero.
The nu path there uses only vector increases, so its coordinates are
nonnegative and (IP6.1) again holds.

Append the tuples: lambda^i=(nu^i,kappa^i). The within-block horizontal
strip inequalities already hold. The only missing inequality across
the block boundary is exactly (IP6.1). Dominance of each appended tuple
follows from it and monotonicity of the current step (and lambda^0=0).
The sizes add. Thus lambda is a genuine k-dimensional source PPath.
Conversely restricting an appended path gives these conditions, although
only the forward construction is needed here.

Each product Psi_nu,I' Psi_kappa,I'' is therefore literally a PGen element.
Multiplying the two integral expansions proves GeneratorSpanInclusion.
No characteristic-zero spanning argument or enlargement of PGen occurs.

IP7. SPLIT COLUMNS, NORMAL DEGREE, AND EXACT PRODUCT IDENTITY

Take a literal PGen element with lambda=joinPT(nu,kappa). For each c put
J_c=I'_c union (r+I''_c), the source joinI. Its split column is

  S_c = Kcol(nu,c,N,I'_c) * Kcol(kappa,c,N,I''_c)

in disjoint coordinate blocks; this is exactly plTanCol(lambda,c,J_c).
Padding both finite column ranges with their identity factors gives

  PsiPl(nu,I') PsiTan(kappa,I'') = product_c S_c.          (IP7.1)

Only the empty-index convention outside each block's column range is
needed. This is the direct source prod_plTan identity.

Let z be the last row of the new frame, let omega_i=p^{z-s_i}, and put
Q=p^{G'}. All exponents are nonnegative, the s_i are strictly increasing,
and omega_0<=Q by the actual frame-gap bound. Normal coordinates are the
last K coordinates at vector letters and the first r coordinates at
covector letters.

A column step is costly when its full height rises from h>=r, or falls
from h<=r. Write

  a_c=sum_{i costly for c} omega_i.                       (IP7.2)

Dominance makes the block heights min(h,r) and max(h-r,0). At a changing
step exactly one of these heights changes. A costly step uses a normal
coordinate in that changing block; a noncostly step uses a tangential
coordinate. Thus every monomial in S_c has normal weighted degree a_c
for the weights omega_i. This elementary induction also covers zero
columns. After the actual phSeg substitution x_{i,b}->X_{s_i mod n',b}^omega_i,
the polynomial S_c has ordinary normal degree a_c. Even n' ensures
that reducing positions modulo n' preserves parity; injectivity of that
renaming is not needed for this homogeneity assertion.

For c<=0, the full height starts at k and falls by at most one at each
letter. The source inequality N+r<=K gives

  h_i(c)>=r+K-i>=r+K-N>=2r>r.                            (IP7.3)

This is the precise reason that negative columns never enter the first
block in the following support and sign argument. It is not legitimate
to omit this size condition from the key proof.

IP8. THE EXPLICIT FROBENIUS COLUMN POLYNOMIAL

Let O be the source DVR, finite and faithful over W(F_{p^{n'}}), with
uniformizer w satisfying w^2=p, p>=13, and even n'>0. (Finite and faithful
are part of OHyp; the calculations below only need the stated integral
algebra structure and w^2=p.) Fix the source chart g in
GL_k(W(F_{p^{n'}})).
Use its adapted row coordinates x_i sigma^{s_i}(g) at vector letters,
and sigma^{s_i}(g)^{-1} x_i at covector letters. These are integral,
because the determinant of g is a unit.

For a fixed column c start from 1_empty. Skip unused letters. At a used
letter of position s, first raise every component of the current exterior
array to p^{s-t}, where t is the preceding used position, and then apply
the usual wedge or contraction using this new adapted letter. At the
first used letter there is no initial powering. At the end raise every
component to p^{z-t}. Denote the J-component by F_{c,J}^g.
This is exactly KKstate followed by KKc, not an ordinary Frobenius
substitution of the finished characteristic-zero column polynomial.

All coefficients are integral, and a used letter has final degree
p^{z-s_i}=omega_i. An unused letter has degree zero. These statements
follow immediately by induction through the powering and linear
exterior operations. Only the positions in the source selected set TT
occur.

We next prove the two properties that turn F_{c,J}^g into a source key:

  Phi_{g'} F_{c,J}^g is in w^{a_c} O[X] for every chart g',
  Phi_g F_{c,J}^g = w^{a_c} phSeg(S_c,J) mod w^{a_c+1}.  (IP8.1)

Here S_c,J means plTanCol(lambda,c,J); no standardness of J is required
for this individual-column assertion.

IP9. ALL-CHART DIVISIBILITY: THE MOVING EXTERIOR-BASIS ARGUMENT

The following finite algebraic calculation is the entire Frobenius
ingredient. Let L_s,L_t be invertible integral matrices and P=p^{t-s},
t>=s, such that entrywise

  (L_s^{-1})^{[P]} = L_t^{-1} mod p.

If V L_s=w^a Z, then the binomial theorem in characteristic p gives

  V^{[P]} L_t = w^{aP}(Z^{[P]}+p R)                    (IP9.1)

for an integral vector R. Brackets mean componentwise powers, not a
matrix power. In particular every component has order at least aP.
If Z_J is divisible by w, the J-component has order at least
aP+min(P,2), since p=w^2. A strict positive position gap gives P>=p>=2,
so an extra w becomes TWO extra powers of w. A zero gap preserves one.

For clarity, (IP9.1) follows by writing V=w^a ZL_s^{-1}, expanding
each component's Pth power, and replacing (L_s^{-1})^{[P]} by L_t^{-1}
modulo p before multiplying by L_t. Both replacement errors and all
mixed binomial terms are multiples of p. No inverse of w is used in
this calculation.

In an arbitrary chart g', the letter coordinates in the raw recursion
are related to the standard scaled coordinates

  y_{i,b}=w X_{i,b} if normal, and X_{i,b} otherwise

by the Frobenius twists of g'^{-1}g and g^{-1}g'. Exterior covariance
therefore supplies the matrices L_s in (IP9.1). For positive columns
they are the direct-sum exterior-power matrices of sigma^s(g^{-1}g');
for negative columns use sigma^s(g'^{-1}g)^t. The wedge and contraction
identities give exact intertwining at each step. Their entries and
inverse entries are integral minors.

Witt Frobenius satisfies

  sigma^s(beta)^{p^{t-s}} = sigma^t(beta) mod p,

including negative s,t interpreted periodically. Reduction to the
constant Witt coefficient proves this identity, because that residue
lies in F_{p^{n'}}. Applied to the minors, it supplies exactly the
congruence required for (IP9.1).

Here are the support sets that carry the induction. At height h, call J
nominal if:

  c>0, h<=r: |J|=h and J is contained in the first r coordinates;
  c>0, h>=r: |J|=h and J contains the first r coordinates;
  c<=0:     J is contained in the last K coordinates.

The two descriptions agree at h=r. For negative columns the exterior
degree is still the fixed n-h, but it need not be repeated in this
support condition.

At every stage, after transfer to the current exterior basis:
  every component has the predicted order a;
  every nonnominal component has order at least a+1.     (IP9.2)

Initially the only nonzero component is the empty subset, so the
assertion holds. An advance of positions multiplies a by the appropriate
power of p and preserves the extra order by (IP9.1). Immediately before
a costly step, every earlier used position is strictly smaller.
Evaluate the preceding state at s-1, then advance once to s; (IP9.1)
upgrades each nonnominal component to a+2.

At a noncostly step, terms using a normal coordinate acquire w. A term
using a tangential coordinate can leave the nominal support only if its
predecessor was nonnominal. This preserves (IP9.2) with unchanged a.

At a costly step, normal-coordinate terms acquire w, giving the new
base order a+1. A tangential-coordinate term must have a nonnominal
predecessor: a positive wedge above the rank boundary cannot add a
first-block index to a subset already containing that whole block;
a positive contraction below the boundary cannot remove a last-block
index from a first-block subset; for a negative column the nominal
subset is in the last block and (IP7.3) rules out the other boundary
case. Thus such terms have order at least a+2. If the output subset is
nonnominal, the remaining normal-coordinate terms also have a
nonnominal predecessor, so again have order at least a+2.
This proves (IP9.2) with new base order a+1.

The final advance to z gives a=a_c. The exterior transfer is invertible
over O, so divisibility of all transferred components implies
divisibility of all original components. This proves the first
assertion of (IP8.1), for every chart.

IP10. THE OWN-CHART LEADING TERM AND UNIFORM ORDER BOUND

In the chart g itself, the adapted coordinate changes cancel exactly,
so the recursion uses the y_i above without a moving basis.
Divide out the predicted w-power and reduce modulo w.

At a noncostly step only tangential coordinates survive. At a costly
step only normal coordinates survive: an unwanted tangential term has
a predecessor whose normalized residue is zero; strict separation of
positions raises that predecessor at least to the pth power before
the step. Dividing by the one newly counted w still leaves a multiple
of w, since p>=2. Thus the reduced normalized recursion keeps exactly
the block required by the path at every step.

Its resulting exterior array is the product of the first-block and
last-block column arrays. For a first-block operation the last-block
array is supported on the empty subset, so the source right-wedge
sign receives no extra contribution from that block. A last-block
operation has no first-block indices to its right, so its sign also
agrees exactly with the source split-column convention. Negative
columns stay wholly in the last block by (IP7.3). This proves the
identity with plTanCol, including its sign.
