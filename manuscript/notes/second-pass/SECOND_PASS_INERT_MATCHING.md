# Inert vacuum generation on the retwisted grid

At every inert place of a realized second-pass square, the translates of the lattice vacuum under either member of the unitary pair span the invariants of the other member's integral group. This holds for both source ranks, including odd ranks. It supplies the two compact-averaging assertions used to identify the common local quotient in B8.11. The rank-free argument below expands the proof of Roadmap B8.14 and verifies its hypotheses for the actual inert oscillator. Liu's Proposition 3.4 states one-sided lattice-vacuum generation in his even-dimensional unramified setup, citing Howe's Theorem 10.2. Liu's Theorem 1.1 also calculates a spherical Hecke module in those conventions. The grid has an even common target and allows odd intermediate ranks and unramified nontrivial splitting characters. The argument here needs neither a Hecke-polynomial calculation nor a parity assumption on the intermediate rank. ## The integral oscillator at an inert place

Let $L/k$ be the unramified quadratic extension at the place, with integers $\mathcal O$ in $L$, common uniformizer $\varpi$, and involution $\sigma$. Write $U,V$ for free self-dual Hermitian lattices over $\mathcal O$, with matrices $J_U,J_V$, groups $G_U,G_V$ of rational isometries and lattice stabilizers $K_U,K_V$. Use $U_L,V_L$ for their ambient spaces. Choose an anti-invariant unit $\delta$. In an integral frame of the given Hom lattice the alternating pairing is
$$
 B(x,y)=\operatorname{Tr}_{L/k}\!\left(\delta\operatorname{tr}(J_U^{-1}y^*J_Vx)\right),\qquad x,y:U_L\longrightarrow V_L.
$$
Here $*$ is conjugate transpose. Changing the sign of the oscillator changes $\delta$ to $-\delta$. The local additive character $\psi$ of $k$ has conductor $\mathcal O_k$, the residue characteristic is odd, and $\Lambda=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal O}(U,V)$ is self-dual for this pairing and character. The source and target lattices and the local generator of the coefficient ideal give this normalization via trace duality. Both splitting characters are unramified here because the marked places of the square are all split. The local splitting fixes the vacuum under $K_U\times K_V$ for both models. Unramified determinant twists on these integral groups contribute one, even when their value on a uniformizer is $-1$. [Trace duality for the actual Hom lattice](../theta/THETA_LATTICE_VACUUM.md#a-characteristic-function-at-the-finite-places) · [Both inert models and their unramified characters][actual]

Let $s$ be the lattice vacuum, $\rho$ the Weyl translations, and $\omega$ the Weil action. The multiplication phase of $\rho(x)\rho(y)$ is $\psi(B(x,y)/2)$, and covariance changes $x$ to $hxg^{-1}$ for $(g,h)\in G_U\times G_V$. To check cyclicity, use a lattice-adapted symplectic basis. In the resulting Schrödinger space $s$ is the characteristic function of an integral position lattice. A smooth compactly supported function is supported on finitely many cosets of that lattice and constant on cosets of a smaller open lattice. Finite Fourier inversion on the quotient of the two lattices writes each restriction as a sum of additive characters multiplied by its coset indicator. These are Weyl translates of $s$ up to scalars. Thus the smooth oscillator $M$ is spanned by the $\rho(x)s$.

Integral Weyl translations act on $\rho(x)s$ by the character $u\mapsto\psi(B(u,x))$. The characters distinguish $x$ modulo $\Lambda$ by self-duality. Hence $M$ is the algebraic direct sum of the lines indexed by $x+\Lambda$, and its integral-translation-fixed line is $\mathbb C s$. The compact group $K_V$ permutes the lines in finite orbits. One such orbit contributes an invariant precisely when the stabilizer acts trivially on its line. In that case a single nonzero coefficient determines the invariant up to scalar. Set $S=\operatorname{span}\omega(G_U,1)s$, which is contained in $M^{K_V}$. We prove equality by producing this invariant for each contributing orbit. [Roadmap, invariant lines and phases][phases]

## From a contributing orbit to a self-dual lattice

Write $P=J_U^{-1}$. For a skew-Hermitian $C$ divisible by $\varpi$ with $Cx$ integral, let $D=J_V^{-1}C$. The Cayley isometry $h=(1+D)(1-D)^{-1}$ lies in $K_V$ and $hx-x$ is integral. On a contributing orbit the stabilizer phase $\psi(-B(x,hx-x)/2)$ equals one. In $hx-x=2Dx+2D(1-D)^{-1}Dx$, the second term pairs integrally with $x$ because one can move the skew-adjoint $D$ onto $x$, and $Dx$ is integral. The first term therefore tests the scalar $\delta\operatorname{tr}(CxPx^*)$. This scalar lies in $k$. A sign or factor of two from taking the trace preserves the family of tests, since $2$ is a unit and $C$ varies over an integral module.

Use Smith coordinates on $U,V$, carrying both Hermitian forms through the changes of frame. Subtract an integral map to leave a rectangular diagonal matrix with nonzero entries $\varpi^{-a_i}$, $a_i>0$. Replacing the representative this way preserves the line and its stabilizer condition. For active indices $i,j$ vary the $ij$ entry of $C$ through $\varpi^{\max(a_i,a_j)}\mathcal O$, together with its forced negative conjugate. On the diagonal vary through the anti-invariant elements of this ideal. The conductor and the perfect trace pairing give
$$
                    P_{ij}\in\varpi^{\min(a_i,a_j)}\mathcal O.
$$
Choose an integral rectangular matrix $T$ supported on the active block such that $P_{ij}=\varpi^{a_i}T_{ij}+\varpi^{a_j}\overline{T_{ji}}$. For an off-diagonal pair use the term with the smaller exponent. On the diagonal divide by two. With $l=TP^{-1}$ the two cross terms in $(x-l)P(x-l)^*$ cancel $xPx^*$, leaving the integral matrix $lPl^*$. Thus the given class has a representative, again called $x$, with $xx^\dagger$ integral, where $x^\dagger=J_U^{-1}x^*J_V$. [Smith-coordinate argument][phases] · [Native integral-Gram lemma][gram]

Put $N=U\cap x^{-1}V$ and $A=N+x^\dagger V$. Then $A\subseteq x^{-1}V$ and $A\cap U=N$. Indeed $xx^\dagger V\subseteq V$. By duality $N^\vee=U+x^\dagger V$ since the dual of the right hand side is $U\cap x^{-1}V$. Consequently
$$
      A^\vee=(U+x^\dagger V)\cap x^{-1}V=N+x^\dagger V=A.
$$
The middle equality follows by subtracting the $x^\dagger V$ component, which already lies in $x^{-1}V$. No full-rank condition on $x$ entered this construction.

For any such self-dual source lattice $A'$ put $\Lambda_{A'}=\operatorname{Hom}(A',V)$. It is self-dual for $B$, and its image modulo $\Lambda$ is finite. For representatives in $\Lambda_{A'}$ sum $\rho(y)s$ over this image. The sum is nonzero because its summands belong to distinct lines. Weyl translations in $\Lambda_{A'}$ fix it because the pairings within this lattice are integral and the representative ambiguity is in $\Lambda\cap\Lambda_{A'}$. Every unimodular Hermitian lattice here has an orthonormal basis. Over the residue field one finds a vector of nonzero norm, lifts it, scales by the surjective norm on units and splits its line. The norm is onto on residue units, and successive corrections on principal units use surjectivity of the residue trace. Induction on rank gives the basis. Thus $A'=gU$ for some $g\in G_U$, and its vacuum sum belongs to $S$ by covariance and uniqueness of the fixed line. [Construction of the source lattice and its vacuum sums][averages]

## Isolating one compact orbit

Choose $M_i\in U$ lifting cyclic generators of $U/N$ of orders $\varpi^{e_i}$, $e_i>0$, and put $M_0=(M_i)$. Let $Y\subseteq\Lambda_A$ represent its image modulo $\Lambda$, including the chosen $x$. For $y\in Y$ put $\Phi_y=(yM_0)^*J_V(yM_0)$. Use the finite additive group of skew-Hermitian matrices $G$ with entries in $\varpi^{\max(e_i,e_j)}\mathcal O$ modulo entries in $\varpi^{e_i+e_j}\mathcal O$. The scalars
$$
                    \chi_G(y)=\psi(-\delta\operatorname{tr}(G\Phi_y))
$$
are well-defined characters in $G$, since $\varpi^{e_i}yM_i\in V$.

For a skew lift of $G$, set $E=M_0GM_0^*J_U$ and $k_0=1+E$. Then $E$ is skew-adjoint and divisible by $\varpi$. Also $EU\subseteq A$ by $\varpi^{e_i}M_i\in A$, and $EA\subseteq U$ by $M_i^*J_UA\subseteq\varpi^{-e_i}\mathcal O$. It follows that $k_0^\dagger k_0=1-E^2$ maps $A$ into itself and has unit determinant, hence preserves $A$. Thus $k_0A$ is self-dual. The maps $yk_0^{-1}$ represent the image of $\Lambda_{k_0A}$ modulo $\Lambda$, in the same respective classes as $y$, since $yEU\subseteq V$ and $k_0$ preserves $U$. In the Weyl phase for this integral shift use $k_0^{-1}-1=-E+E^2k_0^{-1}$. The second term pairs integrally with $y$ by skew-adjointness and integrality of $yE$ and $k_0^{-1}$. The first term contributes $\chi_G(y)$ in the displayed convention. The vacuum sum for $k_0A$ is consequently
$$
                      w_G=\sum_{y\in Y}\chi_G(y)\rho(y)s\ \in S.
$$
Averaging $\chi_G(x)^{-1}w_G$ over the finite group retains $x$ with coefficient one. Testing individual diagonal and conjugate off-diagonal entries shows that each surviving $y$ satisfies $\varpi^{\max(e_i,e_j)}(\Phi_y-\Phi_x)_{ij}\in\mathcal O$. [Finite Gram average][averages] · [Native construction][family]

For $N_y=U\cap y^{-1}V$ one has $N\subseteq N_y$. If $[U:N_y]=[U:N]$, the kernels coincide. Then the vectors $X_i=\varpi^{e_i}xM_i$ in $V$ are independent modulo $\varpi$, and the same is true of $Y_i=\varpi^{e_i}yM_i$. Indeed the order-$\varpi$ generators $\varpi^{e_i-1}M_i$ of the cyclic summands are independent in $U/N$, which embeds via $x$ (or $y$) in $V_L/V$. Their Gram matrices agree entrywise modulo $\varpi^{\min(e_i,e_j)}$ by the average. Witt extension over the residue field matches the two residual frames, including when the restriction of the pairing to their span is degenerate. The residual isometry lifts to an integral isometry by successively correcting its Gram matrix with $Z+Z^\dagger=H$ for a Hermitian error $H$, using $Z=H/2$.

Suppose an integral $h$ matches the columns modulo $\varpi^{\min(t,e_i)}$, $t\ge1$. On columns with $e_i>t$, the prescribed first-order changes are the reductions of $(Y_i-hX_i)/\varpi^t$. The Gram congruence modulo $\varpi^{t+1}$ says that these changes satisfy skew-adjointness on all pairs of active columns. Extend their pairing with the ambient space to a skew-Hermitian form by completing the independent columns to a basis, filling the transposed entries by skew symmetry. Lift its skew-adjoint endomorphism to a skew-adjoint integral $Z$ by antisymmetrization. Left multiplying $h$ by $(1+\varpi^t Z/2)(1-\varpi^t Z/2)^{-1}$ improves every active column by one power and preserves all earlier congruences. This gives $hX_i\equiv Y_i\pmod{\varpi^{e_i}V}$ for all $i$. Hence $y-hx\in\Lambda$ because the $M_i$ and $N$ generate $U$. [Frame extension and lifting][lift] · [Native integral isometry construction][unitary]

Now induct on $[U:N]$. The averaged vector belongs to $S$ and is $K_V$-invariant. Its component at smaller index is a finite sum of invariant vectors already in $S$ by induction. All remaining terms lie on the orbit of $x$, with a nonzero coefficient there. Subtraction gives a generator of this orbit's invariant line. At index one the line is $\mathbb C s$. This proves $M^{K_V}=S$. Exchanging source and target by the adjoint model gives $M^{K_U}=\operatorname{span}\omega(1,G_V)s$. A reversal of $B$ under this exchange just conjugates the finite characters. [Roadmap conclusion][lift]

## Passage to the actual common quotient

In the source coinvariants $M_{G_U}$, normalized averaging over $K_U$ preserves the class of every smooth vector and is a finite average. Its result belongs to $M^{K_U}$, so target translates of $[s]$ span $M_{G_U}$. The normalized $K_V$-average lies in $M^{K_V}$, so its class is a scalar multiple of $[s]$. The class is nonzero here: the source-invariant Siegel evaluation at zero takes the value one on $s$. The relative character is trivial since the two unramified characters have the same restriction to the common uniformizer by the square's rank parity. These arguments work for each tuple separately, without any bound on conductors at its split places. [B8.14a][coinv] · [Evaluation, splitting characters and the common spherical function](../construction/ROW_DEFINITE_LOCAL_QUOTIENTS.md#the-inert-spherical-functions)

In particular, sending a vector to its target matrix coefficient against the target-averaged Siegel evaluation gives a quotient generated by the normalized spherical function. The complementary ranks give reciprocal parabolic inductions after the modulus twist. The compact integration pairing and inversion of the Cartan double coset then identify the two normalized spherical functions. Their common span of translates is irreducible by compact averaging at a nonzero function value. This is the required common quotient and common nonzero vacuum for the B8.11 comparison. The assertion proved about the full source coinvariant is its generation and its compact-fixed line. Passage to the function quotient is the further operation used here.
