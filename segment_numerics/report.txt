SW12. Literal standard hypotheses imply the three numerical lift interfaces
==========================================================================
Source pin: 7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4

Status. This is a complete elementary proof of the following implications
from the literal Std(C0,Cde,CE1,CE2,1,X) of BinomialRatio/Transported.lean:

 (i) Every actual kept segment block satisfies BLk(C0,Cde,CE1,CE2,X,0,j,b).
 (ii) Every such block has oPlus <= localOrderCap <= (147/250)a <= aPlus.
 (iii) If all blocks of a family are kept and Ct>=30, then
       sum_j blockOrd(p,S_j,b_j) <= X.t0(C0,Ct).

The third assertion in fact needs only Std with any real H in place of 1,
because none of its steps uses the Hb or Hc lower bounds. Assertion (ii)
proves the numerical conclusion in OPlusAt_of once its explicit hypothesis
supplying Std is given. This report does not prove universal HIext => Std,
the quantified threshold supply called thr_std, or t0 <= the expanded tI.
The scalar, parity and finite-count arguments below are proved here; SW10
is a compatible earlier result, not a dependency of this claim.

1. Exact notation, kept blocks and the consumed Std clauses
-----------------------------------------------------------
Put p=X.p, k=X.m+1, N=|TT|, N1=X.N1, a=X.a, b'=X.b',
lambda=X.La(C0), delta=X.de(C0,Cde), beta=be(X,C0), Q=p^(X.G'),
E0=X.E0(C0,CE1,CE2), epsilon=N1*lambda/k, and c=X.c.
Here a,c,N1 are natural numbers, while lambda,delta,beta,b',Q are real.
Std in particular supplies

 p>=5, n'>=2 even, Y>=1, k^2 Y^2<=p,
 N1>=1, 2N1+3<=k, N>=2, lambda>=1, 2N*lambda<=k,
 40epsilon<=delta<1/6, 0<=beta<=delta/2-18epsilon,
 b'>0, p<a/b', E0>=0,
 k^3 N Q^4 Y<=b',
 a<=aT(x) on TT, and b'(1-beta)<=bT(x)<=b'(1+beta) on TT.

The omitted Std clauses remain hypotheses; they are not silently replaced
by weaker assumed numerical conditions. The displayed subset suffices.
There are at least two segments, their lengths G_j satisfy G_j<=X.G',
and their interior counts s_j satisfy s_j<=N1. The excluded-band clause is

 p^d not in [(a/b')(1-2delta),(a/b')(1+2delta)] for every odd d.       (1)

Segments are the forward cyclic intervals between successive selected
even points of TT. Interior selected odd points have distinct increasing
distances 0<d_0<...<d_(s-1)<G. As n' is even, G is positive even and each
d_i is odd. Thus G>=2 and successive d_i differ by at least two. This is
immediate by subtracting endpoint indices, adding n' only for a wrapping
interval. Sorting the interior points gives strict monotonicity. Put

 P_i=p^(G-d_i),  g_i=bT(segPos(j,i)),  bPlus=bT(segNext(j)),
 aStart=aT(segStart(j)),  aPlus=aT(segNext(j)),
 A0=floor((a-E0)/p^G),
 A_i=(g_i-sum_c rz_(i,c))/P_i,
 C_i=(aTop_i-sum_c ry_(i,c))/P_i.

The last two are the source natural quotients. DigitsOK says that every
digit rz_(i,c),ry_(i,c) is <P_i, their sums do not exceed the corresponding
degree, and the indicated residual is divisible by P_i. Consequently

             g_i-kP_i < A_i P_i <= g_i.                            (2)

The split sigma counts the distances with p^d_i<a/b'. Since those distances
are strictly increasing, i<sigma is equivalent to this inequality.
Define the effective order and signed integer cap by

 e_i = w_i+sg_i if i<sigma, and e_i=o_i otherwise;
 cap_l=A0+sum_(i<l)(A_i-e_i).                                      (3)

The cap and A0 are integers, not necessarily nonnegative. Every estimate
below uses an upper bound for them, so no positivity assumption is hidden.

The literal Kept inequalities, in addition to DigitsOK, are:

 late i>=sigma:
   o_i<=A_i, o_i<=cap_i+(lambda/k)A_i, w_i=sg_i=0;
 early i<sigma:
   o_i=0, w_i<=A_i, sg_i<=C_i,
   w_i+sg_i<=A_i(1+4/p^2)+(lambda/k)cap_i,
   sg_i<=2lambda*A0/k;
 terminal:
   oPlus<=Oplus,
   Oplus=cap_s+(lambda/k+4/p^2)sum_i(2a/P_i)+lambda*bPlus/k.          (4)

BLk(0) keeps DigitsOK, the displayed late o_i<=A_i and zero conditions,
and the early o_i,w_i,sg_i bounds except for the bound on w_i+sg_i. Its
additional requirements are o_i<=A_i(1-delta/4) in the late case and
oPlus<=aPlus, oPlus<=localOrderCap. We prove exactly these missing clauses.

2. The geometric cap estimate and the late upper cutoff
-------------------------------------------------------
Since A0<=a/p^G and A_h P_h<=g_h<=2b', (3) gives

 P_i cap_i <= a/p^d_i+2b' sum_(h<i) p^(d_h-d_i)
              <= a/p^d_i+2b'/p.                                  (5)

For the last inequality, the odd distance gaps give
sum_(h<i) p^(d_h-d_i)<=sum_(n>=1)p^(-2n)=1/(p^2-1)<=1/p.
In particular this is a bound independent of the number of frames.
The inequality g_h<=2b' follows from beta<=delta/2<1/12.

We need three other immediate Std consequences:

 lambda/k<=epsilon,  2/p<=epsilon,  kP_i<=epsilon*b'.               (6)

Indeed epsilon>=1/k and k>=5. Since p>=k^2, 2/p<=1/k. Also Q>=1,
N,Y>=1 and k^3 N Q^4 Y<=b' give k^3 Q<=b'; hence
kP_i<=kQ<=b'/k<=epsilon*b'. No claim that Q is an integer is needed.

Suppose i>=sigma. By (1), the failed strict test p^d_i<a/b' improves to
       p^d_i>(a/b')(1+2delta).
Writing t=a/p^d_i, A=A_i, P=P_i, (2), (5), (6) therefore give

 t(1+2delta)<b',
 P cap_i<=t+epsilon*b',
 AP>g_i-kP>=b'(1-beta-epsilon)
                 >=b'(1-delta/2+17epsilon).                       (7)

Put u=1-delta/2+17epsilon and v=1-delta/4-epsilon. Both are positive.
The two required polynomial inequalities have explicit nonnegative
remainders:

 uv-epsilon-(1-delta/2)(1-delta/4)
     =epsilon(15-15delta/4-17epsilon)>=0,
 (1+2delta)(1-delta/2)(1-delta/4)-1
     =delta(5/4-11delta/8+delta^2/4)>=0.                            (8)

Here 0<=epsilon<=delta/40 and 0<=delta<1/6. Thus
1/(1+2delta)+epsilon<=uv. From (7),

 P cap_i < b'uv < APv.

Adding P(lambda/k)A<=epsilon*AP and dividing by P>0 proves

       cap_i+(lambda/k)A_i <= A_i(1-delta/4).                      (9)

The literal kept late bound now gives the late BLk(0) clause. The early
BLk clauses are already present in Kept. These are exactly the source
PolyUpperBoundDelta.kept_upper_level_bound and the nonterminal part of
keptBL_of_std, with the scalar expansion displayed rather than deferred.

3. Extra order, the 147/250 bound and OPlusAt
--------------------------------------------
The positive integers G-d_i are distinct. Therefore

          sum_i P_i^(-1)<=sum_(n>=1)p^(-n)
             =1/(p-1)<=5/(4p).                                   (10)

Since A0<=a/p^G<=a/p^2, A_i<=2b'/P_i, and e_i>=0, (3) and (10) imply

 cap_s<=a/p^2+3b'/p,       sum_i 2a/P_i<=4a/p.

Also bPlus<=2b'. Substituting these bounds in (4) proves

 Oplus<=Lcap:=a/p^2+3b'/p
              +(lambda/k+4/p^2)(4a/p)+2lambda*b'/k.               (11)

Lcap is precisely the source localOrderCap(C0,X), with no rescaling.
The bounds p>=5, p<a/b', and 2Nlambda<=k with N>=2 give
b'<=a/p<=a/5 and lambda/k<=1/4. Each summand in (11) is thus bounded by

 a/25, 3a/25, (1/4+4/25)(4a/5), a/10,

respectively. Their sum is exactly (147/250)a=0.588a. Since aPlus>=a,

              oPlus<=Oplus<=Lcap<=147a/250<=aPlus.                 (12)

This supplies both terminal BLk(0) conditions and the OPlusAt conclusion.
The published OPlusAt_of uses a longer pointwise bound with s; (10)--(12)
are a proved alternative using the same literal quantities. The bound
147/250 strengthens the source maximal_order_le_a, which states only <=a.
The source OPlusAt universally quantifies X under D1num,HIext,CBound.
Here its conditional proof is: supply Std for that X, then apply (12).
Supplying Std uniformly is not part of SW12.

4. A kept segment's order: the minimum term and all error coefficients
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Define five nonnegative real quantities, common to all actual segments:

 Aerr=a/p^2, Berr=b'/p, Cerr=lambda*a/(pk),
 Derr=lambda*b'/k, Eerr=kQ, R=Aerr+Berr+Cerr+Derr+Eerr.                (13)

The source errTop is

 T=2b'/p+2lambda*b'/k+8b'/p^2
                  +lambda*a/(pk)+2lambda*b'/(pk).

We first prove the exact pointwise source bound

    P_i e_i <= min(g_i,aStart/p^d_i)+T.                            (14)

Early case. The excluded band improves p^d_i<a/b' to
p^d_i<(a/b')(1-2delta). Because
(1+beta)(1-2delta)<=1 and aStart>=a, it follows that
g_i<=aStart/p^d_i, so this is the minimum in (14). Multiply the early
kept bound in (4) by P_i, use A_iP_i<=g_i, g_i<=2b', (5), and d_i>=1:

 P_i e_i<=g_i+8b'/p^2+lambda*a/(pk)+2lambda*b'/(pk)<=g_i+T.

Late case. The excluded band gives a/p^d_i<b'/(1+2delta).
Since (1-beta)(1+2delta)>=1, this is <=b'(1-beta)<=g_i; it is also
<=aStart/p^d_i. Multiplying the late kept bound in (4) by P_i yields

 P_i e_i<=a/p^d_i+2b'/p+2lambda*b'/k
             <=min(g_i,aStart/p^d_i)+T.

The two scalar product inequalities just used follow from
0<=beta<=delta/2 and 0<=delta<1/6. For the second one,
(1-delta/2)(1+2delta)=1+(3/2)delta-delta^2>=1.

For each i, the digit contribution in blockOrd is
       q_i=sum_(c!=0)rz_(i,c)+ry_(i,0).
DigitsOK and P_i<=Q imply q_i<=2kQ=2Eerr. (Counting the k terms exactly
would improve this, but the literal source's weaker bound is retained.)

For this total-order estimate it is useful to use the coarser terminal
bound that retains s. From P_i>=p and (4),

 Oplus <= a/p^2+2s*b'/p
              +(lambda/k+4/p^2)(2s*a/p)+2lambda*b'/k
       <= (1+2s)Aerr+2s*Berr+2s*Cerr+2Derr,                        (15)

because 8/p<=8/5<=2. Also
       T<=4Berr+Cerr+4Derr,                                      (16)
since 1/p<=1/5. The literal blockOrd is
       sum_i(P_i e_i+q_i)+oPlus.
Adding (14)--(16) and the digit bound gives its exact error coefficients:

 blockOrd <= sum_i min(g_i,aStart/p^d_i)
      +(1+2s)Aerr+6s*Berr+3s*Cerr+(4s+2)Derr+2s*Eerr
   <= sum_i min(g_i,aStart/p^d_i)+30(s+1)R.                        (17)

Every coefficient is nonnegative and at most 30(s+1). In fact the
displayed calculation permits 6(s+1), but SW12 retains the archival 30
and does not propagate a changed source constant. This proves the actual
SegHyp.top_ord, dig, oplus and seg_ord estimates. It is valid even when
s=0: the pointwise sum is empty and the terminal bound still applies.

5. Sum the actual segments and identify t0 exactly
-------------------------------------------------
Each segment contributes one distinct selected even start and s_j odd
interior points. The open forward intervals between successive even
starts are disjoint on the cyclic orbit. Thus their odd selected points
are disjoint, and are disjoint from all even starts. The injection into TT
gives
                    sum_j(s_j+1)<=|TT|.                          (18)
This proves the exact counting fact sum_segS; it does not replace the
cyclic segments by a different cover or count repeated endpoints twice.

Summing (17) over the actual kept family and using (18), R>=0, c>=0 and
Ct>=30 gives

 sum_j blockOrd
  <= sum_(j,i) min(bT(segPos(j,i)),aT(segStart(j))/p^d_(j,i))
         +30|TT|R
  <= sum_(j,i) min(bT(segPos(j,i)),aT(segStart(j))/p^d_(j,i))
         +Ct|TT|(R+Qc)
   = X.t0(C0,Ct).                                                 (19)

The last equality is the literal definition t0G4 in ObjSeg and Loc.t0
in ColumnSystems. In particular R contains lambda*a/(pk), not the
lambda*N1*a/(pk) appearing in the distinct E0 definition. The extra Qc
term in t0 is merely nonnegative here; it is not an additional order loss
introduced by the proof.

Equation (19) is ord_all, with all floor operations, natural-number block
orders, integer caps, segment windows and the full minimum term retained.
It leaves the separate comparison t0<=tI explicitly unproved.
