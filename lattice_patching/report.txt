BACKGROUND
We use unique factorization of nonzero ideals in a Dedekind domain, the
fact that nonzero prime ideals of a number ring are maximal, and the
Noetherian property of a number ring. No class-number-one or principal
prime assumption occurs. The vector space V need not be finite dimensional
for LP1-LP3 existence, provided each vector has a nonzero integer multiple
in the reference module. Finite generation is used only for the additional
conclusion that the resulting module is a finite full lattice.

LP1: PRIME-WISE DENOMINATOR CLEARING
For a nonzero c in R and a designated prime P, write (c)=P^a Q with
Q+P=R. Pick t in Q but outside P. For another prime Q', P^a is not
contained in Q'. Pick s in P^a but outside Q'. Then st is in (c).
The same t works for all Q'; only s changes. This uniformity is what
allows the infinite intersection in LP2. If a=0, take any s outside Q'
from R; the same proof applies. This is a direct translation of
GlobalTheory.EX.Glue.exists_clear.

LP2: THE INFINITE INTERSECTION WITH A COMMON DENOMINATOR
Each B_Q is an R-submodule of V stable under division by every element
outside Q. Assume every x has one nonzero c with cx in every B_Q.
Then N=intersection_Q B_Q has localization N_P=B_P.
If sx belongs to N for s outside P, local saturation gives x in B_P.
Conversely choose x in B_P and a common denominator c. LP1 gives t
outside P. At P, tx belongs to B_P. At Q != P, choose s outside Q
with st=cd; then s(tx)=d(cx) belongs to B_Q, and local saturation gives
tx in B_Q. Thus tx lies in N and x lies in N_P.
Localizing arbitrary infinite intersections would be invalid in general;
the common-denominator argument is the complete justification here.

LP3: FINITE MODIFICATION AND GLOBAL BOUNDS
Given full M, a finite S, and local modules A_P satisfying n_P M <= A_P
and n_P A_P <= M_P for positive integers n_P, define B_P=A_P on S
and B_P=M_P off S. For x choose m>0 with mx in M. Then
c=m product_{P in S} n_P is a common denominator, including when S is
empty (empty product equals 1). Apply LP2.

The proof in MemLocSub.exists_glue needs the lower commensurability
condition for this construction; its upper condition is present in the
literal interface and is useful for the following strengthening.
Let d=product n_P. Then dM <= N <= d^{-1}M. To prove the second
inclusion, dx belongs to M_P for every P: outside S this is immediate,
and on S use n_P x in M_P. Membership at all localizations implies
membership in M. Indeed, if y is not in M, the ideal I={r:ry in M}
is proper and lies in a maximal ideal P; y in M_P would give an element
of I outside P, a contradiction. This also proves uniqueness of the
patch from all its localizations. If M is finitely generated, N is a
submodule of the finite module d^{-1}M, hence finite. It is full because
it contains dM. These bounds, uniqueness and finite-generation statements
are derived consequences, not claims that the Lean wrapper itself states
them.

LP4: ACTUAL INPUTS IN ExistsLedPart.exists_lat
Fix a word u. The reference is DV.MramL D u in (D.div.Nu u).V.
The coefficient number field is D.what_2.LL, with its ring of integers.
The place bijection is D.plEq. The global injection is D.nuIn u.

At a served admissible place w, A_w consists of x whose actual image
c.cmp u w hg (D.nuIn u x) is in G.NP X Y K u w hg.
This set is an additive R-module because the comparison is additive and
semilinear. For a scalar a integral at w, c.embW_val converts that
integrality into membership in the coefficient DVR; closure of NP under
such scalars proves that A_w is local at w. For t outside the prime,+a=t^{-1} is integral at w, so t x in A_w implies x in A_w.

At the own type prime T_u, replace NP by LT.Nlam D u w. This is already
a module over the local integral subring of the coefficient number field,
so the same inverse-scalar argument applies. Its definition is retained
unchanged; no simplification of its Hecke operator or exponent is used.

The set S consists of (GIdx and nonempty VV) or p_w=T_u. Nonempty VV
implies the candidate condition and hence p_w<X'. Each rational prime
has finitely many primes above it, so S is finite. GIdx excludes the
own type prime, which makes the two prescriptions disjoint even without
using a numerical inequality between T_u and X'. The source carries
such numerical inequalities elsewhere, but they are not needed in this
patching argument.

The supplied hfull is exactly the integer-multiple fullness needed in
LP3. At a served place, hS gives A_w <= M_w and n M <= A_w. Since
M_w is an R-module, also n A_w <= M_w, giving both conditions of LP3.
At a type place hT gives both conditions directly. Apply LP3 after
transporting S through D.plEq.

Off the type prime, a served patch is contained in M_w. An unserved
patch is equal to M_w. At the type prime, retain the separate target
LT.Nlam. It can be larger than M_w. The argument does not assert
N <= M globally and does not assert that N is a saturated submodule.
For example, R=Z, M=Z, S={(2)}, A_(2)=2 Z_(2) gives N=2Z.

LP5: LATTICE INSTALLATION AND THE LENGTH CONVENTION
The source setLat replaces NuLat by N and defines
lenMN(u,w)=(lenLoc(w,N_u,Mram_u).toNat)/ramIdx(w).
This is a rational-valued normalized length. ENat.toNat is the source's
conversion function, not an assertion that every local length is finite.
If the local length equals a natural number l, it converts to l; since
ramIdx(w)>0, multiplying by ramIdx(w) gives l exactly. This is precisely
the conditional hlen field proved in ExistsLedPart.exists_ledPart.
At an unserved non-type place, local equality makes the length zero.
All other size fields are copied. The transported instance setInst has
identical canonical selector, offsets, epsilon-depth and deficit fields.
There is no change of the scale, selected degrees or valuation convention.
