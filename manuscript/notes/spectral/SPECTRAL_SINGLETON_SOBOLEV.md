# Derivative bounds and removal of the last Gram cutoff

In the empty differential-word singleton of [the terminal slice](SPECTRAL_TERMINAL_SINGLETON.md), the Fourier density is the pushforward of the product of two coefficient vectors on $U(k,1)$. The arithmetic estimate now uses derivatives of orders two and three. On the group these density bounds follow from ordinary $L^2$ derivatives of orders four and six, respectively. The argument includes the Gram wall and the cost of removing its cutoff. All constants below are independent of the prefix coefficient, its multiplicity space, the scale of Haar measure, and the final norms $T_p,T_q$. [Exact lattice and sampling criterion](SPECTRAL_CM_FREQUENCY_LATTICE.md)

## Differential operators for the physical coordinate

Put $A=\sqrt{T_pT_q}$. In fixed frame bases the final column of $g\in U(k,1)$ is $(x,w)$ with $t=\|x\|$, $|w|^2=1+t^2$, and the Fourier coordinate is $\zeta=Aw$ up to a fixed sign or conjugation. Let $X_u F(g)$ be differentiation along $g\mapsto\exp(h B_u)g$ at $h=0$, where
$$
 B_u=\begin{pmatrix}0&u\\u^*&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 u=e_1,ie_1,\ldots,e_k,ie_k .
$$
Write $X_i$ for this list of $2k$ operators. They are skew-adjoint on compactly supported smooth functions for the chosen Haar measure. Their actions on the coordinates are $X_u x=u w$ and $X_u w=u^*x$. For a real unit direction $e$ in the complex plane set $W=\Re(\bar e w)$ and
$$
 L_e=\sum_i h_i X_i,\qquad h_i=(X_i W)/t^2 \quad(t>0).
$$
Indeed $\sum_i(X_iW)X_i w=t^2 e$ by summing the real and imaginary basis vectors in pairs. Thus $L_e$ projects to the **constant** directional derivative along $e$ in the $w$-plane. In the $\zeta$ coordinate each such derivative costs the factor $A^{-1}$. For example its Haar divergence is $2(k-1)W/t^2$: use $\sum_iX_i^2 W=2kW$ and $\sum_i (X_iW)X_i(t^2)=2t^2W$.

For a Hilbert-valued smooth compactly supported coefficient $F$ write
$$
 S_a(F)^2=\sum_{i_1,\ldots,i_a}\|X_{i_1}\cdots X_{i_a}F\|_2^2,
 \qquad S_0(F)=\|F\|_2 .
$$
For every integer $0\le a<k$,
$$
 \|t^{-a} F\|_2\le \frac{S_a(F)}{(k-1)(k-2)\cdots(k-a)} .            \tag{1}
$$
For the proof write $x=tn$, $w=e^{i\theta}\sqrt{1+t^2}$. Haar disintegrates as a fixed multiple of $t^{2k-1}dt\,dn\,d\theta$ times Haar probability on the right stabilizer $U(k)$, by integrating the defining quadratic delta measure in $w$. Along a radial ray use the lift generated by $B_{e^{-i\theta}n}$ from the base column $(0,e^{i\theta})$, with group parameter $\operatorname{arsinh}(t)$. Hence $\partial_t$ on that lift equals $(1+t^2)^{-1/2}X_{e^{-i\theta}n}$. The right stabilizer variable is fixed. Integrate $\partial_t(t^{2k-2a})\|F\|^2$ along the ray, with zero boundary term at $0$ since $k>a$. Cauchy–Schwarz gives
$$(k-a)\|t^{-a}F\|_2\le
 \Big(\sum_i\|t^{1-a}X_iF\|_2^2\Big)^{1/2}.$$
Iteration gives (1). This is the radial weighted Hardy argument in real dimension $2k$, in the physical Haar normalization of the singleton. [Radial Hardy and Rellich inequalities, Ruzhansky–Suragan](https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.06239)

## Bounds on the pushed-forward density

Let $D_{F,H}$ be the $\zeta$-density of the pushforward of $\langle F,H\rangle\,d\mu$. For the averaged tests in the reconstruction this is exactly the density with factor $C_H(|\zeta|^2-A^2)_+^{k-1}$. Suppose $k>2j$ and both vectors satisfy $S_a(F)\le M^a\|F\|_2$, $S_a(H)\le M^a\|H\|_2$ for $0\le a\le2j$ with $M\ge1$. Then uniformly in the unit direction $e$,
$$
 \|\partial_e^j D_{F,H}\|_1
 \le C_j(2k)^j A^{-j}M^{2j}\|F\|_2\|H\|_2.                         \tag{2}
$$
In particular a bound $M_v$ on the actual residual coefficient span gives $z_{v,j}\le C'_j\, g k M_v^2/A_v$ in the two sampling budgets. Only $j=2,3$ are needed.

To prove (2), integrate against a smooth bounded test of $\zeta$ and move $L_e$ across Haar measure $j$ times. One adjoint step is $-\sum_iX_i(h_i\,\cdot\,)$. A term with a total of $l$ derivatives on $F,H$ has $j$ coefficient factors $h_i$ with $j-l$ derivatives distributed among them. If $t\le1$, $|X_{i_1}\cdots X_{i_d}h_i|\le C_d t^{-1-d}$. In fact the numerator is $O(t)$ and each higher derivative is bounded. For $t^2=|w|^2-1$ the first derivatives are $O(t)$ and higher derivatives are bounded. Differentiating the quotient proves the claim. If $t\ge1$, derivatives of $x,w$ are $O_d(t)$ and derivatives of $t^2$ are $O_d(t^2)$, so the coefficient derivatives are bounded. These constants are independent of $k$ for any fixed string of basis directions by their coordinate action above.

There are at most $C_j(2k)^j$ terms. On the small-$t$ part bound each by the integral of $C_j t^{-(2j-l)}\|X_I F\|\|X_J H\|$, with $|I|+|J|=l$. Apply (1) to $X_I F$ with $a=2j-l$, and Cauchy–Schwarz. The derivative orders on the two factors sum to $2j$, and yield the right side of (2). The large-$t$ terms use ordinary Cauchy–Schwarz. For justification at the wall one can insert a smooth cutoff at $t\asymp\delta$ and let $\delta\downarrow0$. A cutoff derivative of order $d$ costs at most $C_d\delta^{-d}$ there. All the discarded terms for smooth compactly supported inputs have integrals $O_{F,H,k,j}(\delta^{2k-2j})$ on that layer. The adjoint expressions are integrable and have absolutely continuous pushforwards, since the projection is a submersion off the measure-zero wall. This proves the stated $L^1$ bound for the weak derivative.

The same proof works without a common number $M$, with $M^{2j}\|F\|\|H\|$ replaced by a constant depending only on $j$ times the sum of $S_a(F)S_b(H)$ over $a+b\le2j$. In particular it extends to the completion of the compactly supported smooth inputs in these norms. On a finite coefficient span it applies to every linear combination if the assumed derivative bound holds in its $L^2$ norm.

## A sufficient bound using the residual Casimir operator

There is a way to estimate the required derivatives without computing individual boost derivatives. It applies when the *full* left compact types, including the phase character, and finitely many residual Casimir moments can be bounded. Let $K'=U(k)\times U(1)$, and normalize the invariant Lie algebra form by $\frac12\Re\operatorname{tr}(XY)$. The boosts $B_i$ above are orthonormal in its positive part. Take an orthonormal compact basis for its negative part with generators $Y_\beta$. The invariant quadratic operator and compact positive operator are
$$
 \Omega=\sum_i X_i^2-\sum_\beta Y_\beta^2,\qquad
 C_{K'}=-\sum_\beta Y_\beta^2 .
$$
Invariance of the form implies that $\Omega$ commutes with every generator. For any smooth compactly supported $G$, skew-adjointness gives $S_1(G)^2=\langle(C_{K'}-\Omega)G,G\rangle$. Suppose the left translates of $F$ by $K'$ have types in a finite set $\mathcal T$. For $0\le l<a$, the types of the derivatives $X_I F$ of order $l$ occur in the tensor products of $\mathcal T$ with $l$ copies of the complexified boost space. Indeed conjugation by $K'$ acts on boosts by its standard matrix action. Let $c_a$ be the largest compact Casimir eigenvalue among these finitely many types. Suppose $B\ge1$ and $\|\Omega^q F\|_2\le B^q\|F\|_2$ for $0\le q\le a$. Then
$$
                         S_a(F)\le(c_a+B)^{a/2}\|F\|_2 .
$$
To see this, sum the first-order energy identity on $X_I G$ and use Cauchy–Schwarz and commutation to get $S_{l+1}(G)^2\le c_a S_l(G)^2+S_l(G)S_l(\Omega G)$. Apply it to $G=\Omega^q F$ and induct on $l$. The resulting bound is $(c_a+B)^{l/2}B^q\|F\|_2$ when $q+l\le a$. The estimate also applies in the corresponding graph norm closure. For the last compact cutoff this gives the sufficient choice $M^2=\max(1,c_6+B)$ in (2) if the hypotheses hold uniformly on the residual test span. The pre-existing $U(k)$ averages alone impose no bound on the $U(1)$ phase frequencies or on the residual Casimir moments of an actual prefix.

## Quantitative removal of the last cutoff

Let $\chi_\delta$ equal zero for $t\le\delta$ and one for $t\ge2\delta$, using a fixed smooth function of $t/\delta$, $0<\delta\le1/2$. For integers $0\le a<p<k$ one has
$$
 S_a((1-\chi_\delta)F)\le C_{p,k}\delta^{p-a} S_p(F).                \tag{3}
$$
Expand a product of $a$ generators. When $d$ hit the cutoff their coefficient is $O_{a}(\delta^{-d})$ on $t\le2\delta$, also for $d=0$. The remaining $a-d$ hit $F$. Estimating the norm on that support by $(2\delta)^{p-a+d}$ times its norm weighted by $t^{-(p-a+d)}$, and applying (1), gives (3). For $d>0$ the derivatives of the cutoff are supported on the transition annulus. The bound on them follows by differentiating $t=(|w|^2-1)^{1/2}$ there. Summing over the basis strings contributes only a constant depending on $p,k$.

In particular $p=7$ controls removal in all the norms needed by (2) when $k>7$. For a fixed prefix the full Gram cutoff $0<G_{\rm loc}<\varepsilon$ corresponds to $t<\sqrt\varepsilon/(A|D_r|)$, where $D_r$ is the nonzero prefix minor. The estimate gives that exact prefix dependence. It permits passage through the last wall on inputs with the indicated Sobolev control. Bounding these residual generator norms across the actual prefixes and earlier cutoffs, and converting the tower energy to the ambient formal degree, remain analytic requirements for the full mixed-word estimate.
