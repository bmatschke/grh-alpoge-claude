2. HOW TO CHOOSE THE NEXT PROOF WITHOUT SHORTENING ITS EXPLANATION

The detailed mathematical exposition remains the deliverable. Efficiency should come from choosing the right theorem, tracing its actual call site, sharing already fixed objects and reusing completed arguments. It should not come from replacing the intermediate proof with a declaration, dependency diagram or reference to a verified Lean body.

For each assignment, first record the exact original conclusion, all hypotheses, the order of universal and existential choices, and the next consumer. Then inspect the substantial producer behind the wrapper. Identify which inputs already have human proofs and which still contain mathematical work. Expand only the latter. A useful simplification proves its implication to the actual consumer and preserves every needed map, normalization and common witness.

The recent chapters illustrate this method. APD uses compact recovery of coefficients only where the original finite-residue arithmetic scope permits it, and evaluates finite Laurent polynomials only after returning from the completion. RCV proves a common arithmetic subgroup and one explicit triangular domain before transferring to every supplied domain. PBC proves the arbitrary-base gluing construction once. WPR separates generic division and tensor descent from the actual prism fields. Each simplification retains a full, readable argument.

In the manuscript, introduce ordinary mathematical notation for each original object and explain the dictionary once. State the full theorem before its proof. When a proof passes through coordinates, first show that these coordinates describe the actual group, lattice, module or scheme in the target; then prove the calculation; finally translate the result back. Explain why a chosen auxiliary basis or section does not restrict the original quantifiers. Keep determinant signs, powers, units, finite-dimensionality and positivity arguments visible where later cancellation uses them.

For an analytic identity, establish measurability and the needed finite or nonnegative integration statement before changing order or taking real values. For an algebraic construction, show that the homomorphism belongs to the prescribed scalar tower and that inverse or uniqueness assertions hold at the stated coefficient-ring scope. A standard theorem may remain a named background input when its precise hypotheses and application are supplied. A central original producer cannot be treated as background merely because the formal source invokes it in one line. These distinctions protect readability and make independent review focus on the actual mathematical risks.

Give every common construction one owner. A worker may prove an independent property of that construction, but may not silently choose another coefficient field, local ring, polarization, character, measure, basis, section or threshold. If transport is needed, the transport theorem and its compatibility with the next consumer are part of the assignment. Existential data must be chosen before exactly the families quantified after them in the original statement.

A completed conditional theorem is genuine progress when its premises are printed accurately. It is different from supplying those premises. In particular, the completed BS-plus-LocTied Wach theorem, the SWa1-based volume theorem, arithmetic entry under P, and the supplied-Space deformation results keep their original boundaries. A source-level dependency inventory should distinguish mathematical constructors, transport maps, standard background and retained external inputs, rather than promoting every wrapper to an independent research target.
