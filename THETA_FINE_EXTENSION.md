# Extending a prescribed fine theta datum

**Theorem.** Work in the eventual range of the pinned-column construction, with the same seven analytic inputs. They concern the parameterized models covering the discrete series up to unitary equivalence, Harish–Chandra modules, rapid decay of cusp forms, Casselman–Wallach globalization, the unitary Weil representation, existence of the required discrete series, and Harish–Chandra finiteness. The [pinned analytic implication][finish] specifies their precise forms. Fix a pinned enlarged column. Take any old pinned column with the same underlying arithmetic host, character frame and full preliminary parameters (`PreX`), including the marked prime choices. Write $e:L\hookrightarrow\mathbb C$ and $e':L'\hookrightarrow\mathbb C$ for the large coefficient embeddings. Assume that the embedded small fields $K_s$ agree and that the embedded intermediate and large fields on the old side are contained in their enlarged counterparts. Let $z$ be **the given** fine datum on an old group-compatible stage, for any old test set, satisfying the local identities called `HoldsL`. On every given enlarged stage with its group and volume hypotheses there is a fine datum $z'$ for all places whose residue characteristics belong to the enlarged twist set. It satisfies the reference and geometric comparisons `ExtN` with $z$. Combined with the comparison of nonzero-index places for fine data satisfying `ExtN`, this gives the original extension assertion `DatExt0`. [Exact quantifiers][ext0] · [Fields of the comparison][extn] · [Final implication][finish]

On the prescribed retwist, the group and volume comparisons instantiate the enlarged side of this construction. The [integral evaluation argument](RETWISTED_INTEGRAL_EVALUATIONS.md) proves fullness at the required weights, identifies the small-field lattices, and gives the large-field target lattices for transported and newly defined types. It uses the marked integral model, its boundary comparison, the initial fine datum and the analytic extension hypotheses.

## Keeping the prescribed core and reference row

Its restriction identifies the two small fields and their rings of integers. If $j$ is the given map from the core coefficient field to $L$, use $j'=a j$. Its image lies in the enlarged intermediate field, and it covers the enlarged $K_s$ because the old map covers the old $K_s$. Keep the old PEL host, level, coefficient datum, signature rows, moduli spaces, uniformizations, Hermitian spaces, charts, multipliers, compact groups, component constants, characters, base frames and embeddings. Compose the identification of the small rings of integers with the old integral identification. These choices transport the old core laws, its canonical field and level pins, positive row dimensions and prescribed marked primes. The Petersson law compares the actual normalized measures: the compact-dual Haar systems on both stages agree by the uniqueness of the family with its compact probability and compact-dual comparison, so replacing the stage in the measure formula leaves the same measure. Their hypotheses on the new stage are verified by the [reference Weil calculation](THETA_REFERENCE_WEIL.md) and the [lift, measure and rationality calculation](THETA_REFERENCE_LIFT.md). Complete the remaining tuples with their characterized models, keeping the reference fields fixed on their distinguished branch. The prior comparison of row coordinates, lattice and model data is invariant under these completions and the subsequent scalar update. ## Cusp forms and their coordinates

Index the rows by the same integers on both sides. Write $C_i$ for the old small-field space of cusp sections. Its defining moduli space, coefficient field and weight agree on the new stage when we copy both highest-weight arrays and their even integer $n_i$. Write $b_i:C_i\to W_{K,i}$ for the old cusp-coordinate isomorphism. The distinguished small-field subspace of the enlarged row is exactly the coordinatewise image of $W_{K,i}$ under $a$. Thus there is an isomorphism on these subspaces, and the only possible transported coordinate map is
$$
   b'_i(f)_k=a(b_i(f)_k),\qquad e'(b'_i(f)_k)=e(b_i(f)_k).
$$
This assertion concerns the small-field vectors inside the respective large rows. Large-field linear combinations are allowed in the enlarged row. For each embedding $s':L'\hookrightarrow\mathbb C$, take the old smooth unit vector at $s' a$ in the identified archimedean model. Its norm, weight, and evaluation follow by evaluating the same model with the same frame. The marked reference characters agree on all active rows since the level reading and the character frame agree. Only those indices enter the geometric laws. The finite local points here lie above primes of the small coefficient ring. The identification of its rings of integers and of the framed moduli problem transports the old point equivalence. Under the displayed formula, the new coordinate norm at each such point is the old coordinate norm there by the local model comparison. It consequently equals the integral geometric norm by the old point law. The section reading on each archimedean frame is unchanged because $s'(a(x))=(s'a)(x)$ for every coefficient, and the model, extremal vector and frame are the corresponding old ones. In particular the *embedded Hilbert vector* of each geometric section, as well as every individual coefficient, agrees at the distinguished embeddings. ## The normalization of the polynomial coefficient map

The theta expansion uses an additional polynomial-to-vector map. A new admissible map $c''$ and the specified old map $c$ intertwine the same definite irreducible representation on old polynomial inputs. Comparing both maps on the polynomial basis after embedding into $\mathbb C$, Schur's lemma gives one scalar $d$ with $c''(a v)=d\,c(v)$. The definite reference inputs can all be zero on a row. In that case each required polynomial reading there is zero, since the old polynomial map is injective: the old coefficient field in this model is exactly $e(L)$, and the images of its polynomial basis are complex-linearly independent. The zero polynomial supplies the enlarged reading. [Schur comparison][schur] · [Injectivity and the zero row][inject]

If the old reference input space is nonzero, choose a nonzero input and a representative where its polynomial value is nonzero. Extend its decorated function coefficientwise. The two vector-valued readings agree there by the preliminary comparison. On each side the ratio between the reading and the candidate polynomial coefficient map is a root of unity in its own coefficient field. Indeed, the class presentation and its inverse identify the decorated integral coefficient lattices and their Hermitian pairings. Here is the descent of this scalar. A nonzero value fixed by the finite stabilizer gives a nonzero fixed vector $v$ with coordinates in the maximal CM subfield, by solving the stabilizer equations there. Use the equivariant function supported on this one class. Since its reading is nonzero, the corresponding point on the other side of the presentation belongs to that class. The ratio of any matching pair of coordinates with nonzero denominator then gives a scalar $u$ in the same subfield. Both presentations identify the integral lattice on the line through $v$, so $u$ and $u^{-1}$ preserve the fractional ideal of integral multiples on this line. Its multiplier ring is the ring of integers, so $u$ is an integral unit. Equality of the two self-pairings on $v$ gives $u\bar u=1$. Hence every archimedean conjugate has modulus one and Kronecker's theorem gives a root of unity. Applying this on the two sides at the corresponding values gives roots $u\in L$, $u'\in L'$ with $e'(u')d=e(u)$. Rescale the enlarged map on this row by $e'(u'/a(u))$. The ratio is again a root of unity, so it preserves the integral and pairing conditions of the coefficient-map family, and the rescaled map agrees on **every** old polynomial. A separate family may be used for each row's geometric datum. [Stabilizer, line and fractional ideal][line] · [Integral-unit argument and matching point][root] · [Rescaling the family][rescale]

Use $e'(L')$ as the coefficient field of the enlarged theta presentation. Embed each old polynomial and every coefficient of the finite theta data. Keep the old class representatives and their subgroup data. The reference has empty extra twist-prime set on both sides and the same marked lattices, so the representative conditions transport directly. The polynomial-valued test functions and their class sums commute with the coefficient inclusion, term by term. In the zero-input case the test reads zero by injectivity as above. In the nonzero case it reads the required vector by the rescaled coefficient identity. Thus the *same* representatives work before the input, the Fourier test and the section are specified. This also verifies the level and covariance pins of the new presentation. [Construction on the enlarged stage][om] · [Representative and polynomial comparison][inject]

## One central action for the whole row

Fix a row. The old uniform theta identity, supplied by `HoldsL`, and the old fine geometric phase give the one-map identity with phase parameter $1$ on that reference row. Unpack its witnesses: a central element $g$, a root of unity $\zeta$ in $e(L)$, and representatives with their subgroups, all chosen before the rational input. Send $g$ by the identified marked finite group and retain $\zeta\in e(L)\subseteq e'(L')$. The image of $g$ is central by surjectivity of the group isomorphism. Retain the representatives just compared. For every old rational input the old identity produces coefficients and a cusp section. Extend both along $e(L)\subseteq e'(L')$. Base change of the section commutes with the marked action and with the adelic evaluation on corresponding frames; the Lie and component factors agree. The analytic reference theta map has the same evaluation on the identified Hilbert vector. These identities give the enlarged equation with the chosen $g$ and $\zeta$ on each such input. [Transport of the section and its reading][sect] · [Common witnesses and base change][ties]

The old rational inputs span the old complex reference Hilbert space under its specified comparison map, and the reference Hilbert identification carries their images to a spanning set of the new complex model. To see exactly which identity extends by linearity, choose a level lift $k$ of $g$. The geometric action formula and uniqueness of the section with the prescribed class sum identify the transformed reading with a multiple of the theta value at $qk$. The multiple is $\zeta$ times the product of the marked determinant characters at $g$, times the ratio of the prescribed and the series-normalized component constants at $qk$. Both constants have modulus one. For each fixed $q$ the readings at $q$ and $qk$ are linear functionals of the Hilbert input and the multiplier is independent of that input. Their equality on the spanning family gives equality for every input. Existence and uniqueness of the class-sum cusp section then recover the full geometric identity. [Reduction to two linear functionals][span] It supplies the central comparison and the phase tie with phase parameter $1$. The scalar $\zeta$ in its witnessed identity need not equal $1$. Conjugation transports by restricting the complex embeddings, and the geometric finite group action transports by the section-coordinate identity. The equivariance over the identified levels follows from the same frames, group action, and component constants. Applying the pinned geometric input laws on this assembled datum gives the geometric part of fineness. [Spanning argument and other geometric ties][ties] · [Pinned geometric input laws][ing]

The tuple-independent identifications, embedded sections and coordinate identities above give the fields of `ExtN`. Its last definite-form clause applies to any pair of functions with the same complex polynomial readings at corresponding decorated points, by the original definite-form comparison. The transported core, new analytic existence, standard readings and geometric input laws give all four clauses of fineness. Intersecting the finitely many eventual conditions preserves the order of all stage choices. Finally the nonzero-index comparison applies to **this** $z'$ and every pair of corresponding places and contributes the sole extra clause of `Ext`. This proves `DatExt0` under the same seven analytic inputs as the original.
