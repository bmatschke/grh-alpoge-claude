CA3 — FINITE NORM GAUSS SUM
For k/k0 of size q^2/q, the cyclic multiplicative group gives q+1 points
in each nonzero norm fiber and one over zero. Additive-character
orthogonality therefore gives 1+(q+1)(-1)=-q. A sigma-invariant additive
character kills im(sigma-1)=ker(trace), so factors through trace. Trace
is onto also in characteristic two: X+X^q is nonzero of degree q<q^2.
The descended character remains nontrivial. Trace(w)=1 turns the precise
chi(w*t*sigma(t)) sum into the norm sum, with the negative square root.
No division by two in the residue field is used.

CA4 — SIGNED FINITE COMPOSITION
The source relative index is [B:A intersect B], and the operator sums
in the B direction. Self-duality gives perfect pairings of the finite
quotients, so reverse-forward composition is n(A,B) times the identity
on A-fixed vectors. The representative and fixed-vector statements use
isotropy of the R-module, including stability under the integral w.

For adjacent A,B, the two intermediate-lattice cases have the stated
indices and raw scalars 1 and q_P. Multiplicativity of lambda and
lambda(q_P)^2=q_P remove these factors. In the remaining case the
intersections with C coincide. The perfect pairing gives x=a+c_0;
chi(t)=gamma(a,t*c_0) factors through R/P and is nontrivial. Isotropy of
B gives chi(bar(t))=chi(t). If P were not stable under conjugation,
a unit modulo P arising from bar(P) would make chi trivial. Thus the
fixed-residue case of CA3 applies.

I checked the scalar orientation independently: the cocycle gives
W_(t*c_0) W_(t*a)=chi(w*t*bar(t))^(-1) W_(t*x).
Since W_(t*a) fixes v, W_(t*x)v equals chi(w*t*bar(t)) W_(t*c_0)v.
Averaging over C therefore produces exactly the positive-w Gauss
argument written in CA4, and hence lambda(q_P)=-sqrt(q_P). This is
not an arbitrary choice of a positive square root.

The adjacent replacement (A intersect (Rx)^perp)+Rx is self-dual:
annihilators in the finite perfect pairing reduce its orthogonal to
(A+Rx) intersect (Rx)^perp, which is the same module since Rx is
isotropic. Its index toward B decreases by q_P>1. The finite induction
therefore proves exact signed-J composition for the stated modules.

The actual GoodNorm choice is consistent across all residue fields.
Abelian Galois symmetry makes the residue degree f_p constant and
conjugation fix all or none of the primes over p. Taking
lambda(p)=sqrt(p)*exp(pi*i/f_p) in the fixed case yields
lambda(p^f_p)=-sqrt(p^f_p). The unramified CM hypothesis makes the fixed
residue involution nontrivial, including at dyadic primes.

CA5 — LOCAL LENGTH PARITY AND THE ACTUAL TWO-STEP FAMILY
The local-DVR argument is valid without a global O_E basis. For a source
lattice change, Hom is b copies of the source dual after choosing a
basis of the unchanged target; for a target change it is a copies.
Every local quotient length is correspondingly divisible by the
unchanged even rank. Tensoring by the same locally principal period
ideal preserves these lengths. In lambda(n), each fixed-prime sign
thus occurs an even number of times. At other primes the chosen value
is already positive. This proves J=Iop for precisely the two kinds of
one-factor moves used to define the actual thMoved.

The manuscript correctly avoids asserting arbitrary positive-Iop
composition. Signed composition identifies the literal two-step
thMoved with J(A0,A)theta0. The naturality P_T W_x=W_(Tx)P_T follows
by substituting T^(-1) into the actual Heisenberg formula and using
preservation of H and Q; representatives and index are preserved.

CA6 — GAUSSIAN TRACE AND ITS PHASE
Normal convergence follows from the displayed negative quadratic
bound in the lattice variable, together with polynomial lattice-point
count. Fixed derivatives introduce only polynomial factors. The
kernel is entire in its theta variable and satisfies the same theta
law by an index shift, so uniqueness applies without choosing a new
vacuum. Isometry supplies unit modulus but is not used as a substitute
for the phase computation.

With a=a0=u^(-1), A_m=(1-a)^(-1)(mA) is contained in A intersect uA.
The finite-Iop diagonal integrand has character
exp(2*pi*i*E_H(x+y,z)). Its integral over the A_m torus vanishes unless
x+y=0. The only surviving quotient class is y in A; the coefficient
is alpha(-y), and the outer alpha(y) gives one because Q(y) is integral.
The resulting factor is c_u*n(A,uA)^(-1/2)*vol(F), exactly as stated.

For the graph integral, writing x=s-(1-a)y with y in A_m gives the
literal shift z -> z+y. I checked its phase using the semicharacter
identity and integrality of E_H(a*y,s); the residual factor is
exp(2*pi*i*E_H(a*y,s))=1. Unfolding is therefore exact. The unitary
matrix has eigenvalues zeta!=1, with Re(1-zeta)>0. Ordinary convergent
complex Gaussian integrals yield det_C(1-a)^(-1), multiplied by a
strictly positive measure constant. No oscillatory-integral convention
or unspecified square-root branch is used.

The remaining exponent has the claimed sign. Set B=(1-a)^(-1).
Then aB=B-1 and B+B*=1. Consequently
  -H(s,s)/2-H(Bs,a^(-1)s)
    =H(s,s)/2-H(Bs,s)=-i*Im H(Bs,s).
Since C=B-1/2 is skew-adjoint, this is exactly -H(Cs,s). Thus CA.19
and CA.21 have the correct determinant reciprocal and Gauss phase.
Absolute Gaussian integrability also justifies the unfolding exchanges.

CA7 — DYADIC NORM LIFTING AND POSITIVITY
The inactive even matrix factor supplies the orthogonal half projection
P and half swap S on the actual standard period lattice. They commute
with the action and C; the finite Gauss function factors over the two
halves and the two sums agree. The modulus-periodicity follows from
mA contained in (1-a)(A intersect a^(-1)A), the semicharacter law and
integral alternating pairings. It is not presumed for a general m.

At each prime dividing 4m, the split norm or unramified quadratic norm
supplies a unit of norm -1 modulo 4m. DR1's residue-trace lifting covers
residue characteristic two. CRT gives an integral ell with
ell*bar(ell)=-1+4m*b0, where b0 is integral and conjugation-fixed.
Multiplication by ell preserves A and is injective, hence bijective,
on the finite quotient A/mA. Equivalently -bar(ell) is its inverse
modulo m; the manuscript's pairing/self-duality argument is also valid.

I checked the conjugation error explicitly. Its exponent divided by
pi*i is
  4m*(sqrt(d)/2*Re H(B0*x,x)-Im H(B0*C*x,x)).
The first term equals 2m times the integral polarization
Q(B0*x+x)-Q(B0*x)-Q(x). Writing m*x=y-a*y gives
2m*C*x=y+a*y in A, so the second term is twice an integral alternating
pairing. The entire error is in 2Z, proving phi(ell*x)=conjugate(phi(x)).
Because ell preserves each half, the half sum is real. The full Gauss
sum is its square, hence nonnegative; CA6 and |c_u|=1 make it nonzero.
The positive scalar can then be removed, giving precisely
|det_C(1-u^(-1))|/det_C(1-u^(-1)).

CA8 — DETERMINANT EXPONENTS AND THE EIGENVALUE-ONE CASE
The action T(z)=gamma'*z*gamma^(-1) and pull f(T^(-1)z) match the source
orientation. For the source action, det(1-T^(-1)) is the product of
tau(det(1-gamma))^b. Even a implies
  det(gamma)*conjugate(det(1-gamma))=det(1-gamma).
With b=2r this gives inverse determinant exponent -b/2. The target
action uses gamma'^(-1) and gives the opposite exponent +a/2. Both
are exactly the exponents in the literal rational-pull source theorems.

The Cayley norm-one scalars (1+t*sqrt(-d))/(1-t*sqrt(-d)), t rational,
form an infinite set. Excluding 1 and finitely many inverse eigenvalues
allows gamma=(z*gamma)*(z^(-1)I), with both factors satisfying the
nondegenerate Gaussian hypothesis. Signed-J naturality and composition
then extend the identity to all rational unitary matrices. The
characters multiply; no continuity or density assertion replaces this
exact factorization. Rank zero is handled directly by constants.
