1. Categories describe difficulty; completion is recorded separately

A means a direct application of an identified published theorem, with the hypotheses matched to the
original object. A theorem name or a related result does not establish an A certificate. B means an
elementary or moderately difficult argument that can be reconstructed independently. C means a substantial
mathematical producer whose target is understood but whose proof or compatible construction needs
significant reconstruction. D means an unresolved identification in our reading of the source: an actual
representation, instance or quantifier bridge still needs to be matched. D describes the human
reconstruction and makes no allegation about the checked formal proof.

In particular, a B classification does not mean that its proof is already complete. The register contains
explicit unfinished B tasks. Conversely, a complete B theorem can have a C antecedent: a finished proof of
“the original bundle has properties P and Q, therefore the required rank equality holds” still requires a
producer of that original bundle with both properties.

The selected dependency graph uses complete sufficient AND rules. Every listed premise must be supplied,
including an unfinished proof of the implication itself where appropriate. Shared work and optional
stronger routes are recorded separately. A list of promising common ingredients is not silently treated as
a sufficient theorem. The inherited 245 continuation records and 107 compound scope groups remain
available for navigation, with the new subclaim reductions attached. Their counts do not measure progress
toward completion.
