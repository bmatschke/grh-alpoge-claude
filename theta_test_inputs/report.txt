PURPOSE AND STATUS

This component follows the five common-test inputs isolated in TD15--TD16
back into their actual finite local, polynomial, period and coefficient-
field producers. Its strongest completed endpoint is the actual SumOKK,
followed by the literal SumRatK under FieldOK. It also constructs NFChars,
NFRoot and NFPhase from the actual Hida/level setting. These are not the
whole common-test theorem. The source's convergence, separation, matched
tower, critical L-ratio and positive scalar operator conclusions remain
separate. No RH/GRH conclusion, kernel check or replacement formalization
is claimed.

The prose does not infer existence of Choice.Is or FieldOK from the fact
that these are records. In particular Choice.Is has an additional RealOK
condition: vanishing for its chosen real match must imply vanishing for
all alternative real matches. That is a genuine separation assertion.
Likewise NumbersIn has eight fields, not just a field of Hecke character
values. Its bottom field is existential over actual bottom triangles;
its jet field quantifies over all expansions at a specified least depth.

All statements use one fixed actual Choice R, its grid, representations,
row measure, normal datum Z, reference twist, frames and representatives.
The HidaSetting assumptions are retained. They include the core level
laws, prime exclusions, the actual arithmetic host, full endpoint Weil
types, standard character, standard source lattice and betaX = 1. These
are not replaced by generic isomorphic objects.

CLAIM-BY-CLAIM EXPANSION

TT1. Locally constant compactly supported additive integrals.

A finite collection of nonzero fibers of a locally constant compactly
supported function is compact open. Common small translation subgroups
preserve them, and also preserve a fixed compact open normalization set
H containing zero. Their intersection U lies in H and is compact open.
Consequently H is a union of a positive finite number N of U cosets and
vol(U) = 1/N. The support is another finite union of U cosets. This gives
an actual finite sum for the integral and proves it is in any prescribed
subfield containing the function values. The normalization set need not
itself be a subgroup. This is important because the generic source
integral_mem_range has precisely that stronger set-valued hypothesis.
The argument also proves integrability. It never supposes the coefficient
field is closed in the complex topology.

TT2. Matrix balls for multiplicative Haar measure.

On a ball containing a singular matrix X, a primitive left-kernel row w
and a ramified unit u produce M = I + (u-1)e_i w. Its determinant is u,
its inverse is integral, and MX=X. The ball and Haar measure are preserved
by left multiplication while the integrand is multiplied by nu(u) != 1.
This proves zero. The source totalizes nonintegrable integrals by zero;
this arithmetic equality does not prove absolute convergence.

On an entirely invertible ball, a sufficiently deep right principal
subgroup lies in the fixed normalized group J and preserves the ball.
The ball is finitely many cosets gU. Their volume is 1/[J:U], because
vol(J)=1. The determinant character and norm factor are constant on each
coset. Do not replace this by a statement that GL_n(k) has finite measure.
The zero-rank case has no singular matrix and is handled by the ordinary
finite/group formulas.

TT3. Actual smallF values.

The full-rank matrix Y=Y0 h has a right inverse. This bounds g whenever
Psi(gY) is nonzero. The given depth of Psi supplies an additive matrix
period. Only finitely many matrix balls occur. TT2 treats balls containing
singular matrices and balls contained in GL separately. If the original
integrand is integrable, the finite decomposition computes the integral;
if it is not, its source value is zero. Thus field membership is proved
without pretending this lemma supplies the separate analytic convergence
theorem used by the global theta matching argument.

TT4. Finite Fourier exchange, primitive frames, actual flips.

The finite transform has exactly the source's q^(-e*r) normalization.
Character orthogonality proves both the inversion/support identities and
field preservation. The middle phiA operator uses smallF, determinant
characters and half-integral absolute values. Its depth is proved by
extending a primitive frame to an integral basis and realizing a small
translation by a principal congruence matrix. Inverse transpose acts on
the dual lattice, not on the original lattice without changing sides.
The determinant lies in the conductor kernel. Iteration follows the
literal flipOrder, updating the new vertex group to the integral group.
This proves the IsTensor support/period clauses as well as arithmetic.

The local character is mu0_j * xi_t / chi_(m-i). Half absolute values use
the fixed positive sqrt(q). The character root order q^e is justified by
q in the maximal ideal; no equality of q with a uniformizer or of their
valuations is asserted. The moved tensor includes the inverse character
factor from its literal definition and the actual frame substitution.

TT5. Flat integrals and phase bounds.

Every free variable is recovered from an actual stepPt/downMap matrix
entry, so the tensor's compact lattice support bounds all integration
variables. This makes the additive flat integrand compactly supported and
locally constant and genuinely integrable. The last-row bound for the
external variables uses translation invariance of a nonzero integral:
periodic translations force the phase character to vanish on a small
ball, and the annihilator of that ball bounds the remaining coordinate.
The phase itself is a sum of degree-one or degree-two expressions, hence
bounded on the required support. Both nonvanishing conditions in
PhasesIn are retained. If the local flat datum is zero, no phase bound
outside its support is claimed. TT1 then proves actual term.datum field
membership. The AA integrand has its own compact-support argument and its
scalar is the prescribed product of q^(n/2) at AA places.

TT6. Finite unfolding support.

This is the main finiteness input, not a consequence of local constancy.
At each vertex, lattice Gaussian elimination writes h=b*kappa with
kappa integral and integral inverse. The translation d=b*E*t is legal
for the exact Free pattern: no core-core entries, tail-core l+1<p,
core-tail p<j+1, and tail-tail l<j. The midLev levels are 2*rho+1 on the
core and 2*(l+1) on the tail, with rho=floor(c/2). In column i+1 the
triangularity conditions imply exactly those inequalities. d has zero
core columns and changes the phase by b_ii*t. Most importantly,
h^(-1)d=kappa^(-1)Et. Thus the ball on which this is a tensor period does
not depend on the possibly unbounded b. It depends only on the already
bounded previous vertex. Character annihilator bounds control the tail
diagonal entries. Fixed stepPt entries control inverse tail columns.

The displayed block factorization removes the arbitrary core GL block
and leaves both a matrix and its inverse uniformly bounded. The removed
factor is in the source's symmetric R_j group, not an enlarged arbitrary
unipotent group. The set with bounded matrix and inverse is compact by
using pairs (w,v) satisfying wv=vw=1 inside two compact boxes. Its image
modulo the open right group is finite. Induction on vertices then gives
finite local support; the active place set is finite. This closes the
literal finite_unfSupport input.

TT7. Pure-coset expansions and the universal depth issue.

Bounded support and a small period give a finite quotient of half-point
lattices. Common denominator clearing gives a positive integer k and
actual cosets x0+k*Lambda_half with k*x0 integral. Products of their
indicators form the expansion. Coefficients are actual local datum
values evaluated at centers. The prime set, idempotent and z=1 are fixed.
At a fixed k, distinct actual pure data have disjoint supports. Evaluating
at centers shows every nonzero datum in any expansion occurs in one
chosen expansion and with the same coefficient. Thus the union over
ALL expansions at expDepth is finite. This is the finite-generation step
needed later for JetsIn; pointwise finite support would not suffice.

TT8. Expansion-independent class pairing and SumOKK.

The normal datum vector is the actual finite theta-vector construction,
linear in its finite datum function, identified by its defining theta
condition. Kernel chains and the terminal pairing are multilinear in
the finitely many slots. A finite tensor relation of functions is detected
by finitely many point evaluations on each finite-dimensional span. This
proves independence of arbitrary expansions, not only the disjoint
indicator expansion of TT7.

Positive definite lattice class finiteness and compact levels give
finite class sets. Stabilizers have positive finite order, and invGam is
the exact product of their reciprocal integers; no neatness/trivial-
stabilizer assertion is required. Weight, interlacing and reading clauses
use the actual coefficient representations. The eleven SumOK fields are
flat, flatA, fin, ex, vec, indep, cls, gam, wts, br, read. They are all
supplied, giving SumOKK with additional thresholds zero. The corresponding
Lean rowSum_of_hypotheses takes but ignores a FunPr.Hypotheses2 argument;
the human proof does not add it as a primitive hypothesis.

TT9. Polynomial field descent and actual source values.

An integer-coefficient bideterminant basis has a nonzero rational minor
of its monomial coefficient matrix. Inverting that minor proves equality
between field coordinates and field polynomial coefficients. The minor
need not be unimodular. No statement equates integral Delta and Nabla.
Frame actions are rational substitutions and integer determinant powers;
normalized kernel matrices are supplied by JetsIn. The Fock Gram inverse,
branching projection, adjugate substitution and extremal normalization
are rational. The source's polynomial bilinear convention is retained;
one does not assume arbitrary coefficient fields closed under complex
conjugation. Actual formC(jX x) is treated through the rational value
model and its level character, not inferred from rank alone.

TT10. SumRatK.

The exact vK formula is a finite sum over unfolding labels, classes and

This proves literal SumRatK under literal FieldOK, using TT8's actual

TT11. Character/root/phase fields.

For an algebraic Hecke character, a finite ray-class system and the
algebraic infinity-type monomial put all finite idele/local values in one
finite extension. This is a common field argument for infinitely many
places, not a separate algebraicity claim at each place. Finite-order
characters use a single cyclotomic field. The half-normalized eta values
use the exact HFrame HF16/TL14 shift 1/2. Square roots are adjoined only
for the finite active/AA set. q^e roots, eighth roots and bounded phase
roots also form finite collections. The phase uniformity follows from
TT5 and the finite data/support sets of TT6. These give NFChars, NFRoot
and NFPhase without new asymptotic bounds.

TT12. Actual moved geometry.

The moved lattice in frame B is E^n intersect B^(-1)(x*u)B O_hat^n.
The order x*u is checked from the inverse defining globLat. The resulting

inverse. Heisenberg covariance transports the lattice and half-lattice

vacuum normalization but deliberately does NOT infer arithmetic of a
scalar relating two vacuum lines.

TT15. Compact averaging.

For a continuous finite-dimensional compact representation with algebraic
matrices on a dense set, the averaging projector is algebraic. The proof
uses the rationally defined fixed subspace and span of differences, and
the direct-sum splitting given by Haar averaging. It does not commute a
field embedding with an integral and does not integrate an arbitrary
algebraic-valued function and call the result algebraic.

The actual negative-source compact vacuum character is det^(N-k), where
b=k-N/2 is the splitting-character infinity exponent. The Gaussian half-
trace and the negative splitting exponent give N/2-b. Integer parity
removes branch choices. Finite-order twists are trivial on the connected
real group, so this character is common in t. Central scalars e^i then
force at most one surviving multidegree. For that finite monomial space,
Haar projection has algebraic entries. This supplies the substantive
fixed-degree compact averaging argument in avg_kernel_alg, using the
actual supplied real Weil normalization.
