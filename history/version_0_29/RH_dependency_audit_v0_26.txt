1. Outcome and exact scope
=========================

The main new milestone is a complete human chain from the four actual
count predicates to the original odd-plus theorem PointHV, and hence,
using the previously completed branches, to the cohomological windows.
The four predicates themselves remain substantial arithmetic inputs:
CountI, CountII, CountIII and CountV. Their uniformity and original-object
requirements have not been weakened. The new assembly proves the full
CountVs and CountIV suppliers, the character reduction at every odd
prime, both finite-prime sums, and the final reference-layer assembly.

Several independent suppliers of the paired CountII/CountIII branch
are now complete as well:

  RV3: literal SoFin for every original finite-order character.
  RV7: literal SoV and the exact coefficient-independence endpoint.
  BR3: literal LemmaX at the prescribed large power-of-two conductor.
  BR6: the original-residue-field Bernoulli-unit cocycle theorem.
  BR7: literal CorX, for both the character and its inverse.
  SP1: literal LemmaMprime, allowing the specified additive injection.
  SP6: literal LemmaM, including the exact factor two.
  SP7: shaCoc and mPrime on an actual Model with its source grading.
  FP8: literal LemmaP, with its full framed weighted presentation.
  HV5: literal CountVs, including its uniform choice of exponent.
  HV6: literal CountIV, on the actual strict-Selmer reference layers.
  HV10: CountI and CountII and CountIII and CountV imply PointHV.

The new SP9 trace-product theorem and SP10 paired-count join are
complete conditional results. SP9 retains a uniform finite catalogue
with explicit algebraicity, conjugation, size, purity and classification
properties. SP10 retains that catalogue and two actual-data assertions,
called G1 and G2 in its chapter. G1 constructs the specified Raw object,
local characters, level and admissible representations for each original
host case. G2 constructs its compatible Model, verifies the hypotheses
of LemmaM, and identifies the two-sided points, trace values and orbits.
Their construction is not supplied merely by naming the now-proved
general algebraic or rank-one theorems.

The result is therefore a sharper, explicit frontier in the human
reconstruction. Neither RH nor Dirichlet GRH is marked complete. The
actual common arithmetic family, its host, denominator tables, geometric
type/theta/rank identities, metric and spectral comparisons, and final
arithmetic gap remain independent global requirements.

2. What the new complete downstream chain proves
================================================

Relative to the matched classical inputs already recorded in the
component registries, the selected sufficient route now reads:

  CountI + CountII + CountIII + CountV
      -> PointHV                                      [HV10]
      -> ThmBab + PointHV + ThmBcFree                 [TW8, OB8]
      -> JointWin + ThmBc -> literal VWinAt           [VW3, VW4]
      -> one prime pair on the whole late window     [QP22].

Here ThmBab and ThmBcFree are already supplied, rather than additional
bespoke assumptions hidden in the second line. The odd-minus constant
remains 240. The two-primary theorem still chooses one complete BConst
before every Ct>=1. CountZ remains supplied with exponent zero by CZ6.
The four listed counts are sufficient for PointHV because HV supplies
CountVs and CountIV and writes all the remaining sums and field joins.

This route concerns the cohomological windows. It does not identify
VWinAt with the final positive character/scalar H-window. The latter
still needs the original arithmetic gap or the compatible family route.
WB's character balance, subgroup averaging, scalar estimates and error
normalizations are already written, but their actual arithmetic input
remains. AN/GH then supply the analytic zero-free conclusion and the
functional-equation extension. Ordinary RH is the conductor-one case;
that specialization does not construct the missing arithmetic supply.

3. Rank-one cohomology and coefficient return
============================================

RV starts with the actual module O tensor_(Z_ell)(Q_ell/Z_ell), its
finite-order character and negative cyclotomic twist, and the original
family of local restriction maps. Finite free scalar extension commutes
with these continuous cochain kernels. The length and the denominator
v_O(ell) both acquire exactly the relative ramification factor. Thus
normalized lengths and finiteness return to the original coefficients,
including when two allowed coefficient rings are initially nonnested.

RV3 proves SoFin by passing to an abelian extension in the same closure
that kills the finite character and contains the necessary roots of
unity. The finite restriction kernel is estimated directly, with the
original quotient action and its finite H0 module. Character order is
not assumed prime to ell. The root-containing strict-Selmer comparison,
finite duality and LemmaK31 then give actual finite length. This proves
the endpoint needed by the count branch without claiming the source's
sharper abelian generator-count inequality as a separate new theorem.

The vector-valued part addresses a genuine issue: an arbitrary compact
subset of the algebraic closure of Q_ell need not lie in one finite
extension. RV4 proves the required capture for a cocycle. A countable
cover by finite intermediate fields is obtained by rational polynomial
approximation and a proved Krasner argument. The cocycle equation makes
the relevant preimages closed subgroups. The compact Baire argument
gives an open subgroup, and finitely many coset representatives then
capture the whole cocycle in one finite extension. The argument concerns
the given algebraic closure, not its metric completion.

RV5--RV7 construct a compatible integral lattice, reduce scaled vector
cocycles to the finite-length quotient, and use one explicit coboundary
witness to return to the original vector coefficient field. They retain
every local witness and valuation condition. There is no unsupported
limit of independently chosen coboundary witnesses. SoV, SoFin and
coefIndep are now available as literal suppliers; actual automorphy,
rigidity and deformation-model construction are separate obligations.

The only new classical input is the usual uniqueness of the local
valuation and completeness of finite extensions, matched in RV-LF to
Milne's Algebraic Number Theory, Theorem 7.38 and Corollary 7.40. RV's
other inherited inputs are the explicit LD/LK/KB results and their
existing registries. The Baire capture and vector descent are proved
in the chapter, rather than retained as unnamed control theorems.

4. The complete finite Bernoulli/residual branch
===============================================

BR proves the generalized Bernoulli unit at the specified conductor,
then the residual cocycle theorem on the original residue field. It
does not substitute a nonvanishing theorem at some unspecified larger
level or replace the residue field with a convenient finite extension.

The finite character-sum argument first gives the precise order of
1+2^e a modulo powers of two. Averaging its Frobenius iterates projects
a zero character sum onto the roots killed by 2^e. The unit-group
description at the prescribed dyadic conductor forces the surviving
residue classes to be +1 and -1 modulo the required smaller power.
An exact partial Bernoulli identity, with no division by the possibly
ell-divisible conductor f, then gives a contradiction 0=-2. This proves
LemmaX with its actual cutoff f*4^e<=2^n and the stated unit norm.

For the residual theorem, BR4 uses precisely the index in UnrEv.
The restricted inertia image is a finite ell-group; its order divides
an index prime to ell and is therefore trivial. The restricted cocycle
over the actual cyclotomic field becomes a finite unramified abelian
character. Finite class-field theory identifies it with a map from that
field's ordinary ideal class group into the original field k.

The annihilator comes from the already proved OS1 principal-ideal
identity. BR extends that identity to every required positive odd
integer a, including a larger than the conductor, and proves the exact
group-ring reindexing. Division by two takes place only after mapping
to the original odd-characteristic k. The unrestricted integral
ClKill and HomKill predicates are not claimed. This is a sufficient
finite specialization of the actual source route.

BR5 identifies units in the original finite DVR and the kernel of an
arbitrary unital reduction map O->k. Surjectivity of that map, finiteness
of k and algebraic closedness of k are unnecessary. The noncyclotomic
condition chooses an annihilator factor whose reduction is nonzero.
The exact inverse-character Bernoulli scalar is a unit, so the residual
class-group character vanishes. An explicit cocycle calculation on the
remaining abelian quotient supplies a global witness in the same k.

BR7 verifies the product character and inverse-character hypotheses
simultaneously by one Chinese-remainder construction in the original
algebraic closure. It preserves conductor, parity, order, coefficient
inclusion and the tame-index condition. Thus CorX is supplied without
an Iwasawa main theorem, a Kato theorem, a generic Stickelberger theorem
or a prescribed Bernoulli-unit theorem as a new leaf. The direct
classical leaves are finite Hilbert class-field reciprocity and the
finite cyclotomic Galois identification; OS1 carries its previously
matched finite arithmetic leaves. The inherited OS9/OB2 nonvanishing
join remains acyclic, and BR introduces no dependency back into it.

5. General algebraic suppliers and the actual-model distinction
==============================================================

SP1 proves LemmaMprime for the exact saturated cotangent quotient.
Its map need only be additive and injective as in the source; the
finite-module cardinality argument converts that injection to the
required length bound. It does not silently strengthen the premise
to O-linearity.

SP2--SP6 prove the full LemmaM. The multigraded Koszul Euler argument
gives a lower bound from the elementary divisors of the cotangent
lattice. A Rees construction controls the relevant support; the
one-sided-point exclusions remove the unwanted horizontal primes.
Two-sided slices have zero tangent spaces and hence finite reduced
generic fibres. Replacing linear equations by squares contributes
exactly the tracked powers of two. The explicit Hilbert-series
coefficient and the determinant/valuation formula leave the final
factor two in

  length(cotangent quotient) <= 2 sum_T v(a(point)).

The finite cover T consists of two-sided points and covers every
two-sided orbit. Duplicate representatives do not invalidate the
inequality. The trace element is nonzero at each such point. Neither
an undefined valuation at zero nor a hidden reducedness or generic
multiplicity assumption is used. SP7 supplies the source's shaCoc and
mPrime consequences whenever the actual Model and the source
GradedAlgebra structure on its first-weight-zero algebra, with pieces
Model.gr, have been supplied.

FP1--FP8 supply the general framed weighted presentation LemmaP.
They construct the finite-level entry representation functor, the
formal torus grading, the local small-extension lifts, the framed
tangent comparison and the homogeneous relative presentation. The
obstruction and relation-detection argument fixes the number and
weights of the relations by the actual framed obstruction spaces.
FramedUniv and ObsClass are not left as additional bespoke suppliers.
The coefficient ring retains the source's full scope: complete
Noetherian local with finite residue of odd characteristic, without
an added domain or DVR hypothesis. The auxiliary graded local-lift
mechanisms are proved in the finite-residue scope needed for LemmaP;
their unrestricted infinite-residue formulations are not separately
promoted.

LemmaP has genuine hypotheses. They include PhiCond, residual and
integral normalization, residual triviality on the prescribed local
normal subgroups, each of the five local-condition axioms, the
required local base points, finite framed Ob_j and the specified
graded base algebra with its local framing data. Proving this universal
theorem does not verify those hypotheses for a particular Galois Raw
object. In particular the source's exModel construction and the
verification of its GradedAlgebra Model.gr, M1--M4 and the
trace-point-orbit comparison remain
part of the actual G2 obligation. The semantic map records these
edges explicitly and contains no automatic implication from a list
of named general theorems to the existence of that model.

The remaining general-algebra leaves are explicit: finite modules
over a DVR and Smith bases, Noetherian prime filtrations, Nakayama,
the Nullstellensatz and the description of finite etale field
algebras. FP uses Noetherian finite-variable power-series rings and
Nakayama. Their exact forms and primary Stacks/PID references are
recorded in the SP and FP classical registries. Neither a general
deformation-representability theorem nor an obstruction-dimension
bound is retained as a substitute for FP's construction.

=======================================================

  G1: actual host/character data, Raw/Good/OfCase construction,
      exact local characters and level, and admission of the
      actual irreducible points to the specified AdmRAlt class.

  G2: a Model for that same Raw object, its source GradedAlgebra
      structure and M1--M4 on the actual first-weight-zero algebra,
      and the trace-value, irreducibility
      and conjugacy-to-orbit comparisons for its two-sided points.

  Catalogue: one uniformly polynomial-sized set of integral
      complex trace data, closed under conjugation, with the
      specified purity bound and a classification of every actual
      AdmRAlt representation at the prescribed level and weight.

The catalogue is chosen before ell, the algebraic closure, the
embedding into C and the representation family. Equal labels imply
isomorphism. Its trace formula uses arithmetic Frobenius, with the
inverse of the source's geometric Frobenius convention explicitly
accounted for. The trace-product proof is now elementary once those
inputs are supplied: all conjugates of all prescribed root-of-unity
differences form one nonzero rational algebraic integer. The complex
bound controls its ordinary absolute value; omitted integral factors
have ell-adic norm at most one. The integer is fixed before the varying
prime and family, which is necessary for the uniform valuation bound.

The generic cotangent estimate, normalized Selmer comparison and
coefficient return are all written. A larger coefficient DVR may be
chosen for an individual character to contain the required roots;
both its Selmer length and v_O(ell) acquire the same ramification
factor. Character order, kernel, conductor, parity and case survive
the return to the original ring. The enlarged prime support gives
the needed Selmer inclusion because the added unramified local H1
vanishes for the nontrivial negative-twist Frobenius scalar. The
chapter proves that inclusion rather than an unjustified equality.

The source route to G1/G2 and the catalogue still contains actual
SymplecticFourImageDense, CohomologyHyp, RigiditySymplecticFour and
the specified local/automorphic classification and counting packages.
These are mathematical suppliers, not harmless theorem aliases.
CohomologyHyp includes differently packaged global finiteness,
Euler and orthogonality/Greenberg--Wiles statements and a local
period-ring clause. LK2--LK4 and LD3 alone do not match all those
fields. The automorphic input must concern the same Raw, local
conditions, characteristic-zero points, weights and safe admissible
representation class used in the model. A generic automorphy or
rigidity theorem without those identifications does not close the edge.

7. Original-field PointHV assembly, including the difficult primes
=================================================================

HV1 descends the original cyclotomic realization to the actual host
level N and retains E, H and every character component. Its host
hypotheses give N>=16. HV2 proves the restriction-kernel bounds on
the actual local fields and coefficient modules. Compactness upgrades
the finite-level cyclotomic image statement to the full cyclotomic
image used in the H0 estimate.

At primes dividing the field degree there is no integral character
averaging. HV3 instead filters by the factors of the cyclic character
polynomials and iterates a kernel-length inequality. The two parity
eigenspaces separate because ell is odd. HV4 then applies global and
local restriction-inflation with the psi-inverse twisted quotient
actions. The underlying invariant module and its quotient action are
distinguished. For e>=1 the unramified H1 away from ell vanishes by
divisibility, and pure-twist inertia vanishing descends through the
exact closed normal inertia subgroup defining the source GS quotient.

The resulting original-field error is at most 7*N^4*e<=(Ne)^6.
Outside the finite exceptional-prime set, ell does not divide the
field degree and averaging makes that error exactly zero. The latter
fact is essential when the source permits an arbitrary finite set of
nonexceptional primes. The proof supplies the combined estimate used
downstream; it does not claim that its polynomial is the exact source
lossAll formula. All lengths are shown finite before normalization
or the passage to real-valued sums.

HV5 supplies the small-conductor CountVs branch with its constant
fixed by the original primitive character before the host varies.
HV6 supplies CountIV on the separately prescribed reference layers.
LayerLe and VVat are unweighted strict-Selmer bounds, not H2 bounds.
The layer field, its degree, original closure and local witnesses
are retained in the comparison. No extra parity condition is imposed.

HV7--HV10 give an exhaustive case split and the two finite-prime sums.
After the primitive character and the entire layer-index function j
are fixed, one exponent and two auxiliary prime thresholds are chosen
before every host, field, local-data family and twist cutoff t.
Only finitely many exceptional primes are combined; each constituent
bound was already uniform in the subsequently varying model indices.
There is no maximum over an assumed finite family of field models.

The two-primary, odd-plus and odd-minus summands in VW all use the
same original E. The reference cyclotomic layers L0 and L1/L0 are
separately quantified models, not fields silently identified with E.
Their estimates are tied to the same fixed prime pair and the same
whole real window. This distinction is preserved in both the proof
and the map. The available conditional chain is now complete through
literal PointHV and the cohomological window endpoint.

8. Remaining CountI and CountV branches
=======================================

The four actual count predicates retain their v0.25 meanings.
CountI still follows the inspected OneChar -> PropGK -> CorGK route.
It needs the actual InpZ/cohomology package, KatoTwo/RigII and adjoint
vanishing, rank-three congruence-number control, modularity and
AutomorphyUnramifiedCoefficients. The general LemmaM/Mprime/P leaves
are now supplied, but their actual geometric inputs and the arithmetic
trace comparison are not. The separately defined CorG has a different
fixed-exponent shape and is not substituted for CorGK.

CountV still uses the actual rank-three host frame, marked point and
universal deformation ring. ThmS6_s, HostVanE, HostXpp, HostRigid_s
and HostNumber_s remain. LemmaM/Mprime are available, but the native
non-K local/global and normalized duality packages require their own
precise matching. HostNumber_s must provide one trace-separating
element and the valuation sum uniformly before the original frame
and point vary. Neither the finite algebraic inequality nor the
small-conductor CountVs theorem supplies the large-conductor CountV.

The current map therefore does not turn PointHV into an already
proved unconditional theorem. It separates its four actual count
inputs from its now-proved downstream assembly. This is a stronger
milestone than a collection of newly named implications: the case,
coefficient, field, layer and uniform-sum joins have actually been
written and reviewed.
