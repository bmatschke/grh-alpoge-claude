# The absolute covolume in the seventh period-height entry

A [direct determinant computation on the sharp row](PERIOD_RELATIVE_DETERMINANTS.md#direct-computation-on-the-sharp-row) gives a second route to the same absolute bound. It uses the sharp multiplicities and gluing at the actual serving places. The signed inequality in (7) there is still open.

Write $S_a=\sum_t a_t$, $D_*=m\sum_t(\varphi(a_t)-a_t\log\Omega)$, and let $F_{\rm ar}$ be the doubled arithmetic base term. With the admissible error exponent $C_{\rm abs}=2$, the seventh entry bounds
$$
 Y_u=\mu(R_1^\vee)+h_u-F_{\rm ar}-D_*+S_a\log T_u
$$
by a common constant times $n_TaL_a+m(B_0+N_c)x^{2\varepsilon}$. At each original member we can test just the distinguished word $u_0$ of the datum. Indeed $Y_u-Y_{u_0}=H_u-H_{u_0}$, where $H_u=h_u+S_a\log T_u$. The proved ninth entry bounds this by $4B_0/(nX')$ in absolute value. Since $m,n,X',x^{2\varepsilon}\ge1$ eventually, the bound for $Y_{u_0}$ extends to every $u$ by increasing its common constant by four. A bound for every word of course applies to $u_0$. This equivalence retains the signed scalar and permits its entire word-independent main term to survive. ## Where the missing absolute information enters

At layer $j$ the absolute quantity is the normalized log covolume of the reference lattice $M_j$ in its actual frame, with denominator the reference dimension $D_R>0$. The admissible-point covolume assertion gives
$$
 \operatorname{lcv}(M_j)/D_R\le
 (k+j-1-(m-j))\log\Omega\sum_\rho w_{j,\rho}
          + C B_0\log\log x.
$$
It is enough to take one $C$ on the fixed family beyond its threshold. Since $\log\log x\le L=x^\varepsilon$ eventually, the sum of the $m$ errors costs at most $Cm L(B_0+N_c)$. The factor of $\log\Omega$ must be retained with its sign, as it cancels a matching term from the word heights. [Main term and admissible-point bound][covol] · [Reference divisor][ref]

In the original common construction, the object predicate for the linked covolume row intersects the primitive, divisor, goodness, weak geometry and universal class-sum conditions. Choose the comparison for the actual selected bundle using its weak geometry. Use the universal comparison on that choice to recover the recursion-field tie. The retained rows give the admissible point conditions on the same realization, and the family assumptions give the seven duties. The native proof applies the *separate geometric covolume theorem* to that realization. This term joins the functional row inside the definite-rows entry and hence inside the actual joint choice. Its left side depends only on $B.Q.Rl,B.Q.F$ retained by every comparison on that bundle. Thus even if the two comparison selectors were chosen independently, the base row, layer lattices, frame, and evaluation $C,C_H$ in the numerical statement agree. The numerical covolume is a conclusion of the independent geometric input and the retained preconditions. Membership in the joint predicate alone does not bound its size. ## A correction in the geometric route

The proof of Roadmap B8.25 asserts that a small section meeting the finite valuation tests descends to a section on the projective image model $X$. For a possibly nonnormal $X$ the valuation argument gives a section on its normalization $\nu:X^\nu\to X$. For instance $R=\mathbb Z[2t,t^2]$ has normalization $\mathbb Z[t]$. The function $t$ is regular on the generic fibre and takes integral values in every valuation ring containing $R$, since $t^2\in R$. It does not lie in $R$, as the image of $R$ modulo two in $\mathbb F_2[t]$ contains only even powers. Thus regularity on all those traits and the generic fibre alone does not prove chartwise descent. This is a defect of that inference, independent of whether particular PEL charts have additional properties. [Passage asserting descent][claim]

For the geometric *volume comparison actually used* in B8.28, the first, valid conclusion on $X^\nu$ suffices under the displayed finite carrying-order and archimedean point-norm conditions. Fix the component, its metric shift and $\eta>0$. The rational evaluation gives an injection of the small sections in degree $r=8a$ into the small sections on $X^\nu$ in degree $r$ for the pulled-back shifted metric; the norm check is pointwise and a proper surjective pullback preserves its supremum. By B8.25h, the logarithms of the two unit-ball counts (on $X^\nu$ and $X$) differ by $o(r^{d+1})$. Its proof uses a fixed conductor section for the finite normalization and the fixed-twist invariance of the arithmetic volume. Divide by $r^{d+1}$ and pass to the limit also along the multiples of eight. The image-model Hilbert–Samuel leading term for the nef shifted line therefore bounds the small-section count on the normalization as well. This gives exactly the displayed volume inequality (8.28f), and then $\eta$ can decrease to zero. The error term here can depend on the fixed model: it disappears in the degree limit before the eventual family estimates are taken. This repair supplies the limiting inequality without proving a false same-degree descent to $X$. The controlled integral sections, deep-cover estimates, comparison of metrics, and paired-index bounds elsewhere in the argument are separate inputs.
