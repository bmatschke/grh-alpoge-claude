# The definite doubling identities on the retwisted column

Two special values enter the proof that the two theta integrals on a square have proportional images. One detects a nonzero theta integral on each side. The other excludes the possibility that the two images are different copies of the same representation. Both special values are instances of the published anisotropic Siegel–Weil formula. Their constants on the project's quotient measures are respectively **one and two**. The argument below applies to every admissible finite ramification set at a square of the restricted datum. [Use on the bottom row](SECOND_PASS_BOTTOM_TESTS.md) · [Nonvanishing and the two-copy issue](../construction/ROW_MATCHED_SQUARE_TESTS.md#nonzero-integrated-kernels-at-the-definite-bottom)

## The normalization of the published formula

Let $A$ have dimension $M$ and be definite at infinity. Double a space $Y$ of dimension $n$ by taking $Y\oplus(-Y)$. Its diagonal is a rational maximal isotropic space. The resulting split unitary group has Siegel Levi $\mathrm{GL}_n(E)$. The Witt index of $A$ over the number field is zero, since an isotropic rational vector would remain isotropic at a definite real place. If the form of $A$ is skew-Hermitian, multiply it by a nonzero trace-zero field element and multiply the form on the other factor by the inverse scalar (with the signs fixed by the trace-pairing convention). This preserves the unitary groups and the underlying alternating tensor pairing. The splitting character on the doubled group restricts to $\eta_{E/F}^M$. Thus the point of the normalized induced representation is $s_0=(M-n)/2$. [Data of the definite assertion][model] · [Classical section and the normalization of induction][yam]

For a polynomial Fock test at infinity and a Schwartz–Bruhat test at the finite places, Yamana's Theorem 2 gives holomorphy at $s_0$ and the value of the Eisenstein series on its Siegel–Weil section. The two alternatives in that theorem cover every $M,n$ here because the Witt index is zero. The quotient measure can be read off without guessing its convention. Gan–Qiu–Takeda §3.1 define $I^{n,0}$ by dividing the integral in Tamagawa measure by the Tamagawa number. They explicitly identify the resulting measure as having mass one. Their Theorem 7.1 then has an additional factor $\tau(U(A))/[E:F]=1$ for our unitary group. In particular for every Haar measure $dh$,
$$
 E(s_0,g;\Phi)=
 \begin{cases}
   1,& M>n,\\
   2,& M\le n
 \end{cases}
 \ \frac{1}{\operatorname{vol}[U(A)]}
       \int_{[U(A)]}\theta_\Phi(g,h)\,dh .
$$
Rescaling $dh$ multiplies the numerator and denominator by the same positive constant. This settles the measure question raised in O'Desky's comparison of THETA.D085 with the literature. [Yamana, Theorem 2][yam] · [Definition of the average][measure] · [Gan–Qiu–Takeda, Theorem 7.1][gqt] · [O'Desky, THETA.1.0025][odesky]

Here is the identification of the functionals used on the column. In the rational double use diagonal and anti-diagonal coordinates, with the trace-dual scaling in the second coordinate. In the Schrödinger model in which the diagonal acts by multiplication by additive characters, its invariant tempered functionals form the line spanned by evaluation at zero. In the real variables, invariance under all characters implies that multiplication by each coordinate kills the distribution, which leaves only a multiple of the point mass. In the finite variables multiply by a character nontrivial on a given coset away from zero. Partitioning the support into compact open sets proves support at zero, where an invariant distribution on locally constant tests is evaluation. Equivalently Fourier transformation turns this assertion into uniqueness of translation-invariant Haar distribution. The native functional belongs to this line. Its sum over anti-diagonal rational shifts is the specified rational theta sum. This fixes the scalar of evaluation once the rational Schrödinger intertwiner is normalized to preserve that sum. The intertwiner is built from rational changes of bases, multiplication by rational quadratic phases and partial Fourier transformation in self-dual measure. The product formula and Poisson summation preserve the rational theta distribution in these three operations. The anti-diagonal translations enumerate the whole rational lattice, since the factor two in the change of coordinates is invertible over the number field. [Functional and rational shifts][model] · [Project's Schrödinger and finite models](../theta/THETA_FIXED_STAGE_REALIZATION.md)

The two actions in these coordinates are fixed by their Heisenberg covariance and their splittings. For precision, two unitary actions implementing the same coordinate action have scalar quotient at each group element by the unitary Stone–von Neumann theorem on its Hilbert completion. Restrict the resulting scalar identity to the common smooth Schwartz vectors. This scalar is a character. On $U(A)$ invariance of the nonzero evaluation functional fixes that character. On the doubled group the specified Siegel transformation fixes it on the Siegel parabolic, hence on the group. Locally the parabolic contains the positive root groups, and a character trivial on them is trivial on their conjugates. The opposite root groups and the Levi together with the positive groups generate the split unitary group by Bruhat decomposition. At split finite places this is the same statement for the block parabolic of the general linear group. Single-place groups generate a dense subgroup of the restricted adelic product, and continuity gives the global assertion. The Siegel section is therefore exactly $g\mapsto(\omega(g)\Phi)(0)$ in the stated splitting. At $s_0$ its transformation is $\chi(\det t)|\det t|_E^{M/2}$. [The native Siegel transformation][model] · [Classical section][yam]

At the real places the box test used for nonvanishing and the polarized box tests used in the reverse norm are polynomials times their compatible Gaussians. A change of polarization takes these to the same polynomial oscillator space for the transported positive structure, by applying the change of variables or Fourier transform also to the differentiated Gaussian. Thus these tests are in the range of Yamana's theorem. More generally, in this definite model the central circle of the real maximal compact of the doubled group, acting by opposite rotations on the two blocks, rotates the entire polarized tensor space in one direction (reverse both rotations for the negative definite sign). Its oscillator weights are a fixed shift of the total Hermite degrees, up to an overall sign. A finite-dimensional compact orbit uses only finitely many such degrees and hence consists of Fock polynomials. The compact frame and the Gaussian in this statement are transported together. [The project's definition of real polynomial vectors][vectors] · [Yamana's archimedean test space][yam]

## Why continuation can be taken inside the compact pairings

The extension of a section from $s_1$ is flat on the compact picture and equals $a(g)^{s-s_1}f(g)$ in the project coordinates. The Levi datum is the automorphic character $\chi\circ\det$, with the normalized induction shift $n/2$. Right compact-finiteness gives a smooth archimedean section and some common open finite level. Indeed a finite-dimensional continuous action of a compact Lie group is smooth, and a continuous finite-dimensional representation of a profinite group has finite image after unitary realization in a compact Lie group. Iwasawa decomposition and the parabolic transformation extend these properties from the compact picture. Bernstein–Lapid's meromorphic continuation theorem therefore applies. Near each point multiply the series by a suitable power of $s-s_0$. On a smaller closed disc it has uniform moderate growth. At order zero the corresponding seminorm bounds a constant times a fixed power of the project height: a matrix norm and its inverse control every fixed finite-dimensional algebraic representation defining the usual adelic group norm. On a rational quotient take the infimum over lifts if necessary. A functional bounded on each such weighted supremum ball restricts to a continuous functional for this seminorm on the fixed-growth space. The Cauchy integral on a slightly larger compact disc gives convergence of the Taylor series in this fixed weighted supremum norm. Applying the functional term by term gives a holomorphic scalar. In particular integration against fixed continuous functions on either of the compact definite quotients can be continued, with the same choice of pole-clearing factor. ## Two applications, with different constants

Let $a>a'$ be the two definite source ranks at the bottom of a square, $D=a+a'$, and $T=S_0\sqcup Q$ the actual finite ramification set for this tuple.

* **Nonvanishing of each lift.** Double on the source of rank $n=a$ or $a'$, so $M=D>n$. The special value uses the coefficient one. To see that it detects a nonzero input, take a real polynomial with surviving source-character class and average over the compact source, choose the spherical vacua off $T$, and at each $v\in T$ take a small box about $(I_n,0)$ on which the determinant character of the stabilizer is trivial. A source translate meeting this box fixes it: its first $n$ columns give a matrix in the corresponding principal congruence group. This makes the local matrix-coefficient integral a nonzero volume. Away from $T$ the unramified doubling formula applied to the trivial source representation gives numerator $\prod_{j=0}^{n-1} L_E(\nu^{\pm1},D/2-j)$ at the special point. The partial factors are the primitive ones, because $\nu$ is ramified at both halves at every $v\in T$. Its angular exponents have absolute value greater than $D$. The absolutely convergent Euler product above one, Hecke nonvanishing at one, and the functional equation at the other integers therefore make every factor finite and nonzero. The ordinary unramified normalizer is nonzero there. The box and spherical calculation then gives a nonzero squared norm. At the priming place outside $T$ one takes a full-rank representative of the same coinvariant class, preserving the integral. [Box, Euler factors and functional equation](../construction/ROW_MATCHED_SQUARE_TESTS.md#nonzero-integrated-kernels-at-the-definite-bottom) · [Roadmap B8.11l][nonzero]
* **Exclusion of a second, orthogonal copy.** This time double on $U(W)$, so $n=D$, and average over the small source, so $M=a'<n$. The coefficient is two. The polarized section on the doubled copy of $U(W)$ restricts to the product of the two theta kernels, including the inverse splitting character that occurs in unfolding. Compactness permits Fubini. The compact double integral against $u\otimes\bar u$ at $s_0$ is exactly $2\|T_Pu\|^2/\operatorname{vol}[U(V')]$ in the conventions of the reverse lift. Proportional Petersson pairings for all translates on the convergence half-plane give proportional reverse squared norms at $s_0$. For the difference orthogonal to the primed image, reverse constancy and its zero mean give zero. For a nonzero primed image use its own test: the mean of its reverse lift is its positive squared norm divided by the source volume. This forces the orthogonal difference to have zero norm. Both forward lifts are nonzero and hence the comparison scalar is nonzero. [Doubled matrix coefficient][reverse] · [Reverse constancy](../theta/ROW_DEFINITE_REVERSE_LIFT.md) · [Comparison on the common quotient](../theta/ROW_DEFINITE_THETA_COMPARISON.md)

Consequently the continuation, definite special values and nonvanishing used at the bottom of the existing [induction through all signatures](../construction/ROW_TOWER_INDUCTION.md) apply with the retwisted $T$. Their constants and test vectors may depend on the individual triple. The assertion is valid for each triple satisfying the hypotheses and needs no new lower bound that depends on its twisting conductor. It can be used before selecting the final finite level. Constructing the matched tests on every ramified square, separating the full bottom row, and obtaining its arithmetic field of values involve further comparisons.
