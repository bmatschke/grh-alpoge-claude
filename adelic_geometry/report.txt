1. Why these constructions matter
=================================
The chart construction needs a base point kStd with the standard carried
lattice, identity outside the level prime and a prescribed last-row
residue. It also needs an adelic realization of each actual decorated
lattice. For naturality, a new neighbor must have a realizing point that
differs from the old point only above its specified type prime.

These are separate assertions. AD1 completely supplies the base point.
AD3 supplies the supported point change once two realizers and the
neighbor's local equalities have been supplied. AD4 proves that any
initial realizer can be adjusted to the source's exact rational frame
matrices. The arithmetic assertion that the initial self-dual lattice
and its finite decorations have a realizer is retained. In particular,
we do not infer realizability from the existence of abstract local bases.

The proofs work in the original finite adelic algebra AfE F E. Its
canonical isomorphism afIso with the finite adeles of E allows component
checking; it does not change the coefficient embeddings, the CM
involution or any chosen local residue fields. The conjugation of an
adele at P depends on its component at the conjugate prime, not its
component at P alone. All formulas below retain that distinction.

2. AD1: actual finite-adelic unit and diagonal base point
=======================================================
Let pi be a nonzero algebraic integer, q=conjugate(pi), with (pi,q)=1.
Let S be the finite prime support of pi. If P and conjugate(P) were
both in S, then pi and q would both lie in P, contradicting a Bezout
identity. Thus S and conjugate(S) are disjoint.

The component formula for a is

  a_P=q^(-1)     at P in S,
  a_P=pi        at P in conjugate(S),
  a_P=1         otherwise.

This is an actual adele, because only finitely many components differ
from 1. At P in S, q is a unit. At P in conjugate(S), pi is a unit.
Thus a and its inverse are integral everywhere. Conjugation exchanges
the first two cases: (conjugate(a))_P=q in the first case and pi^(-1)
in the second. Their products with the corresponding a_P are 1.
Every other component is 1. Hence a*conjugate(a)=1 globally.

This is precisely BasePoint.exists_unit, including its requirement
that the inverse component at each selected prime equal q before
reduction. A weak-approximation argument would provide congruences;
the finite adelic idempotents provide exact component equalities.

Set k=diag(1,...,1,a). Its inverse is diag(1,...,1,conjugate(a)).
The unitary equation follows from a*conjugate(a)=1. Both matrices
preserve the integral adelic standard lattice, so k carries the
actual global standard lattice to itself. The empty decorations are
then immediate. If pi*q=T, every changed component lies above T.
For a prime v of F not dividing T, all E-components of the projection
to E tensor_F F_v are 1; injectivity of that finite product gives
the exact identity matrix at v. The inverse's last row at P dividing
pi is (0,...,0,q), hence it has the required residue at every entry.

For n=0 the unique empty matrix is the identity and the last-row
quantifier has no instances. Thus no positive-rank assumption is
silently added to the literal exists_kStd statement. Specializing
to piT(u) and T(u) proves exists_base and exists_kStd under the
supplied primality/norm/coprimality laws. Ordinary simultaneous choice
then gives the existing kStd with kStd_spec, rather than requiring a
different base point for each later argument.

3. AD2: transition matrices and residue cancellation
===================================================
The source Carries condition identifies the completed lattice with
the image of the adelic standard lattice. If a carries N and b
carries N', and N'_P is contained in N_P, then every column of b_P
belongs to a_P O_P^n. Consequently all entries of a_P^(-1)b_P are
integral. For equality of local lattices the reverse argument gives
the inverse matrix integral as well. The transition is then in
GL_n(O_P), not just in M_n(O_P).

To connect this to the source's rational-localization convention,
write any x in N' with s*x in N for s outside P. Since s is a local
unit, the coordinates of a^(-1)x are integral at P. This extends
under addition and multiplication by integral adeles to the completed
module, including the columns of b. This proves the exact valuation
inequality in valued_inv_mul_le. It does not assume that the columns
of b are rational global lattice vectors.

If s is outside P and s*z has prescribed residue residue(s)*c, then
s is a local unit, so z is integral and its residue is c. For the
explicit HasRes definition choose t and r with ts+r=1 and r in P.
If b is an integral lift of the residue of sz, then

  z-tb = t(sz-b)+r*z.

Both summands have positive valuation. Also residue(tb)=c. This
gives the actual integral lift required by HasRes; it proves more
than formal cancellation of two symbols whose integrality was not
known. Applied after clearing a denominator, it extends type and
auxiliary residue identities to rational localizations.

4. AD3: all five fields of the supported-splice conclusion
=========================================================
The source IsPtOf has five fields:

  uni: unitary;
  lat: carries the specified global lattice;
  f: prescribed last-coordinate residues at type primes;
  be: prescribed full residue vectors at auxiliary primes;
  fr: exact prescribed rational matrices at twist primes.

Supply realizers h for X and h0 for X0. Choose P in the type-prime
set ST. The prime sets ST, SG and Q are pairwise disjoint, and their
union contains no conjugate pair. The local lattices of X and X0
agree outside P and conjugate(P); their type functionals agree at
other primes in ST, their auxiliary reductions agree and their
rational frames agree.

Let e=e_P+e_conjugate(P). It is a conjugation-fixed idempotent.
Define

  g=(1-e)h+e h0,
  G'=(1-e)h^(-1)+e h0^(-1),
  epsilon=h^(-1)g.

The cross terms vanish because e(1-e)=0, so gG'=G'g=1. The
conjugate transpose of g is G', since the coefficient idempotents
are fixed by conjugation. Hence g and epsilon are unitary.
At P and conjugate(P), g=h0. Elsewhere g=h and epsilon=1.

For the carried lattice, compare g to h0. At the changed primes
their transition is identity. At all other primes, both directions
of the transition are integral by AD2 and the assumed local equality.
Thus g^(-1)h0 belongs to GL_n(O_hat), proving that g carries exactly
the same lattice as h0, not just an isomorphic lattice.

At P, use h0's type residue. At another type prime P', use g=h there.
A vector x of N0 belongs to N_(P'), so choose s outside P' with
sx in N. Apply h's residue identity to sx, use equality of the
prescribed type functionals and their residue-linearity, then cancel
s by AD2. This proves the residue identity for x. The same argument
proves every coordinate of every auxiliary residue vector.

No auxiliary or twist prime can equal P or conjugate(P): disjointness
excludes the first, and orientation of the union excludes the second.
Thus g=h at all twist primes and the exact rational matrices are
unchanged. This checks fr as an equality of matrices, not merely
congruences. All five IsPtOf fields hold for g=h*epsilon and X0.

The literal source theorem obtains h0 using exists_isPtOf_cm. AD3
does not independently prove that supplier. Its result is the full
splice conclusion from h0; it removes the supported-patching step
from the remaining realization problem. The source's actual up/down
neighbors must still satisfy the local-lattice, functional and
coupling equalities assumed here. A generic splice is not a proof
of those neighbor-specific assertions.

5. AD4: exact frame modification without changing the lattice
============================================================
We first prove the actual Carries(globLat(x)) input which the source
uses automatically for an arbitrary x in GL_n(A_f). Both x and its
inverse have integral entries outside a finite set S. Choose a nonzero
rational integer d whose valuation at every exceptional prime is large
enough to make d*x_P and d*x_P^(-1) integral. This is possible by taking
large powers of the finitely many rational primes below S. Then

  d O_(E_P)^n <= M_P:=x_P O_(E_P)^n <= d^(-1)O_(E_P)^n

at every P. Define B_P=M_P intersect E^n inside the completion. B_P is
closed under the rational local ring O_(E,(P)); if s is outside P and
s*v belongs to B_P, the local unit s^(-1) shows v belongs to B_P.
Outside S, B_P is the standard rational localization O_(E,(P))^n.
Moreover d O_E^n is contained in B_P and d B_P is contained in that
standard localization. These are precisely the LP2–LP3 local saturation
and common-denominator bounds. Therefore

  N=intersection_P B_P,
  N localized at P = B_P,
  d O_E^n <= N <= d^(-1)O_E^n.

The last bounds make N a finite full lattice, by Noetherianity. They
also follow directly by intersecting the displayed local bounds; hence
the same d works, even if the generic LP3 product bound is larger.

For each fixed P, density of E in its single completion E_P allows
one to choose rational vectors v_j close to the columns x_P e_j so
that x_P^(-1)(v_j)_j belongs to I+M_n(m_P), where m_P is the maximal
ideal in O_(E_P). Its determinant reduces to 1, so this matrix is in
GL_n(O_(E_P)). This is also the exact openness condition needed for
the approximation. Consequently the v_j lie in B_P and are an
O_(E_P)-basis of M_P. The completed span of N localized at P is
therefore exactly M_P. This reasoning uses density at one place at a
time, never a simultaneous rational strong-approximation theorem.

To pass from individual completions to the integral adelic span, set
M=dN, so d^2 O_E^n<=M<=O_E^n. The finite O_E/(d^2)-submodule
M/(d^2 O_E^n) decomposes into its prime-power components by the finite
Chinese remainder theorem. Its extension to the integral finite adeles
is exactly the inverse image of those same local submodules modulo
d^2. This identifies the adelic span of M, and hence that of N, with
the product of their completed local modules. Thus N_hat=x O_hat^n.
By definition a rational vector v belongs to globLat(x) exactly when
x_P^(-1)v is integral at every P, so N=globLat(x). This proves the
source carries_globLat assertion with its exact carried lattice. The
zero-dimensional case is immediate. This recovery argument has
dependencies LP2 and LP3, beyond the denominator lemma LP1 alone.

Supply a unitary x carrying N, a finite oriented prime set Q and
matrices M_P over E which are local bases for N at P in Q. Set
g_P=M_P, g_conjugate(P)=conjugate(M_P)^(-transpose), and g=x
elsewhere. The same idempotents construct these entries. The inverse
uses the corresponding inverse matrices. The paired prescription
is exactly the unitary equation, so g is unitary.

Let b=x^(-1)g. At P in Q, x_P and M_P are bases of the same module;
AD2 gives b_P and b_P^(-1) integral. Since b is unitary,

  b_conjugate(P)=conjugate(b_P^(-1))^transpose.

Thus the transition and its inverse are integral at the conjugate
primes too. Elsewhere they are identity. Therefore b is in
GL_n(O_hat) and g carries precisely N. At each P in Q the frame is
M_P exactly. Outside Q and conjugate(Q), inverse pullback coordinates
are the same as those of x. This proves all assertions of the actual
PtExist.exists_modify.

When Q is disjoint and jointly oriented with ST and SG, this keeps
all the type and auxiliary residues while imposing the rational
twist frames. The unresolved initial realization is now the
self-dual lattice and its finite ST/SG reductions. In the pinned
proof it is supplied by decoPtSurj_of, whose inputs include
unimodGenus and reduction_surjective_bis. The former invokes the
local self-dual-lattice genus theorem with the CMData.cmUnram
hypothesis. No statement that arbitrary ramified Hermitian lattices
share a genus is used or asserted here.

6. Integration with the other v0.14 proofs
==========================================
FC3 uses AD1 for kStd_spec and retains the initial realizing points.
FC6 then reads the last row of kStd inverse without another choice.
FC's dilation proof uses the same identity. For up/down naturality,
AD3 supplies the supported adele once the actual neighbor satisfies
the required local equalities; FC11 handles the rational quotient
representative and character correction. AD4 isolates the remaining
arithmetic realization from the exact-frame bookkeeping.

The dependency graph is noncircular: AD uses only the earlier
local-lattice denominator/intersection/patching arguments LP1–LP3,
density in a single completion, finite Chinese remainders, and its supplied
CM/local data. It does not invoke the final FC assembly or CV values.
FC may therefore use AD's base points and supported moves before
assembling its own field and naturality records.
