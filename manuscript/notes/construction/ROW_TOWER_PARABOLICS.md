# From the line constant term to all isotropic parabolics

Let $D$ be a finite linear combination of the big polynomial theta integrals and the small polynomial theta integrals with full-rank support at the distinguished place. The target is $G=U(W)$, $d=\dim_E W=a+n$, and $e=(a-n)/2>1$. Assume the integrals are continuous, automorphic and of moderate growth, and that the split Weil actions and source invariance apply after replacing a finite factor by an arbitrary Schwartz–Bruhat vector at a fresh split place. Assume the constant term of $D$ along one rational isotropic line vanishes at **every** right argument. Then the constant term along every rational totally isotropic subspace of positive dimension vanishes. In particular $D$ is cuspidal. The characters, tests and choices may be fixed for one tuple throughout. [Pinned statement][pa-st] · [Strong induction and its inputs][pa-ind]

## What the induction needs from smaller dimensions

Write $N_U$ for the unipotent radical of the stabilizer of a rational $k$-dimensional totally isotropic space $U$. Choose a basis $u_1,\ldots,u_k$, a dual isotropic frame, and the Levi embedding $m:\mathrm{GL}_k(\mathbf A_E)\to G(\mathbf A_F)$ acting on $U$ by its argument. For $x\in G(\mathbf A_F)$ put
$$
 H_x(g)=\int_{N_U(F)\backslash N_U(\mathbf A_F)}D(nm(g)x)\,dn .
$$
Use probability measure on every compact unipotent quotient. Rational conjugation preserving $U$ preserves this measure. Thus $H_x$ is continuous and left $\mathrm{GL}_k(E)$-invariant. For fixed $x$ it grows at most polynomially in a matrix height of $g$ and its inverse, since the unipotent quotient has compact representatives and $m(g)$ has blocks $g$, $1$ and the conjugate inverse transpose of $g$ in a fixed rational frame. These bounds may depend on the tuple, tests, $x$ and frame. [Levi slice and rational invariance][pa-lev] · [Moderate growth on the theta span][pa-growth]

For $0<r<k$ let $U_r$ be the span of the first $r$ vectors. The block radical $B_{r,k-r}$ on $U$ has identity diagonal blocks and an arbitrary upper-right $r\times(k-r)$ block. Averaging $H_x$ over its adelic quotient integrates $D$ over the radical of the flag $U_r\subset U$. In the first order one integrates over $N_U$ and then $B_{r,k-r}$. Projecting instead to $U_r^\perp/U_r$ integrates first over $N_{U_r}$ and then over the radical stabilizing $U/U_r$ in its unitary group. Both describe the same rational flag group and its probability quotient measure. Compactness and continuity justify Fubini. Once $D_{N_{U_r}}$ vanishes everywhere, the inner integral in the second order is zero at every remaining argument. This gives **all** proper block means of $H_x$ on $\mathrm{GL}_k$, using for each $r<k$ the already established result on the same target. At $k=1$ rational Witt extension carries the assumed line identity to every rational isotropic line. [Line conjugacy and dual frames][pa-fr] · [Flag mean via the smaller radical][pa-flag]

Fix a standard additive character $\psi$ of $\mathbf A_E/E$. For $N_k$ the upper unitriangular group put
$$
 W(H_x)(g)=\int_{N_k(E)\backslash N_k(\mathbf A_E)}
       H_x(ug)\,\psi\!\left(-\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}u_{i,i+1}\right)du .
$$
If $H_x$ is nonzero and its block means vanish, $W(H_x)$ is nonzero somewhere. This holds for *continuous* functions by integrating one column at a time, starting from the last. For some nonzero value, Fourier uniqueness on the compact additive column quotient gives a nonzero Fourier coefficient. The zero frequency is excluded by the block mean. A rational change of frame in the remaining mirabolic group carries the nonzero frequency to the last coordinate row. The resulting column transform retains rational mirabolic invariance in the shorter block and the simple-root character laws already obtained. For each earlier cut its block-mean vanishing persists. Combine the relevant part of the column just integrated with the earlier upper-right rectangle, on which the column character is trivial, and average it first. This is the zero block mean from the preceding stage. Repetition gives the assertion without requiring pointwise convergence of a multiple Fourier series. Also $W(H_x)(g)=W(H_{m(g)x})(1)$, so it suffices to kill the latter coefficient at every $x$. [Nonzero column and its frequency][pa-col] · [Propagation and iteration][pa-mir] · [Whittaker transform on this Levi slice][pa-wh]

## The different arguments in dimensions two and three

For $k=2$ the [two-column calculation and smoothing argument](ROW_MIXED_PLANE_TERM.md) give vanishing in every such geometry. The single lower mean comes from $r=1$. At a good split place the big and small coefficients have proportional radial sequences on the original unramified tests, with a common multiplier growing like $(|R|/Q)^j$, where $Q$ is the residue cardinality and $|R|=Q^e$. The same multiplier works in a neighborhood of the fixed right argument. Convolving the $\mathrm{SL}_2$ restriction with small smooth bumps and using its zero mean and moderate growth yields bounded convolutions, forcing the original coefficient to vanish. This treats the **combination** $D$ and uses $e>1$. [Rank-two proof and hypotheses][pa-two]

For every $k\ge3$ the Whittaker coefficient is zero **separately on each theta summand**, at any right argument, before using smaller-dimensional constant terms. Fix a split finite place $v$ away from the distinguished small-source place, ramification and the finitely many exceptional test factors. Put $K=F_v=E_w$ at one branch. Complete the rational frame of $U$ to coordinates and absorb the frame change and right argument into the local map and test. Freezing the other tests gives a functional $L$ on $\mathcal S(M_{h,d}(K))$, $h=a$ or $n$. Source invariance and the split oscillator give $L(\phi(\gamma Y))=\xi(\det\gamma)L(\phi)$ for a character $\xi$. If $y_i$ are the first three frame columns numbered $0,1,2$, the simple-root laws are
$$
 \begin{aligned}
  L(\phi(Y+y_0\rho))&=\psi_v(\rho_1)L(\phi) &&(\rho_0=0),\\
  L(\phi(Y+y_1\rho))&=\psi_v(\rho_2)L(\phi) &&(\rho_0=\rho_1=0).
 \end{aligned}
$$
Here $y_i\rho$ is an outer product and $\psi_v$ is nontrivial. For instance the first target transvection acts on $U$ by an upper unitriangular matrix whose only possibly nonzero *simple*-root entry is $\rho_1$. It acts trivially on $U^\perp/U$, so its plane-radical part disappears from the functional. The second transvection gives $\rho_2$ in the same way. Both determinants are one. Substitution at $v$ preserves the priming at the other place. [Split transvections and both laws][pa-laws] · [Use at arbitrary frame rank and right argument][pa-three]

Fix $t\in K$ with $\psi_v(t)\ne1$.

1. Where $y_0,y_1$ are independent, choose a row $\lambda$ satisfying $\lambda y_0=0$, $\lambda y_1=1$, by inverting a nonzero minor. The column change with $\rho=t\lambda Y$, followed by the left transvection $I-t y_0\lambda$, fixes the specified matrix $Y$. The determinant character is one, while the column character is $\psi_v(t)$. To pass from this stabilizer to locally constant tests, regard the two increments as commuting square-zero operators $A,B$ on the ambient matrix space. At a chosen center replace a lattice $\Lambda_0$ by $\Lambda_0\cap A^{-1}\Lambda_0\cap B^{-1}\Lambda_0\cap(AB)^{-1}\Lambda_0$. It is stable under both increments and their negatives. The row from the fixed minor varies continuously near the center, so its neighboring operators still preserve this lattice by openness and compactness. Each nearby center's own stabilizer thus preserves the indicator of its coset modulo every sufficiently small scalar multiple of the lattice. The nontrivial character kills these indicators. Finite compact-open partitions treat every test in the independent locus. Consequently $L$ depends only on a test's restriction to $Z=\{y_0,y_1\text{ dependent}\}$. [Independent columns][pa-indep] · [Compact-open localization][pa-lattice]

2. On $Z$ with $y_0\ne0$ write $y_1=b y_0$. The second shear adds $t y_1$ to $y_2$ with character $\psi_v(t)$. The first shear adding $-tb y_0$ to $y_2$ has character one. For the test-function argument, take a test supported in $y_0\ne0$. Partition the compact projection of its support onto the first two columns into finitely many compact-open pivot charts, choosing a nonzero entry $y_{0,i}$ on each. Further partition by the value of $y_{1,i}/y_{0,i}$ modulo a sufficiently small lattice. The cutoffs depend only on the first two columns and are preserved by both shears. On each piece choose one representative $b_0$. The net change of the third column on $Z$ is $t(b-b_0)y_0$, uniformly within the additive invariance lattice of the original test because $y_0$ there stays bounded. The other columns are unchanged. Thus the transformed and original cut-off tests agree on $Z$, also off their projected support. The character law forces the value of each piece to be zero. This reduces the support of $L$ to $y_0=0$. [Dependent columns with nonzero pivot][pa-dep]

3. On that closed locus the first shear adding $t y_0$ to $y_1$ fixes every matrix and has character $\psi_v(t)$. Its pullback agrees with the original test there, so $L=0$. At each reduction, a locally constant compactly supported test vanishing on a closed locus has compact-open nonzero set disjoint from it. Thus the argument includes singularly supported functionals and uses no absolute continuity of $L$. [Final closed locus][pa-zero]

It follows that $W(H_x)=0$ for every translate. The flag and Fourier argument gives $D_{N_U}=0$. Strong induction on $k$ handles every rational totally isotropic subspace. Integrating in stages also kills the radicals of proper flags. At $(p,q)=(0,2)$ the real target signature $(d-2,2)$ bounds the rational Witt index by two, so the line and two-column results suffice. At $(1,2)$ the real signature is $(d-3,3)$, and only the three-column result is added. If a dimension is not realized over $F$, there is no parabolic of that dimension to test. The original inequalities give $e>5$ and $e>7$ respectively. To use this conclusion on a normalized comparison, the line identity for that particular difference at all right arguments and the stated analytic estimates must be supplied. [Rank conditions][pa-range] · [Cuspidality on all rational radicals][pa-st]

## Orthogonality when both sources are rationally isotropic

Suppose both sources contain a rational hyperbolic plane. The [two-source reverse-lift proof](../geometry/ROW_MIXED_CUSP_ORTHOGONALITY.md) applies in any Witt ranks using absolute forward integration for unrestricted big and primed small tests, the common good-split quotient with source augmentation kernel, the full-rank estimate polynomial in target height and uniform in source height, and cuspidal rapid decay for the polynomial $D$. For each source use a full-row-rank test, replacing the big test at an auxiliary split place by the sieve. At each place above one good completely split rational prime $\ell$, the realization of $D$ in the complementary rectangle and the equivariant square-matrix distribution give right source invariance for the reverse integral. This uses $h+(d-h)=d$, independent of the Witt ranks. Scale one rational hyperbolic pair by $\ell^j,\ell^{-j}$ and fix the orthogonal complement. The finite components away from $\ell$ fix the smooth data after restricting $j$ to multiples of a positive integer. Rational left invariance and the local right invariances identify the reverse value at the identity with its value along this real torus. In a rational basis for the pair, a full-row-rank matrix has a nonzero entry in the expanding row. Clearing finite denominators bounds its archimedean size below at some embedding, so spending extra Schwartz powers in the full-rank lattice bound gives arbitrary decay for fixed target argument as $j\to\infty$. The general [full-rank estimate](ROW_SQUARE_REGULARIZATION.md) is polynomial in target height uniformly in the source. Target rapid decay gives an integrable majorant, so dominated convergence makes the reverse value zero. Applying the same proof to every source translate and then using Fubini makes the forward lift orthogonal to $D$. Doing this for both sources kills $D$'s Petersson norm. Only one rational hyperbolic pair per source is needed. This applies at $(1,2)$ once the tower planes and indicated estimates are available. At $(0,2)$ the big source is definite. Remove two rational hyperbolic planes from the target to reach the definite target $W_0$, leaving that big source $V$ unchanged. If the definite nonvanishing input holds there, its lift $\pi=\theta_{V,\psi}^{W_0}(\mathbf1)$ is nonzero cuspidal and irreducible. Wu’s return identity with the inverse additive character sends $\chi_1^{-1}\pi$ to $\chi_2\mathbf1$. His orthogonality for every positive number of added planes applies here with two, so the big upper lift of $\mathbf1$ is orthogonal to all target cusp forms for the compatible splittings. The indefinite small source is treated as above. [Wu, Theorems 5.1(2), 5.4(1) and 5.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1409.0770#page=15) [Both source orientations][pa-orth] · [Use of the full-rank estimate][pa-torus]
