# Common marked levels for all the chosen retwists

On the forward construction of a retwisted column, the auxiliary marked levels and their ray fields can be shared across its active coefficient places. Fix **any** built first-pass origin with its all-powers export and the side coefficient setup used for the normalized row reading. Retain the finite allowable set $\mathcal P$ of coefficient characteristics, the caller's avoidance family, and the final ambient-field request. Assume the original marked core and its laws, including the side embeddings and the fixed-field ray condition at $g_0g_1p_\delta p'_\delta$. There is a choice of the actual retwist with the following properties.

* The variable conductors for distinct selected indices have disjoint sets of underlying rational primes. Their joint squarefree rational support $N$ is prime to every member of $\mathcal P$ and to $g_0g_1p_\delta p'_\delta$.
* At every active index $i$, the auxiliary level $p(i)N$ serves its actual conductor, where $p(i)=p'_\delta$ if $\ell_i=p_\delta$ and $p(i)=p_\delta$ otherwise. Thus at most two choices of the upper marking level $g_0g_1p(i)N$ are needed across all indices, rows and sides. The ray field even at the joint level $g_0g_1p_\delta p'_\delta N$ belongs to **every** active side reading field in the prescribed embedding. Each side can then take its own permitted finite character coefficients there.
* For sections defined over the lower coefficient field, their integral evaluation lattice is the exact inverse image of the upper lattice on each of these covers. This includes the original finite-residue tests with semiabelian reduction. A determinant twist with a root-of-unity value on each component preserves the equality of valuation tests.

The previously exported bases, row, Selmer and toric inequalities at every coprime power all apply to the characters chosen here. [First export](FIRST_PASS_COMMON_PRIMES.md) · [Original order of retwisting and field enlargement](SECOND_PASS_RETWIST.md)

## Choosing the rational supports on the exported origin

Use one old representative for each active pair of small-field places, as in the seed construction. Only finitely many indices occur. For a finite set $U$ of rational primes let $U_E$ be the union of the complete fibres in $E$ above $U$. At each new index include in the export's finite set of forbidden places the caller's exclusions, the complete fibres over $\mathcal P\cup\{g_0,g_1,p_\delta,p'_\delta\}$, and the complete fibres above every variable prime used at earlier indices. The export quantifies over **every** finite set of forbidden places after the index and bases have been fixed. It returns a single $(Q_i,k_i,\psi_i)$ for all allowed powers with those bases. Enlarging this forbidden set preserves the earlier requirements by restriction. Repeated occurrences of an index use the original choice at that index. Consequently $N_i=\prod_{p\text{ below }Q_i}p$ are pairwise coprime and $N=\prod_iN_i$ has the asserted coprimality. For no active indices take $N=1$. The allowed class condition on each prime in $Q_i$ gives the source's split quadratic class, and the excluded bad places give coprimality with the fixed host factors. The two distinguished primes satisfy the analogous split and coprimality conditions. Multiplicativity over distinct primes now gives the native auxiliary split squarefree condition for each $p(i)N$. In particular $p(i)N$ itself is prime to $\ell_i$. This concerns the **auxiliary** level, since $\ell_i$ may equal one of the two original marked primes. [Seed with arbitrary additional exclusions][seed] · [Split class and product calculation][product] · [Definition and alternatives for the distinguished prime][pdel]

The enlargement of the ambient coefficient field still comes after these choices. Restriction of coefficient places is onto and keeps their residue characteristic. The chosen twists pull back along that restriction, so the full range of their characters and their host conductors is the one at which the final-field request is evaluated. None of the avoidance decisions depends on the enlarged field. [Enlargement on the chosen twist][enlarge]

The same ray field can be included in the global final field. The packet lists $1,\delta,\delta'$ and the products $\delta_{\ell_i}\psi_i$. Away from the two distinguished primes, the union of the ramification supports of these products recovers exactly the underlying rational support $N$ of the chosen variables. Thus $N$ is determined by the packet sent to the final-field request. Adjoin generators of the quadratic ray field of modulus $g_0g_1p_\delta p'_\delta N$ to the caller's finite list on that packet. The order of selection and enlargement is the one already allowed in the second pass. [Contents of the twist packet][packet]

## One ray field inside every active side field

Put $M_0=g_0g_1$, $m_*=M_0p_\delta p'_\delta$, and let $K$ be the distinguished imaginary quadratic field inside $\mathbb C$. Write $H_a$ for its ray field modulo $a\mathcal O_K$. Apply the original side-field lemma at index $i$ and side $b$ with $m=m_*$ and with the **joint** $N$ just chosen. Its hypotheses on the two integers are $M_0\mid m\mid m_*$, $N>0$ and $(N,\ell_i)=1$. It puts $L_0H_{m_*N}$ inside the given complex image of the fraction field of the side coefficient ring. Here $L_0$ denotes the originally embedded fixed field. The same embedded field works at every side because $H_{m_*N}/K$ is normal and all the embeddings restrict to the given map on $K$. It is also normal over $\mathbf Q$ since complex conjugation preserves the integer modulus. It contains all $m_*N$-th roots of unity. Indeed the norm of a principal ray generator is $1$ modulo $m_*N$, so its Artin action is trivial on these roots. [Precise integer conditions and returned field][side] · [Embedded compositum and roots][compositum]

There is a local point in this conclusion when $\ell_i$ divides $m_*$. Set $m'=m_*/\ell_i^{v_{\ell_i}(m_*)}$. One has $H_{m_*N}=H_{m_*}H_{m'N}$. To check this, represent ray classes by ideals prime to $m_*N$. If an ideal is principal with ray generators $a$ at $m_*$ and $b$ at $m'N$, their quotient is a unit of $K$, hence $\pm1$ on this host. The common modulus contains one of the distinct odd $g_0,g_1$ different from $\ell_i$. Since $a,b$ are both $1$ there, that unit is $1$. The same generator is now $1$ modulo the least common multiple $m_*N$. The Artin kernels give the stated compositum. The first field is already present by the fixed-field ray hypothesis. The second is unramified over $K$ above $\ell_i$ and is read by the side's complete valuation ring with algebraically closed residue field. Concretely, a finite unramified local extension embeds after extending the given local base embedding to that strictly henselian field, and normality identifies the resulting image with the fixed complex image. This explains why using the product of variable primes from the other active indices does not create an extra local field assumption. [Original treatment of the two ray factors and the local embedding][local]

For a given row, side and index, enlarge this common number field within that side by the finite-image original type and distinguished residue characters. A finite-order element in the image generates a cyclotomic field there, and the row setup gives membership of these specific values. For each rational prime dividing $p(i)N$, choose one of the two primes in the distinguished quadratic field. Equivalently choose one of the two distinct roots of the minimal polynomial of $w_0$ modulo that prime. The Chinese remainder theorem gives a single residue $a$ at $p(i)N$ with those reductions. The ideal $I=(p(i)N,w_0-a)\mathcal O_E$ then satisfies $I\bar I=(p(i)N)$ and $I+\bar I=\mathcal O_E$. Both generators are in $\mathbf Z[w_0]$, as required by the cover construction. The same choices at the primes dividing $N$ can be used for the two levels. When a translating half is specified at the actual twist support, choose that side at its rational prime provided the pinned common-contraction law holds there. Rational disjointness means another index's variable conductor adds no competing prescription; the fixed distinguished half at each of its two rational primes is handled by its own marking. The conclusion on the level and ray field also holds with any one consistent orientation and the original Fourier changes of half. The full level is now $M_0p(i)N$. It is divisible by the index's prescribed distinguished conductor and by every rational prime below $Q_i$. Finiteness, the modulus and ray field, the small-field readings and these ideal identities are the input of the original marked-cover existence theorem. It supplies the upper marked PEL scheme, its uniformization dictionary and dominant-weight constants on the supplied row. The coefficient presentations and the constants may depend on row and side. ## What the cover tests recover on the original row

Fix a place $\lambda$ of the lower coefficient field $F$ and a place $w$ above it in the finite cover field. For a finite-residue integral frame $y$ over $\lambda$ use its original complete discrete valuation ring $V$. In an algebraic closure of $\operatorname{Frac}V$, first embed the cover field extending $F$ at the prescribed $w$. This is possible by extending $F_\lambda\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Frac}V$ to the finite extension at $w$. On the generic fibre choose the extra compatible marking over this algebraic closure. The finite-level point is defined over a finite extension of $\operatorname{Frac}V$. Over its valuation ring keep the scalar extension of the original semiabelian group, the tangent bases and the rigidified generator. The integral-frame predicate puts the extended marking on the generic fibre. Its tangent basis condition is preserved by flat base change of the eigenspace kernels, and its generator condition by flat base change of sections of the line on the semiabelian model. The generic group and line identifications pull back along the forgetful map. This yields an upper integral frame $\tilde y$ with finite residue field and the given lower frame after base change; conversely an upper frame gives a lower integral one on forgetting the additional marking. This also treats residue characteristics dividing $p(i)N$. [Actual integral-frame predicate][test] · [Finite descent of the upper point and integral lift][lift]

For a lower-field section $s$, its pullback at $\tilde y$ is the scalar extension of $s(y)$. Thus $v_{\widetilde V}((\pi^*s)(\tilde y))=e(\widetilde V/V)v_V(s(y))$ for nonzero evaluations, with zero preserved as well. Dividing by the respective valuations of a uniformizer of $F_\lambda$ identifies the normalized orders. This works for a whole finite list on the *same* lifted frame. It proves exact contraction of the upper integral test lattice to the original rational cusp space at $\lambda$. A root-of-unity component multiplier has order zero and hence gives the same valuation identity. For a general section with new field coefficients, the earlier trace-dual estimate addresses its descent. [Arithmetic cover evaluation including its component scalar][evaluation] · [Descent for extended coefficients](PEL_GOOD_FRAMES_AT_ALL_PRIMES.md)

This constructs the auxiliary marked covers for every exported origin satisfying the stated core and side-reading hypotheses, even with several nontrivial conductor indices. It also allows the individual short-section estimates on those marked models to be tested upstairs at the indicated coefficient places. To finish common-family existence one still needs the initial arithmetic realization and the localized Galois and row comparisons in the same second pass. The common cover levels do not bound $N$ as a function of the scale or the caller's arbitrary excluded sets. [Quantifiers of the two-pass construction](continuation_v78/producer.tex#L218-L276)
