PURPOSE AND COMPLETED SCOPE

WA11 supplies the actual same-vacuum finite-Weil algebraic-span assertion
left explicit in TT13 and TT16. It determines the actual phase, not just
the norm of an unspecified scalar. A positive theta inner product gives
the scalar's absolute square; the actual split normalization, evaluated
against a separately constructed invariant functional, determines its
phase. Positive-power closure and a local unitary group argument extend
the result to every finite adelic pair.

WA14 consequently supplies the literal NFJet for the original Choice,
using TT7's finite set covering all least-depth expansions. WA16 supplies
SumsIn for every supplied literal bottom triangle, with its original Haar
volumes and separate nonzero periods. These are the new main endpoints.
The argument has not been formalized, compiled or kernel-checked. It is
not a complete proof of RH or GRH.

Common hypotheses are the actual CM host, additive character, embeddings,
self-dual lattices, Cartan involutions, actual unit vacuum and actual
IsWeilDatumO. On the grid these are the original Choice's data. Finite
arithmetic does not construct a new full adelic representation. WA17--18
narrow the remaining full existence for new bottom spaces.

CLAIM-BY-CLAIM EXPANSION

WA1. Finite Heisenberg rational form.

An integer clears the finitely many local denominators of a finite adele.
Applying this to two vectors proves their full phase has finite order;
its half-phase squares to it and also has finite order. The actual
Heisenberg product law gives closure of the finite Qbar span. Nothing is
asserted about arbitrary phases at infinity. The eigen-sign epsilon is
determined by the actual nonzero vacuum, rather than freely chosen.

WA2. Pairings and scalar rigidity.

The vacuum has norm one for the actual covolume-one pairing. Its inner
product with a finite translate is epsilon(x) on Lambda and zero off it.
Self-duality supplies a lattice vector with nontrivial commutator in the
second case; invariance forces zero. Thus all pairings in the algebraic
Heisenberg span are algebraic. Positivity gives the nonzero denominator
in c=<F,F'>/<F,F> when F'=cF. This detects the scalar itself, in contrast
to the norm identity alone.

WA3. Projective algebraic expansion.

The source uses signed finite averaging and Heisenberg irreducibility to
write omega(g)v=kappa F, where F is an algebraic finite combination and
kappa is still unknown. The human proof gives an equivalent finite
character model. On Fock theta functions fixed by 2M*Lambda, the operators
epsilon(lambda)U(lambda) factor through Lambda/2M*Lambda. Finite character
projectors have algebraic coefficients. Self-duality represents their
characters by translates in (2M)^(-1)*Lambda. Translating an eigenvector
back gives the principal theta line.

Its dimension one follows from the positive Fock Fourier recurrence,
as in VI6: one set of integral half-periods gives a Fourier expansion,
the opposite half-periods determine all coefficients from one, and the
positive Gaussian gives normal convergence. The real coordinate change
used for this analytic uniqueness is not claimed algebraic.

The resulting finite basis consists of actual finite Heisenberg
translates. The moved-lattice eigenconditions are algebraic matrix
equations in this basis and have a one-dimensional common kernel.
A finite subset determines that kernel, and Qbar row reduction gives a
nonzero algebraic generator. Thus only the remaining scalar is unknown.

Commuting finite action with Fock derivatives is justified by actual
unitarity as an L2 isometry. On a fixed smooth finite level, real
Heisenberg difference quotients converge in L2 on the compact additive
quotient. Covariance passes them through the isometry. The output is
smooth by IsWeilDatumO, so the resulting derivatives are its ordinary
ones. This avoids assuming that an arbitrary ambient linear map
commutes with differentiation.

WA4. A congruence fixes the actual vector.

A sufficiently deep compact lattice stabilizer preserves the sign and
Fock laws and hence the vacuum line. Its scalar is a continuous unitary
character: evaluate the actual representation at one nonzero vacuum
value. Its compact image is a profinite GROUP quotient, hence a
profinite closed subgroup of the circle and therefore finite. The
kernel is open and contains a principal integer level. This group
argument is essential: an arbitrary continuous image of a profinite
space need not be totally disconnected. The resulting level fixes the
vector exactly, not just its line.

WA5. A nonzero split coinvariant.

For every outside half-translate, an opposite-half lattice operator
minus its nontrivial eigenvalue kills that translate but not the
vacuum. Such operators commute. Finite products prove that v is not in
the span of the outside translates, giving a counting functional with
values 0 or 1.

For differences U(y)phi-phi, finite smoothness gives a geometric operator
sum which telescopes to zero. Products of those commuting sums kill
any finite list of differences, while the counting functional evaluates
their value on v as a positive integer, with the identity summand
contributing 1. Thus v survives in the coinvariant quotient. Linear
extension yields ell, normalized by ell(v)=1 and invariant on every
theta function. The functional is not required to be continuous.

WA6. Algebraic values on a prescribed finite list.

The decomposition by e, conjugate(e), and q=1-e-conjugate(e) separates

Finite induction gives the exact finite-list claim; no infinite

WA9. Subgroup and positive-root closure.

WA10. Short local unitary generation.

The source endpoint requires a subgroup containing a principal level,
every split-supported point, and every positive root of its elements.
The source proof uses rational/split approximation. The replacement
proof is local.

At a nonsplit finite place, Hermitian quasi-reflections generate the
unitary group. For equal nonzero norm x,z with a=z-x nonisotropic,
A=h(z,a), u=-bar(A)/A, and h(a,a)=A+bar(A), the reflection sends z
to x. If z-x is isotropic, z+x has norm 4h(x,x)!=0 and two
reflections suffice. Orthogonal induction gives generation. This
argument works over a characteristic-zero quadratic local field;
the number-field hypothesis in the analogous pinned Lean theorem
is not being applied formally to a completion.

For R=I+(u-1)P the exact power is I+(u^m-1)P. Norm one makes u a
local unit. A finite unit quotient gives a positive m making u^m
arbitrarily close to one. Choose depth after clearing the projection
matrix's actual lattice denominators. Then R^m belongs to the
prescribed congruence, and positive-root closure gives R. The exponent
m need not be a DVR unit, so ramified and dyadic places are covered.
Only finitely many local factors of an adele are outside the fixed
principal level. This proves the global group assertion without prime
distribution, global approximation or a mass formula.
