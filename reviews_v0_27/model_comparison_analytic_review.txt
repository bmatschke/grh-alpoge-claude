PX6 — residual lattice and trichotomy
The boundedness argument correctly applies integrality to an individual
algebraic value and reverses its monic polynomial to a unit of the original
complete local ring.  It subsequently captures all coefficients using the
finitely many presentation variables, rather than making an assertion about
an arbitrary compact subset of Qbar_ell.  The normalized finite-order block
matrices and the self-adjoint H element give actual integral matrix
projectors.  The matrix span is an algebra and is stable under adjoint, so
the induced link relation is transitive and duality reverses its arrows.

For a strongly connected two-vertex component, products cannot pass through
an outside vertex; otherwise that vertex would belong to the same component.
Dividing one off-diagonal entry ideal by a generator gives a nonzero residual
cocycle.  The integral combination producing e_ss rules out a global
coboundary.  At a prime in Sb the same specified sigma_p kills both H0 of the
root character and H0 of its cyclotomic dual, so actual finite local Tate and
Euler give H1=0.  At ell the two stable-line identities eliminate the
coefficient generator; the remaining terms have zero reduction because
the slope is integral and the opposite off-diagonal entries are strictly
small.  The resulting scalar denominator is a unit by residual separation.
The Good.xvan statement extends to the actual finite residue-field extension
coordinate by coordinate.  The contradiction and the final four-vertex
component enumeration are sound.

PX7 — exact diagonal characters and one-sidedness
The character matching uses the ordinary flag and character independence,
not merely equality of values at s0.  Distinct s0 eigenvalues, preservation
of the first weight and symplectic duality leave exactly the two permutations
listed in PX.20.  The finite-order assertion for the remaining diagonal
character is justified by one open subgroup and the absence of
everywhere-unramified finite extensions of Q; no unproved finiteness of an
arbitrary algebraic-valued image is used.

The cover-link reduction removes all intermediate matrix-product terms.
A reversed cover link is a negative cyclotomic twist by a finite character.
The ordinary flag supplies a complementary local line at ell, while the
other inertia conditions descend through finite quotients and are
coboundaries in characteristic zero.  RV7 therefore applies to the actual
character and local conditions.  The same projector argument kills the
global coboundary.  The resulting sign vanishing and the two distinct
s0 diagonal values prove M3.  Two-sided irreducibility also uses NoEnd:
the intermediate block-triangular case has an algebraic first-torus limit
retaining both 0-to-2 links, and these links make the surviving block span
M2.  The actual-point return is supplied by PX5.  These hypotheses are kept.

PX8 — exact torus orbit and resolved issue
The maximum-norm argument for an intertwiner's off-block entries correctly
uses a unit residual eigenvalue difference against strictly smaller errors.
The normalized H equation kills its two indicated corner entries.
Irreducibility makes U*U scalar by the actual full matrix span, so U is a
symplectic similitude; this forces the remaining off-diagonal block entries
to vanish.  The diagonal product relation gives exactly the two-parameter
torus, with a scalar acting trivially.  On the first-weight-zero algebra,
the first torus parameter disappears, producing the original SameOrbit.

The initial draft stated the two-sided-point consequence under Fin0 alone,
although its cited torus result required irreducibility and PX7 obtains
that irreducibility using NoEnd.  I reported this and, with the author's
authorization, changed the consequence to Fin0 plus NoEnd and inserted an
explicit PX7 citation before applying PX.22.  The final metadata also adds
PX7 to dependencies and PX-R2 to retained input IDs.  All downstream uses
already have NoEnd.  The first conjugacy statement for irreducible actual
points is unchanged.  The issue is completely resolved in the frozen bytes.

PX9 — derivation extension and actual dual-number points
After inverting a nonzero balanced product uv, the first grading is a
Laurent extension of its degree-zero part.  The explicit balancing formula
therefore extends x+epsilon delta, independent of the chosen A lift because
the derivation kills I.  Its reduction need not equal the initially chosen
lift, but agrees on A, which is exactly the required hypothesis.

The actual-point restriction of RigidAt is respected.  Finitely many
dual-number coefficient values with integral first parts are integral over
O, including their nilpotent parts.  Adjoining these values and the required
variable powers divided by a uniformizer gives a finite complete O-algebra
in which the variables are topologically nilpotent.  The earlier
weight-zero/monomial/truncation argument then extends the map to the
original complete universal ring.  This supplies an actual PtIn witness,
rather than only a formal tangent to its graded algebra.  Differentiated
normalization forces a rigid derivative to be diagonal, and the Lie
symplectic relation makes it exactly an infinitesimal torus action.
Restriction to A leaves only b n x(f).  The final linear-dependence argument
covers also b=0 and is precisely M4.

PX10 — final inequality and normalization
The trace difference is an actual degree-zero function vanishing at the
original origin.  Its assumed nonvanishing is required on every irreducible
actual point; it is not asserted without that premise.  PX7 supplies M5.
All hypotheses of SP6 hold on the same algebra, equations and origin.
Selecting a cover of minimum cardinality removes orbit repetitions; the
corrected PX8 gives nonconjugate actual representations without enlarging
or duplicating the sum.

SP7 gives the original unnormalized length inequality D.sha <= cotLen.
The valuation used by SP6 is
v(z)=v_O(ell)(-log|z|)/log(ell), with |ell|=ell^-1.
Dividing by v_O(ell) and multiplying by log(ell) therefore yields exactly
the source nrm_O(D.sha) log(ell) bound with constant 2.  Finiteness is proved
before ENat-to-real normalization.  The exact target is the original
D.Ineq(g), including actual points, irreducibility and distinct conjugacy
classes.
