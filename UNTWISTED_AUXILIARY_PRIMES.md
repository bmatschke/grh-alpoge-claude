# The auxiliary primes can be fixed before retwisting

Fix the scale context, the arithmetic host before its coefficient extensions, its frame, and the small coefficient field as a subfield of $\mathbb C$. In the untwisted pass one needs an adelic realization whose exceptional primes can already be named when the coefficient fields and the twisting characters are requested. Two facts make this possible. There are only finitely many relevant choices of the CM generators on this host. Moreover the two integral indices used to detect exceptional primes are compatible with extension of the coefficient field. The second fact uses the lattices of the same reference theta map, as in [Alpöge’s items 15–18](ALPOGE_REMARK_GUIDE.md).

## A finite request before the remaining choices

**Proposition.** There are a finite set $S$ of rational primes and a finite set $U$ of algebraic numbers, depending only on the four fixed inputs above, with the following property. For every choice of the remaining preliminary parameters for which an unrestricted seed exists, one can choose a seed for those parameters whose entire embedded coefficient field lies in $\mathbb Q(U)$ and for which all reference-index places have residue characteristic in $S$. Here a seed includes a pinned column, a fine geometric and automorphic realization, and the original lower-row conditions (`HoldsL`). The fixed intermediate coefficient field of the seed may be chosen along with it. This is the assertion with the existential field *inside* the conclusion in the [pinned uniform seed theorem][uniform].

At a reference row $j$ write $T_j$ for the definite projected lattice, $H_j$ for its nondegenerate Hermitian form, $M_j$ for the target lattice, and $\Theta_j$ for the rational theta matrix. The two indices whose nonvanishing is tested at $v$ are the signed local indices of
$$
       (M_j,\Theta_j T_j),\qquad (T_j,T_j^{\vee,H_j}).
$$
They are also tested at the conjugate of $v$. The lower-row assumptions give finite generation of $M_j$, fullness and finite generation of $T_j$, and nondegeneracy of $H_j$. The dual is finitely generated, since pairing with a generating set of $T_j$ embeds it in a finite free module over the ring of integers. [Exact place tests][places] · [Finiteness on the actual row lattices][support]

Here is a proof valid with the index convention even for unequal rational spans. For two finitely generated modules $A,B$ in the rational vector space, put $C=A\cap B$. The index is the length of $A_v/C_v$ minus the length of $B_v/C_v$, converting an infinite length to zero on each side as prescribed. For either quotient, if the rational rank is zero a single nonzero element of the integer ring kills it, by finite generation. Its support is finite. If its rank is positive it has infinite length over every local DVR and its converted length is zero everywhere. Thus both indices have finite support. Take the union over the finitely many rows and their conjugates, then take residue characteristics. Finally, the image of a rational basis of the seed coefficient field gives finitely many algebraic generators in its distinguished embedding. This proves the asserted bounds for one seed. [Index convention][length] · [Local length proof][finite]

The choices that matter for passing to a uniform request are the two marked CM generators $\pi_{g,i}$ and the generator $\pi_u$ for each word $u$. They satisfy $\pi\bar\pi=n$ for fixed positive rational integers $n$ on this host. At each complex embedding, $|\sigma\pi|=\sqrt n$. The Minkowski lattice of the CM integers meets the resulting compact product of circles in a finite set. There are finitely many words, so the tuple of all these generators takes only finitely many values. Among tuples admitting a seed choose one witness per tuple. A second choice with the same generators may change the auxiliary real size parameter and its inequalities. The column equations, finite levels, theta functions, field, and reference-index tests use only the generators in that part of their input. One may copy the column, its realization and its lower-row proofs, inserting the second choice’s size and its inequalities. In particular this operation preserves $M_j,T_j,\Theta_j$ and $H_j$. Taking the unions of the prime and field requests of the chosen witnesses proves the proposition. The bound is an existence result at a fixed host. It supplies neither a numerical bound for the indices nor a single prime set for different hosts. [Parameter replacement and retained structures][resize] · [CM finiteness and coefficient field][cmfinite]

## Why the reference places pull back under a field extension

Work in the eventual pinned range of the [index comparison][comparison], with its seven standing analytic inputs, the prescribed equality of frames and preliminary parameters, both stages’ group identifications and the enlarged stage’s volume equality. Suppose the smaller column has its fine data and lower-row laws. On the enlarged admitted column take a fine realization satisfying all the comparison laws except the assertion about nonzero index places (the source calls this comparison `ExtN`). Let $L\subset L'$ denote the inclusion specified by the distinguished embeddings. It respects the involutions because both are complex conjugation in those embeddings. If $v'$ lies over $v$, then the reference-index test on the smaller realization at $v$ holds exactly when it holds on the enlarged one at $v'$. [Statement and comparison hypotheses][comparison]

To see why the matrices in this assertion really are calculated on the same rational forms, first use the common small-field geometric core. A cusp section at the original weight belongs to its small integral lattice precisely when its evaluations on every prescribed integral frame have nonnegative valuation. The comparison preserves the family, weight, frame tests and coordinate reading of this section. Thus this *small-field* lattice has precisely the same coordinate vectors under inclusion. One next spans it over the respective integer rings and intersects it with the level-character and good subspaces to form $M_j$. The marked action and character correspond term by term, including the coefficients of the good idempotent. The level and good subspaces therefore extend by scalars. Flatness of $\mathcal O_{L'}$ over the Dedekind ring $\mathcal O_L$ preserves their intersections with the integral span, by tensoring the kernel of the map to the quotient space. This gives $M'_j=\mathcal O_{L'}M_j$ under the induced change of row basis. This argument uses the small integral sections first, before the good subspace is cut out. [Section norm and coordinate comparison][small] · [Integral span and good subspace][good]

On the definite side use the values on the same decorated classes and their polynomial coefficients. The integral coefficient modules extend by scalar extension in those coordinates. The converse implication for a form whose values satisfy these extended lattice conditions follows from finite projectivity. Choose $b_a\in\mathcal O_{L'}$ and $\mathcal O_L$-linear functionals $\ell_a:\mathcal O_{L'}\to\mathcal O_L$ with $y=\sum_a b_a\ell_a(y)$, and extend the functionals to the fields. Apply $\ell_a$ to the expansion of the form in an extended rational basis. The resulting old forms have integral values on every old decoration because $\ell_a$ carries each extended coefficient lattice into its original one. The expansion above reconstructs the enlarged form as an integral combination of their extensions. The fixed finite-group characters and class-sum Hermitian pairing correspond on these evaluations. The level and good idempotents are the same finite sums after scalar extension. The cut and its orthogonal projection correspond as well. The range of the reference lifts is identified by the common reference map at the distinguished embedding, and the projection onto a nondegenerate span is given by its Gram matrix and inverse. Applying the projection to the extended level lattice gives the extension of $T_j$, with its prescribed pairing. The two bases used to express this result may differ by an invertible matrix over $L'$. [Integral forms on the reference classes][definite] · [Passage to the projected lattice and its pairing][project]

The old theta matrix solves the reference evaluation identities, by the separating rational tests in the lower-row assumptions. The comparison preserves the reference theta values on the old basis vectors. Since the enlarged target readings are independent, these identities uniquely determine the enlarged theta matrix on the transported basis. Linearity gives $(\Theta_j T_j)'=\mathcal O_{L'}\Theta_j T_j$ in the same target coordinates as $M'_j$. The Hermitian dual of the whole definite lattice extends as well. Indeed its pairing identifies it with the dual of the conjugate lattice, and the dual of a finite projective module commutes with flat scalar extension. [Reference matrix comparison][matrix] · [Hermitian dual comparison][dual]

Localize at $v,v'$, with ramification index $e$. The two lattices in each pair, and their intersection, are obtained from the old ones by flat scalar extension and a common change of basis. Each residue-field factor of a composition series gives a quotient by the old uniformizer in the new DVR, of length $e$. A positive-rank quotient has infinite length on both sides. With the convention above the signed index therefore multiplies by $e$, including if the positive and negative terms cancel. This also applies at the two conjugate places since contraction commutes with conjugation. Taking the union over rows proves the claimed equivalence. [Length under extension][ram] · [Conjugate places and the final index test][conj]

## Application to the untwisted stage

Request that the new **fixed** coefficient field $L_0$ contain $U$. Request also that the allowed rational integers contain $S$, the two marked primes, the host discriminant prime and every integer through $\lfloor X''(n)\rfloor$. These are requests permitted at this stage of the construction. On a pinned input the [true-volume stage](common_compatibility_v48/main.tex#L805-L894) can be chosen first. The auxiliary row, division and group slots can then be filled while retaining it. This step uses Green’s finite general linear group theorem (the source input `GreenCuspidal`). The finite group uses the primes above the two split marked primes and their Green series idempotents. In this preliminary stage the row operators can be zero, with the center acting via a character annihilating the good idempotent. The tuple with all labels $(X-1)^r$ occurs in its complementary series. The actual finite-group element defining the projector is preserved in this choice. This gives the marked group and the prescribed weight functions on the same volume stage. [Construction with the chosen volume][stage] · [Finite-group choice][green]

Apply the [initial construction](THETA_PINNED_COLUMNS.md) and the [construction extending a prescribed datum](THETA_FINE_EXTENSION.md) in their stated eventual range under their analytic inputs. The preceding proof supplies the place clause of that extension. Assume the lower-row implication `LowerUnitaryCaseCondition` on this range. On the requested untwisted column this implication and the initial construction furnish an unrestricted seed for its parameters. Apply the proposition to choose a bounded seed for the same parameters. Its **entire** coefficient field $L_s$ lies inside $L_0$ in the specified embedding, and the two bases have the same host, frame, preliminary parameters and small field. Thus this is an allowed enlargement from the bounded seed to the requested column, even if the bounded seed’s own fixed intermediate field is smaller than $L_0$. Use the extension construction on that very pair. [Exact enlargement relation][enl] · [Application of the finite request][saturation]

On the resulting realization the exceptional set is the union of the reference-index places and the places of nonzero forced content, allowing conjugation in both tests. The first set is the inverse image from $L_s$ just proved. In particular it is a union of full fibers over the finite places of $L_0$. The second test depends, for each row, only on the rational residue characteristic. Its defining alternatives can be nonzero only at the marked primes, the discriminant prime, or a prime in the requested range. Consequently the entire exceptional set is supported on the requested primes and is a union of full fibers over $L_0$. In the untwisted pass every twisting character is trivial off this set as required. The construction singles out one such realization. Its existence therefore uses the lower-row implication on the common eventual range. Producing the retwisted column and the later bundle of prescribed size are further steps in [the family construction](NEXT_STEPS.md). [Forced-prime calculation][forced] · [Saturation over the fixed field][saturation]
