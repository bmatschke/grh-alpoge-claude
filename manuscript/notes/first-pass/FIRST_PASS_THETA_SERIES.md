# The theta series and class-sum map at the first series level

Starting from the [twist forms and character list](FIRST_PASS_TWIST_SETTINGS.md), make the [common choice of tame characters](FIRST_PASS_SERIES_CHARACTERS.md) at the marked primes. For the resulting spaces we construct the complex readings, the theta series and the class-sum map. Two distinctions will matter later. Each base datum includes a proof that its ordinary theta series lies in the target reading, and for a variable twist the class-sum identity holds on the source vectors for which the sum belongs to that reading. The empty-level identities hold for every base datum.

## The definite forms used in the series

Fix a coefficient index $i$, a row $j$, a side $b$, a cyclotomic level $k$ and an admitted pair $(Q,\psi)$. The coefficients here are in $K_{i,k}=\operatorname{Frac}(O_{i,k})$ with the complex embedding $e_{i,b,k}$ chosen for that side. The definite space comes from the original pinned $V_j$. Its standard frame identifies the rational groups and the finite adelic groups, including the entries reduced at the marked primes. We must identify both the weight **and the character of the level** to evaluate a vector of this space as a polynomial-valued adelic form. [Exact evaluation and spanning requirements][b1target]

For the weight, the coefficient embeddings followed by $e_{i,b,k}$ select precisely the original CM embeddings. The frame character $\eta$ has weight $1/2$ at the selected halves. Substituting it in the infinitesimal character calculation identifies the ordered dominant weight with the dual definite weight of row $j$. In the polynomial model, sending a harmonic polynomial to its coefficient function is injective and has the same image as the matrix coefficient realization of this weight. Invert the first map on that image and compose with the second. This gives the required injection $\omega$ of the algebraic representation into the polynomial space, semilinear for $e_{i,b,k}$. The coefficient lattice is identified in the same construction by evaluating its defining coordinates. [Weight comparison on this frame][b1weight] · [Polynomial realization and coefficient lattice][b1poly]

For the character, the frame's finite-place bijection matches the full product of marked finite groups and its coordinate matrices. The selector $P_q$ picks the same halves at $Q$ and the same halves at the chosen $\delta$ prime in both levels. The unipotent character and its local scalars agree by the marked type comparison. At the extra parahoric places, evaluate the tame Galois characters on local units by Artin reciprocity. This gives the determinant character of $\psi$ at $Q$ and the indicated side of the order-$15$ character at the $\delta$ primes. The calculation at the inverse character uses the inverse value on the same units. Thus the full level subgroup and its character match, also at the marked places where a spherical Hecke comparison would give no information. [Half selection and the order-$15$ character][b1half] · [Level groups and characters][b1level]

For an abstract type form $f$ set
$$
  r(f)(u(g))=\omega(f(g)),
$$
where $u$ is the finite adelic frame map and $f(g)$ is its value in the given coefficient representation. This is injective. To recover a lattice-valued adelic form, evaluate it on the finite class representatives, lift each value through $\omega$, and use equivariance to extend. Injectivity of $\omega$ gives independence from the chosen decompositions. Conversely the values of any form on this finite list have a common denominator in $O_{i,k}$. Clearing it and using the matching integral level character gives a lattice-valued form. Hence the preimages of the given lattice span over $K_{i,k}$. The equality is pointwise for every $g$, and holds in any of the allowed frames inducing the same finite matrices. The data can be given the zero map for a tuple outside the admissible range, since its evaluation and spanning clauses only apply in the range. [Reconstruction and common denominators][b1abstract] · [Application and frame independence][b1input]

The reading of the rational row on the other member of the theta pair must also preserve independence after scalar extension. Evaluate at the given lowest-weight vector, with the nonzero vacuum scale for this row and side. The complex readings of a rational basis are independent. The reason is that the uniformization identifies the original row, and the lowest-weight evaluator is injective on its highest-weight multiplicity space. Independence of a finite family of coefficient rows is witnessed by a nonzero minor. Its image under a field embedding is still nonzero. Expanding in the rational basis proves that the complex reading preserves independence of every finite independent family over the local coefficient field. This permits the actual theta images to be read in these coordinates. [Faithfulness on a rational basis and after scalar extension][b1faithful]

Finally the good split Hecke action is the same double-coset sum as on the old geometry. For the $h$th generator at $P$ its rational reading carries the scalar $\mu_{0,j}(P)^{-h}(NP)^{-h n_V/2}$, with $\mu_{0,j}$ indexed here by the row's character-frame slot. The new scalar on the coefficient form is the $h$th power of the side's auxiliary character on Frobenius. Under the complex reading this is exactly the factor dictated by the auxiliary determinant character and its reciprocity normalization. The net character is the product of the frame and auxiliary characters in the new level formula. The same nonzero row scale multiplies both sides of the translated sum. These identities give the normalized Hecke comparison with the specified representatives, on the spaces just identified. [Comparison of the two sums][b1hecke] · [Installation on the given reading][b1input]

At a fixed place $i$, row $1\le j+1\le m$ and reading $b$, write $S=V_j$, $T=W_j$, $n=\dim S$, $r=\dim T$, with $r\le n$. Let $O$ and $O_k$ be the coefficient rings. Let $r_0$ be the faithful reading of the target forms, $r_k$ its extension to $O_k\otimes_O\mathrm{Fm}$, and $r_V$ the semilinear reading of the source forms. The comparison for $r_V$ at an admissible twist says that it is injective, its image contains the analytic source lattice, and the inverse image of that lattice spans over $\operatorname{Frac}(O_k)$. These are the input and the output spaces for the constructions below. [Readings used at the active row](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/IwasawaDecomposition/ExistsNewThetaDataSeriesClassSumsLattices.lean#L52-L76) · [Precise source-image conditions](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/InertiaEigenvalues/ExistsTieWithSeries.lean#L19-L49)

## The local test and its modulus

The pinned setup takes $\mathrm{ch}(i)=\operatorname{range}(P_q)$ and $B_i=\prod_{P\in S_0(i)}NP$. It extends the prescribed coefficient embeddings on both sides to compatible embeddings of the fraction fields of $O$ and $O_k$. The source and target frames, orthonormal bases, lattices, additive character, vacuum, and splitting characters come from the prescribed real realization. The transported target level and its character pull back to the given $U$ and $\psi_U$. The fixed twist uses the selected half of the distinguished prime set and the appropriate side of $\delta_i$. For a valid variable character $\psi$, its adelic twist is obtained by pulling $\psi$ back along reciprocity and taking its circle-valued complex reading. Its selected test places comprise the chosen half of $Q$. Its characters on local units factor through the residue units there. In particular the local test at an auxiliary prime uses reduction modulo that prime. The modulus $\ell^M$ below indexes universal characters, with a separate function from the residue units into $\mathbf Z/\ell^M$. In the source's finite model, a variable test of character $\nu$ on a residue matrix $y\in M_{r,n}(k_P)$ is $\nu(\det y_{[1,r]})$ if the first $r$ columns give a nonsingular square matrix, and zero otherwise. These factors are multiplied by the corresponding fixed distinguished-prime factors and inserted into the pinned finite vacuum. Write $\Phi_t$ for the resulting finite vector and $\mathcal F(\Phi_t,P)$ for the Fock vector with polynomial $P$ at infinity. For the splitting characters specified by the twist $t$, the actual complex series at source argument $h$ is
$$
\Theta_{\omega,\Phi_t}(h,P)(g)
=\bigl[\omega(h,g)\mathcal F(\Phi_t,P)\bigr]\!(0).
$$
The ordinary base series uses the base vector. A twisted generator uses the *untwisted* Weil action, the flat vector at $t$, a further source translation supported on the selected half, and multiplication on the target by its determinant character. The twisted class sum below uses the Weil action with the *twisted* splitting characters. The identity representation allowed as a default when there is no good twist with a given character is never needed for these good twists. For the universal series filter $Q$ to conjugate pairs admitting the prescribed anticyclotomic cyclic quotient of order $\ell^M$, and take one member of each pair using $\mathrm{ch}(i)$. Write $A$ for this half and $C_M=\mathbf Z/\ell^M$. At $P\in A$, let $\kappa_P:k_P^\times\to C_M$ be the additive reading of the universal character. For a codimension-$r$ plane $U\subset k_P^n$ write $d_U(y)$ for the determinant of the projected rows with the chosen determinant basis of $k_P^n/U$. The local coefficient at $a\in C_M$ is exactly
$$
\Phi_{P,a}(y)=|k_P|^{-r(n-r)}
 \#\{U:d_U(y)\ne0,\ \kappa_P(d_U(y))=a\}.
$$
Its denominator is prime to $\ell$ at the retained places. Its sum over $a$ is the full-row-rank indicator. For rank $r$ the planes counted are complements to the row span, hence graphs of the $|k_P|^{r(n-r)}$ maps from a fixed complement into that span. For smaller rank no projected rows form a basis. Moreover $d_U(v^{-1}y)=\det(v)^{-1}d_U(y)$, giving $\Phi_{P,a}(v^{-1}y)=\Phi_{P,a+\kappa_P(\det v)}(y)$. Multiplication by the group-algebra monomial of the target determinant shifts the coefficient index. Thus the coefficient $\xi_a(g)$ of the global universal series is the ordinary untwisted-action series with the product of local tests indexed by $a-\delta_A(g)$, where $\delta_A(g)$ is the vector of universal determinant-character values. ## Which series are installed

A base datum consists of a split support $s$ over primes avoiding $B_i$, a finite source translate $h$ supported there, and a polynomial in the specified harmonic coefficient lattice at $h$. Its ordinary theta series must also lie in the image of $r_0$. The installed form is its unique preimage by faithfulness. Its recorded level consists of **all** primes over rational primes dividing $\prod_{P\in s}NP$. For a variable twist the installed set is exactly the inverse image under $r_k$ of the twisted generators described above. At the empty set and trivial twist this is the singleton $\{1\otimes\theta\}$. The datum is empty outside the row range. In an active row the zero polynomial gives at least one datum. [Datum and theta tests](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Definitions/NonGaloisDescent/GoodSetupCountEqZeroOffBadPlaces.lean#L71-L102) · [Coefficient choices and level](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Definitions/NonGaloisDescent/FiniteDimensionalSpan.lean#L148-L181) · [Trivial specialization](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Definitions/NonGaloisDescent/FiniteDimensionalSpan.lean#L224-L232) · [Zero datum](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Definitions/NonGaloisDescent/GoodSetupCountEqZeroOffBadPlaces.lean#L365-L374)

When $Q$ is split, conjugation-closed, disjoint from $S_0(i)$, and every $\xi_a$ lies in the image of $r_0$, put $x_a=r_0^{-1}(\xi_a)$. The installed universal series is the finite sum
$$
\sum_{a\in C_M^A} x_a\otimes[\operatorname{spread}(a)] .
$$
The map $\operatorname{spread}$ is the identity on the chosen half, uses the prescribed conjugate-coordinate multiplier on its mates, and is zero outside the retained pairs. If the range or readability condition fails the definition assigns zero. At $Q=\varnothing$ the unique local index contributes the ordinary base series, giving $\theta\otimes1$ for every $M$. ## The stabilizer weights and the class-sum reading

For an admissible twist $t$ choose the finite double-coset representatives $h_c$ of the definite source at its level $J_t$. The source-level character combines the fixed and variable residue characters on their indicated support. Positive definiteness and openness of $J_t$ give finitely many classes, with finite stabilizers $\Gamma_c=S(F)\cap h_cJ_th_c^{-1}$ in unitary-group notation. The complex-linear class sum on a polynomial-valued function $H$ is
$$
\Sigma_t(H)(g)=
\sum_c\frac{\Theta_{\omega_t,\Phi_t}(h_c,H(h_{c,f}))(g)}
 {|\Gamma_c|}.
$$
The polynomial in each term is evaluated on the finite source class. This formula uses the reciprocal stabilizer cardinality with no further scalar in the chosen class sum. Integral source forms transform on the left by the given polynomial representation and on the right by the source-level character, with their values in the harmonic lattice at each class. Evaluation at the representatives injects that lattice into the finite product of harmonic coefficient lattices, since the two transformation laws determine every other value. Finite generation follows over the Noetherian coefficient ring. Its inverse image under $r_V$ maps onto it and is finitely generated by injectivity. The spanning hypothesis on $r_V$ gives full span after passage to fractions. Off the admissible positive rows the construction retains the supplied source lattice. The precise comparison for the installed source-to-target map is
$$
r_k(\theta_P f)=\Sigma_t(r_V f)
\quad\text{if }\Sigma_t(r_V f)\in\operatorname{im}(r_k).
$$
Indeed the readable subspace of source vectors is closed under $\operatorname{Frac}(O_k)$. Its closure under division follows because the target $O_k\otimes_O\mathrm{Fm}$ is divisible by every nonzero element of $O_k$. For $a\ne0$ in this finite integral extension, some $0\ne d\in O$ is a multiple of $a$, and $d$ acts invertibly on $\mathrm{Fm}$ over $\operatorname{Frac}(O)$. On the readable subspace, injectivity of $r_k$ gives a unique linear inverse for the image of the class sum. Choose a linear projection onto that subspace and compose to obtain the stored map on the entire source. Its values on the subspace are independent of the projection. The construction keeps exactly the original $r_V$. Thus this stage supplies the series comparisons, the finite source lattice, and both empty-level normalizations. To remove the range qualifications one must establish the corresponding rationality of base and twisted generators, universal coefficients, or class sums on the test vectors at issue. ## Returning to the given state

Use the prepared character data and the neutral standard frame for each row of the original definite spaces. The semilinear source reading and the target reading just constructed supply the hypotheses for installing the series, including the lattice, Hecke and class-sum conditions. Transport the fitted identifications, level data and old row reading to the state carrying these series. The identity-frame matrix input is available on this transported state on the same eventual range. Its use there preserves the fitted row and its normalized evaluation. The grid, its field of values, the specified roots of unity and the two directions of the character correspondence transport along the equalities of the previous level and its character data. At each depth at which the first intermediate level had a list, the new second intermediate level has a list at the same depth. The construction uses the geometric and representation-theoretic hypotheses stated in the component proofs. It supplies the input for the [moduli-point evaluations](FIRST_PASS_MODULI_POINTS.md).
