## What the detailed edition includes

Edition 5 assembled 65 texts into eight chapters. They comprise the overview, 42 mathematical notes, one guide to the formal source, 13 research modules, three TeX texts from an earlier round and five texts prepared for publication. The repository at that revision contained 313 Markdown notes under `manuscript/notes/` and 252 files named `main.tex` under `research/modules/`. These counts compare distinct source paths in the book’s contents with files in those two directories. Some notes revisit the same calculation with different hypotheses or notation. Historical checkpoints and source indexes also serve purposes other than exposition. The number of files left out therefore gives little indication of how many mathematical arguments still need to be written. [Edition 5 contents at the published revision](https://github.com/bmatschke/grh-alpoge-claude/blob/c2fc76a890d33ecb9f6357cdefc0406acaf14a97/publication/full/contents.json)

In the prime-selection argument, QW11 of edition 5 deduced the existence of sufficiently many norm primes from a weighted count in the principal ray class. The modulus and the length of the interval both vary, so a prime number theorem for each fixed field and modulus would leave the needed uniformity unresolved. The research module on ray primes gives the intervening argument. It constructs the ray-class characters, bounds the loss at a possible exceptional real zero, and proves the short-interval inequality with constants chosen before the field and interval. Those pages now follow the reduction. Earlier in the construction, the rational primes at which either of two lattice indices is nonzero must be fixed before the twisting characters are chosen. Finiteness of the bad primes for one lattice does not by itself give a finite set for all the later choices. A separate research note explains how to choose a bounded collection of initial data and how the two indices behave when the coefficient field is enlarged. Its argument uses the original integral theta image and its Hermitian dual. A reader following this point in detail should consult that note alongside the two-pass construction. The cohomological window also uses an argument that still has to be read across several texts. For each twist and each odd prime, complex conjugation splits the strict Selmer group into two parts. One must bound both parts on the same CM field and on the same interval as the contribution at 2. The final bound has to hold for every finite set of primes, with thresholds fixed before choosing the field. The chapter on simultaneous windows proves the estimate at 2 and at the reference layer. The odd-prime character estimates and their passage to a single bound on the original field are worked out in separate research modules. ## Published results whose scope can be checked directly

* **Galois representations on the cohomological window.** Theorem 2.1.1 of Barnet-Lamb, Gee, Geraghty and Taylor applies to the polarized cuspidal representations in B9.02 in every rank. The detailed edition identifies its Hodge–Tate numbers with B9.04, computes the Weil polynomial for every spherical place and compatible period comparison, and uses Chebotarev and semisimplicity to obtain B9.38 for any weak attachment. At ramified places the toral and full tame comparisons use the same global representation. The separate account of the Roadmap's construction in even rank derives the subtraction of two families of auxiliary characters under its odd-rank and stable-range hypotheses. The manuscript applies the proposition to the map on a filtration step composed with projection onto a quotient, and accounts for its squared-norm convention. This is a result that can be cited from the journal. Jean-Benoît Bost and Huayi Chen, *Concerning the semistability of tensor products in Arakelov geometry*, J. Math. Pures Appl. 99 (2013), 436–488.
