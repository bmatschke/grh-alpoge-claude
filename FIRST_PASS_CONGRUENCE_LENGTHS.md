# Finite support and coefficient choices before the spectral extension

Fix a fitted pre-stage $P$, a state $x$ above it, and a level with the [required character and theta comparisons](FIRST_PASS_ACTIVE_CHARACTERS.md). The support calculation below applies as soon as a localization with the stated defining properties is given. Write $K$ for the row coefficient number field and $W=P.W_t$ for its distinguished set of finite coefficient places. A place in this note is a place of $K$ unless explicitly identified as a place of the CM host $E$. The integer $m$ counts the rows. Three numbers must be distinguished. The input stage has a function $\eta^{\rm in}$. The definite row family of $P$ specifies a target $\eta^{\rm fam}$. A new localization $\mathcal L$ produces a length $\eta^{\mathcal L}$. The initial saturated state has $\eta^{\rm in}=0$ and allows $W$ to be nonempty. On rows $1\le j\le m$ the required comparison is $\eta^{\mathcal L}_j(w)=\eta^{\rm fam}_j(w)$. [Two original functions][old] · [Initial zero][start] · [Localization sum and out-of-range convention][new]

## The support and its size on the given state

The set $W$ is finite and invariant under conjugation. Under the chosen identification with primes of $\mathcal O_K$, it is the distinguished finite set of the row realization. Equivalently $w\in W$ precisely when the tuple at $w$ differs from the reference tuple. The localization changes the length component of the row record and retains these tuples and the place identification. Every local Hecke block $i$ comes with $1\le j(i)\le m$ and $w(i)\in W$. For any $j,w$ its fiber $I_{j,w}$ is the prescribed finite set of precisely the $i$ with these two labels. Thus the entire index set is a finite disjoint union over $[1,m]\times W$, including when the ambient index type initially came without a finiteness assertion. For $w\notin W$, its fibers are empty and their sums are zero. The definite row target is also defined to be zero there. This proves equality off $W$ for every localization, before making any coefficient enlargement. Outside $[1,m]$ the definite family function is zero and the localization retains the old input function at those indices. [Distinguished set][finite] · [Twisted-place equivalence][tied] · [Local index fibers][fiber] · [Empty-sum argument][off] · [Definite family formula][family]

There is a bound uniform over **all** automorphic semisimple realizations of this fixed state, including realizations obtained after further coefficient requests. Put
$$
 d_{j,w}=\dim_K M_j(\mu^{\rm tw}(w)),\qquad
 N_P=\sum_{w\in W}\sum_{j=1}^m d_{j,w},
$$
where $M_j$ is the original rational intermediate row space. At each admissible active pair the normalized local comparison gives
$$
 \sum_{i\in I_{j,w}}\operatorname{rank}_{O_i}\Lambda_i=d_{j,w}.
$$
Indeed the semilinear local identifications carry the generic fibers of $\Lambda_i$ onto the summands for the different maximal ideals of the commutative integral Hecke algebra. They list each maximal ideal once. The commuting generators are split with integral eigenvalues and the sum of these summand dimensions is the full dimension of the scalar-extended row. This comparison is part of the construction of the realization and uses no identity of congruence lengths. [Normalized local comparison on this realization][norm] · [Sum of generic ranks][rank]

Let $X_i$ be the finite nonempty set of joint eigencharacters in the $i$-th block. Its integral Hecke algebra separates these characters, and each has a nonzero joint eigenspace after passing to the fraction field. Their sum is direct. For a fixed character, choose for each other character a Hecke element separating the pair. Multiply the operators obtained by subtracting the other character's eigenvalue, and divide by the product of the nonzero differences at the chosen character. This acts as the identity on that eigenspace and kills all the others. Hence
$$
 \#I_{j,w}\ \le\ \sum_{i\in I_{j,w}}\#X_i\ \le\ d_{j,w}.
$$
In particular $N_P$ bounds all local blocks and even all these eigencharacters counted separately in each block. A nonzero local congruence length needs both a good character and a character in its complement. If the good set is empty the numerator space is zero. If the complement is empty the numerator equals the denominator. Therefore at most $\lfloor d_{j,w}/2\rfloor$ blocks in this fiber can have nonzero length. A row-place pair of dimension zero or one has zero localized length in every such realization. The [original projector calculation](FIRST_PASS_PROJECTOR_LENGTH.md) also gives zero on the family side in these dimensions and proves the required equality there for every realization. The bound counts the contributions. Their sizes depend on the integral lattices. [Characters and eigenspaces][chars] · [Upper and lower lattices][loclen]

The original family gives an exact support test on $W$. For row $j$ and the definite family tuple $t$ selected by $w$, take its projected lattice $T_{j,t}$ and its cut lattice $C_{j,t}$ in the same rational space, with level and chosen subspace as in the original row. The row hypotheses give full lattices and $C_{j,t}\subseteq T_{j,t}$. Put $\mathfrak a_{j,t}=\operatorname{Fitt}_0(T_{j,t}/C_{j,t})$. If $w\in W$ and $1\le j\le m$, then
$$
 \eta^{\rm fam}_j(w)=\frac{\operatorname{ord}_w(\mathfrak a_{j,t})}{e(w/\ell)},\qquad \ell=\operatorname{char} k(w).
$$
Indeed $T/C$ is a finitely generated torsion $\mathcal O_K$-module. Over the DVR at $w$, write it as a sum of modules $\mathcal O_{K,w}/(\varpi^{a_s})$. Its length and the valuation of the product generating its zeroth Fitting ideal are both $\sum_s a_s$. This also works when the quotient is zero, with unit Fitting ideal. The definition divides this length by the absolute ramification index. Thus the possible nonzero pairs and their target lengths can be specified before any localization. Each target belongs to $e(w/\ell)^{-1}\mathbf Z_{\ge0}$. [Family and normalized length][etaU] · [Projected and cut lattices][pair] · [Fullness and inclusion][lattices] · [Localized length][len]

## A single finite request before localization

A request is a function $b:w\mapsto b_w$ with each $b_w\subset\mathbf C$ finite. A realization consists of the local objects, their specified coefficient embeddings, an automorphic specialization using precisely these embeddings, and the semisimplicity statements. Its request clause says that for $i$ over $w$, each $z\in b_w$ lies in the image in $\mathbf C$ of $\operatorname{Frac}O_i$ under the designated reading. This is the only use of $b$ in the specification. If $b,c$ agree on $W$, exactly the same realizations, with exactly the same embeddings, satisfy the two requests. To see this replace just the proof of the request clause at $i$, using $w(i)\in W$. More generally a realization meeting a larger request at every active place meets the smaller one. In particular every forcing request may be set to the empty set outside $W$. The resulting set of requested pairs $(w,z)$ is finite. It can even be replaced by the same finite set $U=\bigcup_{w\in W}b_w$ at every $w\in W$, since this strengthens the request there and arbitrary finite requests can be realized under the automorphic existence input. The sets and this assertion depend on the fixed state. They give no uniform numerical bound on coefficient degrees over varying states. [Two quantifiers][quant] · [Only request-dependent clause][spec] · [Transport of a realization and finite unions][union] · [Automorphic existence on the state][exists]

In detail, a property is forced when *one request works for every realization meeting it*. To impose finitely many such properties alongside initial prescribed lists, take the pointwise union of the requests first, then realize the union, and apply each property to this very realization. Initial entries at inactive places may be retained literally when a set-inclusion assertion is requested. They impose no conditions on any local embedding. This also explains the quantifiers of the eventual family statement. The finitely many eventual conditions are first intersected in the project’s family filters. For each state and each fitted character level in that range the request is then chosen, independently of the realization. [Request calculus][union] · [State and level order][etaalt]

## Where the equality on the active pairs enters

Write $\Lambda$ for the local lattice in a block and $G,B$ for the sums of the selected and complementary joint eigenspaces. With the spectral decomposition, its congruence module is
$$
 \frac{G\cap(\Lambda+B)}{G\cap\Lambda}
     =\frac{\operatorname{pr}_G(\Lambda)}{G\cap\Lambda}.
$$
The equality uses projection along $B$. These are full lattices in $G$ and the normalized length is the module length divided by the local ramification index. To recover the original family quotient, two comparisons matter. First, the good spectral subspace must identify with the rational family projector and its complement. The pointwise spectral theorem supplies eigenspace spanning, the isotypic condition and, under integral coefficient realization, its residual and characteristic-zero conclusions. On the pinned route the pointwise theorem follows from the third-layer inputs and geometric Galois compatibility by combining linked local inputs, filtered-dimension jumps and eigenspace spanning on a common request. The row supplies its theta action and the span of its complement from a rational reading constructed without assuming the length equality. Second, rational Hecke integrality makes the lattice Hecke-stable. The local summands of this lattice then sum to the global completed lattice. For their separate good and complementary spaces the projection quotient of a direct sum is the direct sum of the projection quotients. Length is additive. The rational form and integral coordinate identifications finally take the projected and intersection lattices to the original family pair above. The index depends on the two integral lattices identified in this argument. [Pointwise spectral inputs][point] · [Geometric Galois compatibility and common request][supplier] · [Identification of the projector][cut] · [Direct-sum length proof][add] · [Original family comparison][rat] · [Use on these actual rows][etafam]

The construction forces integral coefficient realization, splitting of irreducible summands, and realization of the required wild Galois data at every live index. Together these three clauses are called `Large` in the source. Combine that request with the request giving length equality *conditional* on integral coefficient realization. Every realization of the combined request satisfies the integral coefficient hypothesis, so it gives both `Large` and the equality for every row and every place at once. At $W=\varnothing$ this consequence already holds for every realization without any coefficient request. All three clauses quantify over the empty set of local indices. Both sides of every required equality are zero. The empty-index condition on the *starting input* alone gives no such shortcut when $W\ne\varnothing$. [Three clauses][large] · [Integral clause][integral] · [Splitting clause][split] · [Combination in the stated order][combine]

## Passage to the extension and its origin

Given automorphic realizability, choose the combined request and then a realization $\mathcal L$ for it. The archimedean comparison on the chosen realization provides the full intermediate levels. The fitted second-level theta identities supply the theta datum containing every prescribed row index on both sides. With the active-place spectral and integral comparisons above, the row-existence input gives the moved rows, their rational reading and the corresponding column. The normalized core then transfers any grid list imposed on $x$ at depth $c$ to a list of the same depth on that column. The resulting extension has exactly $P.W_t$ and transports the Galois and twist data, form data, local comparison, and character choices $\mathrm{twI},\mathrm{side},Q,k,\psi$ in the precise sense of `Kept`. The extension with origin remembers the witnesses $P,x,l$, the given list at depth $c$ and the saturation of $P$. [Levels and transported rows][extend] · [Theta data][alltheta] · [Meaning of preservation][kept] · [Origin witness][origin]

The active-place spectral identification and archimedean comparison here retain their stated hypotheses. To go further to a seed one uses a **built** column for the extension with origin and the separate export statement on that column. For each new avoidance function $A$ assigning finite sets of places of $E$ to twist indices, transport the export to the stored original state. Choose one representative among the active places above each permitted coefficient-prime class up to conjugacy. This makes the final twist index compatible with conjugation and constant on the required active fibers. For the finitely many active indices, choose auxiliary sets successively avoiding $A(i)$ and the previously chosen sets. The export supplies a character whose every power prime to the residue characteristic still has the required good-twist property. The seed thus lives on the original state. Its representative-based index function and newly chosen auxiliary sets are separate from the character choices preserved by the intervening extension.
