# Reading a prescribed tame generator

At a type prime the local construction specifies an element of inertia in advance. We need the value of the chosen Galois character at **that element** in the original coefficient embedding. Local reciprocity describes this value by evaluating a Hecke character on an ordinary integer prime to the residue characteristic. The calculation also fixes the exponent in the tame symbol.

## The unit representing inertia

Let $P$ be the designated prime over $p=g_k$, with $p$ distinct from the coefficient characteristic $\ell$. It splits completely in the host, so $E_P=\mathbb Q_p$, the residue field is $\mathbb F_p$, and $p$ is a uniformizer. Work at the fixed extension of this place to the algebraic closure. Put $e=p-1$. Choose $\pi$ with $\pi^e=p$. For the prescribed tame generator $\gamma$, let $u$ be an integer in $\{1,\ldots,p-1\}$ lifting the residue of $\gamma(\pi)/\pi$. This quotient is an $e$th root of unity. Its residue lies in $\mathbb F_p^\times$, so the choice is possible and gives exactly the congruence with exponent $+1$ in the definition of a tame representative. [Definition of the congruence][tame] · [The split-prime and inertia hypotheses][split]

To compare $u$ with local reciprocity, take a primitive $p$th root $\zeta$ and put $\lambda=\zeta-1$. The polynomial $\Phi_p(1+T)$ is Eisenstein, so $\lambda/\pi$ is a unit. Every inertia element fixes its residue. If $\sigma(\zeta)=\zeta^a$, taking residues therefore gives
$$
  \overline{\sigma(\pi)/\pi}
  =\overline{\sigma(\lambda)/\lambda}
  =\overline{1+\zeta+\cdots+\zeta^{a-1}}=\bar a ,
$$
where $1\le a<p$. In the geometric reciprocity convention the local Artin image of $u$ acts on $\zeta$ with exponent $\bar u$. Thus this image and $\gamma$ have the same action on the maximal quotient of tame inertia of exponent $p-1$. Indeed the extension generated by $\zeta$ is totally tamely ramified of degree $p-1$, and tame inertia is procyclic. Any continuous scalar character of inertia killed by $p-1$ factors through this quotient: wild inertia is pro-$p$. This includes $p=2$, when the quotient is trivial. Apply the local-to-global compatibility of reciprocity to the idele supported at $P$. Its Artin image has a lift in the decomposition group that gives the same character value as $\gamma$. This last assertion only concerns abelian character values. [Kedlaya, *Notes on class field theory*, Example 4.1.4](https://kskedlaya.org/cft/sec_localrecip.html) · [Lubin–Tate reciprocity, Theorem 4.6.16](https://kskedlaya.org/cft/sec_lubin-tate.html) · [Prescribed-generator statement][repr]

## The value in the chosen coefficient field

Let $j'=j(i)-1$ in the source's zero-based indexing. At $P$, the local rank-one avatar used for the base character agrees on units with $\mu_{0,j'}$. The norm power, the auxiliary character $\delta$ and the chosen $\eta$ factor are unramified there. The character $\mu_{0,j'}$ is tame on units and is therefore killed there by $p-1$. Reciprocity proves the same exponent bound for the avatar on inertia. The remaining auxiliary twist is unramified at $P$ because $P$ lies in the prescribed exceptional set $S_i$, disjoint from its ramification set $Q_i$. It contributes $1$ at $\gamma$. The comparison just proved now identifies the value of the whole character at $\gamma$ with $\mu_{0,j'}(u)$ under the specified coefficient embedding. [Rank-one comparison](LOCAL_AUTOMORPHIC_SPECIALIZATION.md) · [Restrictions of the individual factors][restriction]

The type in the original automorphic data gives the latter value explicitly. For a residue unit $c$ equal to the prescribed $\chi_T(k,v,j')$ or its inverse, it is the specified complex Teichmüller lift of $c$. This is the disjunction allowed by the character frame at this place. The comparison uses the residue embedding attached to the same local specialization. Together these statements give the required value of $\chi_i(1)(\gamma)$ for all $i,k,v$ with $\ell(i)\ne g_k$. [Character-frame identity][chit] · [Comparison with the twisted character][result]

## Assembling the local specialization on a given input

Fix the original character frame and automorphic data, the common Hecke spaces and their rational and integral realizations, the original compatible choice of twists, and the splitting, angular-weight, coefficient-order and character-rationality conditions of the source. Then any prescribed finite exclusions at the coefficient places can be used in the construction. The [integral specialization](LOCAL_HECKE_SPECIALIZATION.md) supplies the local rings, Hecke lattices and comparison maps. Its [automorphic realization](LOCAL_AUTOMORPHIC_SPECIALIZATION.md) supplies the classes and the local comparison at the coefficient prime. The [base-change block calculation](LOCAL_BASE_CHANGE_BLOCKS.md) gives the well-defined shape on the actual cuspidal frame. [Residual compatibility](LOCAL_RESIDUAL_REPRESENTATIONS.md) supplies the semisimple reductions with the required comparison. The prescribed-generator calculation above and the [isotypic subspace formula](LOCAL_PARAMETER_SUBSPACES.md) supply the remaining type data. These constructions share the same local rings, labels and coefficient embeddings and hence give the original conclusion of `locAll_exists` on its stated hypotheses, including its block-uniqueness hypothesis for an abstract frame. With the original row, cuspidal and automorphic realizations in hand, use the resulting augmented datum as $D'$. Its equality to the datum just constructed is reflexive. This gives an actual local certificate at this input. The [selected-family proof](LOCAL_SELECTED_FAMILY.md) explains why a certificate is also available when $D'$ has already been fixed by the common family.
