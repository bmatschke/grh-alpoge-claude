# Auxiliary twists as characters of ray class groups

The local construction uses a finite-order Hecke character to introduce ramification at selected split primes while requiring the twist to be trivial on the full decomposition groups above the coefficient prime $\ell$. There are two arithmetic questions. Can one choose primes with such characters? And if the global construction has already selected a character in a particular coefficient ring, how does one recognize its inertia? Ray class groups answer the first question. Reciprocity and comparison at unramified primes answer the second.

## Constructing primes with exactly the required inertia

**Auxiliary-prime theorem.** Let $E/F$ be a CM field over its maximal real subfield, with conjugation $c$. Fix $m=\ell^a>1$, a finite set of forbidden primes and a positive integer $t$. There exist $t$ distinct rational primes, split completely in $E$, and a pair $P,cP$ above each. For each pair there is a Hecke character $\chi_P$ with values in $\mu_m$, trivial on $\mathbb A_F^\times$ and on each $E_v^\times$ for $v\mid\ell$, with conductor exactly $P\,cP$. Its local unit character at each of $P,cP$ has order $m$. The rational primes can also be required to split completely in any fixed finite extension of $\mathbb Q$ and to avoid the forbidden primes.

Let $S$ be the places of $E$ above $\ell$. The global elements that can give relations among residue-unit classes after imposing full splitting at $S$ belong to the finite group

$$
V_S(m)=\{x\in E^\times\mid v(x)\in m\mathbb Z\text{ for every }v\notin S\}/E^{\times m}.
$$

In fact, dividing the valuations of $x$ outside $S$ by $m$ gives a fractional ideal of the ring of $S$-integers, whose class is killed by $m$. The kernel consists of $S$-units modulo their $m$th powers. Finiteness follows from the ideal class theorem and the $S$-unit theorem. Choose representatives $x_1,\ldots,x_h$ generating $V_S(m)$. By Chebotarev there are infinitely many rational primes $q$ splitting completely in the normal closure of $E(\mu_m,x_1^{1/m},\ldots,x_h^{1/m})$, even after taking its compositum with the normal closure of the additional extension and omitting the forbidden primes and $\ell$. At every $P\mid q$ every element representing a class in $V_S(m)$ is an $m$th power in $E_P^\times$. The group $k(P)^\times/k(P)^{\times m}$ is cyclic of order $m$ since $q\equiv1\pmod m$. [Sharifi, *Algebraic Number Theory*, Proposition 10.7.5](https://math.ucla.edu/~sharifi/notes/algnum-ch10.html) · [Milne, *Algebraic Number Theory*, Theorem 8.31](https://www.jmilne.org/math/CourseNotes/ANT.pdf)

Take the ray class group of $E$ modulo $P$. Quotient it by the images of $E_v^\times$ for $v\in S$ and by all $m$th powers, and call the result $A_P$. It is finite abelian of exponent dividing $m$. The local units at $P$ induce an **injection**

$$
k(P)^\times/k(P)^{\times m}\ \hookrightarrow\ A_P.
$$

To see this directly, suppose that the idele supported at $P$ with unit value $u$ represents the identity in $A_P$. In the ideles it has the form $x b^m s h$ with $x\in E^\times$, $b$ an idele, $s$ supported at $S$, and $h$ an arbitrary archimedean idele times an idele of finite units, principal units at $P$. Taking valuations outside $S$, including at $P$, shows $x\in V_S(m)$ modulo powers. Our splitting condition gives $x=z^m$ in $E_P^\times$. Hence $u=(zb_P)^m h_P$, where $zb_P$ is a unit and $h_P\equiv1\pmod P$. The residue of $u$ is an $m$th power. This proves injectivity and accounts for relations coming both from units and from $m$-torsion ideal classes. [Ray class finiteness and the residue-unit exact sequence, Sharifi, Propositions 7.3.10–11 and Corollary 7.3.12](https://math.ucla.edu/~sharifi/notes/algnum-ch07.html)

Choose a faithful character of the displayed cyclic subgroup. It extends to a character of $A_P$ with values in $\mu_m$. Indeed a character into $\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z$ extends from a subgroup of a finite abelian group by adjoining one generator at a time and taking a root for its first multiple in the subgroup. Since $mA_P=0$, the extension takes values in the $m$-torsion. Let $\eta$ be the resulting idele class character, and set $\chi_P(x)=\eta(x)/\eta(cx)$. At $P$ the character $\eta^c$ is unramified, since $cP\ne P$. Thus $\chi_P$ retains the full local unit order $m$ there, and has inverse conjugate inertia at $cP$. Away from this pair it is unramified. The character is trivial on base ideles, which are fixed by $c$, and at every place over $\ell$, since both factors are trivial on the full local groups. This proof includes $\ell=2$.

Let $Q$ be the union of the chosen pairs. For **every** $P\in Q$, its pair's character, read by geometric reciprocity and an isomorphism $\mu_m\simeq\mathbb Z/m$, gives a continuous anticyclotomic Galois character. It is surjective on inertia at $P$ for every valuation above $P$, unramified outside the pair and trivial on the full decomposition groups at $S$. The equivariance of reciprocity under field isomorphisms gives anticyclotomicity for the prescribed lift of $c$, since inner conjugation acts trivially on an abelian target. Since $P\ne cP$, an element of $P\setminus cP$ witnesses the source's splitting condition. This proves precisely the auxiliary-set predicate `NAdm.AdmQ` at exponent $a$. The case $a=0$ uses the same kind of completely split pairs and the trivial character into $\mathbb Z/1$. [Exact source definition, including the separate character for each prime](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/LevelLetterCocycles/TWSet.lean#L12-L25)

For $m>1$, the product of the $t$ characters for disjoint pairs has conductor exactly $\prod_{P\in Q}P$, is tame everywhere, and retains order $m$ on inertia at every member of $Q$. This supplies a single witness to the six conditions of `IsPsi`. If the prescribed coefficient field $L$ contains a primitive $m$th root of unity, every character value, in particular every unramified uniformizer value, lies in the prescribed complex image of $L$. This gives `ValuesIn 0`. These existence statements concern freely chosen auxiliary primes. Restricting those primes to an independently specified Frobenius class would require compatibility with the Kummer splitting field. [The six conditions on the Hecke character](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/UnitaryNorms/CharHost.lean#L77-L88)

## An example at 2

Take $E=\mathbb Q(i)$, $F=\mathbb Q$, $m=2$ and $P=(5+4i)$ above $41$. Modulo $P$ one has $i=9=3^2$ and $1+i=10=16^2$ in $\mathbb F_{41}$. Since $\mathbb Z[i]$ is a principal ideal domain, the rule $(\alpha)\mapsto(\alpha\bmod P\mid41)$, using the quadratic residue symbol on ideals prime to $P$, defines a ray class character $\eta$ modulo $P$. Changing $\alpha$ by a unit preserves the symbol because $i$ generates the units and is a square modulo $P$. The character is nontrivial on inertia at $P$. It is unramified above $2$, and the value on $(1+i)$ is one, so its entire local component there is trivial. The quotient $\eta/\eta^c$ consequently has conductor $(5+4i)(5-4i)$, is trivial on the ideles of $\mathbb Q$, and is trivial on the full local group at $2$. Here checking the value on $1+i$ is the extra calculation that upgrades unramifiedness to full local triviality.

## Testing primes already specified

For a given finite $Q$ of split primes disjoint from $S$, form the ray class group of modulus $\prod_{P\in Q}P$ and divide by the images of $\mathbb A_F^\times$, the full local groups at $S$, and $m$th powers. Write $B$ for the resulting finite abelian group and $I_P$ for the image of the units at $P$. Then the number of $\mu_m$-valued `IsPsi` characters at these primes, of order *dividing* this fixed $m$, is exactly

$$
\sum_{T\subseteq Q}(-1)^{|T|}
 \left|B/\langle I_P:P\in T\rangle\right|.
$$

Indeed the characters trivial on the units at all $P\in T$ are the dual of the displayed quotient. It has exponent dividing $m$, so it has as many $\mu_m$-valued characters as elements, by cyclic decomposition. Inclusion-exclusion counts the characters nontrivial on every $I_P$. The definition of trivial restriction to $F$ tests the products of local characters on diagonal $F^\times$ at each place of $F$. Density of $F^\times$ in each completion, continuity, and finite-support approximation in the ideles give exactly triviality on $\mathbb A_F^\times$. Thus this test imposes the source's base restriction also at $\ell=2$. [The source's restriction to the real subfield](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/UnitaryNorms/IsUnitaryNormOf.lean#L185-L196)

For example, if $Q$ consists of at most $\ell$ conjugate pairs and every $I_P$ is nontrivial in $B$, such a character exists. In $B$ the unit images at paired primes are inverses of each other under local conjugation, because the diagonal local group from $F$ is killed. Their annihilators in the dual therefore agree. Each annihilator is a proper subgroup, of index at least $\ell$. At most $\ell$ such subgroups cannot cover the group, since all contain the identity and their sizes are at most $|B|/\ell$. The inclusion-exclusion formula also applies beyond this simple bound. The stronger per-pair surjectivity in `NAdm.AdmQ` uses the one-pair construction above.

## Identifying the character already selected in the local construction

Fix a twisted coefficient place $w$. Write $\chi=\mathrm{tp}.\psi(w)$, $Q=C.Q(w)$, $L=D.\mathrm{what}_2.LL$, and $\Psi=C.\psi'(w)\circ D.\mathrm{ntw.art}$. The latter is the **specified** character in the coefficient ring $O_k$. Its kernel is open since it contains the open kernel of the map to the finite ring-class group. Write $e:L\hookrightarrow\mathbb C$ for the specified complex embedding, $e_w:L\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Frac}(C.Oe(w))$ for the selected place embedding, and $j:O_k\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Frac}(C.Oe(w))$ for the prescribed integral inclusion. [The native ring-class map](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/LevelLetterCocycles/DataN.lean#L389-L407)

For any such $C$ and $\mathrm{tp}$, the predicate `NX.AdmTw` itself reduces to the avoidance of $D.\mathrm{ntw}.pD(i)$ and of the two type primes $g_0,g_1$ by $Q$, where $i=C.\mathrm{twI}(w)$. Indeed `IsPsi.split` gives splitting, and `IsPsi.atl` with `IsPsi.ram` gives $Q\cap S=\varnothing$. The ring-class map has open kernel, is anticyclotomic, takes values in a finite $\ell$-group, and kills inertia outside $Q$. Each statement passes through composition with $C.\psi'(w)$. This proves all the character conditions of ordinary admissibility before identifying any nontrivial inertia. Conversely the avoidance is written explicitly in its definition. On the freely chosen prime sets above it can be arranged by adding those finitely many places to the exclusions. [The exact admissibility predicate](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/NormalizedHeckeFactors/RingHomOfBaseOrderToFraction.lean#L32-L36)

By global class field theory, geometric reciprocity identifies the idele class group modulo its identity component with $G_E^{\rm ab}$. The kernel on ideles is the closure of $E^\times$ times the connected archimedean subgroup. The quotient map is open. Locally the units map onto the abelianized inertia image, the image of $E_P^\times$ is dense in the abelianized decomposition image, and the images of all uniformizers form the inverse-arithmetic-Frobenius coset modulo inertia. These statements follow from the local reciprocity isomorphism on the profinite completion of $E_P^\times$, its valuation on the maximal unramified quotient, and compatibility of global reciprocity with the local maps. They supply the stated `GlobalArtinReciprocity` in every number field and every chosen algebraic closure. [Global map and kernel, Sharifi, §10.4, especially 10.4.4 and 10.4.9–12](https://math.ucla.edu/~sharifi/notes/algnum-ch10.html) · [Kedlaya, *Notes on class field theory*, Theorem 4.1.2](https://kskedlaya.org/cft/sec_localrecip.html) · [The five source properties](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/PublishedTheorems/AutomorphicForms%E2%82%82/ExistsUniqueHcharOfFiniteOrder.lean#L24-L42)

When the character at $w$ is still free, its ring-class factorization can be chosen compatibly. Suppose $L$ and $O_k$ contain primitive $m$th roots of unity. Read the Galois character of the constructed $\chi$ first in $L$ via $e^{-1}$ on $\mu_m$. In the common fraction field the images of $\mu_m(L)$ under $e_w$ and of $\mu_m(O_k)$ under $j$ coincide because each consists of all $m$ distinct roots of $X^m-1$. Thus the same character has a unique reading in $\mu_m(O_k)$ with the same values in that field. The defining universality of $D.\mathrm{ntw.art}$ factors this latter character through the actual ring-class group since it is finite, $\ell$-primary, anticyclotomic and unramified outside $Q$, with $Q\cap S=\varnothing$. This gives the desired Frobenius comparison for that freely chosen descent. [Universality and the coherent-descent argument](../../../research/modules/construction/arithmetic_compatibility/main.tex#L208-L250)

Let $r:G_E\to\mathbb C^\times$ be the finite character corresponding to $\chi$. At every arithmetic Frobenius $g$ away from $Q$ and $S$ one has $r(g)=\chi_P(\varpi_P)^{-1}$, where here $\chi_P$ denotes the local component of $\chi$. The hypothesis `ValuesIn 0` puts this inverse in $e(L^\times)$. The source's `PsiChoiceCompatible` then asserts that the pair $(r(g),j\Psi(g))$ lies in the subgroup

$$
\{(e(y),e_w(y)):y\in L^\times\}.
$$

Both characters have finite image and open kernel. Their joint image is a finite abelian Galois quotient. In that quotient the Frobenius elements outside any finite exceptional set generate the group, so the pair lies in the same subgroup for **every** $g$. This argument uses agreement at all good places, as given by the definition. It also follows from the weak-approximation proof RC4. No continuous embedding between complex and $\ell$-adic coefficient fields enters the comparison. Injectivity gives $r(g)=1$ if and only if $\Psi(g)=1$. [Original Frobenius comparison](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/UnitaryNorms/CharGal.lean#L129-L135) · [Sharifi, Corollary 10.7.8](https://math.ucla.edu/~sharifi/notes/algnum-ch10.html) · [RC4–5](../../../research/modules/construction/arithmetic_compatibility/main.tex#L146-L206)

Local reciprocity now gives the exact inertia of the *original* $\Psi$ at every valuation above $P$. For $P\in Q$, some local unit has nontrivial $\chi_P$ and its inertia class detects $\Psi$; for $P\notin Q$ all local units give $1$. At $P\mid\ell$, $\chi_P$ kills the entire multiplicative group and so $r$ kills its closure in the Galois group after abelianization. Thus $\Psi$ is trivial on every element of the decomposition group there. Under the prescribed coefficient inclusions the integral character of HLP24 has the same kernel. On $Q$ the base twist is unramified under the stated disjointness from the selected exceptional primes, so multiplication by the base twist retains the nontrivial inertia element. This supplies the arithmetic hypotheses retained in HLP25 from the chosen tuple's finite character, its value field and its Frobenius comparison. Producing that comparison and the common level, coefficient and tuple choices on the original design remains part of the joint obligation.
