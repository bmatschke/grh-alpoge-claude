\subsection{The actual Hilbert-valued period Gram expansion}
\label{global-gram-period-identity}

Retain an existing original arithmetic setting and its original
\texttt{Full} data, in the gap range of
Theorem~\ref{actual-seed-decay-theorem}.  In particular retain its
actual framed lattices, finite column representatives, compact types,
characters, Haar measures and rational return representatives.  Write
$K=K_2$, $v_K=\mu_f(K)$, and
\[
 a_{u,\rho}=h_{u,\rho}x_u,\qquad
 \widehat\Lambda_{u,\rho}=a_{u,\rho}L_f,\qquad
 K_{H,u}=H_u(\mathbb A_{F,f})\cap x_uKx_u^{-1}.
\]
The column representatives are the original representatives for
$H_u(F)\backslash H_u(\mathbb A_{F,f})/K_{H,u}$.
Their finite set and its original row-lattice dictionary are part of
the existing datum, not conclusions about the existence of a new
arithmetic setting.

\begin{proposition}[The original column stabilizers are trivial]
\label{global-gram-original-column-free}
For every original word and column,
\[
 H_u(F)\cap h_{u,\rho}K_{H,u}h_{u,\rho}^{-1}=\{1\}.
\]
This is the original \texttt{NeatKu} assertion; it uses the two
distinct original rational primes $g_0,g_1$ in $g=g_0g_1$.
It imposes no inequality between either prime and the growing rank.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
An element $\gamma$ of this intersection preserves
$\Lambda=\Lambda_{u,\rho}$ and fixes the original hyperbolic pairs
and $z_u$.  Its restriction to their positive definite orthogonal
complement preserves the projected trace lattice.  The finite
automorphism argument of Theorem~\ref{global-gram-levi-fibres}
therefore gives finite order.  The restriction is faithful because
$\gamma$ fixes the complementary hyperbolic and $z_u$ summands.

Put $\kappa=x_u^{-1}h_{u,\rho}^{-1}\gamma_fh_{u,\rho}x_u\in K$.
At a place above either $g_i$, all original common denominators
coprime to $g$ are units.  Consequently $\kappa$ fixes the reductions
of $e^0_a,f^0_a,z^0$.  Their displayed Gram relations split off a
nondegenerate hyperbolic summand and the line of norm $-1$ in
$L_f/gL_f$.  The remaining summand is generated by the reductions
of the original $b_j,b_j^{\vee}$.  The integral idempotents
$e_w,\overline e_w$ are orthogonal and sum to one on this summand;
the original pairing relations make the two lists dual bases of
the two summands.  In particular no choice of a rationally integral
basis for an auxiliary field has been made.

The defining $K_2$ condition sends $b_j$ to $b_j$ plus earlier
$b$'s and hyperbolic $e$'s.  Projection off the nondegenerate
hyperbolic summand removes the latter terms.  Thus the action on
the $e_w$ summand is upper unitriangular.  Unitarity makes its
action on the conjugate summand the inverse dual, also unipotent.
The action on the fixed summands is the identity.  Reduction of
$\kappa$ is therefore unipotent at both residue characteristics.
This uses precisely the original integral vectors, spanning
condition and dual-pairing congruences in
\texttt{HidaSetting}, including the conjugate idempotent.
\cite{global-gram-period-source}

Here is the elementary torsion argument, including ramified places.
If a finite-order integral matrix has unipotent reduction in
characteristic $p$, write its order as $p^ab$ with $(p,b)=1$.
Its $p^a$-th power has order dividing $b$ and has unipotent
reduction, so its reduction is the identity.  For this matrix $M$,
\[
 (M-I)(I+M+\cdots+M^{b-1})=0.
\]
The second factor reduces to $bI$ and is invertible over the local
ring, so $M=I$.  The original order is a power of $p$.
Applied at $g_0$ and $g_1$, this forces $\gamma=1$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{The exact support cells and return set.}
Put $N_{u,\rho}=N_u(F)\cap\operatorname{Stab}(\Lambda_{u,\rho})$.
The phase theorem gives, for every $n\in N_{u,\rho}$,
\begin{equation}\label{global-gram-column-unipotent}
 a_{u,\rho}^{-1}n_fa_{u,\rho}\in K,
 \qquad
 \chi_2(a_{u,\rho}^{-1}n_fa_{u,\rho})=\psi_{u,f}(n_f).
\end{equation}
Indeed conjugate by $h_{u,\rho}$ and use its invariance of the
original unipotent character.  The converse follows from lattice
preservation.  No finite index or finite character is suppressed.

The unipotent group has $N_u(\mathbb A_{F,f})=N_u(F)U$ for every
open compact subgroup $U$: logarithm and exponential are finite
polynomial inverse maps in characteristic zero, and ordinary finite
adelic approximation gives density of $N_u(F)$.  Together with the
phase theorem's integral Levi splitting and
Proposition~\ref{global-gram-original-column-free}, this identifies
the support of the actual seed in $S_u(F)\backslash G(\mathbb A_F)$
with the disjoint measure cells
\begin{equation}\label{global-gram-small-level-cells}
 (N_{u,\rho}\backslash G_\infty)\times a_{u,\rho}K.
\end{equation}
One chooses an infinite-place fundamental domain in each cell.
The finite component of a rational unipotent element also changes
the point of $K$; the assertion is a measure decomposition.
To check it, reduce the finite Levi component by its original
column representative and then remove the remaining finite
unipotent component by approximation.  Equality of two cells
first forces equality of the Levi double classes.  Integral Levi
splitting and the proposition kill the remaining rational Levi
element.  The residual ambiguity is exactly $N_{u,\rho}$, by
\eqref{global-gram-column-unipotent}.  Translation invariance
therefore gives precisely the original product Haar measure.

For two columns define the literal rational return set
\begin{equation}\label{global-gram-level-returns}
 \Gamma_{\rho,\rho'}=
 \{r\in G(F): k_r:=a_{u,\rho}^{-1}r_fa_{u',\rho'}\in K\}.
\end{equation}
Every such $r$ carries $\Lambda_{u',\rho'}$ onto
$\Lambda_{u,\rho}$.  It also satisfies the original
\texttt{PD} admissibility condition.  Here are the arithmetic
details of the latter assertion.  Transport by this exact lattice
isometry preserves the original lift, flag-ideal and
\texttt{FrameOK} conditions.  At primes dividing $g$, the original
frame congruences and the first two $K_2$ conditions give an upper
unitriangular change of the $(e_0,\ldots,e_{r-1},z)$ frame.
Lift its finitely many coefficients from
$\mathcal O_E/g\mathcal O_E$ by the Chinese remainder theorem.
The resulting upper unitriangular matrix has coefficients in
$\mathcal O_E$.  The difference of the transported rational frame
and this matrix applied to the original frame is in $g\Lambda$
at primes dividing $g$; at all other primes $g$ is a unit and
the actual $e_a,z_u$ frame vectors are in $\Lambda$.
Indeed the original flag ideal $a_a=\mathfrak d^{a-r}$ contains
$\mathcal O_E$, so $e_a\in a_ae_a\subset\Lambda$; and
$z_u\in L_b\subset\Lambda$.  The column transport fixes these
vectors and $x_u$ preserves $L_f$, so the same assertions hold in
each actual row lattice.  No assertion that the $f_a$ belong to
$\Lambda$ is used.  Thus the difference
is in $g\Lambda$ globally.  This is exactly the original
admissibility with this integral matrix, not a weakened local
condition.  The inherited row conditions provide the original
lift and flag ideals throughout this argument.
\cite{global-gram-period-source}

It follows that
\begin{equation}\label{global-gram-original-return-bijection}
 N_{u,\rho}\backslash\Gamma_{\rho,\rho'} /N_{u',\rho'}
 \longleftrightarrow
 \{t\in\mathcal T_{u,u'}:
    \Lambda_t=\Lambda_{u,\rho},\
    \Lambda'_t=\Lambda_{u',\rho'},\ k_{r_t}\in K\}
\end{equation}
is the actual bijection, with $\mathcal T_{u,u'}$ the original
\texttt{GTerm} set.  A double class contributes once.  Its
stabilizer is exactly
$R_t=N_{u,\rho}\cap r_tN_{u',\rho'}r_t^{-1}$.
Membership $k_{r_t}\in K$ is unchanged by either unipotent
representative change, by \eqref{global-gram-column-unipotent}.

\paragraph{The original operator.}
Use the conjugate-first convention for Hermitian inner products.
For an original column $T\in C_u$, let $v_{u,\rho}(T)\in V_u$ be
its Riesz vector:
\[
 T(\xi)(1,h_{u,\rho})=\langle v_{u,\rho}(T),\xi\rangle.
\]
Initially $C_u$ is the original algebraic column space.  Equip it
with the original \texttt{ColT}/\texttt{colCore} inner product
\[
 \langle T,T'\rangle_{C_u}=
       \sum_\rho\langle v_{u,\rho}(T'),v_{u,\rho}(T)\rangle.
\]
This is precisely the source's transported column norm, not an
assertion that the initial function-valued submodule already had
a different Hilbert norm.  The column representatives and the
proved trivial stabilizers show that arbitrary lists of linear
functionals extend uniquely by the original covariance laws.
Thus $J_u:C_u\longrightarrow\bigoplus_\rho V_u$ is bijective;
the displayed norm makes this original \texttt{sharp} an
antilinear isometry.\cite{global-gram-period-source}  Let
$P_uT$ denote the original scalar Poincare series, and retain
\[
 c_u=\frac{v_K\,\mu_{N,u}(\mathcal F_{N,u})\,
                    \mu_{H,u}(H_{u,\infty})}{\dim_{\mathbb C}V_u}>0,
 \qquad A_u=c_u^{-1/2}P_u.
\]
These are the original \texttt{c0X} and normalized maps with the
original fundamental domains.  For the original representative
$r_t$, define
\[
 \mathcal O_t=\int_{R_t\backslash G_\infty}
 W_u(x)\,\langle W_{u'}(r_t^{-1}x),\,\cdot\,\rangle\,dx,
\]
and let $B_{u,u'}:\bigoplus_{\rho'}V_{u'}\to\bigoplus_\rho V_u$
have blocks
\begin{equation}\label{global-gram-actual-block}
 (B_{u,u'})_{\rho,\rho'}=
 \frac{v_K}{\sqrt{c_uc_{u'}}}
 \sum_{\substack{t:\Lambda_t=\Lambda_{u,\rho},\\
                    \Lambda'_t=\Lambda_{u',\rho'},\\
                    k_{r_t}\in K}}
       \chi_2(k_{r_t})\,\mathcal O_t.
\end{equation}
The sum is the original \texttt{blk}/\texttt{aTermL} sum.
Its absolute operator convergence follows from
Theorem~\ref{global-gram-framed-sum}; the finite column sets,
the exact factor $v_K/\sqrt{c_uc_{u'}}$, and unit phases are retained.

\begin{theorem}[Actual normalized period Gram identity]
\label{global-gram-entry-unfold}
For every pair of original words and original columns,
\begin{equation}\label{global-gram-entry-equality}
 \langle A_uT,A_{u'}T'\rangle
   =\langle B_{u,u'}J_{u'}T',J_uT\rangle.
\end{equation}
Equivalently,
\begin{equation}\label{global-gram-hilbert-equality}
 B_{u,u'}=J_u A_u^*A_{u'}J_{u'}^{-1}.
\end{equation}
This proves the original \texttt{EntryUnfold}, hence the genuine
\texttt{KExpansion}, for the actual Poincare periods in the
established arithmetic gap regime.  It does not define a new
kernel to make the identity true.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The preceding folding theorem gives absolute convergence and
$L^2$ membership for each original period.  Its scalar unfolding,
with the original Haar measures, gives
\[
 \langle P_uT,P_{u'}T'\rangle
  =\int_{S_u(F)\backslash G(\mathbb A_F)}
       \overline{\Phi_u(T)(y)}\,P_{u'}T'(y)\,dy.
\]
Use \eqref{global-gram-small-level-cells}.  On each cell the
integrand is right $K$-invariant: the seed and the full period
have the same unitary right character.  Its finite integral is
therefore exactly $v_K$, without a stabilizer denominator.

For fixed $\rho$, every nonzero term of
$P_{u'}T'(x,a_{u,\rho})$ can be moved by one element of
$S_{u'}(F)$ into a unique target column $\rho'$ using the same
cell argument.  The resulting terms are indexed precisely by
$\Gamma_{\rho,\rho'}/N_{u',\rho'}$, since the original right
$S_{u'}(F)$ ambiguity which preserves this finite column is,
by integral splitting and the proposition, exactly
$N_{u',\rho'}$.  At a representative $r$ its value is
\[
 \chi_2(k_r)^{-1}
    \langle v_{u',\rho'}(T'),W_{u'}(r_\infty^{-1}x)\rangle.
\]
Now quotient these representatives by left $N_{u,\rho}$.  The
orbit-stabilizer bijection is
\eqref{global-gram-original-return-bijection}; unfolding the
infinite-place domain gives $R_t\backslash G_\infty$.
For $n\in N_{u,\rho}$, the factor introduced by
$k_{nr}=a_{u,\rho}^{-1}n_fa_{u,\rho}k_r$ cancels the factor
from $W_u(nx)$, because
$\psi_{u,f}(n_f)\psi_{u,\infty}(n_\infty)=1$.
Thus the integrand for $t$ is exactly
\[
 \overline{\chi_2(k_{r_t})}\,
 \langle W_u(x),v_{u,\rho}(T)\rangle
 \langle v_{u',\rho'}(T'),W_{u'}(r_t^{-1}x)\rangle.
\]
Every rearrangement is absolutely justified by
Theorem~\ref{global-gram-framed-sum}, multiplied by the finite
column-vector norms.  The displayed integral equals
$\langle\chi_2(k_{r_t})\mathcal O_t
v_{u',\rho'}(T'),v_{u,\rho}(T)\rangle$ in the stated convention.
After multiplication by the original factor
$v_K/\sqrt{c_uc_{u'}}$, this is
\eqref{global-gram-entry-equality}.  Antilinearity of $J_u$
gives \eqref{global-gram-hilbert-equality}.

For clarity, this formula also respects the source's arbitrary
return representatives.  Replacing $r$ by $nrn'$ sends
$R_r$ to $nR_rn^{-1}$ and sends the orbital operator to
$\psi_{u,\infty}(n)\psi_{u',\infty}(n')\mathcal O_r$.
The finite coefficient is multiplied by its inverse, by
\eqref{global-gram-column-unipotent} and the rational product
formula.  Original Haar invariance supplies no extra factor.
The product in \eqref{global-gram-actual-block} is unchanged.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Exact restrictions and the remaining frontier.}
The theorem identifies the literal normalized Poincare maps.
If $E_u$ is an original retained source subspace and $Q$ the
original orthogonal target projection, then the Gram of the
restricted maps is exactly
\[
 J_u\bigl((A_u|_{E_u})^*Q(A_{u'}|_{E_{u'}})\bigr)J_{u'}^{-1}.
\]
It equals the corresponding source compression of
\eqref{global-gram-hilbert-equality} when the target projection
fixes these images.  In general one must subtract the source
compression of $J_uA_u^*(I-Q)A_{u'}J_{u'}^{-1}$; this term is
not discarded.  Nor does a period formula identify these maps
with a different arithmetic chain map without the original
integral and metric dictionary.

The theorem closes the actual period-expansion supplier, including
all rational returns, characters, stabilizers and normalizers.
It provides no uniform bound for their sum, and no estimate for
the final two positive traces or the signed table penalty.
The raw scalar-class kernel \texttt{KgsOf} still needs its actual
pair-index dictionary if that kernel, rather than
\eqref{global-gram-actual-block}, is used downstream.  The whole
W08 frontier therefore remains open.
