# Surjectivity on the full reference row of B8.08

For a word on the original linked realization, let $M_j$ be the complex space of cuspidal multiplicity maps at stage $j$ of its reference **row** tower. The finite level, its character, the archimedean discrete series and the theta transition are those of that realization. Write $d_j:M_{j+1}\to M_j$ for downward lift and $\mathcal D=d_0\cdots d_{m-1}$. Under the representation and doubling inputs below, the actual composite satisfies
$$
                      \mathcal D(M_m)=M_0 .
$$
This includes every bottom finite type and every vector of every bottom Hecke eigenspace. The proof need only test the adjoint of the composite on the parameters reached from the bottom. It does not require surjectivity onto every possible intermediate eigenspace. The argument works upstream of a diagonal Bessel estimate. ## The realization and the analytic inputs

Assume the family's package hypotheses and its reference character's row condition. These tie the common Hecke characters and node gaps, the real parameters and their splitting conventions, the self-dual lattices and frames, the marked finite Schwartz tests and the actual theta formulas to the same tower. In the native application the $Y_j$ transitions are checked for $j+1<m$, the final transition goes to $X'$, and $m=2n+2$. The full family map then agrees with the reached composite used below. The source lists eleven analytic inputs by name: Harish-Chandra finiteness, rapid decay of cusp forms, the discrete-series cover and Harish-Chandra module inputs, Casselman–Wallach globalization, unramified Howe correspondence, the unitary-pair Weil representation, the unitary Weil–Siegel formula, the convergent-range unitary Siegel–Weil formula, the local doubling zeta integrals and unramified split general-linear doubling. These refer to the models and conventions in the cited implication. The local calculations and matching of the realization are necessary to use it. [Full implication][inputs] · [Doubling specialization][hecke] · [Family matching][link]

Put $r=k+m-1$, $n_j=r+jt$, with $t$ odd, $t\ge r+5$ and $3m<t$. The smaller real signature index at stage $j+1$ is $\lfloor(m-j-1)/2\rfloor$. Projection onto the summand of smaller sign is injective on a totally isotropic subspace, so this bounds the global Witt index. In particular $n_{j+1}-\mathrm{Witt}(Y_{j+1})>n_j$, as required for convergent doubling. The native host also supplies the dyadic splitting used by its local normalizations. At each real place the centered old blocks have $|\lambda|\ge (t+3)/2$, and the new positive block is the string $I_t=((t-1)/2,\ldots,-(t-1)/2)$. Atobe's criterion gives the stated first occurrence in both directions. On ascent the exceptional entries in that criterion would have $0\le\pm\lambda-1<(t-1)/2$; there are none. Removing any hyperbolic plane makes the required nonnegative tower index negative. In the reverse direction $I_t$ is the maximal central string and the lower signatures again fail the index condition. The theta constant-term formula therefore puts the lifted forms in the cuspidal spectrum. The branching on the prescribed real types and the finite level character place each polynomial lift in the span of evaluations of $M_{j+1}$. ## Why the fixed finite test works on every bottom type

On the exceptional integration set the prescribed test has its selected split half, integral frame coordinates and, at the marked halves, an invertible leading $n_j\times n_j$ block after reduction. These blocks belong to the designated block triangular subgroups, and their upper-left reductions across the marked primes belong to the designated common subgroup. If the test and its source translate both have nonzero value on one finite point, comparison of the two leading blocks determines the source change of frame there. Both blocks have integral inverses. Their quotient lies in the same congruence subgroup, since the triangular and common upper-left conditions are subgroup conditions. Unitarity and the self-dual frame give the complementary halves; the exceptional integration set already imposes the unmarked stabilizers. Hence the finite source translation is in the *actual* reference level $K_j$. [Marked test and stabilizer calculation][support]

Outside $K_j$ the finite support is consequently disjoint from its translate. This persists when the theta kernel is expressed in a different Weil model: an indicator of the support on a common finite quotient has a finite Fourier expansion in Heisenberg modulations. The indicator fixes the test and annihilates its disjoint translate, and the isometric intertwiner transports this identity; real polynomial operations commute with it. Thus the tested Weil matrix coefficient vanishes there. On $K_j$ the oscillator coefficient has level character $\chi_j$ and the conjugated automorphic coefficient has $\overline{\chi_j}$; their product is one. For $0\ne u\in M_j$ the exceptional zeta integral is therefore its positive Petersson multiplicity norm squared times the real integral, up to a positive Haar factor. The argument applies with $u$ any vector of the specified level character, including mixtures in a repeated Hecke eigenspace. [Transport of the support calculation][off] · [Cancellation and product measure][finite]

At a real place the native test is obtained by applying a joint-harmonic polynomial to the row oscillator vacuum and pairing with a unit highest vector of the source discrete series. In the covering-group model its integral is a positive Haar and normalization factor times $\|e_v\Phi\|^2/\operatorname{fdeg}(\pi)$; the matched joint-harmonic input makes $e_v\Phi\ne0$. The determinant phases cancel on transferring both matrix coefficients. The product of these nonzero real integrals, with positive vacuum and Haar normalizations, is nonzero. The archimedean estimate in the same construction supplies an integrable majorant at a real parameter strictly to the left of $t/2$. This allows the exceptional integral to be used at the value of the meromorphic global identity. [Covering-group normalization][archmodel] · [Matched polynomial, vector and majorant][arch]

Independently, Ichino's Proposition 7.2 applies at exactly these real places: the source is a discrete series of $U(p,q)$, the target has dimension $n_j+t\ge n_j$, and the stated splitting characters must be used. Nonzero local theta lift is equivalent to nonzero four-variable local doubling form on **polynomials in the Fock model** and representation vectors. Diagonal tests suffice for existential nonvanishing: if all doubly diagonal values vanished, polarization first in the two oscillator slots with the discrete-series vector fixed, then in the two discrete-series slots, would annihilate the whole form. His Lemma 7.1 gives absolute convergence for the doubled sections for $\Re s>-1/2$. The stronger native joint-harmonic test and its recorded majorant above fix the vectors, factors and transport used in this application. [Ichino, §7.1–7.2, Lemma 7.1 and Proposition 7.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.09148#page=29)

## The global value and the adjoint of the specified descent

For a bottom unramified eigenparameter $B$, there are at most $r$ roots at each good $w$, of absolute value at most $(Nw)^{r/2}$. After $j$ ascents, the partial numerator at the doubling variable $s$ is
$$
 L^S(B\otimes\mu_{j+1}^{-1},s+1/2)
 \prod_{i=1}^j\prod_{\ell=0}^{t-1}
 L^S(\mu_i/\mu_{j+1},s+t/2-\ell).
$$
At $s=t/2$ the absolute size of a bottom root times its local power is at most $(Nw)^{-3}$. The logarithmic Euler series converges absolutely in a neighborhood. The remaining arguments are $t,t-1,\ldots,1$. For $\chi=\mu_i/\mu_{j+1}$, the nonzero angular exponent at a common embedding makes $\chi,\chi^2$ nontrivial there, so both character $L$-functions are holomorphic at one. For $\sigma>1$, the inequality $3+4\cos x+\cos 2x\ge0$ in the prime-power logarithms gives $\zeta_E^S(\sigma)^3|L^S(\chi,\sigma)|^4|L^S(\chi^2,\sigma)|\ge1$. A zero of $L^S(\chi,1)$ would contradict the simple pole of $\zeta_E^S$. All other arguments are in absolute convergence. The doubling denominator is nonzero at $t/2>0$. This proves nonvanishing for each parameter reached by ascent, uniformly in the sense of applying the same argument to each word. No quantitative lower bound for these values is used. [Hecke parameter bound][bound] · [Factorization at the actual row ranks][lvalue] · [Norm formula at the tested value][up]

For $u\ne0$ in such a parameter space choose the polynomial $\Phi$ and the source vector $v$ just described. Doubling gives a nonzero scalar-valued upward lift $U_\Phi(u(v))$. Rapid cusp decay permits the exchange of the two theta integrals, identifying its pairing against each evaluation $y(w)$, $y\in M_{j+1}$, with the multiplicity pairing of $d_j y$ with $u$ times the corresponding archimedean coefficient. Thus $d_j^*u=0$ would make the nonzero lift orthogonal to the evaluation span to which it belongs. The polynomial can vary in this argument because it enters the testing identity for the *same* $d_j$. The identity also shows that the lift is an evaluation of $d_j^*u$: decompose the finite multiplicity space into its line and its orthogonal complement and use the Petersson product rule on evaluations. A nonzero evaluation detects the Hecke parameter of this multiplicity map by irreducibility and cyclicity of the archimedean source. Unramified Howe therefore transports the parameter to its common twist with the prescribed added string. [Adjunction][adj] · [Parameter transfer][param] · [Induction in the given metrics][ind]

Induct on the tower. Every nonzero vector of a bottom joint eigenspace has nonzero image under $\mathcal D^*$, with the predicted terminal parameter. Twisting multisets by one common character and adjoining a common string is injective on multisets by cancellation, including repetitions. Distinct joint eigenlabels therefore stay distinct and their images are linearly independent between eigenspaces. The finite-dimensional bottom space is spanned by joint eigenvectors of its commutative adjoint-stable spherical algebra. Hence $\ker\mathcal D^*=0$, and finite-dimensional adjunction gives the asserted surjectivity. [Separation on all bottom types][all] · [Actual reference composite][link]

## Consequence for one word and height entries 1 and 4

With full translated bottom evaluation $J_z:M_0\to H$ in its prescribed conjugation convention, the attainable evaluation space $J_z(\mathcal D M_m)$ equals $H$. For a word $u$, let $P_u:C_u\to H$ be its unrestricted Bessel period and $\Pi_u$ its ramified column projector. A nonzero descended reference pairing now exists **if and only if** $P_u\Pi_u\ne0$. Indeed, choose $\phi$ in the ramified column with $p=P_u\phi\ne0$, take $T\in M_m$ with $J_z\mathcal DT=p$, and its pairing is $\|p\|^2$. The converse follows by evaluation. Equivalently, the finite one-orbit signed test, or the positive trace test, in [the full reference pairing](PERIOD_FULL_REFERENCE_PAIRING.md) suffices with no projection onto an unknown attainable subspace. The proved dimension count gives $\operatorname{rank}\Pi_u=\rho d_u>0$. The logical need for a Bessel estimate remains even for one word: linear maps with $P_u|_{\Pi_u C_u}=0$ are compatible with positive ramified dimension and surjective row descent. The rank estimate on a domain where the complete diagonal bound is independently proved gives the pairing there. [Dimension and full evaluation][bottom] · [Numerical prerequisites of the one-word diagonal test][num]

After this pairing, the normalized two-step seesaw still uses the tied real and finite row **and column** charts, the common finite central translation, the terminal branching and its scalar, and the identified kernel and volume factors. Under these hypotheses the top relation $\lambda p_{\rm top}+\nu c_t A_t=0$ descends to $\lambda b_0+\nu B_t=0$, with $b_0=\|p\|^2$. Thus $p_{\rm top}\ne0$ and $c_tA_tb_0=p_{\rm top}B_t$. Entry 4 also uses the joined good-row and column identifications and their metrics; entry 1 also uses the all-place arithmetic bound on the same integral rows and shifted column lattices and local invertibility of the relative scalar. The descent calculation supplies the preimage term in this chain. Independent one-word nonvanishing before final selection, tied comparison normalizations and the arithmetic hypotheses still have to be supplied on the domain of the height argument. ## A one-sided estimate for the remaining reference pairing

On the tied model with the convergence and size hypotheses of B8.08q, write the normalized Gram operator as $K=\alpha^{-1}P_u^*P_u=I+Z+X$. The nonidentity zero-Gram cells give $Z$, and the nonzero-Gram cells give $X$. Put $d=\dim C_u$, $h=\operatorname{rank}\Pi_u=\rho d$ and $B=kf\log k$. The [zero-Gram estimate](SPECTRAL_DIAGONAL_ZERO_CELL.md) and Hilbert–Schmidt Cauchy–Schwarz give
$$
 \left|\operatorname{tr}(\Pi_u Z)\right|\le\sqrt{hd}\,e^{-B/2}.
$$
Consequently $\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{tr}(\Pi_u X)>-h+\sqrt{hd}\,e^{-B/2}$ suffices for this word. On the actual window $h\ge3d/4$ and $e^{-B/2}\le1/8$ for $k\ge5$ and $f\ge1$. It suffices, for example, to establish the coarse one-sided bound
$$
                \operatorname{Re}\operatorname{tr}(\Pi_u X)\ge -d/2.       \tag{1}
$$
Under (1), $\alpha^{-1}\|P_u\Pi_u\|_{\rm HS}^2=\operatorname{tr}(\Pi_u K)\ge d/8$. For any transported orthonormal marking basis $\{\delta\}$, its projected vectors have squared norms summing to $h$. At least one nonzero projected vector therefore satisfies $\|P_u\Pi_u\delta\|^2/\|\Pi_u\delta\|^2\ge\alpha d/(8h)$. Each such vector uses only the diamond orbit of its own marking. This gives a quantitative input to the surjective descent above. Condition (1) involves only the signed trace from nonzero-Gram terms on a single word's projected column. Absolute convergence allows taking that trace term by term when the stated hypotheses hold. Establishing its lower bound for the actual Bessel terms remains open.
