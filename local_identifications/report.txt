1. LI1 — THE UNRAMIFIED LOCAL RING AND AN EXACT COMPLETION COMPARISON

1.1 The needed form of Hensel's argument.

Let B be a complete DVR, with p a uniformizer and residue field k=F_(p^m).
Choose a generator alpha of k/F_p and let P_0 in F_p[T] be its monic minimal
polynomial of degree m. Finite fields are perfect, so P_0'(alpha)!=0.
Lift P_0 to a monic P in Z_p[T]. For any lift b_0 of alpha to B, the iteration

  b_(r+1)=b_r-P(b_r)/P'(b_r)

is defined, leaves the residue unchanged, and at least doubles the order of
the error P(b_r). Taylor expansion shows the error is a multiple of
P(b_r)^2. The sequence converges to a root beta. The root with that residue
is unique: P(u)-P(v)=(u-v)Q(u,v), and Q(u,v) reduces to P_0'(alpha), a unit,
whenever u and v reduce to alpha.

Every element of B is uniquely a sum sum_(j=0)^(m-1) a_j beta^j with
a_j in Z_p. To obtain the coefficients, express the residue in the basis
1,alpha,...,alpha^(m-1), subtract a lift of that expression, divide by p,
and repeat. The coefficients converge in Z_p. If a nonzero relation existed,
divide its coefficients by their smallest common p-power and reduce modulo p;
this contradicts the residue-field basis. Consequently

  Z_p[T]/(P) -> B,  T |-> beta,

is an isomorphism. Apply the same construction to W(k), where p is a
uniformizer by BC1. This proves B is isomorphic to W(k), with a prescribed
residue-field identification. It proves uniqueness as well: a continuous
Z_p-map between such rings is determined by the residue map, since it must
send beta to the unique root with the prescribed residue.

This proof also works for a map W(k)->S into a complete DVR extension whose
uniformizer need not be p. The same simple root lifts in S, while p-adic
continuity follows from the ramification relation. Only uniqueness will be
needed for such targets.

1.2 Apply this to a global prime.

Let P be a prime of O_E above p, unramified over Z. The completed local ring
B=O_(E_P) is a complete DVR with uniformizer p and finite residue field
O_E/P=F_(p^m), m>=1. The preceding construction gives an embedding

  i:E -> Q_m

whose integer restriction g:O_E->W_m satisfies

  i(x)=g(x),  g mod p is surjective,  ker(g mod p)=P.       (LI1.1)

The maps into fraction fields are understood in the first equality.
This is exactly the source predicate Good(P,i), not merely a field embedding.
It proves the substantive content of exists_wittHom and exists_good without
leaving a formal-smoothness lifting theorem as an unexplained local input.

1.3 Completion from any such Good embedding.

This useful converse does not assume unramifiedness separately. Suppose
j:L->Q_m is an embedding of a number field and its integer restriction has
the three properties (LI1.1) for a prime v of O_L. Then

  x in O_(L,v)  iff  j(x) in W_m.                        (LI1.2)

In one direction write x=a/b, b outside v; j(b) has nonzero residue and is
a unit. Conversely, if x is not in the local DVR, x^-1 is in its maximal
ideal. Write x^-1=a/b with a in v, b outside v. Its image lies in pW_m,
so j(x) cannot be integral. This also proves the assertion at x=0.

O_L is dense in W_m under j. More precisely, for y in W_m and r>=0 one can
find a in O_L with j(a)-y in p^r W_m. Start modulo p using residue surjectivity.
If j(a)-y=p^r b, choose a' with j(a') congruent to b modulo p and replace
a by a-p^r a'. This raises the precision by one.

O_L is also dense in O_(L_v). Indeed the local ring O_(L,v) is dense by the
definition of completion. A denominator b outside v has an inverse modulo
every power v^r: (b)+v^r=O_L, so Bezout gives an inverse represented by an
integer of L. Thus local fractions can be approximated by global integers.
Since p lies in v, the p-adic and v-adic ideal powers are cofinal in the DVR.

For a in O_L and r>=0, (LI1.2) applied to a/p^r gives the exact equivalence

  j(a) in p^rW_m  iff  a in p^r O_(L,v).                 (LI1.3)

The same equivalence holds for differences. Hence the two dense maps induce
identical quotient rings at every precision. Passing to their complete,
separated inverse limits gives a unique ring isomorphism, then its extension
to fraction fields,

  e:Q_m ~= L_v,
  e(j(x))=x for x in L,
  e(W_m)=O_(L_v).                                        (LI1.4)

This spells out exists_equiv_of_dense and exists_completion_equiv. In
particular (LI1.4) carries p to p, so the prime v is unramified and its
integer-valued valuation agrees with v_p on this completion.

2. LI2 — FROBENIUS, RESIDUE DEGREE, AND EMBEDDINGS AT ONE PRIME

2.1 Finite-field calculation and unique lifting.

Every F_p-embedding of F_(p^m) into an algebraic closure is a power of
a->a^p, and the m powers with exponents 0,...,m-1 are distinct. For completeness,
if Frobenius^r were the identity with 0<r<m, the polynomial T^(p^r)-T would
have p^m roots, more than its degree. Frobenius^m is the identity; division
of exponents by m proves that its order is exactly m. There are at most m
embeddings, since a primitive element has degree m, and these m powers
therefore account for all of them.

Two Good embeddings E->Q_m inducing the same prime P consequently differ
by a unique Fr^s, 0<=s<m. Their residue maps differ by that power; their
maps on O_(E,P) are continuous and extend to the completion. Indeed the
maximal ideal maps into pW_m, hence each of its powers maps into the
corresponding p-power. The lift uniqueness from LI1.1 then proves equality
on the completion and on E. Conversely Fr preserves W_m and its maximal
ideal, so applying it does not change the prime. This is the exact content
of Good.exists_pow / Good.ov in the current application.

2.2 Identify the Frobenius and its degree.

If i:E->Q_m is Good, then Fr composed i is another embedding of E into Q_m.
Normality of E/Q implies that

  Fr composed i = i composed sigma_g

for some g in G. One elementary justification is to take a primitive
element of E/Q: its images are roots of the same polynomial, all lying in
i(E), and the two embeddings therefore differ by an automorphism of E.
Reduction modulo p shows sigma_g is arithmetic Frobenius at P. Thus the
supplied Frobenius law gives

  Fr(i(x))=i(sigma_f(x)),
  Fr^s(i(x))=i(sigma_(f^s)(x)).                            (LI2.1)

The residue degree m is exactly n. Since Fr^m=1, (LI2.1) and injectivity
give f^m=1, hence n divides m. Since f^n=1, residue surjectivity in (LI1.1)
gives Frobenius^n=1 on all F_(p^m), hence m divides n. Therefore m=n.
The order of f is n because ord(f)=lcm(2,ord(h)) and n is even.

  P_g'=P_g  iff  g'=g f^s for some integer s.              (LI2.3)

8.3 What simultaneous choice now gives, and what it does not.

For every supplied word u and coefficient prime w with GIdx, LI7 chooses
