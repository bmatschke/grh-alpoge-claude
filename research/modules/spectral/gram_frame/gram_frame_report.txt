# Gram estimates, retained directions, and the mean-slope comparison

## What is closed in this iteration

The replacement section proves the complete finite-dimensional implication from the released full-family **CorBounds** to the required mean-slope inequality. It also shows, in a few lines, how the pairwise **LinkS_at** estimates and the word-count/dimension conditions imply those CorBounds.

The most useful simplification is to apply the frame lemma **before restricting the source spaces**. A subsequent Harder–Narasimhan cut then removes two kinds of directions: the original ramified source loss and the low-slope directions in the retained source. Counting their dimensions together avoids proving near-isotropy a second time after source restriction.

For full-family frame parameters \((5\tau,e_0)\), source loss \(d\), target loss \(\delta\), adjusted source mean at least \(s\), upper width \(u\), and target deficit bound \(H\), the resulting proved inequality is

\[
\mu(C)\ge s-
\frac{4uH/(u+H)+(5\tau+4d+4\sqrt{e_0})H}{1-\delta}.
\tag{GF-direct}
\]

The integration uses the clipped-deficit argument already introduced in v0.1. With the archive's uniform scale bounds it yields

\[
s\le \mu(C)+\frac{4u+6b}{1-\delta}
\le \mu(C)+5u+7b
\qquad(\delta\le 1/7).
\tag{GF-final}
\]

These are proved presentation alternatives derived in this reconstruction. The archive's corresponding route uses restricted-family parameters \(5\tau+3\sqrt d\) and \(2e_0\), followed by a logarithmic source-tail bound; a later interface also allows a \(\log(\#I)\) loss. The absence of that last loss is already supported by the archive's **TheoremFSharp**. No novelty is claimed for these improvements.

The actual arithmetic estimates for the constructed period operators, their common integral realization and heights, and the admissible parameter families remain input theorems. This bridge is not a standalone proof of RH.

## Normalized maps and the common target [GF1, GF6]

The actual analytic map is D.s.Pu u. The source defines

\[
c_u=\frac{\operatorname{vol}N_u^{\rm an}\operatorname{vol}H_u^{\rm an}}
{\prod_\tau \dim\Pi_{u,\tau}},
\qquad
K_{uv}=(c_uc_v)^{-1/2}P_u^*P_v.
\]

For \(c_u>0\), put \(A_u=c_u^{-1/2}P_u\). Then \(A_u^*A_v=K_{uv}\). Positivity is not silently assumed: **weightConst_nonneg** proves nonnegativity; **unfolding_constant_pos_of_diagonal** proves strict positivity once the full dimension is positive and the diagonal error is \(<1\). In the archive's totalized convention, a zero normalizing constant would make \(K_{uu}=0\), giving squared diagonal defect \(n_u\), a contradiction.

**PMat** records \(P_u\) in orthonormal full-source and target bases; **kp_eq** identifies the weighted matrix Gram block, with weight \(\gamma_u=1/c_u\), with the matrix of KD. The optional common complex conjugation used when selecting the representative of an infinite place preserves adjoints, real scalars, norms and all Hilbert–Schmidt estimates.

**BesselId** contains more than pointwise proportionality: it supplies one common target unitary \(V\), and a source unitary and a nonzero scalar for each \(u\). **iotaC** is the isometric inclusion of the retained source into the full source. The corresponding matrices **Vr0** satisfy \(V_u^*V_u=I\). **block_eq** and **blockReal_at_of** show that the retained target block of \(P_uV_u\) agrees with the arithmetic map D.div.Bk u, after one common invertible target coordinate change and separate invertible source changes.

Thus the replacement's precise geometric hypothesis

\[
YB_u^\sigma=P_0A_uJ_uX_u
\]

matches the archive. The nonzero \(c_u^{-1/2}\) and Bessel scalars can be absorbed into the \(X_u\). These are coordinate identifications used to transport subspaces and dimensions; they are **not required to preserve the lattice metric**. No height is recomputed after such a coordinate change. The original arithmetic map and its original height bound remain the ones used in the slope inequality.

## From pairwise estimates to full-frame isotropy [GF2–GF4]

After increasing \(\eta_0,\eta_1\) to their maxima with zero, the input is

\[
\|A_i^*A_j\|_{\rm HS}^2\le (1+\eta_0)n_in_j/D_*
\quad(i\ne j),\qquad
\|A_i^*A_i-I\|_{\rm HS}^2\le e_0n_i,
\]
\[
D\le (1+\eta_1)D_*.
\]

The archive only assumes upper bounds for \(\eta_0,\eta_1\), not bounds for their absolute values. Monotone enlargement is legitimate; **matKF_of** explicitly does it for \(\eta_0\).

Let \(a=\sqrt{e_0}\), \(Q=\sum A_iA_i^*\), \(M=\sum n_i\), \(t=\operatorname{tr}Q\). Then

\[
t=\sum_i\operatorname{tr}(A_i^*A_i)\ge(1-a)M,
\]
\[
\operatorname{tr}(Q^2)=\sum_{i,j}\|A_i^*A_j\|_{\rm HS}^2
\le(1+\eta_0)M^2/D_*+(1+a)^2M.
\]

The diagonal estimate in the second line is the triangle inequality
\(\|A_i^*A_i\|_{\rm HS}\le\sqrt{n_i}+\|A_i^*A_i-I\|_{\rm HS}\).

The balanced dimensions and many words are used in a specific way:

\[
n_i\ge \frac{D_*}{C_0\Gamma^2},\qquad
\#I\ge \frac{C_0\Gamma^2}{\tau}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad M\ge D_*/\tau.
\]

The large parameter \(\Gamma\) cancels. No constants depending on the number of words enter the bridge.

For \(0<\tau\le1/4\) and \(0\le a,\eta_0,\eta_1\le\tau/2\), put

\[
N_\tau=(1+\tau/2)\{1+\tau/2+\tau(1+\tau/2)^2\}.
\]

It follows that

\[
\frac{D\operatorname{tr}(Q^2)}{t^2}
\le\frac{N_\tau}{(1-\tau/2)^2}\le1+5\tau.
\]

The final polynomial inequality is exact:

\[
(1+5\tau)(1-\tau/2)^2-N_\tau
=\frac{\tau(16-52\tau+4\tau^2-\tau^3)}8\ge0.
\]

For \(0\le\tau\le1/4\), \(16-52\tau\ge3\) and \(4\tau^2-\tau^3\ge0\). The full-frame diagonal defect is \(e_0\le1/64\). This supplies both hypotheses of the previously proved full-frame lemma.

## The direct treatment of retained directions [GF5, GF8]

For each threshold \(v\), transport the adjusted source HN step with slopes \(\ge s-v\) into \(V_i\), and then through \(J_i\) into \(\mathcal V_i\). Its total full-source codimension is

\[
(M-R)+Rq(v).
\]

The fraction is therefore at most \(d+q(v)\). The full frame gives a missing-target dimension at most

\[
\{5\tau+4d+4\sqrt{e_0}+4q(v)\}D.
\]

For any subspace \(S\subseteq\mathcal H\), projection to \(\mathcal H_0\) gives
\[
\dim P_0S\ge\dim S-(D-D_0),
\]
and hence \(\operatorname{codim}_{\mathcal H_0}P_0S\le\operatorname{codim}_{\mathcal H}S\). The common-map coordinate relation and the usual nonzero-morphism slope inequality imply that these projected images lie in the target HN step. This proves

\[
p(v)\le\frac{5\tau+4d+4\sqrt{e_0}+4q(v)}{1-\delta}.
\]

Only invertibility, not isometry, is used for \(X_i,Y\). The mean condition is relative to the retained total dimension \(R\), not \(M\).

For \(H+u>0\), the pointwise clipped inequality

\[
\min\{H,(s-\lambda)_+\}
\le \frac{H(s+u-\lambda)}{H+u}
\quad(\lambda\le s+u)
\]

has three immediate cases: for \(\lambda\le s-H\) the right-hand side is at least \(H\); for \(s-H\le\lambda\le s\), subtracting the left-hand side leaves \(u(H-s+\lambda)/(H+u)\ge0\); above \(s\), the left-hand side is zero. Its rank average is at most \(uH/(u+H)\). Finite layer-cake integration proves (GF-direct). If \(H=0\), the minimum target slope already gives the conclusion.

A slightly stronger, usually unnecessary variant keeps the exact retained fraction \(r=R/M\):

\[
\mu(C)\ge s-\frac{4r\,uH/(u+H)+\{5\tau+4(1-r)+4\sqrt{e_0}\}H}{1-\delta}.
\]

The simpler displayed result is preferable for the main reconstruction.

## Optional restricted-frame theorem [GF9, independently derived]

The direct proof makes this theorem unnecessary for the main exposition. It nevertheless answers exactly what can be concluded about the Gram operator of the restricted family itself.

Assume the full pairwise hypotheses, \(M\ge D_*/\tau\), \(0<\tau\le1/20\), \(0\le\eta_0,\eta_1,a\le\tau/2\), and isometries \(J_i:V_i\to\mathcal V_i\) with \(R\ge(1-d)M\), \(0\le d\le1/4\). Write
\(\widetilde A_i=A_iJ_i\), \(\widetilde Q=\sum\widetilde A_i\widetilde A_i^*\).
Then

\[
\sum_i\|\widetilde A_i^*\widetilde A_i-I\|_{\rm HS}^2
\le \frac{e_0}{1-d}\,R,\qquad
\frac{D\operatorname{tr}(\widetilde Q^2)}
{\operatorname{tr}(\widetilde Q)^2}
\le 1+5\tau+4d.
\tag{GF-ret}
\]

Proof. Orthogonal compression contracts Hilbert–Schmidt norm, so the first sum is at most \(e_0M\). Cauchy–Schwarz then gives

\[
\operatorname{tr}\widetilde Q
\ge R-\sqrt{R e_0 M}
\ge(1-d-a)M>0.
\]

Also \(0\le\widetilde Q\le Q\). Consequently
\(\operatorname{tr}(\widetilde Q^2)\le\operatorname{tr}(Q^2)\).
This uses monotonicity of the **trace of the square** for positive operators, not operator monotonicity of squaring: writing \(Q=\widetilde Q+T\), \(T\ge0\), the difference is
\(2\operatorname{tr}(\widetilde QT)+\operatorname{tr}(T^2)\ge0\).

Therefore the trace ratio is at most \(N_\tau/(1-\tau/2-d)^2\).
For \(b_\tau=1-\tau/2\),

\[
\frac{N_\tau}{(b_\tau-d)^2}
-\frac{N_\tau}{b_\tau^2}
=d\,N_\tau\frac{2b_\tau-d}{b_\tau^2(b_\tau-d)^2}.
\]

The coefficient on the right increases with \(\tau\) and \(d\) on the rectangle in question. Indeed \(N_\tau\) increases with \(\tau\), \(b_\tau\) decreases, and
\[
F(b,d)=\frac{2b-d}{b^2(b-d)^2}
\]
decreases with \(b\) and increases with \(d\); for the first assertion, \(F(b,d)=((b-d)^{-2}-b^{-2})/d\), and for the second,
\(\partial_dF=(3b-d)/(b^2(b-d)^3)>0\).
At \((\tau,d)=(1/20,1/4)\), the coefficient is
\(28577/7605<4\). Combining with the preceding full-family bound proves (GF-ret).

This improves the archive's parameters \(5\tau+3\sqrt d,2e_0\) to \(5\tau+4d,e_0/(1-d)\). For \(0\le d\le1/4\), \(4d\le3\sqrt d\). It has not been implemented or replayed in Lean. The direct slope proof is slightly sharper still, since it retains \(\sqrt{e_0}\) instead of \(\sqrt{e_0/(1-d)}\).

## Uniformity in the actual family [GF7]

Fix the positive parameters \(\varepsilon,C_H,e_z\). **LinkS**, **ChosenProviso**, and **ChosenCode** assert eventual bounds for **every auxiliary index at each \(x\)**. Their thresholds may depend on the fixed family and fixed parameters; they do not depend on that auxiliary index.

Put \(E=e^{-x^{\varepsilon/8}}\), \(\tau=x^{-C_H}\). The exact retained rank is

\[
r_u=n_u\left(1-\frac1{T_u-1}\right)^f.
\]

For \(T_u\ge b^{m/2}\ge b\ge3\) and \(m\ge2\),
\[
1-r_u/n_u\le \frac f{T_u-1}\le\frac{2f}{b}.
\]
The source has \(b=e^{x^\varepsilon}\) and \(f\le x^5\). Thus, eventually,
\[
2f/b\le E^2.
\]
This is **ramShareF_of**, whose bound is stated uniformly for all auxiliary indices and words.

We can choose one \(d=E^2\) for every auxiliary datum at the same \(x\), and one \(e_0\)-bound \(E^2\). For large \(x\), \(E\le\min(1,\tau/8)\), so
\[
5\tau+4d+4\sqrt{e_0}
\le5\tau+4E^2+4E
\le6\tau.
\]
Independently, **LinkIRR** gives \(\delta\le e^{-x^{e_z/7}}\le1/7\) eventually. These choices are all uniform. **LinkDIV_at** bounds the target deficit \(H\) by \(x^{C_H}b\), after the target minimum-slope duality is used. This proves (GF-final).

The archive itself uses common bounds \(d=E^2\) and \(e_B=2E^2\), together with \(Ex^{C_H}\le1/18\), to obtain \((\eta_B+4\sqrt{e_B})x^{C_H}\le6\). Our route changes no essential scale or quantifier.

## Concrete downstream error replacement [GF8]

Use the parent's source notation from LevelOfCongruence.lean:
\[
L_u=\operatorname{wmean}(d,\mathrm{Lo5}),\qquad
U_u=K_3+L_u,\qquad
F=(\bar\eta+4\sqrt{\bar e})x^{C_H}b.
\]
For the direct route, replace \(F\) by
\[
F_{\rm direct}=(5\tau+4d+4\sqrt{e_0})x^{C_H}b\le6b.
\]

The already reconstructed local inequality and the exact cancellation give
\[
\mathrm{Ga}(u)\le\mathrm{Main}(u)+K_0^{(5)}+L_u+
\frac{4U_u+F_{\rm direct}}{1-\bar\delta}.
\tag{GF-Ga}
\]

Thus the old \(\log(\#\mathrm{Words})\) term is absent; the old
\[
\frac{4\log(1+x^{C_H})(U_u+b)}{1-\bar\delta}
\]
is replaced by \(4U_u/(1-\bar\delta)\).
The \(H\le x^{C_H}b\) input remains necessary for \(F_{\rm direct}\).

Since \(L_u\le c_{\rm Sl1}\mathrm{IO}(u)+K_7^{(5)}\), \(\bar\delta\le1/7\) gives the explicit convenient bound
\[
\mathrm{Ga}(u)-\mathrm{Main}(u)
\le K_0^{(5)}
+\frac{17}{3}\{c_{\rm Sl1}\mathrm{IO}(u)+K_7^{(5)}\}
+\frac{14}{3}K_3+7b.
\]

Equivalently, in the source's **ErrX5at** and **errorTermWith**, retain the first four arithmetic terms, the LoP term and MLo5, delete the logarithm of the number of words, and replace the last frame contribution by
\[
\frac{4\,\mathrm{UBar5}+6b}{1-\bar\delta}.
\]
This is a **derived replacement**, not a statement that the released definitions have those values.

It removes an outer logarithmic amplification of the already bounded source-width terms. It does not remove logarithms already inside \(K_3,K_7^{(5)},K_0^{(5)}\), or any local divisibility/height/congruence input. No reduction of the final \(e_D,e_L\) or the \(1/2+(e_D+e_L+7)\varepsilon\) exponent is claimed without redoing the remaining scalar estimates.
