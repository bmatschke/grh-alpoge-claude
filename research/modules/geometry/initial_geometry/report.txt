IG1 — PARTIAL RAY ZETA FROM A SHIFTED PLANE LATTICE

The ray group is identified with the source's quotient of the prime-to-M
ideal monoid. Its finiteness follows from ordinary ideal-class finiteness
and the finite unit group of O/MO. This matters because the source permits
an arbitrary RayData satisfying its quotient clauses, not a single
preferred presentation of that quotient.

For a class c, choose an integral ideal a in c^{-1}, prime to M, and an
element alpha_0 in a congruent to one modulo M. Coprimality gives
a intersect MO = Ma. The coset alpha_0+Ma therefore maps to the integral
ideals of class c by alpha -> (alpha)/a. Its multiplicity is exactly the
number w_M of units congruent to one modulo M. It is not replaced by the
number of all units. Those units act freely; every fiber consists of one
orbit. The lattice covolume is M^2 N(a) A_0, so the factor N(a)^s in the
Dirichlet series cancels the a-dependence of the residue at s=1.

The analytic proof is explicit. Disjoint small disks give an O(R^2)
lattice-point count, which proves absolute and locally uniform
convergence for Re(s)>1. Fourier expansion of a periodized Gaussian gives
the shifted Poisson formula with the stated dual lattice and phase.
Both Fourier series converge absolutely. The shifted lattice excludes
zero, yielding exponential decay at infinity; after subtracting 1/(At),
the Poisson formula gives exponential decay at zero. Splitting the
Mellin integral at one gives an entire remainder. It follows that each
partial zeta is meromorphic on the entire complex plane with its sole
possible pole at one and the same positive residue. The reciprocal
Gamma factor gives its zero at -1.

This is stronger information than continuation on a short real
interval. IG2 explicitly uses that full continuation. No nonvanishing
theorem or prime-in-class conclusion is used to prove it.

IG2 — NONVANISHING AND PRIME NORMS IN EVERY CLASS

Finite character orthogonality makes nonprincipal ray L-functions
entire. If one vanished at one, the product of all ray L-functions would
be entire: that zero cancels the only pole of the principal factor.
At a prime ideal of class order d, the product Euler factor is
(1-N(P)^(-ds))^(-h/d), so its Dirichlet coefficients are nonnegative and
its constant coefficient is one.

The positivity argument specifies the analytic domain instead of
appealing loosely to an abscissa theorem. Fix sigma_0>1. Absolute
convergence permits termwise derivatives there; logarithmic powers are
bounded by an arbitrarily small positive power of n. For every real
sigma<sigma_0, the entire Taylor expansion at sigma_0 has nonnegative
terms after the derivative signs cancel the signs of sigma-sigma_0.
Tonelli therefore identifies the Taylor sum with the Dirichlet series
at sigma and makes it at least one. At sigma=-1 the principal factor
vanishes, a contradiction. No unproved lower bound on an abscissa is
being supplied implicitly.

Nonvanishing permits an analytic logarithm in a disk about one. Its
difference from the Euler logarithm on the real interval is a constant
multiple of 2 pi i. Terms of degree at least two in the Euler logarithm
are uniformly bounded by a convergent ideal-norm square sum. Character
orthogonality consequently gives -(1/h)log(s-1)+O(1) in each class.
In a quadratic field a prime-ideal norm which is not a rational prime
is a square p^2. Their contribution is bounded, as is that of every
bounded collection of primes. Thus arbitrarily large rational-prime
norms occur in every ray class. The theorem is qualitative.

IG3 — ACTUAL FACTORS AND ORDINARY PROGRESSION PRIMES

The source needs an algebraic integer pi, its quadratic-subfield
membership, its norm, and a congruence in the ring of integers. A prime
ideal alone would not suffice. The ray equality xP=y(a), with x,y both
one modulo M, makes ya=x pi with pi integral. Cancellation gives
P=(pi); the equation pi-a=(y-1)a-(x-1)pi gives the literal congruence.
The imaginary-quadratic norm is positive. Mapping this exact element
into the host field gives pi*bar(pi)=p. For rational a, the quotient
(p-a^2)/M is both rational and integral, so the rational prime is in the
required square residue class. M=1 is handled by modulus two.

Some host choices require arbitrary reduced rational residue classes.
They are deduced from IG2 rather than assumed as an extra Dirichlet
theorem. Choose a prime ell=3 mod 4 outside M by the elementary Euclid
argument. The quadratic field Q(sqrt(-ell)) has odd discriminant -ell
and is unramified at every prime of M. The generic local norm argument
of DR1 is onto on units for both the split and unramified quadratic
algebras, including residue characteristic two. CRT gives an integral
a with norm congruent to the desired residue and prime to M. IG2 and
the factor argument then give arbitrarily large rational prime norms
in that residue. The temporary quadratic field is auxiliary; it is
not substituted for the final host field.

IG5 — AUXILIARY PRIMES AND BB DISJOINTNESS

After choosing g0 and its integral factor, form
P=g0 product_{1<=d<=r}(g0^d-1), M=(cP)^(2r+1).
The next actual norm factor is one plus M modulo M^2, so
g1=(1+M)^2 modulo M^2. Every prime in BB(g0) divides P. The explicit
factorization of g1^d-1 and congruence of the residual factor to 2d
bound each relevant valuation strictly below v_l(M)+2r+1. Consequently
l^(4 v_l(g1^d-1)) <= M^8 <= g1 <= g1^d, excluding its exceptional
membership. The large lower bound also excludes equality with g1.
The distinguished primes are chosen afterwards, one modulo 15c and
larger than all previously required primes. Their complete splitting
later follows from the actual conductor congruence.

This is a direct verification of the exceptional set, not a generic
assertion that large primes have disjoint bad sets. In particular the
squared congruence modulus and the exponent 2r+1 are retained.

IG9 — POSITIVE INTEGER SIZE FROM THE DEFINING MASS

The column index i_g is an integer because its denominator is the
order of the upper unitriangular subgroup of GL_k(F_g). A finite
unitary lattice automorphism group reduces injectively at a residue-
degree-one prime if g>[E:Q]k+1. The prime-to-g part of any finite
congruence kernel vanishes by binomial induction: the finite-order
exponent is a local unit, so the proof allows arbitrary ramification.
Cauchy's theorem and the degree of a primitive gth root exclude
g-torsion entirely. Hence its order divides i_g, not merely |GL_k|.

The finite genus is justified by the positive integral trace lattice:
fixed rank and determinant yield bounded reduced Gram matrices.
Multiplication by an integral basis of O_E has bounded operator norm
in those matrices, leaving finitely many integral multiplication
matrices. Trace duality recovers the Hermitian form. Thus the
defining genus sum is finite and nonempty, and mass*i_g is a positive
integer. This argument uses no analytic Siegel mass ratio formula.

The HC dimension is an integer by the determinant of the integer-
valued binomial polynomials evaluated on the common integral shift
of the half-integral tuple. Positivity follows from its ordered gaps.
The factorization with one i_g0 absorbed into the mass proves the
literal duStarOf_nat expression. No equality of integral Weyl and
dual-Weyl lattices is inferred from this numerical argument.

PROVENANCE AND REPRODUCTION

Run python initial_geometry/build_provenance.py --repo /absolute/path/to/
the/pinned/checkout. The builder checks HEAD, obtains each source from
git show PIN:path, compares available checkout bytes, and hashes exact
one-indexed inclusive declaration ranges without text normalization.
It hashes every IG paragraph and this report, records actual record
field inventories, and writes both the source manifest and human
locators. Missing sparse files are read directly from Git. It neither
fetches nor mutates the checkout. Review attachments require the
review records to contain the exact current paragraph hashes.

The source_relation fields distinguish the direct human arguments
from their Lean counterparts. In particular the elementary entire-
product positivity argument and the reduction of rational progressions
to imaginary-quadratic norms are explicit human simplifications, not
claims that identical prose already appears in the source. Residual
targets include their genuine producer ranges and exact source hashes.
