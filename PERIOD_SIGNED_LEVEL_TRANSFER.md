# Transferring the signed finite determinant from sharp to base level

Equation (10) of [the relative determinant calculation](PERIOD_RELATIVE_DETERMINANTS.md) is a sufficient arithmetic estimate on the sharp row. On the original linked range, it is equivalent up to the allowed error to the same estimate on the **base** row. This holds with or without the conjugate-embedding correction, choosing the same convention on both sides. The transfer uses the [fitted maximum-slope bound](PERIOD_SELECTED_BASE_FUNCTIONAL.md) and the standing constructed-row and theta comparisons of the determinant calculation. Individual relative eigenvalues can have unbounded finite valuations. A lower bound on a determinant for each finite-group constituent suffices.

## Determinants of the multiplicity spaces

Let $G$ be the finite row group, $V$ its rational principal good target, and $e_i=e_{\psi_i}$ the two subgroup idempotents. The original level identifications give $E_i=e_i V$. All quotient maps here are the maps at the actual tuples before taking any numerical estimates. On the principal target write $S_t$ for the rational Hermitian form transported from the quotient of the test form at the distinguished embedding. At an arbitrary embedding $\sigma$ let $T_{0,\sigma}$ be the positive Hermitian form obtained from the actual local quotient of the reference test metric and transported to $V_\sigma$. The selector, finite action, and quotient maps are compatible across the levels. The forms at level $i$ are the restrictions to $e_iV$ divided by $|P_i|$. Indeed the principal and level test forms are the same class sums with this constant denominator at level $i$. The idempotent is orthogonal on the principal Hilbert space, its kernel cut is stable under $G$, and the local minimal lift of a vector in the $e_i$ summand is also in that summand. Thus taking the quotient commutes with taking this character summand, including at the reference tuple for every embedding. The finite action on the transported quotient is isometric for each of these forms. [Level realization and finite action](PERIOD_SHARP_ROW_TRANSFER.md#the-representation-and-the-glued-lattices) · [Level and good test metrics and lattices][forms] · [Stable good kernels and common transitions](continuation_v74/bundle.tex#L3-L93)

Decompose $V=\bigoplus_\chi U_\chi\otimes W_\chi$ over the occurring good characters and put $d_\chi=\dim W_\chi>0$. These models can be taken over the given coefficient field. In fact $e_\chi$ has coefficients $\chi(1)\chi(g^{-1})/|G|$ in the conjugation-compatible subfield, and the base Whittaker idempotent $e_0$ has rank one on the irreducible complex representation of character $\chi$. The left ideal $K[G] e_\chi e_0$ therefore affords that absolutely irreducible representation over $K$, and already over the subfield generated by the character and level-character values. All are sums or products of roots of unity. A basis of independent columns gives matrices in that subfield. Equip $U_\chi$ with a $G$-invariant Hermitian form $h_\chi$ by summing the standard dot form over $G$ in these coordinates. Every coefficient embedding gives a positive form, and conjugation in the field agrees with the complex adjoint on these coefficients. Write $n_{i\chi}=\dim e_iU_\chi$. In particular $n_{0\chi}=1$ and $D_i=\sum_\chi n_{i\chi}d_\chi$. [Base ranks and rational projectors](continuation_v73/reading.tex#L3-L59) · [Sharp character multiplicities](PERIOD_CHARACTER_SLOPES.md#the-dual-mean-bound-including-its-normalization)

By Schur's lemma the restriction of $S_t$ to this constituent is $h_\chi\otimes B_{\chi,t}$ for a nonsingular Hermitian form on $W_\chi$, with distinct constituents orthogonal. This follows first at the distinguished embedding, then over $K$ by injectivity, or directly by the commuting matrix algebra. Likewise the restriction of $T_{0,\sigma}$ has the form $h_{\chi,\sigma}\otimes B^{\rm loc}_{\chi,\sigma}$. Use fixed rational bases on the multiplicity spaces. Put
$$
 s_{\chi,t}=\det B_{\chi,t_0}/\det B_{\chi,t},\qquad
 I_\chi=\prod_{w\in{\rm Tw}}\mathfrak p_w^{v_w(s_{\chi,t_w})}.
$$
At either level the $h_\chi|_{e_iU_\chi}$ and $|P_i|$ determinants cancel in the tuple ratio. Hence there is an equality of fractional ideals, with no base-to-sharp lattice index,
$$
                  I_i=\prod_\chi I_\chi^{\,n_{i\chi}}.                 \tag{1}
$$
This uses the rational row comparison at serving tuples. A scalar unit in that comparison leaves (1) unchanged at its serving place.

The embedding correction has the same multiplicities. On the reference base-level $\chi$ cut, define
$$
 E_\chi=[K:\mathbb Q]^{-1}\sum_\sigma
       \log\frac{|\sigma(\det B_{\chi,t_0})|}
                      {\det B^{\rm loc}_{\chi,\sigma}}\ \ge0 .
$$
For the nonnegative sign, this is precisely the reference quotient defect on that cut: the local metric is the actual quotient and at the distinguished place the rational metric is its algebraic Gram. Apply the exterior Cauchy–Schwarz proof of the determinant identity to this restricted surjection. Equivalently the $e_0U_\chi$ Gram factor cancels between the two metrics. For the general level, take a basis of $e_iU_\chi$ in the same conjugation-compatible subfield. The absolute determinant of its rational Gram at $\sigma$ equals the positive determinant of its local Gram. The subgroup-order normalizations cancel as well. Taking determinant logarithms gives
$$
                        E_{Q,i}=\sum_\chi n_{i\chi} E_\chi .           \tag{2}
$$
The embedding average is independent of the multiplicity bases because the embeddings $\sigma$ and $a\mapsto\overline{\sigma(\bar a)}$ permute. This proof concerns the reference quotient only in (2). The metrics used to define $I_\chi$ use all the serving tuples.

## A lower bound uniform over the characters

Write $A_\chi$ for the base reference log-eigenvalue contribution on the $\chi$ summand, normalized by $d_\chi$ as in (6) of the determinant calculation. The finite action preserves the primitive Hecke parts and their row metrics. If $m_{\alpha,\chi}$ is the multiplicity of $\chi$ in the principal part $\alpha$, the base block has dimension $m_{\alpha,\chi}$ and the level-$i$ block dimension there is $n_{i\chi}m_{\alpha,\chi}$. Since the reference scalar is shared across the levels, $A_i=\sum_\chi n_{i\chi}d_\chi A_\chi/D_i$.

There is a full glued Hermitian lattice $R_\chi\subset R_0$ in the base $\chi$ summand. At each serving tuple take the integral class forms cut by the single central projector $e_\chi$ and base subgroup projector $e_0$, and apply the original quotient map. These are integral class forms on the original good cut, hence their local images lie in the original local row. They span the base $\chi$ target by equivariance and surjectivity. They define the desired sublattice by gluing at the finite set of changes. At all infinite places use the restricted original row metric. Taking this smaller lattice does not require equality with the saturated intersection.

The test-lattice dual bound applies directly to the singleton character catalogue: its only character condition is absolute irreducibility, and the constructed class block has denominator one before applying any character restriction. Thus $|G|M_{\chi,t}^\sharp\subseteq M_{\chi,t}$. The orthogonal quotient sends the same containment to each quotient lattice by lifting a dual vector into the test dual and projecting the multiple back. Its normalized Gram term satisfies $Q_\chi\le\log|G|$ by the semilocal inverse-Gram determinant, uniformly for the actual varying tuples. [Test lattice for an arbitrary retained character set][test] · [Constructed denominator one][one] · [Quotient Gram estimate](PERIOD_RELATIVE_DETERMINANTS.md#the-gram-bound-at-the-actual-sharp-level)

Define the signed number
$$
 Z_\chi^{(a)}=A_\chi+\frac{\log N_K I_\chi}{d_\chi[K:\mathbb Q]}
                      -a E_\chi/d_\chi,\qquad a\in\{0,1\}.
$$
The exact determinant-line calculation on this base $\chi$ quotient gives
$\mu(R_\chi^\vee)-q_\kappa=Z_\chi^{(1)}+Q_\chi$. The frame scalar of the base metric is the same $q_\kappa$ on each positive-rank summand. Since $R_\chi\subseteq R_0$ with its restricted metrics, its slope is at most $\mu_{\max}(R_0)$, also when it is unsaturated. The [fitted estimate](PERIOD_SELECTED_BASE_FUNCTIONAL.md) proves the following lower bound simultaneously for all occurring characters and both choices of $a$:
$$
              Z_\chi^{(a)}\ \ge\ -B,\qquad
              B=x^{A_R}b+\log|G|.                                   \tag{3}
$$
Here $Z_\chi^{(0)}\ge Z_\chi^{(1)}$ follows from the nonnegative defect. The lower bound applies to the sum of signed finite valuations and the reference term on a constituent. Applying an absolute value place by place would lose the cancellations in (3).

## Transfer of the signed inequality and a base-only arithmetic test

Set $Z_i^{(a)}=A_i+\log N_K I_i/(D_i[K:\mathbb Q])-a E_{Q,i}/D_i$. Equations (1), (2) and the common scalar rule give
$$
 Z_i^{(a)}=\sum_\chi p_{i\chi} Z_\chi^{(a)},\qquad
               p_{i\chi}=n_{i\chi}d_\chi/D_i .
$$
On the selected family the sharp multiplicities lie in $[(1-\delta)\bar n,(1+\delta)\bar n]$, with $\delta=8fr/g\le1/3$, $g=\min(g_0,g_1)$ and a common $\bar n>0$. Each ratio $p_{1\chi}/p_{0\chi}$ and its inverse is at most $1+\theta$, $\theta=2\delta/(1-\delta)$. Since $Z_\chi^{(a)}+B\ge0$, for either ordering of the two levels $i,i'$ one has the pointwise inequality
$$
 0\le Z_i^{(a)}+B\le(1+\theta)(Z_{i'}^{(a)}+B).                    \tag{4}
$$
The numerical target is $T=\sum_{j,\rho}\beta_jw_{j\rho}\log\Omega+2F_*$ with error unit $mL(B_0+N_c)$. On the original window $T$, that error unit, and $B$ have upper bounds of the form $x^{C'}b$ for fixed family parameters. The bound on $2F_*$ needed here is only an upper bound, supplied by the original prime sum. Since $g\ge(kb)^8$, the extra $\theta(Z_{i'}^{(a)}+B)$ is $o(1)$ whenever $Z_{i'}^{(a)}$ satisfies the upper target. Consequently the existence of a uniform upper bound $Z_i^{(a)}\le T+C mL(B_0+N_c)$ is equivalent at the two levels. The same cutoff works for all words and indices after increasing the single family threshold. Using (9) and the hook reduction in the [sharp calculation](PERIOD_RELATIVE_DETERMINANTS.md#the-remaining-arithmetic-inequality-in-terms-of-hook-lengths-and-one-ideal) proves the same equivalence for equation (10). All the actual sharp quotient Gram terms then fit the budget by (8). [Level spread and target size](https://github.com/rh-lean-roadmap/rh-lean-roadmap/blob/02a95b4c62db610642af5f278ee5d0d6df87b348/docs/src/book/main.tex#L32985-L33008)

In particular one may work entirely with the actual *base layer maps* at the served primes. Write $q_{jw}=[T_{j,t_w}:T_{j,t_w}^\sharp]_w$ and let $i_{jw}$ be the paired index of their images in $M_j$ as in B8.38b. The source product formula in each base layer and the identity for $v_w(s_{0,t})$ give the equality
$$
        Z_0^{(0)}=\frac1{D_0}\sum_j\operatorname{lcv}(M_j)
          +\frac1{D_0}\sum_{w\in{\rm Tw},\,j}\ell_w(i_{jw}+q_{jw}).
                                                                         \tag{5}
$$
Indeed the reference base-layer log scalar contributes $\operatorname{lcv}(M_j)$ plus the sum of its two base image indices and its base pairing index at every place. The relative determinant contributes the corresponding served terms minus the base terms at twisted places. The paired base contribution is zero at unserved places by the selected source conditions. Thus all reference terms cancel with their signs. [Local indices and product formula][divisor] · [Exact off-twist condition][outside]

A sufficient open inequality is that the right side of (5), **after** subtracting $T$, is $O(mL(B_0+N_c))$ from above. The version retaining the deficit subtracts a further $E_{Q,0}/D_0$ before estimation. A family-uniform signed sum in (5) suffices even when the separate positive-part, per-layer, or pointwise bounds fail. The [existing geometric route](PERIOD_REFERENCE_WORD_COVOLUME.md) bounds its first term by the PEL covolume comparison and its other terms by the two-run projection and cut estimates under their stated hypotheses. Equations (1)–(5) transfer such a bound to the sharp determinant itself with the fitted hypotheses and no estimate on the heights of individual type scalars. They leave the required upper bound for (5) open on the full family.
