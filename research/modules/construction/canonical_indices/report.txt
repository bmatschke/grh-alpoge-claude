Conventions and quantifiers
===========================

O denotes O_{E,P}, the localization of the number-field ring of integers at a
nonzero prime P. Unless a claim expressly weakens it, DegOneUnram means q=Norm(P)
is a rational prime and q is not in P^2. Consequently q is an O-uniformizer and
the residue field is F_q. K has characteristic zero. Rational local bases over
O are used, not global O_E-bases of generally nonfree lattices.

The target rank at step j is n_j=k+j(t+1), its tail length is c_j=m-1-j, and
the source at the preceding step has one more tail column. The group R_j has an
arbitrary invertible X_j block, a unipotent flag action on the tail, and fixes
its first tail vector when that vector exists. It is not described incorrectly
as a wholly unipotent group. Its kernel N_j on X_j is unipotent. The scalar
a(r)=n1Arg(r) is the sum of adjacent tail entries and is additive on R_j.

At a twist prime the basic lattice is O^Y. At a type prime it is
Lambda_j=O^X plus sum_a q^(a+1) O e_a. All test functions, characters and residue
flags use these actual coordinates. Nonzero unfolding always means the source
predicate FiniteSumNonzeroLargeDepth: an eventual nonzero finite box-sum function.
It does not mean an unspecified convergent analytic Eisenstein integral.

CI1 — Finite quotients and affine changes
=========================================

Successively reducing modulo q shows that integers 0,...,q^s-1 represent O/q^s O
uniquely. Uniqueness uses the elementary q-adic valuation of a nonzero rational
integer; no global approximation theorem is hidden in this step. Scaling gives
the boxes {0,...,q^(A+B)-1}/q^A for q^-A O/q^B O.

For f supported in q^-A0 O^d and invariant by q^B0 O^d, enlarging A introduces
only zero summands, while enlarging B repeats each old value by the corresponding
power of q. The q^-Bd normalization exactly cancels that repetition. Therefore
the stabilized sum is an exact equality, not merely invariance of nonvanishing.

For an arbitrary invertible affine coordinate change, use a common sublattice
of L and T(L). Every quotient sum is multiplied by a positive finite lattice
index. These integers stay nonzero in characteristic zero; translations permute
quotient support. Uniform denominator bounds follow from the finitely many
entries of T,T^-1 and the translation vector. This proves affine nonvanishing
transport without a determinant-one premise and without treating an analytic
Jacobian formula as a black box.

CI2 — Full-period-group rigidity
=================================

Suppose f(Tv+b)=chi f(v), f is L-periodic and has finite nonzero support modulo
L, and its quotient sum is nonzero. Surjectivity of the affine map and f nonzero
force chi nonzero. Its full period group M is preserved by T in both directions.
Choose v0 with f(v0) nonzero: M/L injects into the finite nonzero support modulo
L through m -> v0+m. Hence [M:L] is finite and the L-sum equals [M:L] times the
M-sum. The latter remains nonzero. The affine map permutes M-cosets in support,
so summing the eigenvalue identity gives S=chi S, hence chi=1.

This is the essential projected-kernel argument. It would be invalid merely to
assume T preserves the initially displayed period lattice; that assumption is
not needed or made. The full period group repairs precisely that issue.

CI3 — Actual finite admitted cosets
===================================

Admission says h^-1 Lambda subset Lambda and q^j h Lambda subset Lambda. Color
h by q^j h in the fixed Lambda-basis modulo q^(j+1). There are finitely many
colors because the quotient ring is finite. Equal colors give
(h'-h)Lambda subset q Lambda. Applying h^-1, and independently h'^-1, proves
that h^-1h' and its inverse are integral and congruent to one modulo q. Thus
they belong to the principal q-congruence subgroup, which lies in the actual
J_Q and J_Gsym flag/vector levels. Equal colors lie in one right coset, so the
admitted right and double cosets are finite. This argument works with any
nonzero q in P and does not require residue degree one or unramifiedness.

CI4 — Constrained local normal form
====================================

For O^Y subset M subset q^-j O^Y, successive tail-coordinate projections of
M intersected with the partial flags are principal fractional O-ideals. Choose
generators q^nu_a, with -j<=nu_a<=0, and eliminate descending tail coordinates.
This gives M=(M intersect X) plus a direct sum of O q^nu_a u_a.

For a>0 the allowed unitriangular operation sends u_a to e_a. For a=0 the
group must fix e0; one instead writes u0=e0+z0 with z0 in M intersect X, using
e0 in M and nu0<=0. Send M intersect X to O^n by an X-block equivalence. The
normal form is O^n plus O q^nu0(e0+z), z integral, and the remaining scaled tail
lines. It still contains O^Y and lies in q^-j O^Y. Its X-intersection is exactly
O^n, so a conjugated level element preserving it projects to GL_n(O).

When the tail is empty, M is simply carried to O^n and the kernel projection
argument below is trivial. Local freeness over a DVR suffices throughout; the
proof makes no global free-module or rational strong-approximation assertion.
