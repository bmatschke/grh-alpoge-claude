# Factorization on the prescribed stage tests

Fix the additive character, physical lattices, Cartan involutions, Fock frames and the two Haar measures of a stage triangle $X$. Its finite tests depend on the tuple $t$. Write $I^X_{b,t}(P),I^X_{s,t}(P')$ for the resulting functions on the target group. Consider the definite stage, or the first beta stage $(p,q)=(0,1)$ with the rational planes of the [integral splitting](ROW_BETA_RATIONAL_PLANES.md). Assume the standing local matching, Weil realization and definite nonvanishing inputs of the [definite comparison](ROW_DEFINITE_THETA_COMPARISON.md) and the [first beta step](ROW_FIRST_BETA_STEP.md). We construct two onto maps from the physical polynomial spaces to a common nonzero real theta module $\Pi_\infty$, fixed before $t$, and for every $t$ an injective linear map $J_t$ into functions on the target and a nonzero scalar $a_t$ such that
$$
 I^X_{b,t}(P)=a_t J_t(pP),\qquad I^X_{s,t}(P')=J_t(p'P').
$$
Both integrals here converge absolutely at every target point for every indicated polynomial. This supplies the stage factorization used to compare constants chosen with different common polynomial witnesses. It concerns the **standard stage tests**. The original square flip can involve other finite tests, which use the separate [regularization](ROW_SQUARE_REGULARIZATION.md). [Exact factorization predicate and its witnesses][factor]

## The finite vector and the convergent small integral

In the fixed lattice realization, the big test at each marked split place is supported where its first $a$ columns are invertible over the integers. The small test in the barred split chart is supported where its final $n$ columns are invertible over the integers. The other block has entries in the specified power of the maximal ideal. The value on the invertible block is the inverse determinant character. In particular the small test has full row rank at the distinguished base place $v_0$ for **every** tuple. The conductor there is one, including at $t_0$. Multiplication on either side by the frame isomorphisms preserves this rank condition. [Actual assigned tests][assigned] · [Realization in the barred chart][fin] · [Permanent marked support][permanent] · [Conductor at the distinguished place][place]

The two local projected vectors agree and are nonzero. Indeed the defining zeta integral at the identity on the standard big matrix $[1\ 0]$ is the integral of $1$ on $\mathrm{GL}_a(\mathcal O_v)$, of mass one. The determinant characters cancel on that subgroup and the test vanishes outside it. The small calculation uses $[0\ 1]$ and gives the same value. At general target argument the two functions agree by the complementary-minor identity for the two frame blocks. Equality in the chosen local quotient is exactly equality of these zeta functions. Comparison with the zero test then proves the common class is nonzero. At every unmarked place both vacuum classes equal the same nonzero spherical vector. This gives a nonzero restricted tensor $u_t$ of their common finite classes. [Equality as local functions][zeta] · [Quotients, vacua and zeta normalization][maps]

Only full rank at one split place is needed for the minor gain in the [rank-one integral estimate](ROW_BETA_SQUARE_INTEGRABILITY.md#the-cusp-exponent). To see why the ramification at $v_0$ is allowed, cut the compact support in the barred matrix chart into finitely many disjoint compact open balls $D$ sufficiently small that every element of $D+D$ has rank $n$. This is possible since $2Y$ has rank $n$ at every support point $Y$, even in residue characteristic two. For the squared-theta estimate pair the summand supported on $D$ with its reflection. Their theta sums have equal absolute value by the parity action and reindexing the rational summation variable. In the local matrix coefficient a nonzero translation must belong, up to sign, to $D+D$. Indeed it must connect a support point of the reflected function to one of the original function. A splitting-character phase cancels between the two vectors in the absolute matrix coefficient. The barred projection of its rational index differs from this translation by invertible Gram and frame matrices. Hence that index has a nonzero rational $n$-row minor. Apply the full-rank lattice count to each summand, then the triangle inequality to the original kernel. This part of the estimate uses the local symplectic chart, the additive character and full rank. The multiplication character at $v_0$ may be ramified. [Matrix coefficient, reflection and full-rank bound][sq] · [Change to the barred projection][sobar]

Consequently for fixed finite stage factors and arbitrary real Schwartz $f$ the small integral is bounded absolutely by $C_t p_t(f)\exp([E:\mathbb Q]aT/2)$ on a target Siegel set of logarithmic parameter $T$. The big integral satisfies the same bound by compactness of its source. Since $n>1$, squaring and including the target density leaves an integrable tail proportional to $\exp\bigl(-[E:\mathbb Q]\,(n-1)T\bigr)$. The constants may depend on the finite data. At the definite stage compactness alone suffices for both integrals. If the analytic comparison requires a primed representative at a *different* good place $v_1$, replace that vacuum by a full-rank representative of the same source-coinvariant class. The [row sieve](ROW_DEFINITE_LOCAL_QUOTIENTS.md#full-rank-representatives-at-the-prescribed-split-place) expresses the difference as a finite sum of source translates minus original tests at $v_1$. Every such term still has the full-rank factor at $v_0$, so absolute convergence and right source invariance preserve the ordinary integral. This also proves the identity at each target point. [Replacement on pure tensors in the stage realization][pass]

## Injectivity and a polynomial shared by the twists

Start with the big-source lift on *all* polynomial and finite Schwartz factors. Its definite source gives unrestricted convergence, and the estimate above gives target $L^2$. On each local slice the integrated kernel is source-invariant and target-equivariant. The finite-length full local theta lift has a unique multiplicity-one irreducible cosocle by local Howe duality. The $L^2$ image is semisimple. One projects orthogonally onto an invariant submodule inside each finite-dimensional space of compact invariants, or each finite-dimensional compact isotypic part at infinity. Compact averaging and skew-adjoint real differentiation show that the complementary spaces are invariant. Thus the individual lift kills the kernel of the local irreducible projection. Tensoring these surjections factors it through $\Pi_\infty\otimes\bigotimes'_{v<\infty}\Pi_{v,t}$, with the given spherical base vectors. The same conclusion follows for pointwise values since the integrated polynomial functions are continuous and target quotient Haar measure has full support. [Proof of local descent and smoothness](ROW_BETA_SQUARE_INTEGRABILITY.md#local-smoothness-semisimplicity-and-the-designated-place)

This full algebraic tensor is simple for the target Lie and compact actions and the finite target groups. Indeed each local simple module has scalar commutant by admissibility (or the countable-dimensional Schur argument). On any finite set of factors the Jacobson density theorem can therefore isolate a nonzero simple tensor in the invariant span of a nonzero sum of tensors. Applying the local actions generates that entire finite tensor. Enlarge the finite set of places to include the support of any desired test. The other spherical factors are nonzero, so the same argument generates the restricted product. The big lift on this module is nonzero at the definite base by the nonzero definite theta test. At the beta stage lift a big lower nonzero polynomial datum through ordinary zero evaluation. Its upper beta coefficient is that lower function times a nonzero scalar, hence its upper integral is nonzero. This argument starts with unrestricted finite factors. Simplicity now proves injectivity, in particular on $x\mapsto x\otimes u_t$. [Definite lift and nonzero starting vector](ROW_DEFINITE_THETA_COMPARISON.md) · [Big zero orbit and beta nonvanishing](ROW_FIRST_BETA_STEP.md)

Choose the two real maps on the physical polynomial spaces using the common real exponents, Cartan involutions and vacuum frames. Finite-order twists are trivial on the connected archimedean ideles, so the same maps work for all tuples. Equivalently, the two Fock realizations send physical polynomials onto the analytic polynomial spaces with the same archimedean maps for every finite factor. The Darboux/Heisenberg intertwiners identify the rational theta sums by reindexing and transport the quotient measures. Thus the matching and the integrals above refer to the specified triangles. On an adapted model the compact or first beta comparison identifies the small lift with the big one divided by its nonzero comparison scalar on matched inputs. Replacing a small test at $v_1$ as above permits its use on the actual finite vector. Re-marking carries the comparison to the physical model by the invertible transformations in the [grid comparison](ROW_FIRST_BETA_GRID_RATIOS.md). Those transformations are obtained from the matching maps and Weil actions before applying any stage factorization. The images $u_t$ agree in each such presentation. Restrict the injective big lift to $\Pi_\infty\otimes u_t$ and divide by the comparison scalar to obtain $J_t$. Any common nonzero normalization of either real projection changes the family $(a_t)$ by a common nonzero scalar. [Physical-to-polynomial maps][real] · [Passage to both specified integrated kernels][ker]

Now choose $P'$ lifting any one nonzero $x\in\Pi_\infty$, and choose $P$ with $pP=x$. Both choices precede $t$. Injectivity shows $I^X_{s,t}(P')$ is a nonzero function for every $t$. Its nonzero target point may vary. More generally, if a second common pair $P_1,P'_1$ witnesses a family $c_t$, injectivity gives $a_t pP_1=c_t p'P'_1$. Since $p'P'_1\ne0$ and at least one $c_t\ne0$, the proportionality $c_t/a_t$ is a single nonzero number for all $t$. This proves uniqueness of the normalized ratio under change of witnesses. [Quantifiers on the two polynomials][isconst] · [Proof of uniqueness][unique]

## The alpha boundary and the mixed square

This argument extends to the other two stages used in the first mixed square. At $(1,0)$ the small source is compact. The [unrestricted big estimate and alpha comparison](ROW_FIRST_ALPHA_STEP.md) give local descent on all tests. A nonzero lower small vector lifted through ordinary evaluation gives a nonzero upper small integral, and the matched alpha identity supplies a nonzero upper big lift. At $(1,1)$ the [two-parameter bounds](ROW_MIXED_INTEGRABILITY.md) give unrestricted big convergence and convergence with full rank at one split place on the small side. Use the permanent marked $v_0$ for this bound on the stage's assigned test, and retain it while making the source-coinvariant replacement at $v_1$. The [mixed beta comparison](ROW_FIRST_MIXED_SQUARE.md) supplies a nonzero big boundary by ordinary evaluation on a nonzero lower big test. For both stages the real maps are still fixed by the physical frames and infinite characters, and the common finite vector is nonzero by the same standard zeta calculation. Thus the argument above gives both injectivity and one detecting polynomial for all twists on each of these actual triangles.
