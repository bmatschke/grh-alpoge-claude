# Choosing the coefficient field before the bottom row

The final coefficient request may depend on the preliminary datum $P$, its character frame $f$, the scale and a finite allowed set $\Xi$ of Hecke characters. It must work for every subsequent Hida datum satisfying these specifications, the reference-frame condition, and the exclusion of twist places above $q_0$. A number field attached to a single completed row would have different quantifiers. The construction below shows how to pass from the arithmetic for one choice per realized character and level pattern to the single advance request of `ExField`. Its analytic existence hypothesis is described explicitly. [Order of the field request on the actual column][actual] · [One field request from finitely many patterns][union]

## Which scalar cancels in a jet?

Let $p$ be one of the definite edges, $P_\tau=\delta/\sqrt{2\pi|\tau(\xi_0)|}$ its fixed period, and $d_\tau(m)$ the total degree at $\tau$ of the monomial $m$. Write $o_p$ for the identity on a top edge and complex conjugation on a $\chi$-row edge. With $v_p$ the chosen vacuum and $c_{0,p}$ its comparison constant, the jet of a finite coset vector at the two specified class representatives, including their frame shifts, is
$$
 J(m)=c_{0,p}\prod_\tau o_p(P_\tau)^{d_\tau(m)}\,a_m,
 \qquad a_m\in\overline{\mathbb Q}.
$$
The placement of $c_{0,p}$ outside $o_p$ agrees with the defining vacuum jet even on the conjugate edge. To see the same formula for the actual finite vector, expand the coset indicator with $z=1$ in finite Heisenberg translates of $v_p$ by the character orthogonality relation. Its coefficients are algebraic. The finite Weil element preserves the algebraic span of these translates. The latter statement requires a scalar calculation. The projective expansion gives $\omega(g)v_p=\kappa F$ with $F$ in the algebraic span. At a split place the invariant functional gives $\kappa\ell(F)=r z$, with $\ell(F)$ and the determinant-character factor $z$ algebraic, $|z|=1$ and $r>0$. Unitarity gives $|\kappa|^2\in\overline{\mathbb Q}$ by the vacuum Gram formula. Consequently $r^2=|\kappa|^2|\ell(F)|^2$ and $\kappa=rz/\ell(F)$ are algebraic. The algebraic-action subgroup contains a principal congruence and is closed under taking positive roots. Each nonsplit unitary quasi-reflection has a positive power in that congruence. Those reflections generate the nonsplit local groups, which finishes the finite-action step. A finite translate of the vacuum corresponds by additive approximation to a rational translate of its period-normalized theta function, up to a finite root of unity. The translated theta germ has algebraic coefficients. In the dictionary formula the inner coefficient is $o_p(c_i)\zeta_i$ for the Fourier coefficient $c_i$ and the lattice phase $\zeta_i$. Application of $o_p$ a second time restores $c_i$, as complex linearity of the jet requires. [Vacuum, periods and orientations][ar-vp] · [Projective statement][ar-proj] · [Upgrade on the actual representation][ar-act] · [Finite-action proof](../../../research/modules/theta/finite_weil_arithmetic/main.tex#L296-L448) · [Dictionary coefficients and orientations][ar-dict]

Let $f_{p,\tau}$ be the weight degree. Homogeneity of the jet operator on the specified weight space, place by place, factors out $c_{0,p}\prod_\tau o_p(P_\tau)^{f_{p,\tau}}$. The source defines the normalized matrix at parameter $1$ with multiplier $c_{0,p}^{-1}\prod_\tau o_p(1/P_\tau)^{f_{p,\tau}}$. Their product is $1$ because the normal datum has a nonzero discriminant root and the periods are nonzero. The residual operator on $(a_m)_m$ uses rational differentiation, projections and bideterminant coordinates. Thus all its matrix entries are algebraic. The admissible setting has finitely many twists, and the finite test map surjects onto the grid data. The support labels and class sets are finite. At the least depth for each label the union of supports over **every** allowed coset expansion is finite. Adjoining all entries indexed by these sets gives the single field required in **NFJet**. ## Which scalar remains in a bottom integral?

For a square $(i,j)$, take the two standard spaces $A,A'$ and the standard target $W_0$ of rank $\operatorname{rank}A+\operatorname{rank}A'$. The square rank identities make the target rank even, so the two products of source and target ranks are even. Preserve the given parity-corrected bottom characters. The prescribed-character construction supplies the representations on $(-A,W_0)$ and $(A',W_0)$ with the two specified character quotients. The same two representations, normalized vacua, standard finite functions, Haar measures and fundamental domains define a bottom triangle $X$. Its positive definite source groups give integrability on the corresponding quotients. [Triangle construction with the two character quotients][ar-tri] · [Actual representation supplier](../../../research/modules/theta/canonical_weil/main.tex#L1240-L1266)

For a fixed place-degree vector $d$, let $b,b'\ne0$ be the comparison scalars of the two vacua with their respective positive period models. A period for the negative side is $\Omega_d^-=\overline{b\prod_\tau P_\tau^{d_\tau}}$. Here passing from the positive vacuum to the actual negative vacuum conjugates **the full positive jet**. On the positive side use $\Omega_{d'}^+=b'\prod_\tau P_\tau^{d'_\tau}$. In the pinned minor-polynomial formula, $n_\tau$ counts full-size minors at $\tau$, so $d'_\tau=\operatorname{rank}(A')n_\tau$. The host gives the same $P_*=P_\tau$ for every $\tau$, and the displayed scalar is exactly $b'P_*^{\operatorname{rank}(A')\sum_\tau n_\tau}$. Finite Weil moves and finite Heisenberg expansions preserve the algebraic span on each side, so the finite-point kernels divided by their respective fixed-degree period are algebraic. [Negative vacuum and full conjugation][ar-neg] · [Positive minor degree and scalar][ar-pos] · [Two oriented periods](../../../research/modules/theta/finite_weil_arithmetic/main.tex#L558-L599)

At a common finite level, let $v>0$ be the actual measure of the finite level times the compact real group, with probability Haar used for the remaining real integral. The quotient decomposes into classes with factors $v/|\Gamma_l|$. Compact averaging on a homogeneous polynomial space has algebraic matrix entries. Indeed, a dense set of algebraic unitary matrices defines both the invariant subspace and the span of the images of $\rho(g)-1$ over $\overline{\mathbb Q}$. The Haar operator projects onto the former along the latter. A scalar in each central circle acts on monomials by its degree character times the fixed vacuum character, so at most one degree can survive on each side. In rank zero only the constants occur. All integrals on the first side therefore lie in $v\Omega^-_{d}\overline{\mathbb Q}$, and those on the second in $v'\Omega^+_{d'}\overline{\mathbb Q}$. If an invariant space vanishes, its zero values satisfy the same assertion with a nonzero period. The level is independent of the monomial because finite-place actions commute with real polynomial creation. [Algebraic projector and central degree selection](../../../research/modules/theta/theta_test_inputs/main.tex#L691-L766) · [First integral and its actual Haar factor][ar-haar] · [Second integral and its own Haar factor][ar-haar2]

There are still infinitely many evaluation points in the target group. Their finite levels and rational invariance reduce them to a finite set of classes up to real substitutions by embeddings of rational $E$-matrices. Those substitutions, and the integral determinant powers that accompany them, have coefficients in the compositum of the finitely many images of $E$. The surviving degrees contain only finitely many monomials. Adjoining the normalized values on these generators, for the finite twist set, puts both families in a single finite field. Taking the compositum over all squares gives **NFBot**. The two nonzero periods can depend on the square and the side. Each works for every twist, finite target point and monomial required on its side. This is the quantifier order of **SumsIn**. The quantitative all-embedding dictionary estimates, the critical-value ratios, and transport to the eventual prescribed embedding each require their own arguments. ## Why there are only finitely many patterns

Record the set $\mathcal K(L)=\{(\xi_t,Q_t):t\in T\}$, where $Q_t$ is the selected finite set of primes of $E$ for tuple $t$. For a character $\xi$ that occurs, let $R_\xi$ denote its finite ramification set. The level condition gives
$$
 R_{\xi_t}=Q_t\cup\overline{Q_t}.
$$
Hence $Q_t\subseteq R_{\xi_t}$. The character map is injective, so a given $\xi$ is either absent or is paired with one subset of $R_\xi$. The trivial character is present with $Q=\varnothing$. Discarding characters that cannot occur, the number of possible patterns is bounded above by
$$
 \prod_{\substack{\xi\in\Xi,\ \xi\ne1\\ |R_\xi|<\infty}}
                  (1+2^{|R_\xi|}).
$$
If the reference is missing from $\Xi$, the set of realized patterns is empty. This bound only uses necessary conditions on $Q_t$. On the actual untwisted pass the potential nonreference characters are $\delta$ and $\delta'$. The exact tuple set for each restriction is already known. Keeping the pair $(\xi_t,Q_t)$ records the choice of places when aligning two stages. [Finiteness proof][keys] · [Untwisted tuple computation](UNTWISTED_BOTTOM_ROW.md)

## One set of algebraic numbers for all later choices

Fix $P,f$, the scale and a realized pattern. Choose one witnessing stage and its Hida datum. Supply a grid with its Weil model and normalized frames. Suppose that on any such base grid one can complete the real tests, square constants and finite choices to an admissible marked choice. For this representative apply the arithmetic assertions to the **same** choice. They concern the character values, eighth roots of unity, roots attached to the local depths, phases, square roots of local norms, finite jets, critical Hecke ratios, and the two bottom sums for each square. The compositum $N\subset\mathbf C$ of the corresponding number fields is finite. Take a $\mathbf Q$-basis of $N$ as the request for this pattern. For an unrealized pattern take the empty set. The union of the requests is finite. Any subsequent coefficient embedding containing that union in its range contains all of $N$ for each representative, by rational linearity. Eventual bounds for the finitely many kinds of input are combined by maxima before the scale is fixed. The construction allows the requested finite set itself to depend on the scale. [Fixed-choice field clauses][clauses] · [Representative and basis][representative] · [Critical ratios](../galois/ROW_CRITICAL_HECKE_RATIOS.md)

Here is why the same field works on another datum of the given pattern.

1. Matching each pair $(\xi_t,Q_t)$ to the identical pair on the representative gives a bijection of the tuple labels, by character injectivity and equality of the two ranges. The trivial character identifies the reference tuple. The common preliminary frame supplies the two frame identities, and both auxiliary characters $\beta_X$ are trivial. These give the level agreement used by the transport. [Reference, frame and reindexing][representative]
2. Retain the auxiliary places, splitting characters, specified grid and top spaces and lattices, and the decompositions with their embeddings. The new base row can have its own lattice. Carry the global additive character and the archimedean data, reuse the Weil actions on the required common definite pairs, and take the realization prescribed by the new datum at its marked pairs. Carry the normalized frames and vacua, the scalars in their normalization, and the marked finite representatives. The agreement is required at the places and pairs entering the arithmetic. [Grid transport][grid] · [Weil transport][weil] · [Normalized choice transport][sum]
3. For a finite test on the new grid, choose a test on the representative with the same local coset sets on the active places. At a changed base lattice this uses simultaneous approximation to the prescribed local cosets. With these sets identified, the finite Fourier operations have identical input functions, characters and normalized measures. Their flipped tensors agree. The finite cells, phases, surviving expansions and least depths can now be compared with the original normalized jet dictionary. Bottom triangles carry their two Haar measures and their two periods through the tuple bijection. The grid characters and the uniqueness of each finite Hecke function give the same $L$-ratios. These comparisons put the eight arithmetic clauses on the retained finite data in the image of the originally requested field. [Local cosets and flipped tensors][flip] · [Jet comparison][jets] · [Bottom-triangle transport][bot] · [Combination of the clauses][carried]
4. Complete the prescribed target grid choice using the row measures, catalogue, nonzero downward vector, coefficient identification and square comparison on its own Hida datum. All real replacements of its period functionals factor through one archimedean line. Choose a monomial tuple and coefficient covector giving a nonzero evaluation on this line whenever the catalogue has a nonzero evaluation. This pair preserves its entire joint kernel and supplies the marking in the target choice. All finite data used in steps 1–3 are retained. [Construction over the actual grid](ROW_GRID_CHOICE.md) · [Common real test, including the zero-family case](ROW_COMMON_REAL_TEST.md) · [Target completion in the source][real]

The outer construction installs the union in its last finite coefficient request. The earlier three requests for this particular purpose may be empty. On an actual permitted restriction, the twist places avoid $q_0$, the frame agrees, and every character lies in the declared finite set. Consequently the image of its specified embedding contains the request, in exactly the order needed to infer `FieldOK`. This proves the finite-field transport implication. The [grid and base-choice construction](ROW_GRID_CHOICE.md) supplies these existence steps on the standing hypotheses once the [common real matching and square proportionalities](ROW_SQUARE_REGULARIZATION.md) of `ConstEx` are available. The [induction over all signatures](ROW_TOWER_INDUCTION.md#passage-to-the-prescribed-grid) supplies this comparison on the standing local and definite doubling inputs.
