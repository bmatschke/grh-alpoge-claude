3. ACTUAL SECTIONS GIVE THE PERFECT-COMPLEX MODEL

ASL1–ASL21 complete the original actual smooth-projective perfect-complex producer. On a qcqs scheme a section which vanishes on a section’s nonvanishing locus is killed by one common power, and a section on that locus extends after one common power. Finite covers and a second common power kill the overlap discrepancies. These constructions preserve all integer twists and actual transition functions. They prove graded-section localization and every restriction in the section cube. Explicit extended local generators and duals give a finite dual basis for affine line-bundle sections; the actual chart algebra is smooth and flat. FCD’s one Noetherian model and the previously proved finite-projective-complex assembly then yield the full original Perf record over arbitrary R, with one comparison before all base changes. Independent polarization, all-cover H1, rank and square-law suppliers remain.

The geometric problem was to connect the algebraic model already proved in FCD with sections of the actual bundles on the actual scheme. A graded module with useful formal properties would not suffice unless its localizations were identified with the given section modules, and unless those identifications agreed with every restriction map. ASL constructs those identifications.

The first argument is clearing denominators on a finite cover. If a section vanishes wherever a chosen bundle section sigma is nonzero, affine trivializations show that some power of the local function representing sigma kills it. Quasi-compactness gives finitely many exponents, so one exponent works everywhere. Conversely, a section defined only on the nonvanishing locus has a denominator on each affine trivialization. Multiplying by a common power of sigma extends it locally. The resulting extensions need not initially agree on overlaps. Their differences vanish on the same nonvanishing locus, so a second common power kills every difference. The adjusted sections then glue. Quasi-separatedness supplies the finite control on overlaps.

These are statements about the original line bundles and their transition units. They apply to all integer twists; a negative twist is handled with the actual dual tensor power, not discarded from the graded module. The proof records the exponent needed to clear local denominators separately from the exponent needed to kill overlap discrepancies. This makes the global construction checkable and explains why one uniform power exists.

Applying these two results to the actual graded sum of twisted sections proves localization and dehomogenization. Every map is obtained from restriction and division by the same chosen sections. This makes the diagrams for intersecting charts commute. Consequently the whole section cube, including every vertex and restriction, is the one used by the original geometric consumer.

The affine projectivity step is also explicit. On finitely many principal opens, choose local generators of the line bundle and their local duals. Extend them after taking powers of the principal functions, then kill the discrepancies in their evaluation products with further powers. A finite identity expressing 1 in terms of these powers yields finitely many global sections and dual functionals whose evaluations sum to the identity. This is a dual basis, and therefore proves finite projectivity of the actual module of sections over an arbitrary ring.

The remaining chart-algebra property is smoothness and flatness of the given affine charts. Together with the previous Noetherian descent, this fixes one model and one finite-projective complex. The already reconstructed GSL assembly then supplies its actual comparison map to sections, before any later coefficient algebra or specified pullback square is chosen. Dickson’s lemma shows that the degree-zero monoid is finitely generated, and each fixed-weight fiber has finitely many representatives modulo that monoid. Next, a strict cocycle with values in an arbitrary k-algebra has finite image under the given continuity conditions. Its values therefore lie in a finite-dimensional k-subspace. Expand them in a finite k-basis and introduce one formal variable for each basis coefficient. This gives a genuine formal deformation, to which the universal property applies. The proof never tries to substitute arbitrary algebra elements into an infinite formal series. This distinction supplies the broad triangular point correspondence without changing the target ring.

For connectedness, choose one nonzero strict cocycle b over the residue field before considering any other core point. A core prime p gives a cocycle a over its own residue field K. Thus {eta,p,q_b} has a dense point and is irreducible. These three-point subsets cover the core and all contain q_b, so a separation of the core is impossible.

This is the full original preconnected_rank_two, and it also proves nonemptiness. The argument does not claim that every specialization of a_T stays in the core: the lower entry may vanish at another value of T. Only the generic prime and the two specified endpoints are used. This is a useful example of finding the exact weaker geometric construction that the consumer needs.

5. THE ARCHIMEDEAN SCALAR IS NOW COMPUTED EXACTLY

Fix the original setting X, bridge, standard character and infinity measures. At a real place, write n=a+k for the ambient dimension and the number a of frame columns. Accordingly the tuple self-dual measure has density 2^(na) with respect to real-imaginary Lebesgue measure, and the Hermitian self-dual measure is Frobenius volume. The same statement holds for every allowed self-dual witness, by the uniqueness already proved in GMR.

Let w be the actual nondegenerate frame and T=w*Jw its Gram matrix. In coordinates adapted to its orthogonal complement, the relevant differential is D_w(Z,H)=Zw+(1/2)wT^(−1)H. The off-diagonal Lie blocks have the Frobenius factor sqrt(2). Counting those coordinates and the Hermitian/skew-Hermitian coordinates gives the exact real determinant |det D_w|=2^(−na)|det T|^k. Multiplication by the tuple self-dual density cancels the power of two. The fiber measure therefore has local density |det T|^k at the base frame.

A matrix square-root chart turns that tangent calculation into a neighborhood calculation. For Q near T, the matrix (1+T^(−1)(Q−T))^(1/2) is T-self-adjoint and carries T to Q by congruence. This works when T is indefinite. Together with the exponential orbit chart it gives a local diffeomorphism in which the Gram map is projection. An almost-everywhere equality would not suffice for the required fixed Gram matrix.

The source gauge is also derived from its literal definition. Give the unitary group the left-invariant Riemannian density whose tangent metric at the identity is Frobenius. The compact stabilizer is U(k) with its Frobenius normalization. The last-column map U(j)→S^(2j−1) has horizontal norm 2|z|^2+t^2, whereas the round sphere has norm |z|^2+t^2. The period-ring chapter works with the supplied maps in the diagram A→E→W and A→B→W. Here A is the product of coefficient power-series rings, B is the integral coefficient tensor with the tilt’s Witt ring, and W is its larger Witt tensor ring. The two routes from A agree. The argument proves properties of these actual maps; it does not construct an unrelated convenient embedding.

The key local calculation reads the first power-series coefficient not divisible by the coefficient uniformizer. In each idempotent component, an element is either divisible by that uniformizer or is a uniformizer multiple plus T^m times a unit. The image of T belongs to the Jacobson radical of B. To prove the latter, take any b in the finite free coefficient algebra and consider multiplication by 1−mu*b. Its determinant is 1 modulo the source Jacobson element mu, hence a unit; the adjugate gives an inverse. This finite matrix argument supplies exactly the needed property without requiring the stronger parameter-adic completeness theorem used along another source route.

The series dichotomy and the Jacobson property force divisibility reflection. A nonzero idempotent cannot be a uniformizer multiple when the uniformizer is in the Jacobson radical. Thus a target element divisible by the uniformizer cannot arise from the nondivisible branch of the dichotomy. Iterating and using separatedness proves injectivity of E→W. The same componentwise argument, tested against maximal ideals, proves reflection of units.

For the integral intersection, take an element of E whose image lies in the integral tensor B. A density approximation gives its first integral digit in A, and the dichotomy forces the power of T in that digit to be large enough. After subtracting it, the remainder is divisible by the uniformizer and remains integral; the p-saturation of B inside W is what justifies that last assertion. Repeat the process. The digits form a p-adic Cauchy sequence in A, its limit exists, and separatedness in E identifies the original element with that limit. This proves the exact original intersection, together with prime saturation, injectivity and unit reflection.

The chapter then supplies limit arguments needed by the invariant Frobenius branch. In a perfect Witt ring, multiplication by p shifts the Witt coordinates with Frobenius, so membership in p is detected by the zeroth coordinate. A finite coefficient basis transfers this criterion to the tensor rings. It proves p-saturation of the fixed subring for an arbitrary set H of automorphisms and allows a class fixed modulo p to be corrected to an actually fixed lift by Teichmuller representatives. No averaging over H is used or required.

For tau=epsilon_s−1 in the tilt, its r-th coordinate is killed by the explicit power p^(s+r). Consequently each coordinate of an infinite tau-adic sum eventually stops changing. This directly constructs a compatible tilt element. Applying the same construction to the tails proves the required divisibility, not merely coordinatewise convergence. The resulting near-parameter subgroups inside W are additive subgroups, not ideals: the parameter is invertible in W, so calling them ideals would destroy the intended filtration.

Their intersection is pW and their weighted tails admit a common limit. For fixed-subring completeness, first construct an unrestricted limit. Since every automorphism fixes the parameter and every term, its difference from the limit lies in every near-parameter subgroup, hence in pW. Correct the limit with the fixed-mod-p lifting result. This establishes the original nearPi_sep_complete and fixSub_nearPi_complete at arbitrary H and the actual source filtration.
