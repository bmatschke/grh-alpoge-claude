# One coefficient request for the integral Galois orders on the supported row

At a fixed strict Henselian state, the last clause of the large-coefficient requirement asks for an integral matrix algebra over a coefficient subring and split characteristic polynomials on the selected block. It is a condition at every *live* localization, one containing a character point with absolutely irreducible residual rank-$r$ complement. The earlier [integral models and splitting of constituents](../first-pass/FIRST_PASS_GALOIS_COEFFICIENT_MODELS.md) give the first two large-coefficient clauses. The [first-pass wild-inertia argument](../first-pass/FIRST_PASS_WILD_INERTIA.md) supplies its ordinary live witness. Kaiwen Jin's reference-localization construction and the following comparison supply the last clause on the admitted ordinary or supported row, with the same marks and the same field request. [Large and its live-index clause][native-def] · [Reference and operative localizations][jin-match] · [Order of the two realizations][native-two]

Use a fixed prescribed real column, with the original twist, all eligible split Hecke generators, complete local coefficient DVRs and the same group closure and prolongations. Use the actual occurrence comparison in both readings, the semisimple rational point representations and labeled character decompositions, and the multiplicity-free, absolutely split reference constituents in the corner construction. These are Jin's hypotheses on a supported start and the original comparison on a fitted character level. In particular, the result does not construct that starting row. The coefficient request is finite at each place. Its size can depend on the supplied row. [Supported-state hypotheses][jin-support] · [Our fitted state](SECOND_PASS_THETA_REALIZATION.md)

## Points and orders over the two coefficient DVRs

First enlarge the reference field to contain the Hecke values and the fixed constituent models. For every reference localization at the finitely many positive stages request a basis of its local field over $\mathbb Q_\ell$ in the prescribed complex embedding. At every live reference localization also request the roots specified below. Unite these with the incoming requests before taking the second realization. Write $O_0\to O_1$ for the resulting map of DVRs in the prescribed readings, with fraction fields $K_0,K_1$. It is a local injection since it extends the embedding of finite extensions of $\mathbb Q_\ell$ and preserves integrality. Retain the original choices on the common Galois closure. Thus arithmetic Frobenius, labeled characters, inertia and the cyclotomic character are compared on the same elements.

The finite point sets in the two readings are identified by their complete complex Hecke eigenvalue systems. At a fixed coefficient place and stage, two such systems lie in one residue localization exactly when every eligible generator has congruent values. This condition is preserved in both directions under a local injection of DVRs. Indeed the two residual evaluation maps have image the common residue field in the given splitting and agree on its constant scalars. Equality of their kernels then implies equality of their evaluations. Thus the point bijection identifies *each* occupied residue block, including its labeled character points. Denote one such point set by $X$ and its diagonal Hecke orders by $T_i\subset O_i^X$. The actual generators agree after extension, so
$$
                         O_1\otimes_{O_0}T_0=T_1 .
$$
The inclusion into $O_1^X$ uses flatness. The image contains every generator of $T_1$, and scalar-linear combinations of the old order form an algebra. This proves the equality on the integral orders, with their evaluation maps. [Generators and block comparison][jin-match] · [Two directions of the generator equality][native-order]

For completeness, the descent of the live condition works in every residue characteristic. The common split Frobenius polynomials extend by density and continuity to the characteristic polynomial of each group element at matched points. At a character point divide out the same $t$ labeled linear factors, then reduce. The remaining characteristic polynomials determine the residual semisimplification over an algebraic closure. One elementary proof works even when the rank exceeds $\ell$. Work over the algebraically closed residue field in this paragraph. For two semisimple modules, the algebra generated on their direct sum is a product of full matrix algebras, one per distinct simple constituent, by the structure theorem for finite-dimensional semisimple algebras. The traces of all group elements agree, hence so do traces on this entire image algebra by linearity. On a rank-one matrix idempotent supported on a single factor the trace gives its multiplicity modulo $\ell$. Cancel the common copies of each factor in all characteristic polynomials. Every remaining multiplicity is a multiple of $\ell$. Taking the unique $\ell$-th root of each remaining product of characteristic polynomials reduces the comparison to representations of smaller dimension. Induction gives equality of all multiplicities. The argument applies to the semisimplifications of any two original stable lattices because taking composition factors preserves the characteristic polynomials. A residual absolutely irreducible complement at an operative point therefore gives one at its reference match. [Residual complement and the full polynomials][jin-live] · [Descent at the original live index][native-two]

Write $R_i(x,g)$ for the integral matrix families and put $A_i=\sum_g T_i R_i(g)$. Rational semisimplicity and equality of full Frobenius polynomials give pointwise rational intertwiners after scalar extension. They may be chosen over $K_1$: the linear equations defining the intertwiner space admit an invertible solution over its algebraic closure, so the determinant is a nonzero polynomial on that space and cannot vanish on every point over the infinite field $K_1$. Conjugating the finite product of rational matrix spaces by these intertwiners gives
$$
     O_1\otimes_{O_0}A_0\simeq A_1,\qquad
     1\otimes\sum_g f_gR_0(g)\longmapsto\sum_g f_gR_1(g).
$$
Here the $f_g$ on the right have been transported. The equality of scalar orders gives surjectivity. For injectivity extend the old order into the finite product of matrices by flatness, then apply conjugation. At the selected character point a rational intertwiner on the rank-$r$ complement can be rescaled so it is integral and nonzero modulo the uniformizer. Both residual complements are irreducible and have the same rank. The reduction is therefore invertible, and the intertwiner identifies the two original complementary lattices. The labeled character lines are transported separately. This proves the exact selected-block assertion on these lattices; on other points the integral assertion concerns the generated algebra. The abstract coefficients certifying membership in the matrix order, its congruence submodule and its corners all transport by the displayed formula. ## Constructing and transporting the polynomials

At a chosen live reference point $z$ the residual image in $M_r(k)\times k^t$ contains $M_r(k)\times0$. Indeed its first projection is onto by absolute irreducibility. The kernel of projection onto $k^t$ has first projection an ideal. If it were zero the algebra would be commutative, although its first projection is $M_r(k)$ with $r>1$. Thus the complementary rank-$t$ projection and a rank-one idempotent in the rank-$r$ factor both have lifts in the finite integral order. For the lifting step, the characteristic coefficients of every element of the order belong to $T_0$. Jin proves this without division by integers by peeling off single group-matrix terms in a truncated formal determinant. For an element vanishing modulo the uniformizer at $z$, its characteristic polynomial therefore reduces to a pure power of the variable at all points of the residue block. Its powers tend to zero uniformly on the finite set. Newton iteration on an approximate idempotent, $a\mapsto3a^2-2a^3$, gives an idempotent in the complete order. Apply it first to the projection and then in its complementary corner. At $z$ the lifted projection is exactly the identity on the character lines and zero on the complement. A matrix idempotent congruent to zero over a local ring is zero by Nakayama. Apply the same argument to the complementary idempotent on a factor where it reduces to the identity. The ranks of the idempotents are constant throughout the residue block, since the multiplicities of zero and one in their reduced characteristic polynomials give those ranks. [Integral characteristic coefficients and idempotent lifting][jin-idemp]

Let $e_B$ be the resulting rank-$t$ projection. At $z$ the $t$ eigenvalues of $e_BR(g_0)e_B$ on its image are $\alpha_j=\chi_1(g_0)u^{-(j-1)}$, with $u$ the prescribed nontorsion cyclotomic value. These are distinct units. At another point $x$, multiplicity-free split constituents make the rational corner a product of full matrix algebras. It has an element with simple spectrum on its image. Multiply such an element by a common denominator and by an element of $T_0$ supported on $x$ and nonzero there. The latter exists by multiplying separating functions, each translated by its value at one unwanted point. The discriminant of the original compressed operator plus a scalar times this correction is a nonzero polynomial of degree at most $t(t-1)$. Thus a multiplier in $\{0,\ldots,t(t-1)\}$ suffices at each $x$. Corrections have disjoint support and vanish at $z$. The resulting characteristic polynomials $P_x$ on the rank-$t$ block are monic, integral and separable, with coefficients given by functions in $T_0$. Adjoin their roots in the reference embedding, for all designated points and required inertia choices, including the marked choice over $3$. The isomorphism of orders transports both idempotents and this same compressed operator. The resulting roots in $K_1$ are in $O_1$ by integrality. This gives the live-index witness on **every** operative realization containing the finite request. Together with the two earlier large-coefficient clauses, it supplies the full large-coefficient input at the fitted stage under the cited occurrence and Galois hypotheses. ## A test for roots already in the reference field

One can often omit roots from this request. The following quantitative criterion uses the actual congruence depth of two Hecke evaluations. Give $O_0$ the integer-valued valuation $v$ and uniformizer $\varpi$. For $x\ne z$ in one reference block put
$$
 D(x,z)=\min_{f\in T_0}v(f(x)-f(z)),\quad
 d_j=\sum_{h\ne j}v(\alpha_j-\alpha_h),\quad
 a_j=\max_{h\ne j}v(\alpha_j-\alpha_h).
$$
It suffices to take the minimum over the original scalar Hecke generators, since sums and products of integral elements preserve a congruence. Assume $t\ge2$; degree one already splits. Whenever $D(x,z)>d_j+a_j$, the original field contains a unique root of $P_x$ in the ball
$$
                    v(y-\alpha_j)\ge D(x,z)-d_j .
$$
Indeed every coefficient of $P_x-P_z$ is divisible by $\varpi^D$, with $P_z(Y)=\prod_h(Y-\alpha_h)$. Put $s=D-d_j>a_j$ and substitute $Y=\alpha_j+\varpi^s Z$. After division by $\varpi^D$, the polynomial $P_z$ reduces to a unit times $Z$, since in each other factor the constant term has valuation strictly below $s$. The difference $P_x-P_z$ after the same substitution and division has integral coefficients and reduces to a constant. The resulting polynomial has a simple linear reduction. Hensel's lemma over the complete DVR gives the root and its uniqueness on that ball. The balls for the qualifying indices are pairwise disjoint. If all indices qualify there is no splitting-field extension for this polynomial. For the unperturbed compressed operator the same test applies, since its characteristic coefficients also lie in $T_0$; when all qualify it already has simple spectrum at $x$.

The strict inequality is sharp as a general criterion even with two labels. Take $K_0=\mathbb Q_3$, $t=2$, $\alpha_1=4$, $\alpha_2=1$, and evaluate the order $T=\{(a,b)\in\mathbb Z_3^2:a\equiv b\pmod 9\}$ at its two projections. Here $D=2=d_j+a_j$. The monic polynomials $P_z=(Y-4)(Y-1)$ and $P_x=P_z+18$ are coefficientwise evaluations in $T$. The discriminant at $x$ is $9-72=-63$, whose unit factor $-7$ is a nonsquare modulo $3$. Thus $P_x$ is separable and has no root in $\mathbb Q_3$. The example tests what follows from coefficient congruence alone on the two-point order.

For odd $\ell$ and $u=1+\ell^a$, $a\ge1$, write $e=v(\ell)$. Then $v(1-u^h)=e(a+v_\ell(h))$ for $h>0$. The maximal $d_j/e$ is $(t-1)a+v_\ell((t-1)!)$, attained at an endpoint, and the maximal $a_j/e$ is $a+\lfloor\log_\ell(t-1)\rfloor$, attained there too. The sufficient all-root precision is therefore
$$
 D(x,z)/e>ta+v_\ell((t-1)!)+\lfloor\log_\ell(t-1)\rfloor .
$$
This includes the specified $u=4$ at $\ell=3$ with $a=1$. For the additional large-prime choice $u=\omega(1+\ell)$ with $\bar\omega$ of order $\ell-1$, if $t\le\ell-1$ the $\alpha_j$ have distinct reductions. Then $d_j=a_j=0$ and every point in the residue block qualifies automatically. For general $u$, the distinct residue roots of $P_z$ group its indices into classes of sizes $m_1,\ldots,m_b$. Ordinary coprime Hensel factorization gives corresponding monic factors of $P_x$ over $O_0$. The Galois group of its splitting field injects into $\prod_i S_{m_i}$ and fixes the already lifted roots. These are bounds on the local root extension. The degree or height of the common global family requires further input. [Prescribed inertia choices][jin-live]
