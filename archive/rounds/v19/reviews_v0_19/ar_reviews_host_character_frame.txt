REVIEWED CONCLUSIONS

HF1–HF4. The norm-one integral-unit reduction to roots of unity is
valid: characteristic polynomials of successive powers have bounded
integral coefficients, hence a finite set of possible roots. The
host involution pairing is the exact AC2/AC3 identity and correctly
uses MUQ for nonconstant weights. The extension from an open subgroup
is proved by the compatible U alpha(A) definition and a one-generator
divisibility/Zorn argument. Its continuity follows from continuity
on the open subgroup; no unsupported closed-subgroup extension
theorem is used. The construction chi(z)=nu(z/Cz) kills the principal
ideles, gives exponent -2e and the positive selected residue value,
and gives the inverse conjugate character at the opposite prime.
Reduction surjectivity proves the exact power-kernel/order statement.

HF5–HF8. Finite global order is proved using one exponent for all
finite residue characters, continuity on the full unit product and
the finite ideal class group. This is stronger than merely checking
separately finite local images. Complete splitting gives e=f=1;
Gal(E/K) acts simply transitively over the chosen quadratic prime,
so the product of transported residue values is the residue of the
norm. The actual root norm equals one. The six IsMu0 fields and four
IsChiPsi fields match the literal source records, including
HasInfQ(-e), integer infinity exponent -2e, and the inverse power
omega^(-2(j+1)). One PiBar and Omg are retained for the whole family.

HF9–HF11. The quadratic product formula is a substantive host-specific
proof. Away from d, Frobenius on sqrt(-d), Euler's criterion and
quadratic reciprocity give the residue-norm symbol. The prime two
case uses the actual host splitting and d congruence. The quadratic
subfield Q(sqrt(qd)) gives A^2-4N=qd B^2 with A integral, B rational;
the parity of the d-adic order proves B is d-integral, which is all
that the square-mod-d argument needs. CRT removes the d-prime
restriction by a norm b bar(b); it has positive archimedean signs
and even inert orders. The denominator ideal can be cleared by an
element congruent to one at the d-primes, so the reduction is valid.

The odd-infinity extension checks the exact overlap of the unit
ideles with diag(k) beta(m). Relative unramifiedness gives all local
orders -m_v. The root-compatible delta makes k/delta real, and the
already proved product formula gives the needed prescribed sign.
The choices delta=1+zeta and delta=sqrt(-d) satisfy their equations.
The phase product is one by the actual involution pairing or
4 dividing |Psi|. Inert primes are outside the host conductor, so
their orders are zero even at residue characteristic two. This
produces all four actual IsEtaT fields without assuming a global
class-field-theory existence theorem.

HF12–HF13. The order-15 residue character on every selected prime
and its conjugate gives exact inertia order 15 at every prime over
p, including the universal quantifier over natural exponents.
The proof only gives a positive finite global order, as required;
it does not incorrectly infer global exact order 15 or IsPsi's
prime-power order condition. The two delta characters have disjoint
nonempty ramification supports. All twelve named HFrame fields
(seven data entries and five certificate families) are supplied.
The specialization to Pre/PreX uses its actual weights, e_even/MUQ
and prime congruences, preserving the host and auxiliary choices.

HF14–HF15. Checked the literal KD/KDq definitions. Subgroup.closure
is algebraic generation; the manuscript now explicitly says this.
The generated subgroup contains an open congruence subgroup and
is open and closed, so its topological closure is the same. Its
literal locF generators use elements of F*, and an element of
order one at a specified prime can be chosen in F*. The principal
F-idele decomposition and finite-character kernel statements are
therefore about the exact source quotient.

The explicit norm-one equation z bar(k)=k determines e1 modulo
F*, and its character value has the inverse exponent prescribed
by chiPsi's infinity type. The relative determinant uses
max(Delta_P,0), precisely the source toNat convention. Self-duality
gives opposite conjugate determinant orders, so the difference of
the positive parts is Delta_P. This proves the cocycle and rational
unitary transport laws without replacing the source representative
by a signed product. The finer SQ congruences leave KDq times unitIdl
unchanged, so the same arguments supply actual trueDtPackQ.

HF16–HF17. The field normalization table is correct: eta uses 1/2;
mu0, chiPsi and both deltas use zero. TL14 supplies one finite
common field over all unramified primes, and the primitive-generator
test supplies exact containment. This is not incorrectly promoted
to the stronger NumbersIn/Choice.FieldOK condition.

The Base extension is constructed using finitely many class
representatives, roots of principal powers, finitely many type
norms and required roots of unity, followed by one Galois closure.
Equality of ideal powers gives equality of the extended ideals.
For the norm equation at every embedding, conjugation is used only
on the embedded CM host, where every embedding does commute with
the CM involution. It is not assumed central on an arbitrary
coefficient extension. Taking Ks=LL0=L gives the literal twenty
new named Base fields, with the inherited Pre equal to the input.

for this canonical modulus, so no field enlargement after forming
the input is needed. Both Pins equations and the nontrivial

The ordinary Hyp statement is literal hyp_of_colIn on any supplied
