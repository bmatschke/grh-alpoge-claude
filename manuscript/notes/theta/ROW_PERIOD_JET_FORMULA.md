# From the bottom theta-period test to the finite jet sum

Fix the row-test choice on the given column. Write $D_t x$ for its unprojected downward theta chain, evaluated on the chosen smooth tensor at the bottom, and $\mathcal P_a$ for the Bessel period after the subsequent reference-character row. On an admissible choice in the stated Hida range, under the theta, cuspidality, doubling and coefficient-field inputs of the row construction, the period satisfies
$$
 \iota(e_t)\,\bigg(\prod_{i,j}c_{ij,t}\bigg)
 \mathcal P_a\big(\Theta_t j_t x\big)=\kappa\,v_{t,a}(x)
 \qquad(x\in \mathrm{goodF}_t).
$$
Here $a$ is the finite test, $\iota$ is the prescribed field embedding, $e_t$ is a root of unity in that field, and $\kappa$ is common to the entire choice. The [square comparison on all columns](../construction/ROW_TOWER_INDUCTION.md) gives the [opposite path](../construction/ROW_SQUARE_PATH_FLIP.md). The [mixed-cell unfolding](../construction/ROW_MIXED_CELL_UNFOLDING.md) evaluates its period on the prescribed measures and tests. Below we remove the projector, compute the resulting jet class sums and track their Haar factors. [Exact identity][formula] · [Four intermediate identities][four]

## Removing the projector on the specified source vectors

Let $p=\sum_{g\in G}a_g g$ be the idempotent for the list of selected types in the group algebra over the specified coefficient field. At the bottom level the additional level idempotent equals one, so every $x\in\mathrm{goodF}_t$ has the form $x=p y$. If $A_g$ denotes the prescribed group action on the bottom functions, then the downward chain satisfies $D_t(r(g)y)=A_g D_t(y)$. To check this, lift the reduction of $g$ to the specified level on the initial definite group. The defining transformation law there is translation by this lift multiplied by the **inverse** flag character. On each successive edge the translated theta kernel has the same transformation law on the output after changing the variable of integration. The quotient measure is Haar, and integrating a rationally invariant integrable function gives the same result on either fundamental set. Changing the level lift changes the translate and the inverse character by cancelling factors. Factoring the identity through the stored real intertwiner uses the Schur relation on the cusp multiplicity space. Iterating gives equivariance for any smooth tensor evaluated at the bottom. [Initial translate][act] · [Compatibility along the chain][actionchain] · [Use of rapid decay and Schur compatibility][proj]

By semilinearity of $j_t$ and linearity of the chain,
$$
 D_t x=\sum_g\iota(a_g)A_g D_t y=e_0D_t y.
$$
The stored bottom realization of the **projected** chain takes values in the image on which $e_0$ acts as the identity. Thus $e_0^2D_t y=e_0D_t y$ on pure archimedean tensors, and linearity extends this equality to their algebraic tensor product. Consequently $e_0D_t x=D_t x$ on every good input, exactly the identity used to insert the actual unprojected chain inside $\mathcal P_a$. This argument uses the core and the cusp rapid-decay input on the given model, with no additional lower bound on either size parameter. [Idempotence and the good-source assertion][fix]

## The definite period is a jet class sum

Fix a finite complex linear combination $\sigma$ of pairs of archimedean polynomials on the two definite boundary chains. There is **one** scalar $k(\sigma)$ such that, for every tuple $t$, finite test $a$, contributing cell $\beta$, admissible term measure $M_\beta$, expansion $\tau_\beta$ of its local product function, and vector $y$ in its definite source,
$$
 \operatorname{defTot}(M_\beta,\sigma,\tau_\beta;\phi_a,F_{t,y})
   =k(\sigma)\operatorname{classSum}
            (a,\beta,\tau_\beta;\Psi_a,F_{t,y}^{\mathrm{fin}}).
$$
This is the source's `JetsAt` on the actual choice under its standing level and finite-sum conditions. For every tuple, finite datum and cell, including cells outside the support, an admissible term measure exists. At each specified definite node normalize Haar measure to give the finite level times the compact real group mass one, choose a measurable rational fundamental set, and use arbitrary admissible Haar data at the other nodes. [Term integral and precise quantifiers][four] · [Class-sum identity on the choice][term]

**One compact edge.** Start with one pure polynomial pair $P$ in $\sigma$ and a functional $\Lambda$ on the incoming weight. In the standard frames the finite vector, the two finite points and their translations determine a linear functional $\ell$ on the Fock polynomials by applying the finite Weil action and evaluating at zero. At the real place the action substitutes the two unitary matrices in the polynomial and multiplies by the vacuum character. Multiplication by the character of the incoming weight leaves, on the compact integration group, precisely a determinant power $\prod_\nu\det(k_\nu)^{n_\nu}$, times a factor independent of $k$. The specified weights give $n_\nu\geq0$. Absorb this power by multiplying the incoming polynomial by the corresponding product of determinant polynomials. Multiplication by that nonzero polynomial is injective, so the incoming functional extends from its image. [Weight and determinant check][det] · [Moved finite-vector evaluation][moved] · [Compact polynomial average][average]

On this finite-dimensional determinant-twisted polynomial module, evaluations at matrices span the dual: a polynomial vanishing on all matrices vanishes identically. For one evaluation point $Y$ form the polynomial-valued compact average of the transformed $P$ against the incoming polynomial evaluated at $kY$. Only the finitely many monomials in the substitution of $P$ occur. Their coefficients are continuous and integrable against probability Haar measure. Left invariance of this measure makes the average equivariant for the incoming unitary group. The rectangular polynomial representation decomposes by the Cauchy formula into pairwise distinct irreducible pairs for the two linear groups. For the specified incoming irreducible weight its intertwiner space is therefore an irreducible representation of the other linear group. The substitution into the first columns is a nonzero equivariant map (set the remaining variables to zero to recover the original polynomial). Its translates span that intertwiner space. For several real places, take tensor products of these spans. The average is consequently a finite linear combination of these substitutions. Applying $\ell$ evaluates the jet whose monomial coefficients are $\ell(Z^m)$. This produces one outgoing functional $\Lambda'$ evaluating the jet, chosen before $\ell$, the finite test, the points and the input vector. The finite and real factors commute. The change of Fock frame is made on $P$ before this calculation. [Coefficientwise integral and equivariance][pol] · [Substitution span at one place][howe] · [Tensor product over the real places][howepi] · [Result for an actual edge][moved]

Iterate this construction separately on the $m$ top edges and the $m$ last-row edges. At each step divide the new functional by the nonzero stored normalization when replacing the unnormalized operator by the stored finite jet map. The top normalization used here is independent of $t$. Induction gives two final functionals depending on $P$ and the initial real functionals. At the terminal node the compact integration is the simultaneous average of their two coefficients: insert the orthogonal summand embedding into the larger group in the first coefficient and the original element in the second. Haar invariance makes the average an invariant bilinear form. The two ranks differ by one and the given weights interlace in the dual pairing convention. The unitary branching rule gives multiplicity one, so the average is $k_P$ times the specified branching pairing. Probability Haar measure is unique. This proves the independence of $k_P$ from $t$, the class, and the finite expansion, and allows $k_P=0$. [The two normalized inductions][jetrows] · [Prescribed branching pairing and terminal average][branch] · [Branching rule, Chapter 8][gw]

**Finite levels and volumes.** All integrating groups on these two boundary chains are definite. For one node with finite level $J$, decompose its adelic quotient into double cosets. A representative $g$ contributes the compact real average with factor $1/|H(F)\cap gJH_\infty g^{-1}|$. Indeed the compact fiber has the normalized level mass one and the stabilizer acts on its covering space with this finite degree. A pure summand need only be invariant under a finer compact open $J'$. Take a common such subgroup for the finitely many kernels. Its actual Haar mass is $1/[J:J']$. Over one $J$-class the finer classes are stabilizer orbits on $J/J'$. Orbit–stabilizer gives the sum of their reciprocal stabilizer orders as $[J:J']/|\mathrm{Stab}(g)|$. Multiplying by the finer level mass recovers precisely the claimed reciprocal stabilizer.

The regrouping uses invariance of the **sum** defined by the expansion. At an interior vertex the two adjacent changes of finite frame cancel in the product kernel. The initial top input is fixed by $K_+$. At the initial row vertex $k$ introduces the level character $\chi(k)$ in the coefficient-valued automorphic form, while the translated first finite kernel product supplies $\chi(k)^{-1}$. The first-row local computation gives this law on the selected polarization, and outside it the corresponding factors vanish. The character cancels before applying the multilinear jet pairing to the full expansion. At the last vertex $y_m$ and the embedded top endpoint $s(y_m)$ must be changed together. These are exactly the invariances needed for the preceding class regrouping. [Four finite-level laws][levels] · [First row translation and character][first] · [Local kernel computation][firstloc]

There are independently summed top classes $x_0,\ldots,x_{m-1}$ and row classes $y_0,\ldots,y_m$. The terminal top point is $x_m=s(y_m)$. The product of their $2m+1$ reciprocal stabilizer factors times the jet pairing is exactly the summand in the specified class sum. Applying the compact-edge evaluation, the terminal pairing and the class decomposition in the defining order proves the displayed identity for $P$. Compactness and finite expansion justify the integrations and summations. Finally $k(\sigma)=\sum_P\sigma(P)k_P$ by linearity. No nonvanishing or algebraicity of this archimedean scalar is used. [Full class indices, last diagonal and weights][glue] · [Return from pure polynomials to $\sigma$][jets]

## The cell mass on the mixed side

The cells preceding the definite period have a different volume factor. For $1\le j\le m-2$, let $K_j$ be the finite lattice stabilizer in the ambient group, $R_j$ the period subgroup, and $b_j$ the representative of the cell. Let $\mu_j,\rho_j$ be chosen Haar measures on the ambient group and $R_j$. Put $x_j>0$ for the ratio of the tuple's top measure to the term's top measure. The quotient cell represented by $b_jK_j$ has weight
$$
 w_j(b_j)=\frac{\mu_j(K_j)}
 {\rho_j(R_j\cap b_jK_jb_j^{-1})}.
$$
This follows by integrating the characteristic function of $b_jK_j$ along the fibers of the quotient by $R_j$. The inner integral on that cell is the denominator and vanishes away from the cell. [Definition of the quotient mass][weight]

In the finite period subgroup, project to the definite top group, with unipotent kernel $N_j$. The intersection in the denominator is a compact open subgroup. Every fiber over its projected image is a translate of its intersection with $N_j$. Haar disintegration therefore makes the denominator $A_j L_j(b_j)P_j(b_j)$, where $A_j>0$ depends on the chosen Haar measures, $L_j$ is the unipotent lattice volume and $P_j$ is the projected subgroup volume. For the represented cells the projected subgroup is the conjugate prescribed term level. Comparing it with the tuple level by their common compact open subgroup gives $P_j(b_j)=B_jx_j$, for a positive $B_j$ independent of the label. The unipotent lattice index gives $L_j(b_j)d_{j,\beta}=V_j$ with a fixed $V_j>0$. Here $d_{j,\beta}$ is the product over selected finite places of the inverse normalized volumes of the conjugated levels at $j$. One can compute the volume ratio by dividing the indices of the intersection in the two compact open lattices. The local coordinates on the unipotent radical identify these indices with the local inverse-volume factors. The reference lattice gives $V_j$. [Semidirect product and Haar disintegration][disint] · [Projected level and index comparison][prvol] · [Unipotent lattice and indices][unvol]

Substitution now gives an exact cancellation,
$$
 x_jw_j(b_j)=\frac{\mu_j(K_j)}{A_jB_jV_j}\,d_{j,\beta}.
$$
On every last-row node and every other top node the measure ratio is one. The change of measure in the iterated definite period multiplies the ratios for top nodes $0,\ldots,m-1$ and row nodes $0,\ldots,m$. Rational invariance allows changes of fundamental set at each step. Thus, after taking the product over the internal nodes, all label dependence in this volume calculation is $\prod_w\mathrm{coef}_{w,\beta}$. With orientation and the underlying choice of measures fixed, the remaining multiplier is independent of the tuple, the finite test and the cell. For $m=2$ these internal products are empty. [Change of measure through the actual period][remeasure] · [Local coefficients and product][prod] · [Exact cancellation][cellmass]

## Convergence in the range used for a square

One of the analytic inputs for the square comparison can be supplied separately. Let $M$ be the Hermitian space over which a theta kernel is integrated, $n=\dim_E M$, $d=\dim_E X$ the rank of its partner, and $f=[F:\mathbf Q]$. Suppose that $M$ has signature $(n-p,p)$ at one real place and $d<2(n-p)$. For every one of the prescribed Schwartz theta tests and either Hermitian sign, the integral of the **absolute value** over the rational quotient converges and is bounded by $C H_X(h)^N$ in the partner variable $h$. The constants may depend on this test, the groups and the Haar measure. This is the input called `SquareSt.WeilConvergence`. [Range and exact statement][sqconv] · [Passage to any Haar fundamental set][ssuse]

**Proof.** A rational totally isotropic subspace remains totally isotropic at the chosen real place, so the rational Witt index $r$ satisfies $r\le p$. Use a Siegel set with simple torus parameters $t_1,\ldots,t_r$ satisfying $t_j\ge c t_{j+1}$ and $t_r\ge c>0$. In a Witt polarization the action of the diagonal torus on the theta value is bounded by its half-determinant times a Schwartz sum on rational matrices with $d$ rows and $r$ columns. The half-determinant on the $f$ complex places over the real places equals $\prod_jt_j^{fd}$. The matrix sum is uniformly bounded in the chamber for fixed finite level and sufficiently high Schwartz seminorm. Indeed the finite support clears denominators into one rescaled integral lattice. At infinity the matrix columns are multiplied by $t_j$, each bounded below by a positive power of $c$ or by $1$ times a fixed constant. A Schwartz bound of degree exceeding the real lattice dimension makes the sum uniform by counting lattice points in shells. After translating in the partner group the lattice denominator and the necessary seminorm are bounded by fixed powers of $H_X(h)$: clear denominators in the conjugated Heisenberg lattice, and commute the real Heisenberg generators past the translating element. The compact remainder of the Siegel set only changes fixed constants. This gives $C H_X(h)^N\prod_jt_j^{fd}$. [Half-determinant and mixed kernel][torus] · [Lattice sum with explicit support and decay][tlat] · [Translation, level and seminorm on the given kernel][tker]

For the unitary parabolic, the inverse modular character times Lebesgue measure in these variables is $\prod_{j=1}^r t_j^{-2f(n-2j+1)}dt_j/t_j$. To see the exponent, the relative root spaces for $e_i\pm e_j$ have dimension two over $F$, those for $e_i$ have dimension $2(n-2r)$, and those for $2e_i$ have dimension one. Summing positive-root weights gives $2(n-2j+1)$ in coordinate $j$. Set $u_j=t_j/t_{j+1}$ for $j<r$ and $u_r=t_r$. The exponent of $u_j$ in the majorant, against $du_j/u_j$, is
$$
 \sum_{i=1}^j f\big(d-2(n-2i+1)\big)=fj(d-2n+2j)<0.
$$
The inequality holds for every $j\le r$ because $d<2(n-p)\le2(n-r)$. Each one-dimensional integral over $[c,\infty)$ converges. If $r=0$ the torus integral is the empty product. Finally rational invariance and coverage of the adelic group by rational translates of the Siegel set bound the integral over any measurable fundamental set by this estimate. The polynomial dependence on the partner is unchanged. The argument supplies the ordinary convergent input. The adjustment of a small-route test by a finite sum of translates of nonzero degree has its own matching requirement. [Cone exponents][tcone] · [Siegel-set quotient estimate][ssuse]

## The identity on the original row tests

The preceding definite computation begins with a term of the unfolded integral. The [opposite-path identity](../construction/ROW_SQUARE_PATH_FLIP.md) follows on the actual finite and real tensors by the [all-column square comparison](../construction/ROW_TOWER_INDUCTION.md). The [mixed quotient integrals](../construction/ROW_MIXED_CELL_UNFOLDING.md) converge in their prescribed order. Each nonzero value lies in one of the [disjoint finite cells](../construction/ROW_MIXED_CELL_SUPPORT.md). The flat and Fock comparisons identify its coefficient and a single real polynomial functional gives the same finite sum across all choices of term data. [Unfolding inputs][unfold] · [Actual use of the common real line and the $s_{AA}$ normalization][real]

For each square the [matched-test construction](../construction/ROW_MATCHED_SQUARE_TESTS.md) gives a split-place pair with local zeta value one and represents every local quotient class by the prescribed flip up to an endpoint block translation. With a nonzero global matched distribution and the indicated local matching inputs, it produces the actual block endpoints and finite tests with $d(z)I_b\ne0$. The [regularized-value argument](../construction/ROW_SQUARE_REGULARIZATION.md) shows how one comparison extends to every convergent translate. On a choice already satisfying the square law, $d(z)I_b=c_{ij,t}I_s(z)$ now forces both the small witness and the stored scalar to be nonzero. At the definite bottom the [anisotropic doubling argument](../construction/ROW_MATCHED_SQUARE_TESTS.md#nonzero-integrated-kernels-at-the-definite-bottom) gives nonzero integrals on both sides. The [compact comparison in grid coordinates](../construction/ROW_DEFINITE_GRID_TRANSFER.md) gives the last-column constant, including both changes of normalization. One step higher the [first beta comparison](../construction/ROW_FIRST_BETA_STEP.md) handles matched tests on compatible models of target Witt index one. The [two tower inductions](../construction/ROW_TOWER_INDUCTION.md) extend comparison and normalized transport through all remaining signatures. [Square law and exact witness][sq] · [Construction on the big route][big] · [Nonvanishing on both sides][const]

The opposite lift equals $z_t(\prod c_{ij,t})$ times the original row function. On applying the period, the mixed-cell unfolding of that opposite lift contributes $(z'_t)^{-1}s_{AA}$. The finite sum defining $v_{t,a}$ already includes $s_{AA}$. Each phase is in the subgroup generated by local values of the same finite-order character. The prescribed field contains the generators and their inverses. Their product is therefore $\iota(e_t)$, with $e_t$ a root of unity whose order divides the order of that character. These identities give the displayed formula with $\kappa=k(\sigma)$ on every good rational input. The [central projection and descent calculation](../construction/COMPOSITE_ROW_RATIONALITY.md#rational-tests-on-the-actual-weight-supported-column) extends the resulting readings to every rational source vector.
