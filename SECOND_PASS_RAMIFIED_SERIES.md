**Conclusion on these choices.** One can choose the enlarged origin, its normalized series reading, and its fitted character data together. If $u(v')$ is the contraction of an enlarged coefficient place and $s$ is the compatible seed chosen on the first pass, the fitted index at **every** $v'$ is $s'(v')=\operatorname{cast}(s(u(v')))$. At every active $v'$ and every interior row $j$, there is a semilinear comparison that carries a rational basis to a basis, spans the prescribed lattice over the local coefficient ring, and gives the exact theta identity. The auxiliary character, its support and its order parameter are the prescribed ones. No uniform bound for universal coefficients on arbitrary second-pass states is needed for this implication. ## Carrying all three rationality tests to the chosen origin

The old state's selected variable character is trivial. Its series theorem has a larger quantifier. The joint Hecke and level comparison runs through **every** admitted conductor $Q$, exponent $k$ and coefficient character satisfying the twisting condition, for each index, row and side. The enlarged bare model extends the large coefficient field, identifies the small fields, and copies the frame and the relevant twisting data. An admitted character on the copied setup pulls back to the original setup. Applying the old joint comparison there and transporting its level and Hecke equalities gives the joint comparison on the new setup, including $Q\ne\varnothing$. This is what the body of the series-restaging theorem does with the retained rows and split Hecke identities. [Enlarged bare model][copy] · [Transport of the joint comparison][join] · [Series restaging][restage]

There is a stronger observation at the indices used for fitting. A normalized rational witness consists of a nonzero scalar satisfying the extremal normalization and three tests on the resulting reading. They concern the base series, every admitted finite-character series over its specified field, and the universal coefficients over the base field with their prescribed support restrictions. Transport the **witness**, since the scalar-selection rule in a bare export is an implication conditional on existence of such a witness.

1. Put the old and enlarged real presentations into the common frame of the move. On changing the extremal vectors by factors $c_{j,\tau}$, their tensor changes by $C_j=\prod_\tau c_{j,\tau}$. Multiplying the scalar by $C_j$ keeps the normalized evaluation fixed. The extremal power condition and all rationality tests transport in both directions. [Common presentations][move] · [Change of scalar and evaluation][scale] · [Normalized witnesses under a move][nmove]
2. In the compatible frame the scalar can be kept. The finite theta kernel, translated generators, universal coefficients and harmonic lattice identify under the copy. Admissible harmonic data go in **both** directions, so the three tests identify for every scale and include all admissible new conductors. This comparison is for the copied local coefficient rings and their specified embeddings. [Test objects and admissible data][objects] · [Equivalence of the three tests][ratcopy]
3. Choose the full theta data on the intermediate state using its export law and the real theta transformation law. At this last restaging, the geometric vectors are fixed. Normalization and rationality again identify at each trial scalar, and the export retains the installed scalar, reading and joint law. The point-restaging argument still uses the geometric Hecke integrality, transformation and reading results. [Choosing the theta data][thetadata] · [Final equivalences and stored reading][theta] · [Integral geometric Hecke law][gint] · [Hecke transformation and reading][geolaws]

The active support of the new state is the inverse image of the old active support under contraction. For the compatible first-pass seed at each such old place one takes an active representative over the pair of small-field places. Its chosen index has a nonzero-index-place witness, using conjugation for the other side. Consequently the old normalized rational witness is available wherever this construction uses it. Transporting it through the three comparisons, then applying the scalar-selection rule, proves the finite-twist rationality statement for the **installed** new reading. The all-state caller takes the rationality component of its general rationality-and-bound theorem. Here the transported witness supplies that component on the chosen state. The argument uses the specified choice of origin and seed. [Representative used for the seed][seed] · [Nonzero index][nonzero] · [General rationality caller][ratall]

## Choosing the character on the fixed indices

Set $Q(v')=Q(P',v')$, $k(v')=T'.k(v')$ and $\chi(v')=T'.\psi(v')$. Coherence identifies $Q(v')$ with the prescribed ramification set. It contains split primes away from the coefficient characteristic $\ell$ and avoids the excluded set for $s'(v')$. Order divides $\ell^{k(v')}$, and the enlarged field contains a primitive root of that order bound. Coherence also identifies $k,\chi$ at equal small-field places, with the required conjugate-place identities. The pulled-back seed reads the selected place and is conjugation invariant. [Transport of the seed][scast] · [Actual coherence and exclusion checks][fitraw]

Artin reciprocity represents $\chi(v')$ by a Galois character in the enlarged field. Choose this character as a function of the pair $(\chi,\ell^k)$, obtaining compatible choices at conjugate places. Descent through the specified ring-class quotient produces the character valued in the units of the cyclotomic local coefficient ring. Pullback gives the required ramification test. Side embeddings, agreement up to the prescribed power, equality of the local levels and agreement of weights give the raw comparison and its rank. With $P'$ fixed this construction uses the local type, parahoric levels, joint-level and weight statements at these data. [Character descent and side embeddings][art] · [Rank, level and choice][raw] · [Application to the prescribed tuple][fitraw]

The character read in the theta tuple also includes the distinguished character selected for coefficient characteristic $\ell$. Its conductor and $Q(v')$ are disjoint, so the ramification of the product is their union, with the specified half of the split places. In each comparison the coefficient field contains the values required of the product. Dividing by the distinguished factor identifies the variable character. For an embedding $\epsilon$ of the local fraction field, extend its action on the large coefficient field to $\tau\in\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb C)$ fixing the embedded small field, and put $\iota=\tau^{-1}\epsilon$. The two local tuples are **conjugate** under $\tau$. Their characters can differ. [Ramification and tuple identification][loc] · [The two embeddings][emb]

## The local factor and the integral comparison

At a ramified place $P$ of the chosen half, write $q=|k_P|$, let $r$ be the determinant-block rank, let $\xi$ be its nontrivial residue character, and let $\lambda$ be its nontrivial residue additive character. Write
$$
 G_P(\xi)=\sum_{Y\in\mathrm{GL}_r(k_P)}
       \xi(\det Y)^{-1}\lambda(\mathrm{tr}\,Y).
$$
If $\xi'=\tau\xi$ and $\tau\lambda(t)=\lambda(mt)$, the substitution $Z=mY$ gives $\tau G_P(\xi)=\xi'(m)^rG_P(\xi')$. The full-rank matrix Gauss-sum formula gives $G_P(\xi)\overline{G_P(\xi)}=q^{r^2}$. Both factors are algebraic integers, so $G_P(\xi)$ is a unit at every prime over $\ell\ne\operatorname{char}k_P$. In the flat comparison the inverse Gauss factors contribute $\prod_P\xi'_P(m_P)^{-r}$. Dilation by $m_P^{-1}$ in the inverse determinant character of the Fourier test contributes $\prod_P\xi'_P(m_P)^r$. These determinant phases cancel at all the ramified primes, including those where the character changes under the two embeddings. The global cover and central-character comparisons can still contribute a root of unity. [Li and Hu, Theorem 2.1][lihu] · [Fourier multiplier][fourier] · [Flat vector][flat] · [Cancellation in the conjugate series][cancel]

Fix one active $v'$ and one interior $j$. The pinned complex comparison constructs a semilinear map $\Psi$, preserving linear independence, whose integral image spans the specified geometric lattice. It compares every tested theta expansion with its rational class sum up to a *single* scalar $z$ for this pair $(v',j)$. Here the proof uses the discrete-series and module comparisons, the fixed geometric fiber and coefficient action, the theta/class-sum comparison, and the just-described conjugation of the two tuples. Both $z$ and $z^{-1}$ are integral after inverting the product $N$ of the distinct residue cardinalities of the ramified half. This $N$ is a unit of the actual local ring, including at the distinguished primes by their separate avoidance condition. The rational finite-character test proved above puts the tested class sums in the specified coefficient-field reading. [Local tuple hypotheses][tuples] · [Complex comparison with one scalar][complex] · [Theta and class sums][thclass] · [Prime product][unitN] · [Rational class sums][rclass]

Lift $\Psi$ to the local fraction field using the injective reading and the identified integral span. Both sides $A,B$ of every tested theta identity are now defined over that field. Their readings satisfy $r(A)=z\,r(B)$. If some $B\ne0$, faithfulness of the scalar-extended reading forces $z=\epsilon(t)$ for a single field element $t$ and gives $A=tB$ for all tests. The two-sided denominator control makes $t$ a local unit. If all $B=0$, faithfulness gives $A=0$ throughout and one may use the unit $1$. Multiplying the lifted map by the inverse unit preserves its integral span and basis property and makes the theta identities exact. Installing the maps for all active $v'$ and interior $j$ leaves the chosen twisting indices equal to $s'$ everywhere. [Lifting and identifying the lattice][lift] · [Local-unit descent, including the zero case][desc] · [Installation on all active pairs][install]

## Finite level, good point and the order of the uniform requests

Fix the depth used in the origin. Under the point-comparison, Weil, discrete-series and geometric Galois inputs of [the localization proof](SECOND_PASS_THETA_REALIZATION.md), the fitted character level just constructed can realize every advance coefficient request. Combine the largeness and congruence-index requests and choose one localization. Its congruence identity holds for every active place and interior row, with zero on the inactive fibers. The full reader, local character and list at that depth belong to this localization. The finite-level constructor returns the prescribed active set and compares its new character with the fitted one. In particular the equality of twisting indices established above is available when transferring the all-powers inequalities. The old saturation and equality of active supports under contraction give the required saturation on the new level. A change of primitive root costs a power coprime to the coefficient characteristic, which preserves the all-powers property. This produces the strengthened third level on the chosen column. Under the fourth-stage volume, representation and integral-kernel inputs, [the good-core construction](SECOND_PASS_GOOD_CORE.md) preserves its character, theta family, depth and inequalities through both changes of realization. It yields one core with its kernel certificate and its goodness witness on the same object. [Finite-level choice and retention][finite] · [Saturation, conductor and power on this level][power] · [Conversion to the good point][good]

The outer assertion runs over **every** built untwisted core with its export in the eventual range, every finite avoidance request, and every algebraic final-field request depending on the terminal character set. At each such core choose the old normalized reading at its retained origin and the seed described above. The small-field and allowed-prime requests for the second construction must already be imposed before retwisting. Then union the remaining avoidance requests pointwise and union the final-field requests as functions of the terminal character set. The restriction-of-places argument preserves that character set. This places the output in the required range of the second construction while meeting the caller's requests. Hence if the cited starting, bottom-test, representation, geometric and fourth-stage inputs are available with these eventual quantifiers, the argument gives the good-point producer on this range. It leaves no additional matching between independently chosen finite-level origins. [Definition of the two passes][passes] · [Combining the four kinds of requests](continuation_v78/producer.tex#L218-L272)

In particular the preliminary datum on the actual restricted fine stage remains an analytic premise in an independent proof of this uniform result. The global common matching quotient and proportionality of B8.11, separation for the family of bottom maps in B8.15, and ramified twisted-square comparisons of B8.16 must hold on these stages with the required advance field request. The fitted maps proved here assume the resulting preliminary datum.
