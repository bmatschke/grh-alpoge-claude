# The residual Galois representation of an automorphic row

An integral Galois representation attached to an automorphic form can change its appearance when one changes its stable lattice and reduces modulo a prime. The simple constituents, counted with multiplicity, are intrinsic because the Frobenius polynomials of the form determine them. The same principle applies here even when two integral representations come from different Hecke localizations. The labels they must share are the automorphic row $\sigma$ and the **coefficient place** $\lambda$. Different places above the same rational prime can give different reductions. [Classical modular-form example, Ribet–Stein, §18.1][rs]

## From split Frobenius classes to every Galois element

Fix the CM field $E$, a row of rank $r$ and a coefficient place $\lambda$ of residue characteristic $\ell$. A realization of the row consists of a local Hecke factor $i$ above $\lambda$, an automorphic list $\Sigma$ and a continuous representation $\rho:G_E\to\mathrm{GL}_r(O_i)$, where $O_i$ is the factor's coefficient ring. The list has positive block sizes $(a,d)$ with $\sum ad=r$. Its parameter at every place $v$ split over $E^+$ equals the given parameter of the row at $v$. The representation is semisimple over the fraction field of $O_i$. At all but finitely many $v$, the polynomial of each lift of arithmetic Frobenius is, under the chosen embedding into $\mathbb C$, the product of $X-\alpha$ over the Frobenius eigenvalues of the list's local parameter twisted by $(1-r)/2$. These are the precise realization and normalization used below. [Automorphic list][list] · [Rank and row parameters][row] · [Frobenius comparison][frob]

**Claim.** Whenever such a realization exists, there is a unique isomorphism class of continuous semisimple representations $\bar r_{\sigma,\lambda}:G_E\to\mathrm{GL}_r(\overline{\mathbb F}_\ell)$ whose characteristic polynomial at every $g\in G_E$ agrees with the reduction of the polynomial of every realization. Here the residual field has the discrete topology, so continuity means that the representation has open kernel.

To compare two realizations $(i,\Sigma,\rho)$ and $(i',\Sigma',\rho')$, discard the finite exceptional set in both Frobenius comparisons. At every remaining split place their two complex Frobenius polynomials coincide because each uses the row's parameter and the same twist. Their coefficients belong to the valuation ring $B_\lambda$ of the specified place in $\mathbb C$. Reducing them in this one ring and applying its fixed residue identification gives equal polynomials in $\overline{\mathbb F}_\ell[X]$. On $O_i$ and $O_{i'}$ that procedure gives exactly the prescribed residue embeddings. This is where the choice of the same place matters. [Compatibility of the coefficient embeddings](LOCAL_AUTOMORPHIC_SPECIALIZATION.md#reduction-at-the-chosen-coefficient-place)

Each reduced representation has open kernel because reducing its matrix to the identity imposes finitely many open congruences on its continuous entries. Both reductions therefore factor through the same finite quotient of $G_E$, obtained by intersecting the kernels. In this quotient every conjugacy class occurs at one of the allowed split places. Indeed, take the Galois closure over $E^+$ of the extension cutting out the quotient, lift an element of the quotient to an element fixing $E$, and apply Chebotarev over $E^+$. The prime splits in $E/E^+$ because its Frobenius fixes $E$, and hence the two residue fields agree. By choosing a prime in the Galois closure with the specified Frobenius, its restriction gives the prescribed class in the quotient. Thus the polynomials of the two reductions agree on the whole quotient. [Milne, *Algebraic Number Theory*, Theorem 8.31][cheb] · [Split-place argument with Frobenius lifts](LOCAL_HECKE_SPECIALIZATION.md#why-split-primes-suffice-to-test-a-galois-character)

Choose a realization, reduce its matrices using the specified residue embedding, and take the direct sum of the simple factors of a composition series over $\overline{\mathbb F}_\ell$. Every matrix preserving the series is block triangular, so this semisimplification has the same characteristic polynomial at every element and still factors through the finite quotient. For a finite group over a perfect field, the Brauer–Nesbitt theorem identifies semisimple representations having identical characteristic polynomials on all elements. This proves the claim, including when $\ell\le r$. Traces alone can lose multiplicities in that range. For example, $\ell$ copies of the trivial character and $\ell$ copies of a nontrivial character of prime-to-$\ell$ order both have zero trace in characteristic $\ell$. [Brauer–Nesbitt, stated in Anschütz, Theorem 10.11][bn]

## Where this applies in the proof

The construction requires a representation at **every** pair $(\sigma,\lambda)$. When the row has no realization at that place, assign the trivial representation of rank $r$. It is semisimple and has open kernel. The comparison with all realizations is then vacuous. When a realization does exist, the claim gives precisely the universal comparison called `RbarTie`. Choosing a matrix model in each isomorphism class gives the required family. The existence of a characteristic-zero lift on the rows where the proof uses one has its own arithmetic argument. [Original choice and its two cases][choice] · [Exact comparison][tie] · [Open kernel and semisimplicity][ker] · [Construction of a lift on good rows](continuation_v86/residual.tex#L343-L402)

For example, at a designated place of residue characteristic $g\ne\ell$, suppose a realized row has finite-order eigenvalues at the chosen tame generator. Its residual polynomial retains every eigenvalue with its multiplicity, reducing each root of unity to the image of its prime-to-$\ell$ part. If the polynomial at every wild inertia element is $(X-1)^r$, the wild inertia image is a finite $g$-group of unipotent matrices in characteristic $\ell$. Each such matrix has $\ell$-power order, so this image is trivial. These are the two uses of the all-elements comparison when the local type of an actual good row has been identified. [Tame and wild inertia for the chosen row](continuation_v86/rowocc.tex#L56-L111)
