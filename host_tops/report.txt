STATUS AND SCOPE

This component proves the downstream finite and algebraic joins from the
biased top-set theorem to the actual host predicates, then to the actual
point and family predicates. It also reconstructs the separate thick-set
construction, the auxiliary-prime selections conditional on the exact
analytic prime inputs, the HostParam.Seam witness, and the elementary
character/numerical parts of hostOfFieldOf and supplyOfAdmOf.

The precise retained inputs are stated in HC9–HC10. In particular, this
does not prove ShortNorm, SmallOne, ThinH, PairEx, VWinAt, or the existence
of the requisite HostAx/character field. A supplied HostAx already carries
substantial Galois and different-ideal facts. Conversely, the source's
predicate named `admitted` requires no further theorem once that HostAx
is supplied: HC4 proves its literal statement by choosing existing
fields. The predicate name is not a Lean `admit` or an assertion about an
unproved placeholder.

BS5 supplies one finite set with all three required properties; BS6
supplies its prime-weighted simplification. The BO probability proof is
used through BS5. No independence of prime-indexed bad events, no
rotation invariance, and no kernel replay are asserted here. The selector
bs(h,t) may vary with t, and the absolute even-index condition is retained.

The exact source declarations, inclusive line ranges, full-file and
range hashes, and pinned Git-blob checks are in provenance.json and
source_locators.txt. This text explains mathematical content rather
than repeating the source's wrapper chain.

HC1. SMALL-PRIME SPLITTING GIVES THE ACTUAL UNIFORM L2 BOUND

Fix C0>=1 and put Co=max(C0,2). Suppose RankVal holds, J<=C0,
o<=C0, Q<=C0n, x>=16, x^(1/J)<=Y<=x, and PairRow(Y,epsilon,H)
holds. PairRow says, among other things, that every prime
p<=Y^(epsilon/5) has Kronecker symbol (−d/p)=1.

An inert-class prime cannot be 2: the host has 2^(e+2)|Q, so 2 is
in the designated small-prime set and not in the inert class. For an
odd prime in the inert class, the prime-class definition gives the
nonsplit quadratic symbol. Thus PairRow implies

  p>Y^(epsilon/5)>=x^(epsilon/(5Co)).                 (HC1.1)

The second inequality follows from J<=Co, J>=1 and x>1. It applies
to every inert prime, not merely those below Xprime.

For such a p, log p>epsilon log x/(5Co). RankVal and x>=16 give

  4 log(k x^26)
    <=4 log2+(122+40epsilon)log x
    <=(123+40/52)log x
    <124 log x.

Consequently

  log_p((k x^26)^4)<=620Co/epsilon.                  (HC1.2)

The actual L1(p) is the natural ceiling of the left side. Therefore
L1(p)<=620Co/epsilon+1 as a real number. Since these are natural
numbers, their supremum over inert primes is bounded by the natural
floor of that real upper bound. The actual Ltwo is twice this
supremum, so

  Ltwo<=Lb:=2(620Co/epsilon+1).                      (HC1.3)

If the indexing set is empty, its natural supremum is 0 and the
same assertion holds. No unproved boundedness of a set of primes is
needed. The group element h_p and TT do not enter this calculation.
This is the mathematical content of topsBase.logb_le', ltwo_le' and
topsChoiceH.lt_of_classC0'.

HC2. THE BIASED SET IS THE LITERAL HOST TT, WITH HostAx UNCHANGED

Apply BS5 with A=the Frame group, A0=its distinguished subgroup,
Co=max(C0,2), and Lb from HC1. The identities |A|=Frame.ord and
[A:A0]=Frame.o give the source's topsFrame specialization. Its
weights are delta on A0 and 3delta/4 on the other cosets. The target
coset proportions are

  lambda_gamma=4/(3o+1) if gamma in A0,
               3/(3o+1) otherwise.

For the host take h_p=hPH(H,p), the actual Frobenius step, and the
actual finite prime set

  P={p<ceil(Xprime): p prime, cls(p)=inertClass,
                         ord(h_p)>=x^(epsilon/4)}.

Equivalently, p<Xprime: for natural p, p<ceil(Xprime) iff p<Xprime.
Use weights omega_p=log(p)/p; they are nonnegative, and p>=2 on P.
The actual canonical bsH(h,t) satisfies bsH(h,t)+i h=t for some
natural i. In its defining witness branch this is part of the choice;
in the other branch bsH(h,t)=t and i=0. It is fixed before TT is
chosen and can depend on t.

For p in P, the actual stepP is h_p, not 2h_p. Indeed stepP doubles
only in the separately designated Sg class; an inert-class prime is
not Sg. The actual period is ord(h_p), and its selected positions are

  {i in {0,...,ord(h_p)−1}: bsH(h_p,t)+i h_p in TT}.

The definitions of Arith.Loc.Good and the good-orbit predicate used
by BS5 coincide on this set, with L2=Ltwo, K=ceil(8(h+2)/epsilon),
r=K+1, delta and h as above. This r is the
good-orbit parameter rE, not the rank r=k+m−1. No orbit event is
identified across different choices of t. Thus the bad top set is
literally

  B_p(TT)={t in TT: not Good(base(H[TT]),h_p,
                                    bsH(stepP(base(H[TT]),h_p,p),t))}.

For constants Cb>0 and x0 fixed before the scale, ranks, host and
Frobenius steps, one obtains simultaneously

  delta n/2<=|TT|<=2delta n,
  | |TT intersect(gamma+A0)|−lambda_gamma |TT| |
                         <=x^(−1+epsilon)|TT| for every gamma,
  sum_(p in P)(log p/p)|B_p(TT)|
       <=4Cb x^(−2) delta n sum_(p in P)log p/p.      (HC2.1)

The last line is exactly BadOK(s,Cb,H[TT]), not a substitute averaged
notion. The middle line is exactly Frame.Shares. BS5 actually gives
the stronger error x^(−1)|TT|, which can be retained throughout.

The host update H[TT]={H with TT:=TT} changes only this Choices
field. Its HostAx, field, Galois map, q,d,Q, prime classes, Frobenius
steps, rank data, T2, level primes and half-different ideal stay
identical. In particular hPH(H[TT],p)=hPH(H,p), and the defining
set of Ltwo and the prime set P stay identical. This checks the
potential circularity: the input data used to select TT do not
change when it is installed.

The quantifiers are the literal topsChoiceH order: for fixed
epsilon and C0 choose Cb,x0; then for every large scale and every
Host with the stated RankVal/HostConst and one PairRow window,
there exists TT with (HC2.1). Taking a maximum with 16 and the

prime.

HC3. EXACT TRANSPORT TO POINTS, ChosenTT, ChosenBad, AND CHARACTER SHARES

Suppose a constructed point p has Pt.From(H,a,b,c,u0). This supplies
equality of the ranks, equality of the TT images under the injective
Frame coordinate map val, equal q,d, equal HostAx after the rank
identification, and equality of every Frobenius step's val coordinate.
After identifying ranks, val-injectivity gives equality of the actual
sets TT and of the actual steps h_p, not merely equality of their
cardinalities. HostAx equality gives equality of the prime classes.

The arithmetic base used in BadOK is baseOf(s,0,0,H). It depends
only on those equal rank, scale, q,d,class and TT fields; the 0,0
parameters are fixed on both sides. Consequently its candidate set,
each L1 and Ltwo, the period, selected positions, stepP and Good are
the same on the host and point sides. If the point's instance has
the canonical selector X.bs(h,t)=bsH(h,t), its badTops is precisely
the host B_p(TT). Its weighted sum and right-hand side therefore
are termwise equal. This proves the literal chBad_of_from theorem.
No off-diagonal, degree, or canonical-kappa assumption is used here;
only the selector part of CanonInst is needed.

Likewise the first line of HC2.1 is exactly ChTT_at at the point's
own scale. Its coset-share statement transfers because the same
Frame, HostAx and TT have been identified. More generally every
predicate of (scale,Frame,HostAx,TT,t) transfers in this way: this
is FReq.at_of_from. In particular EAtH, including its shares
field, and the separate cohomological bound VVle are transported
when they have actually been supplied.

Now suppose an actual family is built by copying such points with
these fields preserved, as in the existing KitS/DesignS route.
Apply the pointwise statement at every family index. It yields
ChosenTT and ChosenBad(Cb) with one family threshold. In the source
famOfKits construction the pointwise properties are already stored
at every index, so DesignS uses threshold 0 for these two predicates.
This is a conditional transfer theorem about a supplied family;
it does not construct the points, NumAt, or the family itself.

BS6 gives a useful literal numerical consequence for the same TT:

  sum_(p in P)(log p/p)|B_p(TT)|
    <=16Cb |TT| x^(−2+epsilon/3)
    <=|TT| x^(−2+epsilon),                           (HC3.1)

provided x^(2epsilon/3)>=16Cb. Its proof uses the elementary prime
harmonic estimate and delta n<=2|TT|. The source candidate-set
normalization is preserved exactly. When o=1, A0=A and the shares
error is identically zero; this statement concerns the shares,
not the existence of the arithmetic host.

HC5. TWO NORM PRIMES AND THREE SMALL AUXILIARY PRIMES

5.1 Exact analytic inputs, retained as inputs.

ShortNorm asserts that absolute A,c,c'>0 exist such that for
d prime, d=3 mod4, d>=100, M>=3, X>=1,

  log(4dM²)<=c sqrt(log X),
  exp(−c sqrt(log X))<=h<=1

imply

  #{prime p in (X,X(1+h)]: NormOne(d,M,p)}
       >=c' h X/((4dM²)^A log X).                    (HC5.1)

Here NormOne is the actual integral equation/congruence predicate

  A1²+d B1²=4p, M|B1, 2M|(A1−2−B1).               (HC5.2)

This is much stronger than merely requiring a prime to split in a
quadratic field. We do not replace HC5.1 by a splitting-density
heuristic. SmallOne asserts that one absolute exponent a>=1 gives,
for every M>=2, a prime p=1 mod M with p<=M^a. Neither analytic
input is proved by this component. Their concrete first-party
suppliers are recorded in the ledger.

5.2 A uniform dyadic norm-prime lower bound.

Assume ModAt(x,epsilon,N,d): d prime, d=3 mod4, d>=100,
N>0, d|N and log N<=3x^(epsilon/5)(log x)². Put u=x^(epsilon/5).
For large x depending only on epsilon and the ShortNorm constants,
require

  (log x)²<=u,
  u>=max(10000,50A,13/c²), 40exp(−u/20)<=c'.

Then B=4dN²>1 and log B<=log4+3log N<=10u². If log Y>=8x^epsilon
=8u^5, the inequality c²u>=13 gives log B<=c sqrt(log Y).
Thus ShortNorm can be applied with h=1. To compare its lower bound
with Y^(9/10), write v=log Y. We have

  A log B<=u³/5,
  log v<=38+v/40,
  log c'>=−u/20.

For u>=10000 and v>=8u^5,
u³/5+38+u/20<=3v/40. Hence

  A log B+log v<=log c'+v/10,
  B^A log Y<=c'Y^(1/10).

Substitution in HC5.1 proves, uniformly in all such N,d,Y,

  #{NormOne primes in (Y,2Y]}>=Y^(9/10).             (HC5.3)

5.3 The exceptional BB sets and the disjointness count.

The source defines

  Exc(g,d,ell): ell prime and
                 g^d<ell^(4 v_ell(g^d−1)),
  BB(r,g)={g} union union_(1<=d<=r){ell:Exc(g,d,ell)}.

For fixed g>=2,d>=1, there are at most three exceptional primes.
Indeed their prime-power contributions divide g^d−1; four such
contributions, each exceeding (g^d)^(1/4), would have product
exceeding g^d. Thus |BB(r,g)|<=3r+1.

Fix Y>=2 and a prime ell. Among integers g in [Y,2Y] satisfying
Exc(g,d,ell), choose a minimum w=v_ell(g^d−1). It is positive and
Y<(ell^w)^4. Every such g solves z^d=1 modulo ell^w. There are at
most 2d such residues: for odd ell the unit group is cyclic and
the number of roots is gcd(d,phi(ell^w))<=d; for powers of 2 its
unit group is cyclic in the first two cases and is
C2 x C_(2^(w−2)) thereafter, giving at most 2d. Each residue class
contains at most Y/ell^w+1<=Y^(3/4)+1 integers in [Y,2Y]. It follows
that the number of g with ell in BB(r,g) is at most

  1+2r²(Y^(3/4)+1).

The 1 covers g=ell; summing 2d over d<=r is safely bounded by 2r².
For fixed g0 in [Y,2Y], union over its at most 3r+1 BB primes gives

  #{g in [Y,2Y]:BB(r,g) intersects BB(r,g0)}
     <=(3r+1)[1+2r²(Y^(3/4)+1)]
     <=20r³Y^(3/4).                                 (HC5.4)

If 20r³<Y^(3/20), this is strictly less than Y^(9/10).
Therefore any set of at least Y^(9/10) candidates contains two
whose BB sets are disjoint. They are distinct because g belongs
to BB(r,g).

Now take Y=(k exp(x^epsilon))^8, k>=1,1<=r<=x^5.
Then log Y>=8x^epsilon, so HC5.3 applies. Eventually
21x^15<=exp(x^epsilon)<=Y^(3/20), which gives the strict bound
in HC5.4. We have thus constructed two prime values g0,g1 in
[Y,2Y], both satisfying the exact NormOne predicate, with
BB(r,g0) disjoint from BB(r,g1). This proves the substantive
typePrimesN consequence with all its separation conditions.

5.4 Three different auxiliary primes from SmallOne.

Let a>=1 be the exponent in SmallOne and N>=30. Apply it successively

  p=1 mod15N, p'=1 mod(15Np), q0=1 mod(Npp'),

  q0<=N^C1, C1=(1+2a+(2a+2)a)a=2a³+4a²+a.          (HC5.5)

The congruences modulo the earlier primes force all three primes
to be different. C1 also bounds the first two exponents. Hence

Thus N^C1<b=exp(x^epsilon)<(kb)^8. The auxiliary primes differ

HC6. THE SEPARATE THICK SECOND SET, WITH CT2=64

We prove the source's elementary finite construction rather than
assuming a large no-relation set. For n>=0,L>=2, let

  Q(n,L)=n exp(−32 sqrt(log n log L))/L.

The source uses totalized log0=0; for n=0 choose the empty set.
For n>0 and Q(n,L)<=1 choose {0}. Otherwise put

  x=log n, y=log L, s=sqrt(xy),
  S=floor(s/y), a=(x−y−3)/S,
  d=floor(exp(a)/(4L)), B=4Ld.

Q>1 implies x>32s+y. Therefore s/y>32, S>=32, s>16,
Sy<=s<(S+1)y, a<=2s and a>=y+7. For the last inequality one may
write R=s/y>32, use S<=R and y>1/2 to bound
(x−y−3)/S >= Ry−y/R−3/R > y+7.
It follows that exp(a)>=8L and

  exp(a)/(8L)<=d<=exp(a)/(4L), d>=1,
  3+S(7+y)+S+2a<=32s.                               (HC6.1)

For completeness, the latter follows from Sy<=s, S<=s/y<2s,
a<=2s: the left side is at most 3+21s<32s.

Take the box {0,...,d−1}^S. There are at most Sd² possible values
of the squared Euclidean norm, so one sphere contains at least
d^S/(Sd²) vectors. Map each such vector v to

  b(v)=sum_(j=0)^(S−1) v_j B^j.

The map is injective. Its values obey 4L b(v)<n because
B^S<=exp(aS)=n/(L exp3) and 4<exp3.
If three distinct images satisfied
j(b(v2)−b(v1))=i(b(v3)−b(v1)) with 0<|i|,|j|<=L and i!=j,
every coefficient in the base-B identity would have absolute
value <2Ld<B. Repeated reduction modulo B makes every coefficient
zero. Hence the three vectors would be collinear. A line meets
a Euclidean sphere in at most two points, so this is impossible.
Equivalently, expanding equal squared norms yields
i(i−j) sum(v3−v1)²=0 and then v2=v1. This is exactly NoRel(T,L).

Finally n=exp(aS)L exp3 and d>=exp(a)/(8L). Since log(8L)<=7+y,
HC6.1 gives

  exp3 (8L)^S S exp(2a)<=exp(32s).

Combining this with the sphere lower bound proves

  |T|>=n exp(−32 sqrt(log n log L))/L.               (HC6.2)

This is hostThickExists with the explicit absolute constant32.

Apply this at n=n0=|A0| and L=Lwv, where

  Lwv=10ceil(405log x)+2ceil(30/epsilon)+22,
  2<=Lwv<=(4084+60/epsilon)log x                    (HC6.3)

for log x>=1. Suppose the actual size bounds hold, o<=Co and
x^h/2<=n<=Co x^h. Then
x^h/(2Co)<=n0<=Co x^h and log Lwv=O_epsilon(log log x), uniformly
over the data. Put E=exp(32 sqrt(log n0 log Lwv)). For fixed
epsilon,Co and the coefficient in HC6.3, choose one large x-threshold
such that

  E>=2, 4Co Lwv E<=x^epsilon,
  x^(−epsilon)n>=1.

The middle inequality follows from
log E=O(sqrt(log x log log x))=o(log x). The first follows uniformly
from log n0>=h log x−log(2Co) and log Lwv>=log2; the third follows
from n>=x^h/2 and h−epsilon>0.
If Q0=n0/(Lwv E), these inequalities give Q0>=4x^(−epsilon)n>=4.
Erase 0 from the set in HC6.2. At most one element is lost, so
its remaining size is at least Q0−1>=Q0/2, hence at least both
Q0/E and Q0/4. Therefore the remaining T2 satisfies

  4Lwv a<n0 for a in T2; NoRel(T2,Lwv);
  |T2|>=n0 exp(−64 sqrt(log n0 log Lwv))/Lwv;
  |T2|>=x^(−epsilon)n;
  4Lwv|T2|<=n0.                                    (HC6.4)

The last assertion follows because all remaining natural numbers
are positive and less than n0/(4Lwv); a set of such numbers has
cardinality at most its largest member. Thus CT2=64 is valid for
the actual source secondSet_card_lower_bound. All thresholds are
uniform over Frames with the displayed bounds. TT and T2 remain
independent choices serving their respective predicates.

HC8. NORM ELEMENTS, SPLITTING, AND REAL-CHARACTER BOXES

8.1 The NormOne equation really produces the required integral element.

Let d=3 mod4, let s²=−d in the supplied field, and let conjugation
send s to −s. Set omega=(1+s)/2, an algebraic integer because
omega²−omega+(d+1)/4=0. If HC5.2 holds, write B1=M u and
A1−2−B1=2M v. Then

  pi=(A1+B1 s)/2=1+M(v+u omega)

is in O_E, lies in K=Q(s), satisfies pi conjugate(pi)=p, and
pi−1 is in M O_E. Also p=1 mod M: writing d=4a+3 and expanding
its norm gives

  p−1=M[u+2v+M((a+1)u²+uv+v²)].                   (HC8.1)

8.2 Full splitting uses only the supplied HostAx Frobenius law.

If a prime p=1 mod N=Qqd, then p is coprime to N. Its Artin value
is 1 by the given cyclotomic Artin law, and HostDer.frob says its
arithmetic Frobenius is the identity and its inertia is trivial.
The decomposition group is generated by Frobenius and inertia,
so it is trivial. The Galois group acts transitively on the primes
above p; orbit-stabilizer therefore gives [E:Q] such primes. This
is exactly the source full-splitting count, including conversion
between nonzero prime ideals and finite places. Moreover 4|Q,
p=1 mod4 and p=1 mod d imply (−d/p)=1 by quadratic reciprocity.
The prime-class definition therefore places p in splitK. Every
chosen level prime in HC5 is 1 mod N, so these conclusions apply.
This uses the already-supplied Frobenius/inertia and Galois fields;
it is not a construction of global reciprocity.

8.3 PairGood supplies the actual QDq and Boxq clauses.

The three characters in QDq are the quadratic characters at q,d
and their product. On units modulo 2Mqd, their Kronecker formulas
are the respective twists with i,j=(1,0),(0,1),(1,1), by quadratic
reciprocity. PairGood.bounds with the principal character modulo M
therefore gives |gChi(x)|<=x^(1/2+epsilon/2) and
|L'/L(1)|<=4log x. The former is <=x^(1/2+epsilon). This proves
the literal QDq(x,epsilon,4,q,d).

The six Boxq cases additionally twist by the real character of
conductor8 or by the trivial one. At coprime conductors, the product
of primitive characters is primitive: CRT lets a smaller period
be tested on one factor at a time. Thus the conductor is f q^i d^j,
with f|8. Its real-valuedness is immediate, and

  f q^i d^j<=8 exp(z) exp(z(log Y)²)^j.

PairGood.boxes applies since f|8M. The zero-free rectangle it gives
has at least this height. Agreement on units determines the unique
primitive inducing character, so it applies to every primitive
character quantified by GoodAllFor. This proves the six literal
Boxq clauses without an extra analytic assumption beyond PairGood.

8.4 All real characters involving q or d have the required boxes.

If Q|lcm(2^(e+2),M), then every prime dividing Q divides 2M, and
the odd part Qodd divides M. Both statements follow by first using
Q|2^(e+2)M and then cancelling the coprime power of2 from Qodd.

A primitive real Dirichlet character with conductor F|Qqd splits
uniquely as rho times the quadratic characters at q and d:

  chi=rho (./q)^i(./d)^j,
  F=cond(rho)q^i d^j, i,j in {0,1},
  cond(rho)|8Qodd.                                 (HC8.2)

Here is the elementary justification of the conductor assertion.
By CRT, separate the prime-power conductor factors. A real unit
character takes only the values +/-1. At an odd prime ell, the
kernel of reduction modulo ell is an odd-order ell-group, hence
maps trivially to {+/-1}; thus the primitive exponent is at most1.
At 2, the subgroup of squares contains every unit congruent1 mod8,
so the exponent is at most3. At the distinct odd primes q,d,
the only nontrivial real character is the Legendre character.
Peeling these factors gives HC8.2; primitivity and coprime moduli
give the exact conductor product. These are the contents of
realSplit/realCond used here, with no analytic zero-free claim.

Every host character has conductor dividing Qqd, since its
primitive character induces the supplied Artin character at that
modulus. If q|F or d|F, at least one of i,j is nonzero in HC8.2,
and j=1 iff d|F. Hence PairGood.boxes supplies its ZeroBox at height

  H_M(j)=8M exp(z)exp(z(log Y)²)^j,

while its conductor is <=H_Qodd(j)<=H_M(j). Restricting the box
to H_Qodd(j) proves the actual BoxH clauses. On the real axis it
gives L(chi,beta)!=0 for beta>1−1/400, which is RealBox.

Finally d's quadratic character is nontrivial and primitive modulo
the prime d. It induces chDir(d), so the QDq logarithmic-derivative
bound gives |dlogH|<=4log x<=x^epsilon for large x. This explains
the actual dlog field, not an unrelated logarithmic derivative.
