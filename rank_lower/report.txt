2. RD2: weighted pairs and the elementary tail shape
---------------------------------------------------
For a dominant integer weight nu of length d, let

 w_d(nu)=product_(u<v) (nu_u-nu_v+v-u)/(v-u),
 S_d(g)=binom(g+d-1,d-1) for g>=0,
 D(A)=S_k(A)*product_i S_k(A_i)S_k(C_i).

A pair consists of a signed Pieri chain mu^0,...,mu^(2s+1) of
length-k weights, starting at zero, with vector degrees
A,A_0,...,A_(s-1) and intervening covector degrees C_0,...,C_(s-1),
and a length-r weight ell interlacing its endpoint:

 mu^(2s+1)_(t+1) <= ell_t <= mu^(2s+1)_t.                (RD2.1)

Here A_i is indexed interior data, while A0 without an underscore denotes
the segment base in (RD1.1); the initial chain degree A can be smaller.
A vector horizontal strip nu/mu of size g means nu_t>=mu_t>=nu_(t+1)
and sum(nu)-sum(mu)=g. A covector step reverses this relation.
Every chain and terminal interlacing weight is dominant, so w_r(ell)>0.
RU2 and RU3 give the exact finite weighted identity

                  sum_pairs w_r(ell)=D(A).             (RD2.2)

This is a weighted identity; treating the pair distribution as uniform
would give the wrong count.

Delete the first coordinate and put K^n_t=mu^n_(t+1). Define

 c_i=mu^(2i+1)_0, rho_i=K^(2i+2)_0,
 W_i=sum K^(2i+2)-sum K^(2i+1),
 O_i=c_i+A_i-c_(i+1), S_i=O_i-W_i,
 u_i=O_i-rho_i, n_i=-ell_(r-s+i),
 D=sum ell-sum K^(2s+1).                                (RD2.3)

The source names are pairC, pairRho, pairW, pairO, pairSg, pairN and
pairD. The scalar D here is terminal extra degree, not the dimension D(A).
Restricting a horizontal strip to the last r coordinates proves

 0<=W_i<=A_i, 0<=S_i<=C_i,
 K^(2i+2)/K^(2i+1) is a vector strip of size W_i,
 K^(2i+2)/K^(2i+3) is a strip of size C_i-S_i,
 rho_i<=c_i,c_(i+1), c_0=A,
 c_(i+1)=c_i+A_i-u_i-rho_i.                             (RD2.4)

The first tail K^1 is zero. At a vector step K^n_t<=K^(n+1)_t and
K^(n+1)_(t+1)<=K^n_t. At a covector step these become
K^(n+1)_t<=K^n_t and K^n_(t+1)<=K^(n+1)_t. Inductively, after i pairs
the first i coordinates of K^(2i+1) are nonnegative, the middle
i<=t<r-i are zero, and the last i are nonpositive. This induction works
because 2s+1<=r; there is always a middle zero coordinate available.
In particular all c_i and rho_i are nonnegative.

The top tail coordinate before step i is at most sum_(l<i)W_l.
A vector strip increases this coordinate by at most its total degree,
and a covector strip cannot increase it. Consequently, by (RD1.6),

 K^(2i+1)_0<=4A_i/p^2,
 0<=rho_i<=A_i(1+4/p^2), u_i>=-4A_i/p^2.                (RD2.5)

For the second inequality use rho_i<=K^(2i+1)_0+W_i. For the third,
S_i-K^(2i+1)_0<=O_i-rho_i, and S_i>=0.

Define negative mass m_-(K)=sum_t max(0,-K_t). Vector steps reduce it;
a covector strip of degree g increases it by at most g, since coordinate
by coordinate max(0,-x)<=max(0,-y)+(y-x) for x<=y. Thus
m_-(K^(2i+3))<=sum_(l<=i)C_l. Following the lower interlacing boundary
through each subsequent pair and through (RD2.1) gives

 0<=n_i<=-K^(2i+3)_(r-1)<=sum_(l<=i)C_l
                         <=C_i(1+4/p^2).               (RD2.6)

For the boundary-following step, each vector step can move a lower
coordinate upward by at most one index in the interlacing inequalities,
while the intervening covector step moves it downward at the same index.
Iterating from the last coordinate at time 2i+3 to index r-s+i at the
terminal weight gives ell_(r-s+i)>=K^(2i+3)_(r-1). The sign follows from
the zero middle coordinates. The analogous upper-boundary induction is

 K^(2s+1)_(t-1)<=rho_(s-t),  1<=t<=s.

Combining it with terminal interlacing gives

 0<=ell_0<=c_s;
 0<=ell_t<=rho_(s-t) for 1<=t<=s;
 ell_t=0 for s<t<r-s;
 ell_(r-s+i)=-n_i<=0.                                  (RD2.7)

3. RD3: a uniform finite pair count
-----------------------------------
More generally, if every interior A_i,C_i<=L, the coordinates at chain
time n have absolute value at most A+nL: a strip changes each coordinate
by at most its degree. Terminal interlacing preserves the same bound.
The first s+1 and last s coordinates contain every possibly nonzero
coordinate of each mu^n and ell, by the shape induction. Thus a pair is
encoded injectively by (2s+3)(2s+1) integers in [-T,T], where
T=A+(2s+1)L. This proves

            #pairs <=(2T+1)^((2s+3)(2s+1)).             (RD3.1)

For the actual digits take L=floor(2a/p). Indeed P_i>=p, b'<a/p,
bTop_i<=2b' and aTop_i<=2a show A_i,C_i<=2a/p. Since
2s+1<=k and p>=k^2, (2s+1)L<=2ka/p<=a. Also A<=A0<=a.
Hence T<=2a, 2T+1<=8a, and s<=N1 gives

                #pairs <=(8a)^(4(N1+1)^2).             (RD3.2)

This proof includes s=0 and makes all weighted sums genuinely finite.

4. RD4: the precise bad-pair bracket
-----------------------------------
Set q=4/p^2. The literal KeptPair conditions are, for every interior i,

 u_i+q(A_i+r) <= lambda*max(c_i,A_i)/k,
 C_i(1-lambda/k) <= n_i.                                (RD4.1)

We prove that failure of either condition forces

 (ell_0+r)*product_i(rho_i+r)*product_i(n_i+r)
       <=theta*A*product_i A_i*product_i C_i,
 theta>=0, theta^(r-1)<=exp(2-lambda/5).                 (RD4.2)

Put eta=1/k^2 and tau=lambda/(4k). By (RD1.3–4), q<=eta,
r<=eta*A,eta*A_i,eta*C_i and 0<tau<=1/16. Introduce

 chat_i=c_i+r, ahat_i=(A_i+r)(1+q),
 rhohat_i=rho_i+r, uhat_i=u_i+q(A_i+r).

Then (RD2.4–5) imply exactly

 chat_(i+1)=chat_i+ahat_i-uhat_i-rhohat_i,
 0<=rhohat_i<=min(chat_i,ahat_i), uhat_i>=0.              (RD4.3)

The upper comparison with ahat uses rho_i<=A_i(1+q). We also have

 chat_0<=exp(eta)A, ahat_i<=exp(2eta)A_i,
 n_i+r<=exp(2eta)C_i, ell_0+r<=chat_s.                  (RD4.4)

The elementary identity

 rho(c+a-u-rho)=ca-(c-rho)(a-rho)-rho*u                 (RD4.5)

proves rhohat_i*chat_(i+1)<=ahat_i*chat_i. If in addition
u>2tau*max(c,a), it improves to <=(1-tau)ca: for
rho>=min(c,a)/2 the last product rho*u is at least tau*ca;
otherwise (c-rho)(a-rho)>=ca/4>=tau*ca. This proof uses only positive
c,a and 0<=rho<=min(c,a), not division by rho.

If the first kept inequality fails at i, then
uhat_i>4tau*max(c_i,A_i). Since chat_i,ahat_i<=2max(c_i,A_i), the
improved quadratic estimate applies and contributes a factor
gamma=1-tau<=exp(-tau) at that step. All other steps contribute at most 1.

If the second inequality fails instead, then
n_i+r<(1-lambda/k+eta)C_i. The nonnegative number
gamma=1-lambda/k+eta satisfies

 gamma<=exp(-lambda/k+eta)<=exp(-lambda/(4k)),

because eta<=3lambda/(4k). Use this improvement for that single n_i
factor and the unimproved quadratic estimate at every step.

In either case, multiplying the quadratic inequalities telescopes:
if x_i c_(i+1)<=y_i c_i with all entries nonnegative, then
c_s product x_i<=c_0 product y_i. This follows by induction and
does not require dividing by any c_i. With (RD4.4) we obtain (RD4.2)
with theta=exp(eta)*exp(2eta)^(2s)*gamma. Indeed

 log upper bound for theta^(r-1)
   <= (k-2)(4s+1)/k^2 - (k-2)lambda/(4k)
   <= 2-lambda/5.                                      (RD4.6)

Here 2s+1<=r=k-1 and k>=10 give the final two inequalities.
For s=0 no pair fails (RD4.1), so the bad-pair conclusion is vacuous.

5. RD5: from the bracket to the actual weighted tail
----------------------------------------------------
For any dominant integer weight ell of length r,

                    w_r(ell)<=product_t S_r(|ell_t|).   (RD5.1)

To see this directly from RU2, build ell by first adding its positive
coordinates from top to bottom, each as one horizontal-strip step, then
subtracting its negative coordinates from bottom to top. Dominance makes
every step a legal signed Pieri strip. This gives at least one path to
ell in a tensor product of symmetric powers of degrees |ell_t|. The
total weighted path count is the product in (RD5.1); all summands are
positive. This argument is valid for signed dominant weights and does
not assume an integral decomposition into rational isotypic lattices.

Using (RD2.7), monotonicity of S_r, and S_r(0)=1,

 w_r(ell)<=S_r(ell_0)*product_i S_r(rho_i)*product_i S_r(n_i).

The elementary binomial product bounds

 y^(r-1)/(r-1)! <= S_r(y) <= (y+r)^(r-1)/(r-1)!

and (RD4.2) now give, for each bad pair,

 w_r(ell)<=exp(2-lambda/5)*S_r(A)*product_i S_r(A_i)S_r(C_i)
          <=exp(2-lambda/5)*D(A).                        (RD5.2)

The final comparison is S_r(y)<=S_k(y), k=r+1. The archival
notKeptWt_of weakens (RD5.2) by the additional factor
(8a+k)^((2N1+2)^2)>=1. Retaining that exact archival envelope makes
the constant C0=880 transparent. Since a>=9 and k<=a,

 8a<=a^2, 8a+k<=a^2,
 4(N1+1)^2<=(16N1^2), (2N1+2)^2<=16N1^2.

The pair count (RD3.2) and this envelope bound the total bad weight by

 exp(2-lambda/5+64N1^2 log a)*D(A)
                         <=exp(-lambda/8)*D(A),         (RD5.3)

because lambda=C0*N1^2 log a, C0>=880 and N1^2 log a>=1:
(1/5-1/8)lambda>=66N1^2 log a>=64N1^2 log a+2.
Subtract (RD5.3) from the exact total (RD2.2). This proves TailAt:

        sum_kept_pairs w_r(ell)>=(1-exp(-lambda/8))*D(A).
                                                               (RD5.4)

There is no appeal to an unproved tail theorem or to independent random
events. It is a finite weighted counting argument with exact source loss.

7. RD7: constructing the actual block, levels and filtered chain
----------------------------------------------------------------
Take A=A0-M, with 0<=M<=epsilon*A0, and a terminal integer
0<=jPlus<=J=floor(lambda*B/k). Since 4lambda<=k, J<=B.
For each kept pair define the literal pairBlock:

 i<sigma:   o_i=0, w_i=W_i, sg_i=S_i;
 i>=sigma:  o_i=O_i, w_i=sg_i=0;
 oPlus=D+jPlus; the digits remain exactly rz,ry.          (RD7.1)

All assigned quantities are nonnegative integers by (RD2.4). For each
late pair use level j_i=S_i, and use terminal level jPlus. The effective
order e_i=w_i+sg_i before the split and e_i=o_i after it is always O_i.
Thus the actual cap telescopes to the exact identity

 cap_l=A0+sum_(i<l)(A_i-O_i)=A0-A+c_l=M+c_l.             (RD7.2)

The late kept clauses follow from (RD6.2) and cap_i>=c_i. The early
clauses follow from (RD2.4), (RD6.4) and cap_i>=c_i. Finally, C_i<=2a/P_i
and (RD6.5) imply the literal terminal kept inequality

 oPlus<=cap_s+(lambda/k+4/p^2)sum_i(2a/P_i)+lambda*B/k.
                                                               (RD7.3)

The extra 4/p^2 term only weakens the inequality. Hence this is an actual
Kept block, not a block in an enlarged surrogate family.

Define the length-r chain kappa by kappa^n=K^(n+1) for 0<=n<=2s,
and kappa^(2s+1)=ell. Its vector/covector degrees in each pair are
W_i and C_i-S_i. Before the split these are exactly the block degrees.
After the split, subtracting the chosen level S_i from O_i and C_i
gives O_i-S_i=W_i and C_i-S_i. The terminal vector degree is
oPlus-jPlus=D. Thus this is exactly the source lettersAt chain, and it
starts at zero because K^1=0.

LevelOK holds: at a late pair, S_i<=O_i and S_i<=C_i; at the terminal
pair, jPlus<=oPlus and jPlus<=B. For each late prefix, the tested first
coordinate is rho_i<=c_i, while its threshold is floor(cap_i-h).
Since cap_i=M+c_i and M>=h, that threshold is at least the integer c_i.
At the final prefix, ell_0<=c_s gives the same conclusion. These are
all tested prefixes, so the actual ChainOK condition holds, with the
margin h and the integer floor exactly as defined in blockData.

The map from (mu,ell,jPlus) to (block,levels,kappa) is injective.
The terminal chain state recovers ell and the terminal level recovers
jPlus. Every noninitial mu^n has its last r coordinates recorded in
kappa. Its total coordinate sum is fixed by the signed degree word,
so its first coordinate is recovered by subtraction. Finally mu^0=0.
This argument recovers the whole original chain, regardless of repeated
weights or multiplicities.

The source summand for this image is exactly
                    w_r(ell)*S_r(B-jPlus).              (RD7.4)
The target support is finite directly: fixed digits leave o_i,w_i<=A_i
and sg_i<=C_i; cap_s<=A0+sum A_i uniformly bounds the kept oPlus
through (RD7.3). Levels are bounded by degrees, and all coordinates of
a fixed-degree chain are bounded by the sum of those degrees. There are
therefore finitely many nonzero source summands. No interchange of an
undefined infinite sum is being used.

Summing the injective, weight-preserving map proves the exact MatchAt
inequality, written C_h=chainCount:

 C_h >= (sum_kept_pairs w_r(ell))*sum_(j=0)^J S_r(B-j).   (RD7.5)

8. RD8: the terminal and margin binomial factors
------------------------------------------------
Write T_r(y)=S_(r+1)(y)=binom(y+r,r). For r>=4, B>=r+1 and
0<=4lambda<=r+1, put J=floor(lambda*B/(r+1)). Then J+1<=B and
the hockey-stick identity gives

 sum_(j=0)^J S_r(B-j)=T_r(B)-T_r(B-J-1).                 (RD8.1)

Set L=J+1. For 1<=t<=r,
(B-L+t)/(B+t)<=1-L/(B+r). Multiplying gives

 T_r(B-L)/T_r(B)<=(1-L/(B+r))^r<=exp(-rL/(B+r)).

Since L>lambda*B/(r+1) and
(r+1)(B+r)<=3rB (valid for r>=4,B>=r+1), the last expression is
at most exp(-lambda/3). Hence

 sum_(j=0)^J S_r(B-j)>=(1-exp(-lambda/3))*T_r(B).         (RD8.2)

For integers A0>0 and 0<=M<=A0,

 T_r(A0-M)/T_r(A0)
    =product_(t=1)^r (1-M/(A0+t))
    >=(1-M/A0)^r >=1-rM/A0,                            (RD8.3)

by Bernoulli's inequality. This remains a valid lower bound if its
right-hand side is negative. We never multiply a positive lower bound
by a negative quantity without checking the direction: in the assembly
below, (RD8.3) is used last, with a nonnegative coefficient.

9. RD9: the literal coarse lower rank
-------------------------------------
Let

 baseline=mmB=T_r(A0)*product_i T_r(A_i)T_r(C_i)*T_r(B),
 L_h=(1-exp(-lambda/8))(1-exp(-lambda/3))(1-rM/A0).

Use (RD5.4) at A=A0-M, followed by (RD7.5), then (RD8.2). Finally
replace T_r(A0-M) by its lower bound (RD8.3), multiplying by the
nonnegative remaining factors. The result is

                         C_h>=L_h*baseline.             (RD9.1)

RU1–RU2 identify C_h with the actual O-lattice quantity rankOnDigits
for any literal UnramifiedQuadraticSetting of the source. Specifically,
the rank is the sum over kept blocks with these exact digits; each block
rank is the sum over its actual LevelOK pairs of the filtered chain sum
times the residual symmetric-power dimension S_r(B-jPlus). This is the
same sum used in (RD7.4–5). Thus

 lowFac(C0,CE1,CE2,X,j,ceil(h))*mmB
                      <=rankOnDigits(O,C0,CE1,CE2,X,j,h,rz,ry).    (RD9.2)

This is the exact LowAt endpoint, with its stated extra margin condition
ceil(h)<=A0*N1*lambda/k. The ring hypotheses are not weakened: O is the
source DVR in the unramified quadratic setting, and the shared rank

11. RD11: a shorter finite harmonic-density calculation
-------------------------------------------------------
We now use the actual Type-I dimension convention from HM1. For r>=1,
put P(t)=binom(t+r,r) for t>=0 and P(-1)=0. For nonnegative integers
alpha,beta the harmonic dimension is

 P1(alpha,beta,j)=H(alpha,beta)/(P(alpha+j)P(beta+j)).     (RD11.1)

The convention P(-1)=0 is essential at zero degree. Equivalently,

     =1-alpha*beta/((alpha+r)(beta+r)).

Define R_j=P(alpha)P(beta)/(P(alpha+j)P(beta+j)). Since t/(t+r) is

 P1(alpha,beta,j)

   >=R_j[1-(alpha+j+1)(beta+j+1)/

             sum_(j=0)^J P1(alpha,beta,j)>=1-R_L.       (RD11.3)
