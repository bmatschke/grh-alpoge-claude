## Which Galois theorems enter

For a regular algebraic polarized cuspidal representation $\pi$ of $\mathrm{GL}_a(\mathbb A_E)$, over the CM host, fix $\iota:\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell\simeq\mathbb C$. Barnet-Lamb, Gee, Geraghty and Taylor, Theorem 2.1.1, give a continuous semisimple $r_{\ell,\iota}(\pi)$. Away from $\ell$ its Frobenius-semisimplified Weil–Deligne parameter is the local correspondence of $\pi_v|\det|_v^{(1-a)/2}$. At $v\mid\ell$ the representation is de Rham, and it is crystalline if $\pi_v$ is spherical. With the infinitesimal character of the dual algebraic coefficient representation, if its highest weight before dualizing is $b_1\ge\cdots\ge b_a$, the entry indexed by $s$ in that infinitesimal character is $z_s=-b_s-(a+1-2s)/2$, up to reordering. Thus $(a-1)/2-z_s=b_s+a-s$, exactly the labelled Hodge–Tate multiset in the theorem. The multiset at the conjugate embedding is its complement in the purity weight. These are the indices and signs in the construction. The filtration-dimension argument in the earlier chapter identifies the cardinality of the jumps once the local period objects have been read in the selected frame. [BLGGT, *Potential automorphy and change of weight*, Theorem 2.1.1, p. 538](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v179-n2-p03-p.pdf#page=38) · [Pinned local conditions][gal] · [Filtered dimensions](FIRST_PASS_FILTERED_DIMENSIONS.md)

At a ramified place above $\ell$ check the local monodromy separately. The condition $\mathrm{NcW}(y)$ on the automorphic Weil–Deligne parameter $y$ includes $N_y=0$. For regular algebraic essentially conjugate-self-dual cuspidals, BLGGT's *Local-global compatibility for $\ell=p$, II*, Theorem 1.1, gives equality of semisimplified Weil representations and domination of the Galois monodromy partitions by the automorphic ones. On every nonempty inertial-equivalence class the largest automorphic block size is now one. The inequality for the first part of its partition forces the largest Galois block to have size at most one. Consequently $N_{\mathrm{Gal}}=0$, and the Frobenius-semisimplified Weil–Deligne parameters are isomorphic. This proves both the inertia characteristic-polynomial comparison and the zero-monodromy assertion for the indicated local tests. The first-pass host contains its distinguished imaginary quadratic subfield. For the conjugate-self-dual cuspidals of rank at least two, Caraiani's Theorem 1.1 gives full Frobenius-semisimplified compatibility at every place above $\ell$. Her geometric convention applies the normalized local correspondence to the contragredient. Apply that formulation to the contragredient to read the project's convention, or use the preceding BLGGT formula directly. Rank one uses the reciprocity character attached to the algebraic Hecke character. [BLGGT, Theorem 1.1 and the partition order, pp. 5–6](https://www.ma.imperial.ac.uk/~tsg/Index_files/localglobalII.pdf#page=6) · [Caraiani, *Monodromy and local-global compatibility for $l=p$*, pp. 1597–1598](https://msp.org/ant/2014/8-7/ant-v8-n7-p02-s.pdf#page=2) · [Zero-monodromy guard][nc] · [Actual semisimple Weil convention][wd] · [Conversion to the pinned tests][convert]

For $M_{j,\sigma}\ne0$, $P_iT_i$ acts on $M_{j,\sigma}$ by a positive real scalar, and $T_iM_{j,\sigma}$ lies in one definite automorphic class $\Pi$. This image is nonzero. Distinct class summands have zero intersection, so any other such class equals $\Pi$. In its definite transfer the split Hecke and theta comparison singles out the rank-one character string $(1,\mu_i,t)$ after undoing the common twist $\eta_T^{-1}$. Removing it gives a rank-$r$ list $\Sigma$ matching the small row at every split place. The definite parameter is $(\Sigma\sqcup[(1,\mu_i,t)])\otimes\eta_T^{-1}$, with the prescribed nonsplit spherical parameters and archimedean weights as well. For a good $\sigma$, every possible split-matching list is cuspidal. To see this choose $g_k\ne\ell_i$. At its marked places the labelled polynomial factors as $\prod_b f_{b,v}^{d_b}$ for proposed blocks $(a_b,\pi_b,d_b)$, with $\deg f_{b,v}\le a_b$. The total degree equals $r=\sum_b a_b d_b$, so each bound is an equality. Squarefreeness forces every $d_b=1$. If two or more blocks remained the smallest would have degree $1\le a_b\le r/2$ and give a divisor of that fixed degree at every marked place above $g_k$. The good labels exclude this. ### Why the residual representation is irreducible

Suppose there is a good point at $i$, and take its smaller parameter and associated characteristic-zero lattice representation $\rho$. For the residual realization in that point the construction also supplies a rank-one twist $\xi$, trivial on inertia at the marked primes of residue characteristic different from $\ell_i$. The exceptional sets $\mathrm{BB}(r,g_0)$ and $\mathrm{BB}(r,g_1)$ are disjoint. Choose $g_k$ whose exceptional set omits $\ell_i$. This already ensures $g_k\ne\ell_i$. For this $k$, removing the $\ell_i$-power part of an inertia label preserves distinctness of its roots. Under the tame comparison they give distinct residual inertia eigenlines. Frobenius permutes those lines by the $g_k$-power permutation of the original roots. For a nonzero proper stable subspace of dimension $a$, the restricted set of eigenlines at each such place would be a Frobenius-stable subset of size $a$, and its complement would have size $r-a$. The product over the smaller subset would be a divisor in $\mathbb F_{g_k}[X]$ of the common degree $\min(a,r-a)$ at every place above this $g_k$. Goodness excludes such a degree. This proves absolute irreducibility. At **each** marked prime $g_h\ne\ell_i$ the two good-label tests, for the marked characters and their inverses, also exclude the specified character value as an eigenvalue of the marked tame generator, including the opposite orientation. This second condition uses the prime-to-$\ell_i$ separation from the distinguished character, which only requires $g_h\ne\ell_i$. [Good-polynomial tests][good] · [The two-prime choice and the two orientations][three]

For any other residual representation $\bar r$ satisfying the prescribed full Hecke-polynomial test, both it and $\overline{\rho\otimes\xi}$ have the same rank-$r$ characteristic polynomial on split Frobenius elements outside the omitted finite set. Indeed their full polynomials have the identical monic character-string factor; cancel it in the residue polynomial ring. Both representations have open kernel. Equality extends to all elements by split Frobenius density, and Brauer–Nesbitt applied to characteristic polynomials identifies their semisimplifications. Scalar twisting preserves invariant subspaces. Since one representation is irreducible, equality of semisimplifications implies irreducibility of the other even without an a priori semisimplicity assumption on that other representation. Triviality of $\xi$ on the marked inertia carries the nonroot assertion across as well. This proves the complete residual condition for every local $\ell_i$ with a good point. ### The image is the whole good eigenspace

Let $x$ be a good point and let $\Sigma$ match $x$. Put $M(\Sigma)=\sum_{\sigma\text{ matching }\Sigma}M_{j,\sigma}$ and $E_x=\sum_{\Pi\mapsto x}M^{\rm def}_\Pi$. The upward parameter comparison gives $T_iM(\Sigma)\subseteq E_x$. The internal direct sum and the positive scalars make $T_i$ injective on the entire row, since $P_iT_i$ is diagonal by nonzero scalars on the summands. The downward comparison gives $P_iE_x\subseteq M(\Sigma)$, and the type-nonvanishing assertion makes $P_i|_{E_x}$ injective. Therefore
$$
 \dim M(\Sigma)=\dim T_iM(\Sigma)
 \le \dim E_x=\dim P_iE_x\le\dim M(\Sigma).
$$
The first inclusion is equality. The compatibility between good types satisfying the residual split Hecke criterion and the good parameters identifies the sum of these $M(\Sigma)$ with the good row part. Its image under $T_i$ is precisely $\sum_{x\ \mathrm{good}}E_x$. Taking dimensions gives $\sum_{x\ \mathrm{good}}\mathrm{mult}_i(x)=\dim M_i^{\rm good}$ with the actual multiplicities. This is the required good-point assertion when the block has a good point.
