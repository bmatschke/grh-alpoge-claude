# The fitted functional on the selected base row

On the selected design class of the period-height contract, its division hypothesis and the stated local theta, toric and real-realization inputs give
$$
   \mu_{\max}(R_0)+q_\kappa\le x^{A_R} b,
   \qquad A_R=16(T+9)+1,\qquad T=\lceil|C_t|\rceil+1 .
$$
Here $R_0$ is the actual glued base-level row, $q_\kappa$ is the mean logarithm of its frame factor with squared norms, and $C_t$ is the outer exponent for the auxiliary rank. The result combines with the divided-column and sharp-row bounds to prove frame entry 2 under the original family guards. Existence of the common design for each requested target remains a separate obligation. ## The changed lattice and the size exponent

At a finite coefficient place $w$ let $t=\mu(w)$ be the tuple used in the glued row. The fitted evaluations give a single injective rational map $\Psi$, a value lattice $M$ and nonzero $r_t$ with $r_{\mu_0}=1$. On representatives of the actual local row the map multiplied by $r_t$ is the evaluation on the $t$-lattice, up to a root of unity. The root preserves the local ideal, so
$$
  \Psi(R_{0,w})\subseteq\mathfrak p_w^{-d_F(w)-v_w(r_t)}M_w .
$$
This includes all changed places. Its summed squared-norm cost is at most $B_F+2G_*$ by the fitted finite evaluation and the square valuations. This argument controls the image of the changed lattice directly; a separate bound on the index between two bottom lattices does not enter. At infinity use the base/level-one equality of block scalars, the lower bound on the admissible point class and the quotient metric divided in squared norm by $\kappa_v$. This gives a mean log squared operator norm bounded by $H_I$. If $E_M$ bounds the maximal slope of $M$, the rank-$s$ determinant norm of the injection is at most the $s$th power of its operator norm. Taking normalized degrees, dividing by $s$, and taking the supremum over sublattices gives
$$
  \mu_{\max}(R_0)+q_\kappa\le E_M+H_I+B_F+2G_* .
$$

Keep the estimate before its last absorption into $x^{C_H}b$. With $Q=x^{T+9}$ its right side is at most $13{,}000{,}000{,}000\,C_1^4 Q^{16}b$; the numerical coefficient is the bound $10^7(20+1280)$. The preliminary and closed thresholds are chosen before the family. Once it is fixed, $C_1$ may absorb its row-size and level-size constants. Taking $x\ge 13{,}000{,}000{,}000 C_1^4$ as part of the common cutoff gives the stated $A_R$. All points and indices are chosen after the cutoff, and the fitted numerical estimate holds for every value of the separate norm parameter $C$. The ranks in this calculation are positive on a common eventual range. The selected primitive realization gives $d_Q(\mu_0)>0$ at both character levels. Fullness identifies these dimensions with the ranks of the glued rows. On the column side the design proviso gives $d_{M,u}>0$ for every word. The chosen level has $T_u>2$, and the ramified column rank is $d_{M,u}(1-1/(T_u-1))^f>0$. The divided lattice is full in that space. These assertions hold beyond the maximum of the proviso, level, setting and fullness cutoffs. The word set contains the distinguished word, so its total rank is positive as well.
