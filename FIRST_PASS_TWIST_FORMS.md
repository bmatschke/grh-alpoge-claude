# Integral forms and Galois representations for the twist indices

Fix an actual first-pass intermediate level, with its rational Hecke datum, character frame, choice $v\mapsto P_q(v)$ over the maximal real subfield of $E$, and occurring good rows. Write $L$ for the row field, $L_0\subset L$ for the designated conjugation-stable subfield, and $K_s\subset L_0$ for the field of the geometric form. The finite extensions that must be chosen large enough in this step are indexed by a rational prime $\ell$ **and** an embedding $u:L_0\hookrightarrow\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell$. The result constructs the integral modules and their Hecke and Galois data before the toric auxiliary list is inserted. [Application to the intermediate level][apply] · [Precise statement and assembly][assembly] · [First-pass Hecke data](FIRST_PASS_HECKE_RATIONALITY.md) · [Good cuspidal rows](FIRST_PASS_CUSPIDAL_ROW.md)

## One integral representation for a row and a coefficient embedding

For an occurring good finite type $\sigma$, choose a row $1\leq j\leq m$ in which it occurs and the cuspidal $\pi_0$ supplied by that row. Its split local components are the assigned ones. For almost all finite places of $E$, the polynomial for $\pi_0\otimes\eta^{-1}$ with shift $(1-n_V)/2$ has coefficients in the specified complex image of $L_0$. Choose an isomorphism $\iota_0:\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell\simeq\mathbf C$ extending the prescribed complex reading of $L_0$ along $u$. The ordinary Galois theorem applies to the regular algebraic polarized rank-$r$ cuspidal representation. To obtain the ambient shift, tensor its semisimple representation with the $\ell$-adic character furnished by Artin reciprocity for $\eta^{-1}|\cdot|^{-t/2}$, where $t=n_V-r$. The half-integral infinity type of $\eta$ and the odd parity of $t$ make the combined character algebraic. At unramified places away from $\ell$, its value on the source’s arithmetic Frobenius is the inverse of the idele-character value on a uniformizer. This multiplies every old root by the required scalar. The outcome has the polynomial for $\pi_0\otimes\eta^{-1}$ with shift $(1-n_V)/2$. [BLGGT, Theorem 2.1.1][blggt] · [Rank and character correction][shift] · [Exact polynomial and its coefficient field](FIRST_PASS_CUSPIDAL_COEFFICIENTS.md)

There is one finite extension $C_{\sigma}(\ell,u)/\mathbf Q_\ell$ and a basis in which the image is integral over it. Indeed $\mathrm{GL}_r(\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell)$ is the union of $\mathrm{GL}_r(K)$ over countably many finite subextensions $K$. In the compact image group these intersections are closed subgroups. Baire's theorem makes one open, hence of finite index. Adjoining the entries of representatives of its finitely many cosets gives a single finite field of definition. Over that field the span of the translates of the standard integral lattice is bounded and contains the standard lattice. It is finitely generated over the discrete valuation ring, free of full rank, and stable under every group element and its inverse. Use a basis of this lattice. [Integral model and basis change][integral] · [Application to an occurring type][pick]

This same representation serves every matching cuspidal $\pi$ and every complex reading extending the fixed $u$. First, changing $\iota_0$ to $\iota$ agreeing on $u(L_0)$ preserves the Frobenius equality for $\pi_0$ on the cofinite set with its polynomial in $L_0[X]$. For another $\pi$ matching the assigned components at all split places, apply the ordinary Galois theorem with the same character correction and this $\iota$. The two representations' characteristic polynomials agree on Frobenius at split places after the union of their finite exceptional sets is removed. These Frobenius elements and their conjugates are dense by degree-one Chebotarev for $E$. Continuity of each coefficient gives equality of characteristic polynomials on every element of $G_E$, hence the cofinite Frobenius equality required for the new $\pi$. This argument needs no irreducibility of the $\ell$-adic representation and gives no equality of the chosen integral lattices. [Change of reading and density argument][reads] · [Exact Galois specification][require]

For each $(\ell,u)$ take the compositum of the fields $C_{\sigma}(\ell,u)$ and $C_{\sigma}(\ell,u\circ *)$ over the finitely many occurring good finite types. Off this range the auxiliary construction may use the trivial representation and $\mathbf Q_\ell$. This gives the pointwise finite field bound $c_F(\ell,u)$, with no uniform bound on degrees over all primes asserted. In the weight construction the second field bound is explicitly $\mathbf Q_\ell$. Indeed the half-integral shift gives a dominant integral weight at every CM embedding. The dual of the tensor product of the corresponding split highest-weight representations, read at these embeddings, is algebraic and absolutely irreducible. Distinctness of the CM-type embeddings and their nonconjugacy give the stated irreducibility, also after each later scalar extension. Its dual has the prescribed weights. [Both readings in the compositum][fields] · [Weight construction and trivial bound][weights]

The assertion holds for **every** ring specification with $c_F(\ell,u)\subseteq b_0(\ell,u)$, arbitrary second lower bound $b_1$ admitted by that specification, and every finite conjugation-stable $S_x$ containing the original bad set $S_b$ and all places above the rational primes in the specified finite set called low. For twist index $i$, $\mathcal O_{0,i}$ identifies with the integers of $K_{0,i}$ containing $b_0(\ell_i,u_i)$. Transport the integral representation to this ring. Its specification allows any embedding $\phi:\operatorname{Frac}\mathcal O_{0,i}\to\overline{\mathbf Q}_{\ell_i}$ fixing $\mathbf Z_{\ell_i}$, and any complex reading $\iota$ which after $\phi$ agrees on the side's copy of $L_0$. Two such finite-field embeddings into the algebraic closure differ by a $\mathbf Q_{\ell_i}$-automorphism of that closure. Apply the preceding change of reading with the adjusted $\iota$ and then transport the representation. The side’s copy of $L_0$ uses $u_i$ on the positive side and $u_i\circ *$ on the negative side. [Field bounds and exact exceptional set][spec] · [Change of the local embedding][localread]

## Full lattice and good-type lattice

Put $O_i=\mathcal O_i$, $K_i=\operatorname{Frac}O_i$. For $b\in\{+,-\}$, let $d_b:L_0\to K_i$ denote that side's coefficient embedding. The rational row has a $K_s$-form $W_j$ and a full $\mathcal O_{K_s}$-lattice $M_j$. Define
$$
 V_{ijb}=K_i\otimes_{K_s,d_b} W_j,\qquad
 B^{\rm sup}_{ijb}=\sum_{x\in M_j}O_i(1\otimes x).
$$
For $j$ outside $1,\ldots,m$, take the zero space and zero lattice. Since $d_b$ carries algebraic integers into $O_i$, the images of finite integral generators of $M_j$ generate $B^{\rm sup}_{ijb}$. The same vectors span $V_{ijb}$ over $K_i$. This proves the finite-generation and full-span assertion for every $i,j,b$, including zero rows. [Definitions][ambient] · [Integral generators and full span][full]

On $V_{ijb}$ let $g$ in the finite marked group act by the base change of the geometric action multiplied by $d_b(\chi_j(g))^{-1}$. On the marked unipotent subgroup $U$, read the fixed generic character from the first-pass $\theta$ using $d_b$. Let $e_b$ be the image of the rational central idempotent, and let
$$
 V^0_{ijb}= e_b\bigl(V_{ijb}[\psi_{i,b}]\bigr),\qquad
 B_{ijb}= B^{\rm sup}_{ijb}\cap V^0_{ijb}.
$$
Centrality and idempotence make $V^0$ the simultaneous solution space of $e_bx=x$ and $u x=\psi_{i,b}(u)x$ for $u\in U$. A common denominator moves any vector of $V^0$ into $B$, because $B^{\rm sup}$ spans the ambient space. Consequently $K_i\otimes_{O_i}B\simeq V^0$. The ring $O_i$ of the specification is a discrete valuation ring. Its finite module $B^{\rm sup}$ is Noetherian, and its submodule $B$ is torsion-free and therefore finite free. This makes the inclusion into $V$ and its range literal. [Action, idempotent, unipotent space and intersection][good] · [DVR hypotheses][dvr] · [Finite freeness and initial theta spaces][forms]

At a split $P\notin S_{0,i}$ set $c_{ibP}=\delta_i(\mathrm{Frob}_P)^{\epsilon_b}$, where $\epsilon_+=1$, $\epsilon_-=-1$, using the map $\mathcal O_{0,i}^\times\to O_i^\times$. The character is unramified there, so every Frobenius lift gives the same value. For $1\le k\le r$, the ambient Hecke operator is
$$
 H_{ijbkP}=c_{ibP}^{\,k}(1\otimes H_{jkP}).
$$
Here the Galois representation already constructed is for $\pi\otimes\eta^{-1}$ with the ambient shift. The extra factor $\delta_i^{\epsilon_b}$ in this formula is the sided Hecke correction. The source imposes $H_0=1$. [Frobenius unit and its power][operators] · [Precise comparison on the two modules][side]

For integral stability there is a field issue: the original Hecke estimate is on the $\mathcal O_L$-span, while the chosen embedding is of $L_0$. Work first with $M_0=\mathcal O_{L_0}M_j$ inside $V_0'=L_0\otimes_{K_s}W_j$. All the good-space equations have coefficients in $L_0$. If $x\in M_0$ solves these equations, the original estimate gives $(NP)^aH_{jkP}x\in\mathcal O_L M_0$ for a fixed $a$ depending on $j,k,P$. The left side belongs to $V_0'$. Faithful flatness of $\mathcal O_L/\mathcal O_{L_0}$, applied to $V_0'/M_0$, gives $(NP)^aH_{jkP}x\in M_0$. The operator also preserves the good subspace, by the original joint eigenvector span. Next $O_i$ is flat over the Dedekind domain $\mathcal O_{L_0}$ because it is torsion-free. Apply this to the common kernel on $M_0$ of the finite system of good-space equations. After extension this is exactly $B_{ijb}$ inside $V_{ijb}$ (equivalently clear a common denominator in the matrix of the equations before extending). Thus $(NP)^a(1\otimes H_{jkP})$ preserves $B$. Since $S_{0,i}$ contains the places above $\ell_i$, $NP$ is an $O_i$-unit. Multiplication by the unit $c_{ibP}^{\,k}$ proves stability for the asserted operator. This descent uses no coefficient embedding $L\to K_i$. [Input estimate and eigenspace condition][rational] · [Flatness and the equations][flat] · [Removing the norm denominator][norm]

The rational good-space decomposition descends to $L_0$ and extends to $K_i$. For detail, for each occurring good row and fixed $j$, the intersection of all its eigenspaces and the good-space equations is defined over $L_0$. In a finite-dimensional space any such intersection is cut out by finitely many equations, by taking a basis of the span of their coordinate functionals. Base change to $L$ therefore commutes with these intersections. Their sum equals the good space over $L$ by the rational Hecke datum; faithful flatness gives equality over $L_0$. Restricting attention to the smaller list of primes $P\notin S_{0,i}$ can only enlarge the constituent eigenspaces. They span $K_i\otimes B$. The eigenvalue associated with $\sigma$ is $c_{ibP}^{\,k}d_b(a_{j\sigma kP})$. Whenever its joint eigenspace contains a nonzero vector, this scalar is a root of the monic characteristic polynomial of an endomorphism of $B$. Since it belongs to $K_i$ and $O_i$ is integrally closed, it belongs to $O_i$. [Passage of the eigenspace span][transport] · [Integrality from a nonzero eigenvector][labels]

At each $i$, record distinct triples consisting of $j,b$ and the integral eigenvalue array on the admitted $(k,P)$, taking one occurring good row with that array. There are finitely many triples, even though there may be infinitely many $P$, since $j,b,\sigma$ range over finite sets. The pinned definition can include any such row with integral array and does not require a separate multiplicity-one claim. Distinct recorded triples for a fixed $j,b$ differ on an admitted operator. Their joint eigenspaces span; dimensions of coincident systems are retained in the space itself. Each chosen signature carries the integral representation of its representative good row. All admitted integral Hecke characteristic polynomials split over $K_i$ because their joint eigenvectors span. Outside the range defining the integral Hecke operator the construction uses the identity, for which the splitting assertion is immediate. [Definition, identifications and normalization at zero][labels] · [Splitting of Hecke characteristic polynomials][split]

## What the construction supplies at this stage

The form record consists of these finite free $B$'s, integral operators, signatures, and integral row representations. At this stage its theta submodules are zero. The ambient record uses $V$, $B^{\rm sup}$ and the indicated finite-group action, $U$-character and central idempotent. The old rational coefficient $e(g)$ is reused for each $g$, so reading it in $\mathbf C$ gives the prescribed original $e_0(g)$, proving the coefficient identity called $h_0$. Its theta-test index is empty. Finally the ambient record includes ordinary finite-dimensional type spaces at each admissible level and the span of a chosen basis as a full lattice. [Form record][forms] · [Ambient record and empty index][ambientrecord] · [Exact meaning of the coefficient identity][h0]

For these type spaces, admissibility gives split auxiliary $Q$ avoiding the coefficient and marked primes, an open-kernel anticyclotomic $\ell_i$-power character $\psi$ unramified off $Q$, and the requisite disjointness. The fixed selector gives one half of $Q$ and one half of the auxiliary set above $p_\delta$ (above the alternative prime if $\ell_i=p_\delta$), using their split conjugate pairs. In the selected split charts take the prescribed marked subgroups, the specified parahorics at the two auxiliary halves, and maximal level elsewhere. On the two auxiliary halves the character uses the tame determinant factors of $\psi$ and $\delta_i^{\epsilon_b}$, respectively; the marked factors use the fixed residue characters. Their product defines a level character with open kernel. The definite automorphic type space is finite dimensional by the finite class set at this level and finite-dimensional weight space. The coordinatewise inertia surjectivity of the universal conductor character uses the witnesses in admissibility of $Q$, in precisely the cases where it is required. [Level and character formula][leveldef] · [Actual disjointness, tame characters and type space][level] · [Inertia at admissible conductors][inertia]

These identifications give the compatibility record for **every** choice of the remaining theta-linear data on this initial empty toric list. The coefficient comparisons, conjugation, universal characters and coverage of the coefficient places use the supplied ring specification and reciprocity. The comparison of forms is the scalar-extension identification, the embedding of $B$ is inclusion, and the final representation at each signature is the uniform representation proved above. The input list of previously realized type spaces is accepted by the result; the proof constructs the admissible type spaces from their explicit levels, and the comparison with previously fixed complex types belongs to the following choice step. In the application one takes the pointwise compositum of $c_F$ and the independent field bound for the ring specification and uses that same compositum in both entries. The theta-linear data for the empty list and for the actual list are then each obtained using the same finite-generation and full-span result for $B^{\rm sup}$. This result supplies the form part of the first intermediate restaging under the standing first-pass hypotheses.
