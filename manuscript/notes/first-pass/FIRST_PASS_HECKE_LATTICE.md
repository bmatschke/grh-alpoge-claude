# Integral split Hecke operators and residual blocks on a fitted row

Fix an active first-pass coefficient place $w\mid\ell$, an original definite row $1\le j\le m$, and put $n=n_V$. Every allowed split Hecke operator preserves the given row lattice after localization at $w$. The conclusion concerns the actual generators of the fitted lattice and holds before comparing any congruence lengths.

Choose a split place $s$ representing the prescribed branch up to conjugation and any uniformizer $\varpi$. The allowed place avoids $\ell$, both marked type primes, the distinguished conductor prime selected for $\ell$, and the variable conductor set, which is empty on this pass. The old level is hyperspecial there with trivial local character. For $1\le k\le n$, the definite operator is
$$
 T_{s,k}f(g)=q_s^{-k(n-1)}
       \sum_{xJ} f(gx).
$$
Here $x$ runs over right coset representatives in the local double coset, embedded at $s$ in the finite adèles. The diagonal representative has local matrix $\operatorname{diag}(\varpi I_k,I_{n-k})$. This is the normalization on the definite source. The operator sums translates without any reciprocal stabilizer orders. [Allowed generators][gen] · [Diagonal and support][supp] · [Coset sum and normalization][op] · [Actual level avoidance][off]

Let $p$ be the residue characteristic of $s$. For every indicated representative, $p$ clears the matrix denominators of both $x$ and $x^{-1}$ in the finite frame. Thus the marked coefficient lattices at $g$ and $gx$ contain each other's $p$-multiples before taking the algebraic weight. Over $\mathcal O_{L,w}$ they coincide. This equality passes to the weight lattice by applying each embedding to the lattice, taking dual frames, and using the same localized basis to construct the highest-weight lattice on both sides. Hence each summand, evaluated on an integral form, lies in the required localized weight lattice. The factor $q_s^{-k(n-1)}$ is a $w$-unit. The transform still has the rational level and character law. Take the finite set of marked double classes which presents the form and multiply the denominators of the transformed coordinates on those classes. This gives a single $d\in\mathcal O_L\setminus w$ for which $dT_{s,k}f$ is in the original lattice. The class presentation requires no division by a class stabilizer. [Denominator of a split representative][denom] · [Localization of the coefficient weight][weight] · [Simultaneous denominator and rational coordinates][den]

The fitted original lattice identifies its stored generators with rational definite forms. For any one generator the integral expression for $dT_{s,k}f$ gives coefficients $b_g\in\mathcal O_L$ on those same generators. Dividing by $d$ gives $v_w(b_g/d)\ge0$ as required by the definition of $\mathrm{LatInt}$. Pulling the equality through the injective rational realization and extending scalars shows that the completed lattice is stable under each generator and therefore under the integral algebra they generate. ## Splitting into residual characters

Over a finite coefficient extension splitting the commuting Hecke operators, the completed lattice splits by residual characters. The fitted construction allows this extension for all generators of the row at once. Indeed their commutative algebra on a finite-dimensional space is finite-dimensional and has finitely many characteristic-zero characters. Integrality puts the values of these characters in the coefficient ring. For one residual character choose, for each of the other residual characters, an algebra element vanishing modulo the latter and nonzero modulo the chosen one. Their product $t$ has unit eigenvalues on the chosen block and nonunit eigenvalues on the others. Products of sufficiently high powers of the corresponding linear factors of $t$ annihilate the two generalized blocks. The two polynomials are comaximal over the DVR since every cross-difference is a unit. Bézout gives a polynomial in $t$ acting as identity on the chosen block and zero on its complement. These integral projectors therefore split the lattice as a direct sum of its intersections with the residual blocks. Once the fitted local comparison identifies the local indices, maps and lattices with these blocks, this is the sum of their transported lattices. Resolving characteristic-zero constituents with the same residual character into good and complementary subspaces is a separate step in the length computation. ## Joint eigenspaces within each residual summand

For each fitted active row $1\le j\le m$ and each allowed split Hecke generator, the complex operator is normal for the definite Petersson pairing. The generators commute. The faithful rational realization transfers their squarefree minimal polynomials to the common fitted splitting field. Their joint eigenspaces therefore span the characteristic-zero row and each of its residual summands. There is also a direct proof on every installed local index $i$. Write $K_i=\operatorname{Frac}(O_i)$. The algebra $T_i\subset O_i^{X_i}$ separates its finite set of characters and is generated by the allowed split operators. For each $x\in X_i$ choose $t_y\in T_i$ distinguishing $x$ from $y$ for each $y\ne x$. The product of $(t_y-t_y(y))/(t_y(x)-t_y(y))$ in $K_iT_i$ is the indicator of $x$. These indicators are orthogonal, sum to one, and their images on $K_i\otimes_{O_i}M_i$ are exactly the joint eigenspaces. Every image is nonzero by faithfulness of the action on the finite free module. Some denominators can be nonunits of $O_i$, so this gives the full decomposition on the fraction-field space even when characters are congruent. The point-stage spanning assertion thus takes an empty additional coefficient request. Identifying the good characters inside each residual block remains a distinct assertion. ## A mixed block with exactly two eigenlines

Suppose a fitted block over a DVR $R$ has exactly two distinct characteristic-zero eigensystems, each with a one-dimensional eigenspace over $\operatorname{Frac}(R)$. Write $\pi$ for a uniformizer, $v(\pi)=1$, and $\lambda_1,\lambda_2$ for the two integral Hecke characters. The image $T$ of the $R$-Hecke algebra in $R^2$ contains the diagonal. Set
$$
 h=\min_t v(\lambda_1(t)-\lambda_2(t)),\qquad
 T_b=\{(a_1,a_2)\in R^2:a_1-a_2\in\pi^bR\}.
$$
The differences form an ideal, and subtracting a diagonal element realizes every such difference on one axis. Thus $T=T_h$. One can take the minimum over the allowed Hecke generators because congruence of their two readings modulo $\pi^b$ persists under sums and products. Distinct characters in the same residual block have $1\le h<\infty$.

Let $\Lambda$ be the actual stable lattice in these two lines. Scale the line coordinates so its two projections are both $R$. The intersections with the axes are then $\pi^{k_1}R$ and $\pi^{k_2}R$. Sending a first coordinate to the class of its second coordinate gives an $R$-linear isomorphism $R/\pi^{k_1}R\simeq R/\pi^{k_2}R$. Hence $k_1=k_2=k$. When $k>0$ the isomorphism is multiplication by a unit modulo $\pi^k$. A further unit change on the second axis therefore identifies $\Lambda$ with $T_k$. At $k=0$ the lattice already equals $R^2$. The multiplier ring $\{a\in\operatorname{Frac}(R)^2:a\Lambda\subseteq\Lambda\}$ is precisely $T_k$, since $T_k$ is a ring containing $(1,1)$. Hecke stability now gives $0\le k\le h$.

If one line is good and the other complementary, the [symmetric congruence module](FIRST_PASS_PROJECTOR_LENGTH.md) is $R/\pi^kR$. Its normalized length is $k/v(\ell)$. Both choices of the good line give this same **single** contribution. If both have the same label, the contribution is zero. Thus a block with $k=0$ contributes zero for every labeling. The bound $k\le h$ also follows from Tilouine and Urban's comparison of the algebra congruence ideal with a module congruence ideal of generic rank one on the selected component. [Tilouine–Urban, §2.1, equation (2.2)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.11166#page=9)

There is an exact test for obtaining $k$ directly from the eigenvalues. Equality $k=h$ holds if and only if $\Lambda$ is cyclic over $T_h$. Indeed $T_h$ generates itself. Conversely, a cyclic lattice of full rank is a diagonal rescaling of $T_h$ by an element with both coordinates nonzero. Its multiplier ring is $T_h$, forcing $T_k=T_h$. In a single residual block, Nakayama's lemma tests this on the given lattice by $\dim_{R/\pi}(\Lambda/\mathfrak m_T\Lambda)=1$, where $\mathfrak m_T$ is the residual maximal ideal of $T$. Under these conditions a mixed block contributes $h/v(\ell)$ from its Hecke eigenvalue differences alone. For example, the same two characters of $T_h$ act on its regular lattice and on $R^2$. Their mixed lengths are respectively $h$ and $0$ before normalization. Thus substituting the spectral congruence depth for the integral index on the actual family requires information about its lattice.

One separating operator suffices to compute the index without a cyclicity hypothesis. Let $A$ be its matrix on an integral basis of $\Lambda$, let $\alpha,\beta$ be its distinct eigenvalues, and let $c$ be the minimum valuation of the entries of $A-\beta I$. This minimum is independent of the integral basis. The projector onto the $\alpha$-line is $(A-\beta I)/(\alpha-\beta)$. It has rank one, and $\operatorname{tr}(A-\beta I)=\alpha-\beta$ gives $c\le v(\alpha-\beta)$. The minors formula thus gives
$$
                       k=v(\alpha-\beta)-c .
$$
In particular, when the chosen generator attains $h$, the lattice is cyclic precisely when its reduction acts by a nonscalar matrix. This test uses the action on the stored lattice in addition to the two eigenvalues.

To use this calculation on both sides of the length comparison, one must verify the two-line decomposition and the action of the original projection on its two lines. The set of good characters defines the module in the length comparison. The separate conductor invariant uses the source’s set $Z$, which can strictly contain its good subset. Identifying $h$ with an exponent from that conductor requires checking its cut as well.
