TP1. THE LITERAL PREFIX SPACES, FREEZING, AND INTEGRAL BASES

There are N ordered letters. Each is either a vector x_i or a covector xi_i
with r coordinates, and has prescribed degree d_i. The prefix Lie action is

  E_ab^[j]=sum_(i<j,vector) x_(i,a) partial_(i,b)
            -sum_(i<j,covector) xi_(i,b) partial_(i,a).

The notation here E_ab^[j] denotes the prefix j, not a divided power.
Divided powers will always be written E_ab^m/m!.

For a prefix j and M in Z union {infinity}, YCond(j,M) is the sum of the
GL_r-stable prefix subspaces whose first-coordinate weights are at most M.
Equivalently it is the sum of prefix isotypic components with highest
weight lambda satisfying lambda_1<=M. This is exactly the source's
maximal-stable-subspace definition, since the greatest first-coordinate
weight of an irreducible is lambda_1. For finitely many cutoffs (J_k,M_k),

  Y(L,J,M)=Pol_L intersect intersection_k YCond(J_k,M_k),
  Y_Z=Y intersect O[all variables].

The normalized form is

  B_L(F,G)=sum_alpha F_alpha G_alpha alpha! / product_i d_i! .       (TP1.1)

T has ordered tie positions a_i, i=0,...,t-1. Literal admissibility means
a_i strictly increase; letter a_i is a vector and a_i+1 a covector; and
some cutoff occurs at J_k=a_i+1. In particular the pairs are disjoint:
a_(i+1)>=a_i+2. The tie parameter z_i is a nonnegative integer. Set the
covector degree at a_i+1 to n_i, leaving all other degrees unchanged.
This is L(n)=LL.setTies T n. Let S_i(n) be the sum of the degrees through
a_i, and impose S_i(n)<=n_i. Let M^(i)_k=M_k for J_k<=a_i+1 and infinity
otherwise; this is thrUpTo i.

For the first K+1=a_i+1 letters, put S=sum d_j and let rho=r-1. The source
MM_w is the subspace satisfying the earlier prefix constraints, full torus
weight w, and the highest-vector equations E_ab F=0 for a<b with a,b!=rho.
MM_w,Z=MM_w intersect O[variables]. This is a GL_(r-1)-highest space with a
full GL_r torus weight; replacing it by a full GL_r-highest space before
freezing would be incorrect.

Append a covector y of degree n>=S. The exact freeze map is

  fr_n(f)=y_rho^(n-S) f(x_i,c -> F_i,c),                         (TP1.2)

where a vector letter has F_i,rho=sum_c x_i,c y_c and
F_i,c=y_rho x_i,c for c!=rho. A covector letter has
F_i,rho=y_rho xi_i,rho and
F_i,c=y_rho xi_i,c-xi_i,rho y_c for c!=rho.
Thus fr_n is integral, preserves every old letter degree, has y-degree n,
and specializes to f at y=e_rho. It is injective.

Here is the converse without an additional integral representation theorem.
Localize at y_rho. Every substitution F_i,c/y_rho is the action of the same
unitriangular matrix g(y) on vectors and its inverse transpose on covectors.
The new positive-root derivations E_(c,rho), c!=rho, are the infinitesimal
translations that take y_c to zero while fixing y_rho. A polynomial killed
by these derivations is invariant under the corresponding unipotent
one-parameter groups: the exponential is a finite polynomial since these
derivations are locally nilpotent. Apply the parameter y_c/y_rho and use
homogeneity in y. It gives F=fr_n(F|_(y=e_rho)) in K[variables,y_rho^-1].
The polynomial inclusion into this localization is injective, so equality
holds already in the polynomial ring. If F is integral, its specialization
is integral. This proves the literal integral FreezeBij.

The remaining upper-root equations, with neither index rho, commute with
freezing. The torus weight changes from w to w-n e_rho. Consequently fr_n
identifies MM_w,Z with the entire integral highest-vector lattice of weight
w-n e_rho in the prefix through a_i+1, subject to the old cutoffs.
Old cutoffs are preserved in both directions: over the localization g(y)
is a group substitution on each prefix, while the action on later letters
commutes with that prefix group; conversely specialize y=e_rho. The
largest-stable-subspace definition of YCond therefore gives exactly the
same cutoff, with no additive shift. Renaming the first a_i+2 letters into
the full word gives source Ffull and hw_is_Ffull.

Each MM_w,Z is a full lattice in MM_w: it is an intersection of a rational
subspace with a finite free coefficient lattice, so it is finite free and
clearing coefficient denominators proves fullness. Its order filtration by
powers of x_(a_i,rho) is saturated. More explicitly, the order-e coefficient
map has kernel Fil_(e+1) in Fil_e and image grLat_e; the latter is a submodule
of a free coefficient module, hence free. Choose and lift a basis of this
image, then continue downwards from the maximum possible order o=d_(a_i).
The resulting O-basis is simultaneously adapted to all orders. This is
exactly AdaptedBasisExists. No simultaneous integral isotypic decomposition
is used. Only finitely many w occur: every coordinate is between -S and S.

TP2. THE SHARPER GAMMA INTEGRALITY WITH THE SOURCE CONSTANT

This proves GamIntC, which was explicitly not claimed in TI4. Let there be
K+1 old letters, the last a vector x of degree o, with total old degree S.
Let f,g be integral polynomials of that multidegree, rho any coordinate,
z>=0 an integer, and r>=1. Write F=fr_S(f), G=fr_S(g), n=S+m, m>=0, and

  D=sum_c partial_(x_c)partial_(y_c)=D_rho+D_perp,
  A=o+n+r+z-1,
  Phi=sum_(j=0)^o (-1)^j E^j D^j/[j! A(A-1)...(A-j+1)],
  Gamma(n)=binom(A,o) B_Fock(Phi fr_n(f),fr_n(g))
            /[m! S! product_i d_i!].                       (TP2.1)

All these conventions agree with TI3 and literal tieGam. The potentially
zero falling denominators never occur: A>=o when n>=S>=o and r>=1,z>=0,
except o=0 where only the j=0 term is present. Set

  B=floor(log_p(S+o+r)),  cI=((K+4)r+6)B.                  (TP2.2)

Claim: p^cI Gamma(n) has O-valued binomial coefficients and degree at most
S+o. In particular it is a sum from k=0 to S+o of c_k binom(n,k), c_k in O.

Proof. Moving E^j across the Fock form replaces it with D^j. Expand
D^j=(D_rho+D_perp)^j. For a term indexed by 0<=a,a'<=j<=o, let h x^beta
and h' x^gamma be terms of D_perp^(j-a) F and D_perp^(j-a') G. The exact
integer coefficients in the contraction expansion imply

  v(h)>=2v((j-a)!)-rB,
  v(h')>=2v((j-a')!)-rB.                                  (TP2.3)

Indeed D_perp^b is the sum over multiindices alpha of size b of
(b!/alpha!) partial_x^alpha partial_y^alpha. Each differentiated monomial
contributes two factors divisible by alpha!. Thus its valuation is at
least v(b!)+sum_c v(alpha_c!). Grouping at most r coordinate factorials
loses at most rB, since each binomial coefficient of upper entry <=S+o+r
has valuation at most B. This is Legendre's floor-sum formula. The bound
also holds for a zero coefficient, using infinite valuation.

The support of F has beta_(x_rho)<=beta_(y_rho); the same remains true
after D_perp powers. All terms differentiated too often are zero. For a
nonzero paired term the final monomials must match. Therefore

  beta=delta+a(e_xrho+e_yrho),
  gamma=delta+a'(e_xrho+e_yrho).

Put e=delta_(y_rho)+a, e'=delta_(y_rho)+a',
c1=o+S+r+z-1-j, d1=(delta_(x_rho)+a) falling a,
d2=(delta_(x_rho)+a') falling a', and Q=product_v(delta+a e_yrho)_v!.
After canceling the m! and the A-dependent denominators, the term is exactly

  C binom(m+c1,o-j) binom(m+e,e) binom(m+e',a'),
  C=(-1)^j h h' binom(j,a)binom(j,a')d1 d2 a'! Q
        /[binom(o,j)(j!)^2 S! product_i d_i!].              (TP2.4)

For example, the y_rho factorial cancellation is
  (m+e)!(m+e')!/[(m+delta_y)!m!]
   =e! a'! binom(m+e,e)binom(m+e',a').
The remaining factorials in Q include this e!. All binomial upper shifts
c1,e,e' are nonnegative. The y-row degree gives e<=S-j+a, so the degree of
the three binomial factors is at most S+o-2j+a+a'<=S+o.

It remains to check C. The row degrees of delta+a e_yrho are the K earlier
degrees d_i, o-j in the x row and S-j+a in the y row. Hence

  v(Q)>=sum_(i<K)v(d_i!)+v((o-j)!)+v((S-j+a)!)-(K+2)rB.

Also v(d1)>=v(a!) and v(d2)>=v(a'!). Combine this with (TP2.3).
Writing F(t)=v(t!), the exact residual factorial expression after these
row losses is

  -v binom(S,j-a)-2v binom(o,j)-v binom(j,a').              (TP2.5)

To verify it, replace v binom(j,a) with F(j)-F(a)-F(j-a)
and likewise for a', then replace F(j)+F(o-j) with
F(o)-v binom(o,j), and F(j-a)+F(S-j+a) with
F(S)-v binom(S,j-a). These are equalities, not asymptotic estimates.
Thus the residual loss is at most 4B. The two differentiated coefficients
and the K+2 row groupings together lose (K+4)rB. The source's cI, with
6B rather than the sufficient 4B, clears every term.

Products of integer-valued binomial polynomials have integral binomial
coefficients: their forward differences at nonnegative integers are
integral, and the finite Newton expansion recovers them. Multiplication by
the integral scalar p^cI C preserves this property. Summing the finite
terms proves it in m. Translation m=n-S preserves integral binomial
coefficients, by Vandermonde with the integer coefficients binom(-S,k).
This proves literal GamIntC, including S=0. The 4B intermediate improvement
is not substituted into any downstream source parameter.

TP4. EXTENDING A HIGHEST-VECTOR MAP INTEGRALLY

We use exactly the two inputs HypP(e1), HypW(e1), supplied by GG from IW.
HypP gives self-adjoint canonical prefix isotypic projectors, their nested
commutation, and p^e1 times each projector integral on the fixed-degree
coefficient lattice. HypW says that for a pure full-GL_r isotypic integral
vector x, p^e1 x belongs to the divided-power span of the integral highest
vectors of that type. These inputs do not assert integral semisimplicity.

Fix a prefix j0. Let C be its fixed-degree module satisfying only those
cutoffs J_k<j0, with later letter degrees zero. This is source Cset. Compose
the canonical cutoff projectors for prefixes j=0,...,j0-1, grouping all
constraints at a common j into one type set. They commute, preserve letter
multidegrees and later variables, and their product Pi fixes C. Its
denominator is p^(j0 e1). It commutes with the full GL_r action on the
prefix j0: a smaller-prefix central isotypic projector commutes with that
prefix action, and later letters commute with it. Pi has the required
weight and cutoff properties on every polynomial multidegree. This is the
actual projC construction, not a projection onto an integral summand.

Let tau:C->C be an equivariant rational map that sends every integral
highest vector of C to an integral polynomial. Extend tau Pi coefficient
by coefficient in the later variables, using the fixed-prefix multidegree
component before Pi. Call the resulting operator T. It preserves Y: its
image has the early cutoffs by construction, and the later cutoffs follow
from equivariance for each larger prefix (the remaining-letter action
commutes with T).

On an integral highest vector for prefix j0, p^(j0 e1)Pi is integral,
and each later-variable coefficient is still a highest vector. Applying
tau therefore shows p^(j0 e1)T is integral on all such highest vectors.
To extend this estimate to an arbitrary integral vector y, first apply the
canonical pure-type projectors and multiply by p^e1. HypW spends one more
p^e1 to write each resulting integral pure-type vector in divided-power
words on integral highest vectors. The operators E_ab^m/m!, a!=b, preserve
the coefficient lattice: they are the coefficients of an elementary
unipotent substitution, with the inverse substitution on covectors. T
commutes with those words. Hence

  p^(e1(j0+2)) T(Y_Z) subset Y_Z.                           (TP4.1)

This is source int_main with K0=j0 e1, followed by bigOp_int. In summing
the pure types there is no cost proportional to their number. The three
denominator contributions are j0 e1 for the early cutoffs, e1 for the
isotypic projection and e1 for highest-vector generation.

TP5. THE ACTUAL PARTIAL INVERSE FOR ONE TIE

Fix a degree vector n, a tie i, j0=a_i+2, and its prefix MM_w,Z lattices.
Choose Smith left and right bases f'_x,f''_x for p^ceil(kappa)Gamma; x ranges
over all pairs (w,basis index). These bases are integral, and f'_x together
span all integral highest vectors after freezing, by TP1. Write

  h_x=fr_(n_i)(f'_x), h''_x=fr_(n_i)(f''_x),
  lambda_x=w_x-n_i e_rho,
  cst=p^ceil(kappa) binom(o+n_i+r+z_i-1,o)
           /[(n_i-S)! S! product_(old j)d_j!].

Smith form gives, when lambda_x=lambda_y,

  cst Fock(phi_i h_x,h''_y)=delta_xy d_x.                  (TP5.1)

Also cst Fock(h_x,h_y) is integral. For clarity, this second assertion
does not follow by deleting phi_i from an arbitrary integral matrix.
For each paired monomial the normalized factorial denominator loses at
most (K+2)r floor(log_p X) relative to the product of its coordinate
factorials, where K+1=a_i+1 and all row degrees, including n_i, are <=X.
This follows by grouping the coordinate factorials row by row; additionally
(n_i-S)!S! divides n_i!. Thus p^ceil(kappa) clears this denominator under
kappa>=(N+2)r^2 ell and ell>=floor(log_p X)+1. Multiplication by the integer
binomial does not worsen it. This is exactly gamma_zero_integral.

Set e=floor(E), c=p^e. A Smith index is good when |d_x|>=p^(-E), or
equivalently v(d_x)<=E. Because v(d_x) is an integer, c/d_x is integral
for every good index. On the highest vectors define

  varpi h_x = h_x if x is good, and 0 otherwise,
  tau h_y = sum_(x good,lambda_x=lambda_y)
               (c/d_x)[cst Fock(h_x,h_y)] h''_x.            (TP5.2)

All coefficients in this sum are integral. A type-preserving map on highest
vectors extends uniquely to an equivariant map on C: over K, C is a direct
sum of irreducible cyclic modules generated by the independent h_x, and
two nonzero highest vectors of the same weight generate isomorphic
modules. This statement is only over K. Formula (TP5.2) proves integral
highest-vector preservation, exactly the premise needed in TP4.

The contravariance Fock(E_ab u,v)=Fock(u,E_ba v), together with the Lie
equivariance of phi_i, extends (TP5.1) to the full identity

  Fock(phi_i u,tau v)=c Fock(varpi u,v),  u,v in C.         (TP5.3)

One can verify this by moving every lowering word across the form to the
other side. The highest-highest equal-weight case is the single surviving

Extend tau and varpi to the full word by the Pi-and-coefficient construction
of TP4. Denote the results T_i and pi_i. Splitting the Fock form by the
later-variable monomials, whose factorial weights multiply the prefix

  B_L(phi_i u,T_i v)=p^e B_L(pi_i u,v),                    (TP5.4)

for u in Y with only the cutoffs J_k<=a_i+1 imposed, and v in full Y.
This precise asymmetry is the thrUpTo i interface. Both coefficient slices

T_i preserves full Y and

  p^K_i T_i(Y_Z) subset Y_Z,
  K_i=e1(a_i+4)<=e1(N+2).                                (TP5.5)

All coefficients/operators defining pi_i are confined to the first j0

For each w choose any nonzero prefix highest vector of that type and let

  codim_Y ker(pi_i-Id) <=sum_w b_i,w(n) D_i,w(n).           (TP5.6)

This is a dimension-weighted bound. It does not count only highest vectors
and does not assume a common continuation dimension for different weights.

TP6. A PRIMITIVE PARTNER BEFORE APPLYING TIES

  |B_L(u,m)|=|u_alpha alpha!/product_i d_i!|>=1,            (TP6.1)

This proves literal YPartnerC. It constructs the partner from a unit

TP8. THE EXACT HIGHEST-WEIGHT MULTIPLICITY IDENTITY

Fix one tie i and all degrees, with n_i>=S_i. Let m_w=dim MM_w for the
prefix before the covector. By the freeze isomorphism TP1, these are the
dimensions of the full GL_r highest-vector spaces in C after adding that
covector, at weights lambda_w=w-n_i e_rho. Choose a rational basis in each
highest-vector space. Over K, it generates a direct-sum decomposition

  C=direct_sum_(w,b=1,...,m_w) V_(w,b).                    (TP8.1)

Here every V_(w,b) is an irreducible copy of highest weight lambda_w.
The sum is direct by complete reducibility and independence of the highest
vectors. This decomposition is rational only.

The full fixed-multidegree space with the early constraints is C tensor the
polynomial space on later letters. The product map realizes this tensor
product faithfully because the variable sets are disjoint. Later-prefix
Lie actions are the sum of the action on C and the action on the remaining
letters. Thus every summand V_(w,b) tensor later polynomials is stable for
those actions. The canonical later-prefix type projectors preserve each
summand. Intersecting with all the later cutoffs therefore preserves the
direct sum (TP8.1), and its (w,b) summand is exactly the source YYsp for
that highest vector.

If b changes at fixed w, the unique equivariant map taking one nonzero
highest vector to the other extends by the identity on later variables.
It and its inverse preserve every later-prefix cutoff. Hence all those
summands have the same dimension D_i,w(n). The result is the exact identity

  sum_w m_w D_i,w(n)=dim Y(n).                            (TP8.2)

This proves MonoCount. It holds with any chosen nonzero fw in each nonzero
MM_w, not just with a special basis. For zero MM_w the multiplicity is zero.
All sums are finite. In particular (TP8.2) uses the dimension of the actual
selected subspace Y, not the ambient monomial dimension.

TP9. WHY EACH CONTINUATION DIMENSION INCREASES WITH THE TIED DEGREE

Hold every degree except n_i fixed, and let n'_i=n_i+u, u>=0. The prefix
MM_w itself is unchanged. If h=fr_(n_i)(fw), then

  h'=fr_(n_i+u)(fw)=y_rho^u h.                            (TP9.1)

Let V=K[GL_r]h and V'=K[GL_r]h'. Their highest weights are lambda and
lambda'=lambda-u e_rho. Put eta=y_rho^u and consider the product space
W=V Sym^u(K^r)^*, in the polynomial ring of the prefix. It is a quotient
of the corresponding tensor product. Every weight of W is dominated by
lambda', and its lambda'-highest line is K eta h. To see uniqueness, the
only way to obtain the maximal weight in the tensor product is to use the
highest weight of both factors: lower weights differ by nonnegative sums
of simple roots, whose sum can be zero only when each difference is zero.
The product eta h is nonzero in the polynomial domain. Thus V' is the
unique Cartan component in W, with multiplicity one.

Let Q be the rational canonical projection of W onto this component, and
define F(v)=Q(eta v) on V. Equivalently, Q can be a polynomial in the
prefix Casimir: for dominant mu strictly dominated by lambda' and with
the same total weight, cas(mu)<cas(lambda'); multiply the factors for the
other finitely many types and divide by its nonzero value at lambda'.
This is the construction used explicitly in the pinned proof. No integral
estimate for Q is needed here.

F shifts every weight by -u e_rho. Since eta is a highest vector, it commutes
with every raising operator E_ab, a<b, in the sense F(E_ab v)=E_ab F(v).
Also F(h)=h'!=0. It follows that F is injective. Indeed, a nonzero kernel
would be stable under weights and raising operators; choose a maximal
weight in it. Its vector is killed by all raising operators and hence
lies in the unique highest line Kh of the irreducible V, a contradiction.
This argument does not claim that F intertwines lowering operators.

Extend F by the identity on later polynomial variables. It remains
injective, since this is a tensor product over K. We check the actual
cutoffs. Those before j0=a_i+2 hold on all of V' times later variables:
the frozen highest vector h' has the same old cutoffs by TP1, and they
are stable under the prefix j0 action and later multiplication. For a
cutoff J>=j0, multiplying an allowed polynomial by eta preserves the
upper first-weight bound. The GL_J orbit of eta consists of covector
polynomials of degree u; all their first-coordinate weights are <=0.
Thus the product of this orbit with an invariant space having first
weights <=M still has first weights <=M. Applying Q also preserves that
cutoff: Q is a central operator for prefix j0 and commutes with the
remaining-letter action, hence with the full GL_J action and its
canonical isotypic projectors. Letter multidegrees change only by u in
the tied covector row, as required.

We have therefore constructed an injection of the actual continuation
spaces, proving precisely WeightSpaceDimMonotone:

  D_i,w(n)<=D_i,w(n with n_i replaced by n'_i).             (TP9.2)

There is no assumption that the original polynomial multiplication by eta
itself lies in V'. The Cartan projection Q is essential to this proof.

TP10. THE EXACT WEIGHTED MULTITIE MEAN, TheoremMXB

For each tie choose a finite progression

  I_i={s_i+d_i j: 0<=j<Y_i},  Y_i>=1, d_i>=1, p not dividing d_i,
  top_i=s_i+d_i(Y_i-1),  G=product_i I_i.                  (TP10.1)

Here d_i denotes the progression step, not a letter multidegree. Assume
S_i(top)<=s_i. Let X>=r be an integer bounding every letter degree at top,
every Y_i, every 2S_i(top), and every z_i plus its preceding vector degree.
Set ell>=floor(log_p X)+1, E>=1, and assume

  kappa >= (3/2)(N+2)r^2 ell,
  kappa >= e1(N+2),
  kappa >= 3ell+kappa4.                                  (TP10.2)

Assume HypP(e1), HypW(e1) on every L(n), and GG with exponent kappa4 on
every tie-prefix MM_w,Z order-graded block. This is the literal list of
TheoremMXB input hypotheses. All grid members satisfy the size premises,
since increasing tied degrees increases every preceding total S_i.

Fix i and the other coordinates. The prefix before the i-th covector is
independent of n_i, so use one adapted basis of each MM_w,Z for the whole
progression I_i. Let b_i,w(n_i) be the bad Smith count for its gamma matrix.
TP3 applies with K=a_i. Its kappa requirement is implied by (TP10.2):
since a_i+2<=N and r>=2,

  (a_i+4)r+6 <= (3/2)(N+2)r^2.

Replacing its actual prefix total by S_i(top), and using
kappa+3ell+kappa4<=2kappa, gives

  average_(n_i in I_i) b_i,w(n_i) <=m_w epsilon_i,
  epsilon_i=2S_i(top)/Y_i
                +(2kappa+2S_i(top)ell/Y_i)/E.              (TP10.3)

By TP9 every D_i,w(n_i) is at most its endpoint value D_i,w(top_i).
The factors b_i,w are nonnegative, so multiplication and summation give

  average_(n_i in I_i) sum_w b_i,w(n_i)D_i,w(n_i)
    <=epsilon_i sum_w m_w D_i,w(top_i)
    =epsilon_i dim Y(n with coordinate i replaced by top_i). (TP10.4)

The final equality is TP8. Averaging (TP7.5) in all the other coordinates
and summing over i proves the exact source conclusion

  Avg_(n in G) bad(B_(L(n)) composed with Phi_n,Y_Z(n),D)
   <=sum_i epsilon_i Avg_(n in G) dim Y(n^(i,top)),
  D=floor[t(E+kappa)+kappa].                              (TP10.5)

This is ordinary finite Fubini summation. The update map n->n^(i,top) is
not injective, and no such assertion is required. It occurs with its
actual multiplicity on both sides of the coordinate average. The result
has exactly the endpoint dimensions required by the downstream segment
proof, rather than a replacement by dim Y(n) or by a fixed ambient rank.

TP11. THE LITERAL TheoremMV7 PARAMETER SPECIALIZATION

Take N=2s+1, with alternating vector/covector letters beginning and ending
with a vector. Assume the literal admissibility and ConditionShape: every
cutoff is either at the end N or at the prefix a_i+1 before a tied
covector. The proof above works for more cutoffs, but we retain these
actual source hypotheses. Let a>=1, E>=1, and let the progressions satisfy
(TP10.1) and S_i(top)<=s_i. The exact SizeHypothesis is

  total(L(top))<=8a+r,
  o_i(top)+n_i(top)+r+z_i+1<=8a+r,
  Y_i<=8a+r,
  2S_i(top)+1<=8a+r.                                    (TP11.1)

The inequalities are real-valued; all displayed degrees, z_i and Y_i are
integers. Put

  ell=log_p(8a+r)+1,
  X=floor(8a+r),
  e1=floor(r^2 ell),
  kappa4=(s+4)r^2 ell,
  kappa=3(s+2)r^2 ell.                                  (TP11.2)

The natural floors agree with ordinary floors because all quantities are
nonnegative. From X<=8a+r and X>=r we have
floor(log_p X)+1<=ell, while (TP11.1) supplies all the X bounds in TP10.
The three numerical comparisons there are immediate but recorded exactly:

  (3/2)(N+2)=(3/2)(2s+3)<=3(s+2),
  e1(N+2)<=(2s+3)r^2 ell<=3(s+2)r^2 ell,
  3ell+(s+4)r^2 ell<=3(s+2)r^2 ell,

where the last inequality uses r>=2.

For completeness, the projection and Gram input sizes match the GG route,
not an untracked larger exponent. For a full grid word of total T<=8a+r,
the quantitative projection/Weyl cost supplied by GG is

  binom(r,2) floor(log_p(T+r-1)) <= r^2 ell.

Indeed T+r-1<=p(8a+r), p>=3, and binom(r,2)<=r^2/2. The bound is natural,
so it is <=e1. For tie prefix K=a_i and total S,

The GG graded exponent is at most

  (K+7)(r^2/2)ell <= (s+4)r^2 ell=kappa4,

because K+2<=N=2s+1 implies K+7<=2(s+4). These are exactly the comparisons
in gram_hypothesis_of_log_bound and theoremMV7, including the separate

Insert (TP11.2) into (TP10.5). With the source notation

  epsSeg(S,Y,kappa,ell,E)=2S/Y+(2kappa+2S ell/Y)/E,

WHAT THIS CLOSES AND WHAT IT DOES NOT

This closes the simultaneous-partner and weighted-mean bridge listed in
TI9(a), once combined with the GG proof of the stated input lattices.
It also supplies the exact partner theorem, with its literal constants,
used in the first step of TI9(b). It does not by itself prove the
segment/digit-class endpoint-dimension comparison, terminal contraction,
harmonic/highest-weight dimension transfer, or paired polynomial lifts
into cleaned Type-I edge lattices. Those are SW/RL work, and are not
inferred merely by counting good Smith blocks.
