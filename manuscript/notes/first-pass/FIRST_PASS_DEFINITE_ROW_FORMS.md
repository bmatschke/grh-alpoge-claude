# Definite forms on the original first-pass row

Fix the pinned first-pass column, its fitted state, and a positive row number $j=i+1$. Its abstract row space at a tuple $\mu$ already has a coefficient model over $L$, a specified map into the complex row, and a lattice. The third-stage argument asks for an injective realization as algebraic automorphic forms on the standard definite unitary group of rank $n_V$. It needs the correct level, character, dual weight and Hecke action. On each tuple used by the state, evaluation in this realization must agree with the stored theta reading. These requirements constitute the input called `SMt`. [Exact data requested][def]

## Identify the forms by their values

Use the frame of the original definite space $V_i$. Its Gram matrix is the identity, its lattice is the standard self-dual lattice, and its finite change of coordinates on this side is the identity. The resulting map $u$ from the standard finite adelic group to the finite adelic group of $V_i$ is a bijection and identifies the rational groups. Pull back the given coefficient representation along the rational isomorphism. This gives an irreducible algebraic weight $a$ and a coefficient-space isomorphism $e_W$ intertwining the two rational actions. In the state's ordering of the embeddings and dominant weights the highest weight of the dual is precisely $\lambda_{n_V,j,\tau}$ prescribed at each $\tau$. [Frame, coefficient action and dual weight][side]

In the finite model a level form is determined by a vector of coefficients satisfying the original equivariance conditions. Extending this vector by the rational left action and the level character gives an adelic form. The extension and restriction maps are inverse on that space. In particular, if $v_1,\ldots,v_d$ is the prescribed coefficient basis, its extensions $f_1,\ldots,f_d$ are complex-linearly independent and span the full subspace with that level and character. The extension uses the inverse of the stored finite character in the level-form convention. Pullback by $u$ carries its equivariance law to the standard group, so the result is the prescribed type subspace there. The map on the complex row is now determined by sending the original complex image of each $v_b$ to $e_W^{-1}f_b\circ u$, with $e_W^{-1}$ acting on values. Linear independence gives injectivity. Semilinearity for the same embedding $L\hookrightarrow\mathbf C$ identifies this map on every coefficient vector. The argument applies also to the zero space. [Linear extension and equality of spans][basis] · [Adelic type space][side]

For a tuple used by the state, verify evaluation on $v_b$ first. The comparison of the original Hilbert model with its adelic form identifies the value at the framed adele $u(g)$. The coefficient identification cancels the rational action introduced in forming that value. Thus, for every vector $x$ in the complex row and every standard finite adele $g$,
$$
 e_W\bigl(m_{\mu,j}(x)(g)\bigr)
     = \bigl(j^{\rm old}_{\mu,j}x\bigr)\bigl(u(g)\bigr).
$$
Indeed, for fixed $g$ the two sides are linear functions of $x$ and the coefficient images of the $v_b$ span. For a tuple outside the used set one still needs a definite form space. Take the full tuple level available from the column, use its decorated-lattice model and the state's identification of its coefficient space and lattice with those of $\mu$, and apply the same basis construction. Equality to a stored reading is imposed precisely at the used tuples. A single frame for each $V_i$ serves all of them. [Used-tuple evaluation][used] · [Full-tuple construction and lattice][all] · [The two cases on the fitted state][state]

## Recover the Hecke and lattice conditions

A permitted split generator at a place $v$ acts by the spherical double coset with the specified normalization. Off the level primes the local level is hyperspecial and its character there is trivial. Hence the local cosets give the finite-level cosets. On the finite marked model the sum changes the lattice at $v$ and keeps the other markings. The rationality and integral lattice comparison for decorated forms applies term by term with the chosen coefficient action. In the exact conclusion, if $x$ is integral, $x'$ represents the normalized Hecke translate, and $\mathfrak w$ is a coefficient prime away from the residue characteristic of $v$, one can choose $d\in\mathcal O_L\setminus\mathfrak w$ with $d x'$ in the stored lattice. This gives the required stability after localization. The column's allowed-generator condition excludes both its marked level and the ramification support of the relevant character, and puts $v$ away from that coefficient characteristic. [Marked-lattice sums](../galois/THETA_DECORATED_HECKE.md) · [Normalized operator and localized integrality][side] · [Allowed generators][gen]

Choosing the realizations just constructed gives the family, its comparison with the given readings and its Hecke data together. The source obtains this directly from the pin on a fitted state. In particular the realization uses the coefficient field and lattices already supplied on the first pass. The separate question of which of its automorphic constituents has a cuspidal transfer is addressed in the [cuspidal row comparison](FIRST_PASS_CUSPIDAL_ROW.md).
