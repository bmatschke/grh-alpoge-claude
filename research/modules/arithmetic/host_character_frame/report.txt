STATUS AND SCOPE

This component constructs the actual five character families in HFrame:
mu0, eta, chiPsi, del and del'. It gives the prescribed infinity types,
restriction to the real subfield, tame local laws, the exact inertia
order fifteen, and global finite order where the record requires it.
The four source producer theorems are expanded into character extension,
root-of-unity compatibility, a quadratic product formula, local residue
characters and a finite class-group argument. They are not retained as
unexplained existence assumptions.

HF14–HF15 construct the actual determinant quotient and the relative
determinant package, including the finer quotient for a finite SQ used
by the column record. HF16–HF17 make the field choices finite and
simultaneous. HF18 constructs one original canonical input I on one
base B, with Ok, Pins, IsTw, JetsIn, the prescribed frame and finite
twist family, ValLL0 for every later assembly, and Per.FieldOK. HF19
then supplies Laws, Law0, a literal Later, TwForms and ordinary
Hyp/JetsAll on that same base.

HF20 is an exact boundary and a conditional join, not an unconditional
V4.Core theorem. The source empty twisting datum cannot have a Core:
its forced field would put g0 into the empty set. The actual geometric,
arithmetic, theta and quantitative same-object suppliers are retained.
In particular the bare NStage of LS21, whose Loc and Tw are empty, is
not identified with a stage admitting NChoice or nonempty twist reads.

All source bytes are read from immutable Git objects at the pin.
The builder records full-file and inclusive declaration-range SHA256,
URLs and the exact marker-to-next-marker SHA256 of every proof paragraph.
It performs no Lean compilation, kernel replay, proof formalization,
PDF build or PDF rendering. Human proofs and source-byte provenance are
distinct forms of evidence. The work does not claim RH or GRH.

CONVENTIONS THAT MATTER

1. E is the specified CM host and F its specified fixed real subfield;
   K=Q(sqrt(-d)) is the distinguished imaginary quadratic subfield.
   AH9–AH12 prove the actual finite unramifiedness and host identities.
   We do not replace this host by an arbitrary CM extension.
2. unitIdl includes every infinite component and finite local units.
   A finite character restriction is chi composed with finPartI.
3. HasInfQ(a) means integer infinity exponent n=2a. Thus mu0 has
   exponent -2e, although its IsMu0.inf field reads HasInfQ(-e).
4. The value normalization is chi(P) N(P)^(-s), with positive real
   powers. Eta uses s=1/2; mu0, chiPsi and both deltas use s=0.
5. Finite valuations in the proof are additive, uniformizer order one.
   Source inequalities for principal units use multiplicative
   valuations strictly less than one.
6. IsDelta requires exact order fifteen on every local unit group
   above its chosen rational prime. Its global order is only required
   to divide some positive integer. The proof gives a divisor of
   15 times the class number; it never claims global exact order 15.
7. The relative determinant representative uses max(Delta_P,0),
   the literal integer-toNat convention. Signed products would have
   a different normalization.
8. Ordinary ideal factorization, finite residue fields, completion
   topology, Chinese remaindering, finite Galois closures, quadratic
   reciprocity and the basic class-group results already reconstructed
   in TL14 are background. No global class-field-theory existence
   theorem or character-existence endpoint is imported without proof.

HF1 — ROOTS OF UNITY AND WEIGHT COMPATIBILITY

The elementary Kronecker argument is included: the characteristic
polynomials of successive powers of an integral norm-one element have
bounded integer coefficients and all roots on the unit circle. Finitely
many such polynomials imply repeated powers. This supplies exactly the
root-of-unity reduction used when a principal norm-one idele is a unit
at every finite prime.

For the actual host, AC2–AC3 give the nontrivial involution sg in Psi
which inverts every root of unity. Pairing tau with tau sg gives both
the CM-type product one and the norm to K equal to one. MUQ carries
integral representatives of the actual rational weights and their
root compatibility. The nonconstant-weight compatibility is retained
exactly until PreX.e_even supplies it in HF13/HF18.

HF2 — CONTINUOUS CIRCLE EXTENSION

For a homomorphism alpha:A->G and open subgroup U, the compatibility
a(y)=psi(alpha(y)) on alpha^-1(U) makes psi(u)a(y) well defined on
U alpha(A). Circle divisibility extends it to G. The proof states the
one-generator extension and Zorn argument: if the new generator has
a least positive power in the old subgroup, choose the matching root;
otherwise choose its value freely. Every such extension is continuous
because its restriction to the open subgroup U is continuous.

This is the substantive existence argument behind the source's
open-subgroup character theorem. The source instead forms a quotient
of G times the discrete A; the resulting compatibility is the same.
No assertion of continuous extension from an arbitrary closed subgroup
is used.

HF3 — TAME LOCAL MODELS AND ACTUAL CONJUGATION

A chosen uniformizer splits a local unit into its power of the
uniformizer and unit part. The residue of that unit part gives a
multiplicative map to the finite residue units, and a residue character
therefore gives a tame continuous local character. This construction
also supplies a value at a uniformizer and hence a character on the
whole local multiplicative group.

The global model on unitIdl is the finite product of the specified
infinite phases and the finitely many residue characters. Its
continuity follows from product topology and open principal-unit
kernels. Conjugation permutes primes, preserves their normalized
valuations and extends by density to their completions; the induced
restricted-product map is the actual IsConjI. It fixes the real
subfield ideles, including all infinite components.

HF4 — ANTICYCLOTOMIC CHARACTER WITH PRESCRIBED TAME SUPPORT

The selected finite set contains no pair P,bar(P), including no
conjugation-fixed prime. Its local model has infinity exponent -e.
Compatibility on roots of unity is the displayed product of its
infinite and residue factors. HF1 reduces the norm-one principal
intersection with unitIdl to those roots. HF2 extends the model while
killing that principal norm-one group.

Setting chi(z)=nu(z/Cz) gives a Hecke character: for principal z,
z/Cz is exactly a principal norm-one element. It is unitary, trivial
on ideles from F, and has exponent -2e. At selected P it is theta_P;
at bar(P) it is the inverse conjugate residue character; elsewhere
it is unramified. This proves the local power-kernel criterion for
every natural exponent, including zero. The inverse at the conjugate
prime does not change the exact order.

HF5 — GLOBAL FINITE ORDER

The claim is specialized to the characters actually needed: finitely
many tame ramified primes, unramified elsewhere, and infinity type
zero. Triviality of infinity type on diagonal global elements extends
to the full infinite component because the rational vector space E
is dense in E tensor R and the character is continuous.

One common residue-character exponent kills every local unit factor.
Finite-support local products are dense in the unit ideles with
infinite component one; continuity kills their full product. The
resulting power factors through the ordinary ideal class group.
The class-group exponent then kills it. This avoids assuming that
separately finite local image already implies finite global image.

HF6 — COMPLETELY SPLIT PRIMES AND THE RESIDUE PRODUCT

The equality of the number of primes with [E:Q], together with the
sum ef degree formula, forces e=f=1 at every prime above p. The
decomposition group is trivial. Conjugation has no fixed prime there
and exchanges the two primes in K.

The group Gal(E/K) acts simply transitively on the primes above a
chosen prime of K. Reduce the norm product of an integral unit using
those automorphisms. Its factors are the residues in F_p, so applying
the same residue character gives the residue character of Norm_E/K.
For the roots in HF1 this norm is one. This is the compatibility that
allows all selected primes over one quadratic prime to be used at
once. Finite-field cyclicity gives the primitive character and each
required exact-order character.

HF7 — THE ACTUAL MU0 FAMILY

primes, tameness everywhere, and the prescribed natural-integer

HF8 — THE ACTUAL CHI_PSI

Take the selected set empty and e=-1 in HF4. The CM-type root product
one proves compatibility. The result is unramified at every finite
prime, unitary, trivial on F and has integer infinity exponent 2.
These are exactly the four IsChiPsi fields.

HF9 — THE HOST QUADRATIC PRODUCT FORMULA

At an inert finite F-prime the local quadratic character is
(-1)^ord, and at a split prime it is one. At an infinite real place
it is the sign. Away from d the finite uniformizer value equals
Jacobi(Nv,d): this follows from the Frobenius action on sqrt(-d),
Euler's criterion and ordinary quadratic reciprocity. At two the
host congruence and splitting give the same result.

For an algebraic integer y unit at all primes above d, reduce its
norm through Q(sqrt(qd)). Writing twice that quadratic integer as
A+B sqrt(qd) gives A^2-4 Norm(y)=qd B^2 with A integral, B rational
and ord_d(B)>=0. The norm
is a unit modulo d, so it is a nonzero square there and its Jacobi
symbol is one. The archimedean signs exactly give the signed norm
needed in this calculation.

For arbitrary y, all primes over d split in E/F. Chinese remaindering
constructs b in E whose norm a=b bar(b) has the prescribed orders
at those finitely many F-primes. Its global quadratic product is one:
the infinite signs are positive and the inert finite orders are even.
The quotient y/a is a unit at the bad primes. A denominator ideal
prime to them has an element congruent to one at them; multiplying
clears the remaining denominators without reintroducing d. Applying
the integral argument to numerator and denominator proves the full
principal-idele product formula. No global reciprocity theorem is
hidden in this step.

HF10 — ETA EXTENSION WITH ODD INFINITY TYPE

The source extension problem differs from HF4: eta is prescribed on
F-ideles rather than trivial on them. A free abelian group on the
finite F-primes gives the finite-uniformizer map beta and its desired
quadratic values b. Prescribe b(m) on diag(k) beta(m).

If this idele is in unitIdl, relative unramifiedness shows the orders
of k are -m_v at all primes over v. Thus k/bar(k) is a root of unity.
By the stated root compatibility choose delta=zeta bar(delta),
with even inert orders and phase product one. Then k/delta lies in F.
Its principal quadratic product formula from HF9 proves that the
prescribed b(m) equals the infinite phase model, using oddness of
all infinity exponents. This is exactly the overlap condition HF2
needs. The extension kills principal ideles, is unramified everywhere,
and has the specified restriction on each real-subfield local factor.

HF11 — ACTUAL HOST ETA

For zeta not equal to -1 take delta=1+zeta. The sg pairing gives the
infinite phase product one. Such delta is a unit outside the host
conductor: at a residue characteristic not dividing the root order,
prime-to-characteristic roots reduce injectively, so zeta cannot
reduce to -1 unless it already equals -1. For zeta=-1 take
delta=sqrt(-d); all CM phases are i and 4 divides |Psi|. All primes
over the conductor split in E/F, so the required inert orders are
zero. HF10 with all exponents one gives all four IsEtaT fields.
This is the actual host eta, not LS's synthetic local-stage eta.

HF12 — TWO ACTUAL DELTA CHARACTERS

For p congruent to one modulo 15 and completely split in E, choose
an exact-order-fifteen character of F_p units and select all E-primes
over one quadratic prime. The infinity weights are zero. HF6 supplies
the root overlap condition, so HF4 gives the local laws. The selected
set and its conjugate exhaust all primes above p, and hence at every
one of those primes the local power kernel is exactly 15|n.

HF5 gives positive global finite order. The two chosen rational
primes are distinct, so the resulting deltas have disjoint nonempty
ramification supports. Each is nontrivial and they are different.
If p is outside a supplied forbidden set, full splitting, e=f=1
and these laws give IsTwist. They do not give the different IsPsi
predicate demanding a prime-power global order.

HF14 — ACTUAL DETERMINANT QUOTIENT

KD is the algebraically generated subgroup of unit ideles congruent
to one above g0,g1 and local ideles of elements of F*. This is the
literal Subgroup.closure. It contains an open subgroup, so it is
open and closed and equals its topological closure. Local
uniformizers may be chosen in F*. The subgroup contains infinity.
For a principal F-idele, remove its finitely many exceptional local
components, including all auxiliary places; the quotient is in the
specified congruence subgroup. Thus the F diagonal lies in KD.

Finite restrictions of mu0 and chiPsi kill KD by their local laws,
finite-support density and continuity. ChiPsi also kills the image
of all unit ideles. For a rational norm-one z, the explicit solution
k=1+z, or k=a-bar(a) when z=-1, gives z bar(k)=k. Two solutions
differ by F, so e1(z)=[diag(k)] is well defined and multiplicative.
The infinity exponent 2 gives chiD(e1(z))=product phi(z)^(-1).

HF15 — RELATIVE DETERMINANT AND THE FINER SQ PACKAGE

KD times unitIdl is exactly the subgroup whose orders at P and
bar(P) agree. Balanced orders are removed with finitely many
F-uniformizers, using finite unramifiedness. For a full self-dual
lattice M, paired localization gives d_bar(P)(M)=-d_P(M) by the
conjugate inverse-transpose basis matrix. No global freeness is used.

For Delta=d(M')-d(M), the literal representative with exponents
max(Delta,0) has P-minus-bar(P) order Delta. This proves its self
and cocycle laws. Under rational unitary gamma, Delta is ord(det gamma),
which equals ord(k)-ord(bar(k)) for the same Hilbert-90 solution.
Thus relDet(M,gamma M) is the image of e1(det gamma). Rank zero
gives the unit determinant and zero orders.

Adding the finite SQ congruences changes KD to KDq but does not
change KDq times unitIdl. The quotient modulo the unit image is
therefore canonically the same balanced-order quotient. All preceding
laws descend with the identical positive-part representatives. This
is exactly trueDtPackQ, the package inserted into I.cols.

HF17 — ACTUAL BASE EXTENSION

The proof expands BaseExt.exists_base. Finitely many algebraic
numbers are adjoined before taking a splitting field. Capitulation
comes from roots of generators of hth powers of finitely many ideal
class representatives. Equality of hth powers of extended ideals
implies equality of ideals prime by prime.

For each required type norm, pair the exponents r_tau+r_bar(tau)=n.
The root b_c of the type norm of a class generator principalizes
the corresponding ideal. Every embedding sigma of the final field
has |sigma b_c|^2=N(c)^n, because sigma restricted to the CM host
commutes with conjugation. This does not assume that all embeddings
of an arbitrary larger coefficient field commute with conjugation.
The class relation (x)a=(y)c then produces the required generator
for every ideal with its exact norm at every sigma.

Choosing Ks=LL0=L and the restricted complex conjugation fills all
actual Base fields and all three requested finite containment sets.
The preliminary part is equal to the input Pre; no host, weights,
spaces, type primes or numerical data are changed by this extension.

CONTINUITY / DEPENDENCY ORDER

HF1–HF12 prove the producers without using a future HFrame or original
ColIn. HF13 assembles the actual frame. HF14–HF15 use it for the
determinant data. HF16 depends on the fixed frame and TL14; HF17 is
