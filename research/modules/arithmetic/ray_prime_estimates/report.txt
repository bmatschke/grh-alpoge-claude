PURPOSE

QW11 in version 0.19 exposed two remaining premises: an actual LFam
with conductor B=4dM^2 and NormOne support, and a uniform lower bound
for its weighted identity-class coefficients in short intervals.
This component proves both suppliers. The cumulative reconstruction
keeps the original QW text as historical mathematics; QR25 records
the new discharge of those explicit premises.

The full human-readable proofs are in main.tex. The precise source
declarations, full-file and range hashes, proof-paragraph hashes,
and reviewed claim coverage are in provenance.json and
source_locators.txt. All source access is through immutable Git blobs.
There has been no Lean build or kernel replay.

ARITHMETIC CONSTRUCTION: QR1–QR9

QR1 retains the exact ray relation modulo M O_K in K=Q(sqrt(-d)),
not just the ordinary ideal class. Units congruent to one are trivial
when M>=3: a nonzero integral t in u=1+Mt has complex norm at least
one, while |u-1|<=2. Thus the ray coset counts ideals without an
extra unit factor. Its covolume is M^2 N(a) sqrt(d)/2.

QR2 constructs an actual reduced Z-basis using a shortest primitive
vector. The shifted binary quadratic form has a,b>=1 and
ab=dM^4/4. Every represented value is a positive integral ideal norm.
QR3 counts its rows and uses a layer-cake proof of the shifted
one-dimensional sum/integral discrepancy. The error is <=10 sqrt(x),
independent of class, shear or shifts.

QR4 proves the uniform shifted Gaussian bound by Poisson summation,
including an absolutely convergent Fourier series. QR5 applies it
twice and groups finite norm fibers. This proves both literal count
and theta laws, with kappa=2 pi/(sqrt(d) M^2), including the uniform
theta error B^10 t for all 0<t<=1.

QR6 proves the ideal von Mangoldt convolution using ideal divisor
factorization. QR7 proves the degree-two bound sum_g lambda_g(n)
<=2 Lambda(n), including ramified primes, and the same-family NormOne
support. The congruences are exactly M|D and 2M|(A-D-2), with
4p=A^2+dD^2.

QR8 factors the real-character ideal divisor sum into geometric sums.
Each is nonnegative, and an even exponent contributes at least one.
This proves both the positivity clause and the square-norm lower
clause of LFam.sq. QR9 checks all fourteen LFam laws, the norm-zero
and norm-one coefficients, the ray group cardinality bound, and
1/B<=kappa<=4. The coefficient objects used in the analytic proof
are the same objects that carry the ray arithmetic support.

ANALYTIC CONSTRUCTION: QR10–QR24

QR10 uses the count remainder to continue the literal regularized
F_chi to Re(s)>1/2 and obtains the uniform bound
|F_chi(s)|<=300 B (|s|+1)^2 for Re(s)>=11/20.
QR11 transforms convolution into a differential equation, proves
L_chi=exp(H_chi) on Re(s)>1, and derives both nonvanishing and the
corrected logarithmic derivative. It does not assume an Euler
product for an arbitrary abstract LFam.

QR12 proves the heat-sum bounds and the nonprincipal identity
integral_0^infinity Theta_chi(t) dt=F_chi(1). The exchange at one
uses the cumulative coefficient remainder and Fubini; it does not
assume absolute convergence of the coefficient Dirichlet series at
one.

QR13 uses W(y)=integral exp(-y/w-w) dw/w. Absolute convergence of the
expanded double integral follows from W(y)<=12 y^-3 and coefficient
bounds of order Bn. Positivity of the real-character convolution
gives I(v)>=W(1)>0. The error from replacing the principal heat sum
by rho/t is bounded by (C B^11)^2 sqrt(v) times 8/3. Taking
sqrt(v)=delta B^-22 proves |F_chi(1)|>=c_* B^-45 uniformly for
real characters, including the principal character.

QR14 obtains |F'_chi|<=18000 B near one by Cauchy's estimate, hence
1-beta>=c_9 B^-46 for every real zero beta>=3/4 of a real character.
This is a polynomial conductor-dependent gap. It is not a fixed-width
zero-free region for conductors tending to infinity.

QR15 proves the required local zero/log-derivative lemma with a finite
Blaschke product and the Poisson estimate for a positive real part.
QR16 fixes the actual disks (center 5/4+it, outer radius 7/10,
zero radius 3/5, evaluation radius 1/2) and obtains constants uniform
in the family and character.

QR17 supplies the two numerical cases of the zero-free argument:
conjugate real-character zeros at small nonzero height, and the
3+4cos(theta)+cos(2theta) positivity argument in all other cases.
QR18 uses two- or four-character positivity to show there is at most
one exceptional zero across the entire family, counting multiplicity.
It is simple. QR19 removes that exact simple reciprocal and obtains
the O(ell(t)^2) bound in the half-width strip.

QR20 proves a truncated Perron estimate with the original full floor
endpoint and an explicit O(log u) endpoint allowance. QR21 shifts
the contour while tracking the principal residue +1, the exceptional
residue -1, and the possibility that the exception lies to the left
of the chosen vertical edge. QR22 chooses T=exp(c0 sqrt(log u)/32)
and obtains the uniform exceptional-zero explicit formula. A coarse
absolute Chebyshev constant is enough for its small-height case;
the existential source conclusion does not require the source's
sharper numerical choice in that case.

QR23 selects the exception sets family-wide and bounds the total
exceptional interval loss using the B^-46 gap. QR24 combines the
character sum with the exact error comparison. It proves

  sum_{floor(X)<n<=floor(X(1+h))} lambda_0(n)
      >= c_9 h X / (4 B^47),

provided log B<=c sqrt(log X) and exp(-c sqrt(log X))<=h<=1,
with fixed positive c independent of the family. All these quantifiers
are uniform, and no exceptional loss is multiplied by the number of
characters.

CONSEQUENCE AND SCOPE: QR25

QW10–QW11 already remove proper prime powers and divide by logarithms.
With the newly proved suppliers they yield ShortNorm with exponent 47
and count coefficient c_9/32 (after the existing fixed shrinkage of
the interval constant). The literal levelPrimeN transport and the
Y^(9/10) dyadic norm-prime count of QW12–QW13 are now supplied without
the former LFam or weighted-short-interval assumptions.

This does not create an arbitrary geometric datum. The original
ModAt, host/rank and endpoint inequalities still specify the later
applications. It does not build nonempty compatible local stages,
simultaneous PairEx, a same-embedding theta field, or the centered
prime-sum objects required in the four global branches. It supplies
the particular arithmetic prime theorem used in those constructions.

STANDARD BACKGROUND AND ALTERNATE HUMAN STEPS

Ideal factorization, the imaginary quadratic integer basis and
covolume, the ray-coset construction from IG1, and the lattice averaging principle from TL14 are earlier results.
QR9 gives the explicit two-dimensional 2sqrt(d) specialization needed
for the class-number constant, rather than assuming TL14 fixed it.
Basic complex analysis used here comprises Cauchy's estimate,
the residue theorem, the maximum principle, Poisson's formula,
and differentiation under dominated integrals; each specialized
estimate is proved in the text. The shifted Gaussian argument,
finite Blaschke proof, layer-cake ellipse count and Perron summation
are human reorganizations of the pinned source arguments.
