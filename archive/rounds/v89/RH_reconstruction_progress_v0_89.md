# Comparing the heights and values of the theta periods

**Edition 0.89 · 25 November 2026**

For each word in the theta-lift construction, two quantities enter the arithmetic argument. One is the size allowed for its period map. The other is the fractional ideal generated by its values on the integral lattices. This edition proves how much the size allowance can vary between words, identifies the exact fractional ideal to test, and isolates the period identities needed to use the analytic realization. [New proofs (PDF)](RH_new_proofs_v0_89.pdf) · [Cumulative manuscript](RH_readable_reconstruction_v0_89.tex)

## The ninth height estimate

Write $T_u$ for the level prime attached to the word $u$, $h_u$ for its assigned height allowance, and $A$ for the sum of the prescribed level weights. The quantities to compare are $H_u=h_u+A\log T_u$. The level term in $h_u$ cancels exactly. The hook products, metric normalization and common size allowances disappear in the difference between two words. What survives is a sum of values of one function

$$g(y)=y\log y-y-\lambda y$$

on the archimedean row exponents, with the same $\lambda$ for both words. At each place and step, the two ends of the first weight block are fixed. Every block appended later is fixed. The interior of the first block has at most $k$ entries, each displaced by at most the chosen width $S$. On the interval of moving exponents, the mean value theorem bounds each change by $S(\log(4b)+|\lambda|)$. There are $m$ steps and $f$ places. The parameter choice makes $kS\le b/(nX')$, with $X'=\exp(x^{\epsilon/3})$. The bound obtained on every sufficiently late member is

$$|H_u-H_{u'}|\le \frac{4B_0}{nX'},\qquad B_0=fmbx^\epsilon.$$

In particular the relative variation is $o(B_0)$, uniformly over both words and all indices at a given scale. The ninth entry allows the larger error $C(|TT|a\Lambda_a+mB_0)$. This argument proves that entry in full. Proving that $h_u$ actually bounds the height of the period map is a separate estimate in entry 6. ## The finite places

For each word, its unshifted module $N_u$ and its prescribed fractional-ideal multiple are full lattices. One subtle place is the prime dividing the level. There the module is given using a Hecke operator on a complex space. Its image on the local integral input is finitely generated over the local ring. Intersecting that image with the rational subspace preserves finite generation by Noetherianity. This suffices for the original fullness statement. [I89M1](continuation_v89/integral.tex) · [Stacks Project, Lemma 10.120.17](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/034X)

Let $B_u$ denote the rational period pairing and $A_t$ the row lattice for tuple $t$. The fractional ideal $C_{u,t}$ generated by $B_u(N_u,A_t)$ has a concrete meaning. Its valuation is the least valuation of an entry of the pairing matrix on any finite generating sets, with value $+\infty$ for the zero pairing. If $k_{u,w}$ is the prescribed exponent divided out at $w$, entry 1 is exactly

$$v_w(C_{u,t_w})\ge k_{u,w}\quad\text{for every word }u\text{ and finite place }w.$$

The proof identifies the localization of the actual global row lattice with the row lattice of the tuple $t_w$ assigned at $w$. Outside the recorded support, only prime divisors of the denominator ideals of the base-tuple contents can matter. This gives one finite set for all words of a given member. The valuations at those primes still require the arithmetic estimates on every sufficiently late member. [I89M2–3, including the exceptional set](continuation_v89/integral.tex)

## Two inputs supplied by the literature

**Rapid decay.** A smooth cusp form $\phi$ in the source comes with one growth exponent valid for all its right derivatives. On each arithmetic component, Borel–Labesse–Schwermer show that Arthur truncation of such a function is rapidly decreasing. Cuspidality kills every proper parabolic constant term, so the truncation equals $\phi$ itself. The group has compact real center. It remains to compare the paper's height on Siegel sets with the project's adelic quotient height. The finite-place factors are bounded on the finitely many component representatives, and the product of the archimedean factors is bounded by a power of the real matrix norm. Thus $|\phi(g)|H_{\rm quot}(g)^N$ is bounded for every $N$, with a constant allowed to depend on the form and exponent. [R89D1–2](continuation_v89/decay.tex) · [Borel–Labesse–Schwermer, §§2.1, 4.4–4.5](https://www.numdam.org/item/CM_1996__102_1_1_0.pdf#page=13)

**Finite dimensionality at fixed level and type.** The source prescribes eigenvalues for differential operators given by cycles of matrix units. At a real place the complexified Lie algebra is $\mathfrak{gl}_r$. The degree-$j$ leading symbol of the $j$th cyclic operator is $\operatorname{tr}(X^j)$. For $1\le j\le r$, these polynomials generate the conjugation invariants by Newton's identities. Induction on degree shows that the operators generate the whole enveloping-algebra center. Their prescribed eigenvalues therefore give the finite-codimension ideal required by Harish-Chandra. The prescribed compact representations supply one finite list of types. His finiteness theorem now applies on each of finitely many arithmetic components, including to forms with nonzero constant terms. These are two of the five inputs in the source's construction of the compatible archimedean realization. [R89D3](continuation_v89/decay.tex) · [Harish-Chandra's theorem in Borel, §8.4](https://homepages.math.uic.edu/~rtakloo/papers/borel/borel3.pdf#page=23)

## The complex periods

At the terminal pair of theta lifts, the two relevant spaces split as $A\perp L$ and $T\perp L'$. The two line factors are paired with opposite forms and reciprocal splitting characters. With the resulting line identification $\kappa$, the common test is $C(u;v,w)=\Phi(v)\,\Xi(u,\kappa w)$. Factoring the Weil action on the orthogonal sums gives the same pointwise kernel in both orders of pairing. Kudla's seesaw identity gives the classical interchange for a common integrable kernel. To carry it down the project's chain of lifts, the argument must also match the prescribed finite-place support, the values on the surviving cells and their volumes. The manuscript explains precisely which simultaneous relations the resulting transfers would prove. [Common kernel and transport, S89K1–3](continuation_v89/seesaw.tex) · [Kudla, §1, equation (1.14)](https://wiki.epfl.ch/tan/documents/1984%20kudla-1.pdf)

The full spectral admissibility condition holds on the selected model at every sufficiently late index. The earlier gap and decay results, with Jiang–Sun–Zhu's real Bessel uniqueness theorem, supply the compatibility of column maps, period series and representatives used here. Given compatible row and column realizations, the complex part of entry 4 only tests the base tuple. Fix a proposed row isometry common to all words and a column isometry for each word. Let $p_i,s_i$ be the corresponding arithmetic and analytic evaluations on coordinate tensor basis vectors for one word. If $p_b\ne0$, it is necessary and sufficient that $s_b\ne0$ and

$$p_b s_i-p_i s_b=0\qquad\text{for every }i.$$

Then the common scalar for that word is $p_b/s_b$. An identically zero arithmetic word requires all the corresponding analytic values to vanish. The coefficients of these two-term relations lie in the embedded coefficient field. That gives explicit test vectors for the common kernel. Producing the compatible realizations and proving the selected periods satisfy these tests is still part of the reconstruction. [Base-tuple proof, R89C1–3](continuation_v89/comparison.tex) · [Jiang–Sun–Zhu, Theorem A](https://www-users.cse.umn.edu/~jiang034/Papers/JSZ09.pdf)
