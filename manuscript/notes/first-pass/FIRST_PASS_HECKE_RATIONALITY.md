# Rational Hecke operators and joint eigenvectors on the labelled state

Fix a fitted state $x$ with its first-pass label data on the common eventual range. Let $K_s$ be the number field of its pinned geometric core, $L$ the row coefficient field, and $L_0\subset L$ the designated coefficient subfield. The field of values condition on the core gives the specified inclusion $K_s\to L_0$, whose composite with $L_0\to L$ is the existing algebra map. The rationality assertion constructs the Hecke operators, their eigenvalues and the integral estimate on this same state and reading. Its spectral input is the pinned lower-row multiplicity comparison together with the indicated theta and representation-theoretic inputs. ## Geometric forms and characters

For $1\le j\le m$, the pinned frame gives the $K_s$-form $W_K$ of the row coordinate space $W_L$ and a full $\mathcal O_{K_s}$-lattice $M_K$ there. Its finite integral generators span $W_K$ over $K_s$. For every element of the group acting on these coordinates, the pinned geometric action restricts to the rational form in the action-preserving case. In the complementary case the model prescribes the identity. For a marked prime $\mathfrak p$ and a residue-field unit $u$, lift $u$ to a nonzero integral element of $E$. The idele supported in its $\mathfrak p$-coordinate has trivial infinity component. The pinned small-field character value there is in the embedded $K_s$, and its inverse is exactly the local determinant character on $u$. Multiplication over the marked primes puts the entire character in $L_0$. At each of the two marked rational primes $g$, the host contains in its small field the specified complex root of unity of order $g(g-1)$. Its $(g-1)$st power gives $\zeta_g$ mapping to $\exp(2\pi i/g)$. Define $\theta_g(a)=\zeta_g^{-a}$ on the additive residue field. Its value at $1$ proves nontriviality. The comparison of the marked frame with the standard unipotent group carries the sum of the first superdiagonal entries to the prescribed residue sum. The residue-field identification carries the reduction of $\xi_0$ to the prescribed $\bar\xi$ since it preserves the equation between an integral numerator and a denominator invertible modulo $\mathfrak p$. Multiplying $\theta_g(\bar\xi\sum_i u_{i,i+1})$ over the marked factors gives the **inverse** of the original frame character. Both the subgroup and its actual character have therefore been identified. ## The three geometric Hecke assertions

Only the integral test, the transform of a cuspidal section, and the reading of the transform from the three Hecke fields of the series package are used here. They have a joint producer with no external arguments, evaluated on the prescribed input moduli, frames and level data. 1. **Algebraic transform.** At an admitted split prime $P$ of residue characteristic $p$, consider a geometric Hecke kernel and its quotient isogeny. The theta structure identifies the translated summands using the inverse generator multiplier. The sum over representatives of the translation kernel modulo the specified torsion is invariant under that kernel by reindexing. Hence it descends through the surjective étale isogeny. The resulting theta section is unique by pullback and the same formula, and the trace of a generator is a generator by the zero-trace test. This defines the summand on a geometric Hecke point. On a reduced affine test ring the finite étale kernel correspondence has algebra $B$. The trace of the universal summand in $B$ is the desired sum on every geometric fibre. Equality on geometric fibres proves base-change functoriality and the required frame covariance. Multiplying by the prescribed normalization gives a section of the original weight. On the uniformizing charts its terms are bounded by cusp estimates on rationally isometric charts, with a uniformly bounded number of terms at this fixed Hecke operator. This proves the cusp condition. 2. **Reading in the actual frame.** At the split place, the split linear model and the fixed vacuum give a bijection between the Hecke kernels on the selected framed point and the right-coset representatives of its double coset. 3. **Integral estimate.** At a finite place $\lambda$ of $K_s$ away from $p$, extend the testing complete discrete valuation ring finitely to define the isogeny and its frames. The isogeny and its inverse up to $p^2$ give an isomorphism of the integral Lie lattices because $p$ is a unit there. For the theta coordinate, the translated pullback sum extends to the integral model. This gives a nonnegative valuation for the change of generator. The opposite estimate applied through the reverse isogeny gives a nonpositive valuation, hence the multiplier is a unit. Rebasing the triangular frame now uses only units. Good reduction is preserved when the good-point condition is imposed. The weight character of a unit frame is a unit, so evaluation of a section bounded on the pertinent integral test points yields integral Hecke summands after the extension. Integrality descends through the finite integral extension. This gives the bound at every $\lambda\nmid p$. For each generator of $M_K$, the image under $H_{j,k,P}$ is in $W_K$ and has only a finite denominator. A power of $NP$ clears the valuations above $p$, while the preceding bounds hold at every other prime. Taking the maximum exponent for the finite generating set gives an exponent $b=b(j,k,P)$ on the whole $\mathcal O_L$-span of $M_K$, in particular on its good subspace, as required. ## The normalized eigenvalues and the span

The spherical Hecke eigenvalue in the reading has to lie in the **specified** embedded $L_0$. To see why an eventual common field works, on each finite-dimensional $K_s$-form choose finitely many of the rational Hecke endomorphisms spanning their linear span in $\operatorname{End}_{K_s}(W_K)$. Adjoin the complex images of a rational basis of $K_s$ and all the roots of the minimal polynomials of these finitely many operators. On a nonzero simultaneous eigenvector in the complex span of the reading, an operator's polynomial relation carries through the reading, so each selected eigenvalue belongs to the resulting number field. The eigenvalues of the other operators are the same $K_s$-linear combinations. The separated archimedean weights on the eventual range ensure that the occurring row characters are realized by nonzero vectors in that span. Taking the union over the finitely many rows gives a finite set of algebraic generators. The eventual selection includes field bounds for the frame, invariant under its transports, among the generators required in the fixed coefficient field. Thus the normalized value $c_{j,k,P}\lambda_{\sigma,k,P}$ has an actual preimage in $L_0$. This last field-selection step is essential. The finite-dimensional argument alone produces a number field that may depend on the frame. Finally, read any vector of the good $L$-subspace as a finite sum in the occurring good complex type spaces. If all supported types have the same eigenvalues, the intertwining identity and injectivity of the reading give a joint eigenvector over $L$. Otherwise choose an operator $H$ separating two supported eigenvalue systems, with values $a\ne b$ in $L_0$. Both $(H-a)v$ and $(H-b)v$ read into strictly smaller sets of types and remain good by the frame comparison. Induct on the number of types and recover $v$ by subtracting these vectors and dividing by $a-b$. This proves the joint eigenspace span for all admitted Hecke operators, including coincident systems. The domain and its span are over $L$, the scalar labels are in $L_0$, and the integral estimate uses the $\mathcal O_L$-span of the $\mathcal O_{K_s}$-lattice. Together with the fixed character and operator reading, these are all the fields of the first-pass rationality record. This construction still takes the fitted geometric reading, the lower-row multiplicity comparison and the indicated local and archimedean representation theory as inputs. Its conclusion is the rationality slot of this intermediate level.
