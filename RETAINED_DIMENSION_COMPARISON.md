# Local weights and a second-moment criterion for the discarded fraction

A row is retained when its types pass the prescribed tests at both auxiliary primes. The distribution of these types in the regular representation of the finite residue groups is accessible by character theory. The arithmetic issue is how strongly the automorphic spectrum can prefer the exceptional types. There is a classical formula available for one part of the existing trace proof. There is also a sufficient estimate which only asks for a second moment of this preference.

## The classical input is a representation-density formula

Let $E/F$ be the CM extension, $V$ an $M$-dimensional nondegenerate Hermitian space, and $H=U(V)$. Fix $2n<M$ and an invertible $T\in\operatorname{Herm}_n(F)$, where the entries of a Hermitian matrix are in $E$. On $V^n$ put $Q(X)=(h(x_a,x_b))_{a,b}$. For a test function $\Phi$ as in the construction, its compactly supported smooth archimedean part may depend jointly on all the archimedean places. Its finite part is a product of Schwartz–Bruhat functions, equal to the prescribed lattice indicators almost everywhere. Choose a nontrivial adelic character trivial on $F$ and the self-dual measures on $V^n$ and the space of Hermitian matrices for the trace pairings. The identity needed is

$$
 \frac{1}{\operatorname{vol}([H])}
  \int_{[H]}\sum_{X\in V(F)^n,\ Q(X)=T}\Phi(h^{-1}X)\,dh
   =g_\infty(T)\prod_{v\nmid\infty}g_v(T),
 \qquad [H]=H(F)\backslash H(\mathbb A_F).
$$

Here $g_v$ is the density of $Q_*(\Phi_v\,dX_v)$ relative to $dT_v$ on invertible matrices, and the same definition at infinity uses all real coordinates together. This is the nonsingular coefficient of Weil's convergent Siegel formula. The group quotient has finite positive volume for each Haar normalization (for rank zero it is a point). For positive rank the unitary center is anisotropic over $F$. Normalizing the quotient to mass one removes the scale of Haar measure, and automorphy makes the integral independent of the fundamental domain. Yamana's Theorem 2 provides a check on the range and scalar for his Fock-model tests. The Witt index $r$ is at most $M/2$, hence $M-r>n$ and the coefficient multiplying the theta integral is one. Weil's orbitwise version applies to the compactly supported tests used here. [Weil, *Sur la formule de Siegel*, §§42–44, 52–53, especially (41)](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02391774) · [Yamana, Theorem 2 and its test space](https://msp.org/pjm/2013/264-1/pjm-v264-n1-p11-p.pdf#page=3) · [Pinned density assertion](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Formalism/UnipotentAdelicQuotients/SiegelWeilUnitaryConvergent.lean#L15-L47)

Two normalizations matter when applying Weil. First, $Q(X)=T$ consists entirely of full-rank tuples when $T$ is invertible. Each nonempty local fiber is a single orbit by Witt extension. Writing $X$ as a matrix of columns, at such an $X$ the differential $Y\mapsto X^*JY+Y^*JX$ is onto. For a Hermitian increment $C$, take $Y=\tfrac12 XT^{-1}C$. In submersion coordinates the quotient volume element $dX_v/dT_v$ on the fiber gives exactly the pushforward density. Smooth compact support gives continuity in those coordinates at infinity and local constancy at finite places. Integrating against a compact set in the regular locus gives precisely the source's defining identity for $g_v$. Second, both trace pairings are defined over $F$. For the standard pairing on the adeles the product of the local self-dual measures gives $\mathbb A_F/F$ volume one. In rational bases the determinants of our two pairings scale the corresponding local self-dual densities by absolute values of rational determinants to the power $1/2$. The product formula cancels those factors globally, including the $1/2$ in the Hermitian trace pairing. Thus Weil's adelic fiber measure is the product for the specified pair of self-dual measures. Weil's §42 proves convergence of this product and §52, Theorem 5, gives the orbitwise identity for tests. At the archimedean product group one may use the single archimedean density above by integrating in the joint fiber coordinates. For $n=0$ the source and target of $Q$ are points and the assertion is immediate. ## A sufficient second-moment estimate at the two type primes

Fix one actual row family member with the row/type identification of ARO23–24, positive total dimension $D$, and the two local weight estimates of ARO29 on the original types. For $j=0,1$, let $t$ range over the possible tuples of local series labels at the places above the $j$th prime. Write $P_{j,t}$ for its projector on the full row space. There are two probability distributions on this finite set:

$$
 \pi_j(t)=\frac{\dim\operatorname{im}P_{j,t}}{D},
 \qquad
 \mu_j(t)=\prod_{v\mid q_j}
 \frac{[1](e_{j,v}E_{v,t(v)})}{[1]e_{j,v}}.
$$

The marked places in this product are the ones used in the local level group. The numerator for $\pi_j$ counts *with* automorphic multiplicities. The bracket $[1]$ denotes the coefficient of the group identity. Left multiplication in the finite group algebra gives $|G|[1]p=\operatorname{rank}(L_p)$ for every idempotent $p$. The local level projector $e_{j,v}$ commutes with the central series projector $E_{v,t(v)}$. Thus all the local weights are nonnegative, summing to one over the labels. If $\mu_j(t)=0$, one local product projector has rank zero in the regular representation, hence vanishes in the group algebra. Its tensor product and inflated operator on the actual row space vanish as well. This proves $\pi_j\ll\mu_j$ even when some series never occur. [Finite character weights](retained_rows_v42/main.tex#L78-L135) · [Actual row labels and operators](arithmetic_rows_v46/main.tex#L911-L975) · [Cylinder factorization](arithmetic_rows_v46/main.tex#L980-L1014)

Define $R_j=\sum_{\mu_j(t)>0}\pi_j(t)^2/\mu_j(t)$. If $B_j$ is the set of bad label tuples at the $j$th prime, the lost fraction satisfies

$$
 0\leq\delta=1-\frac{D_{\mathrm{rows}}}{D}
 \leq \sum_{j=0}^1\sqrt{R_j\,\mu_j(B_j)} .
$$

Indeed the discarded rows are the union of the two bad events. Cauchy–Schwarz on each gives the displayed bound. The two primes may have any dependence in the automorphic spectrum. Only the marginal laws appear. In fact the sum defining $R_j$ can be restricted to $t\in B_j$ in this bound.

Put $\vartheta=\epsilon_z$, with the same parameters as GQT3. The established finite estimates give $\mu_j(B_j)\le x^{3C_s+3}\exp(-x^{\vartheta/6})$ on the indicated eventual range. It suffices, for example, that there be a fixed $0<\eta<1$ and one threshold for both primes and all members such that

$$
  \max_j \log R_j\le (1-\eta)x^{\vartheta/6}.
$$

Then $\delta\le 2x^{(3C_s+3)/2}\exp(-\eta x^{\vartheta/6}/2)\le \exp(-x^{\vartheta/7})$ eventually. This is the original retained-dimension exponent. The argument allows growing discrepancies from finite-group frequencies and needs no estimate of the total dimension by a formal degree once $D>0$ and its row identification are supplied. [Exact retained dimension and local weights](global_quantitative_v42/main.tex#L187-L249) · [Uniform numerical estimate](global_quantitative_v42/main.tex#L251-L339)

For example suppose there are just two labels, one bad, of finite-group weights $p,1-p$. Give them automorphic shares $\sqrt p,1-\sqrt p$. The bad share exceeds its finite-group weight by the unbounded factor $1/\sqrt p$ as $p\to0$, yet the second moment is $1+(1-\sqrt p)/(1+\sqrt p)\le 2$. When $p$ is as small as the bound above, this discrepancy still leaves the required exponential saving. This example illustrates the slack in the sufficient estimate. It makes no assertion about the actual label distribution.

There is an operator formulation suitable for a trace argument. On the finite algebra of functions of the $j$th labels, set $P_j(a)=\sum_t a(t)P_{j,t}$. The normalized automorphic trace $a\mapsto D^{-1}\operatorname{Tr}P_j(a)$ has squared dual norm $R_j$ for the inner product from $\mu_j$. This follows by expanding its Riesz representative $t\mapsto\pi_j(t)/\mu_j(t)$, with value zero on the zero-weight atoms. The same identity holds on the subspace supported on the bad atoms with the restricted sum. One can therefore attack a single norm bound for each of the two finite algebras. The original trace proof instead estimates individual event counts.

## Identification on the original family

For an actual linked member, the source's row identification uses its spectral realization, the agreement of tame generators and the goodness of its actual finite types. The algebraic step identifies the labels supplied by its depth-zero representations with the chosen Green-series projectors. The row isometry must carry those projectors to the ones acting on that very automorphic space. These are the hypotheses behind ARO23–24 in the second-moment argument. In the original approach one must then prove, for the total level projector and the two exceptional projectors, a common lower bound on the total and upper bounds on the exceptional traces. The source uses decay of lowest-type matrix coefficients, the flag-volume estimate, and separate estimates for near, far and deep contributions. Shin–Templier give quantitative Plancherel equidistribution for a fixed reductive group over a fixed field in their level and weight aspects. Here the rank and degree also grow, and the test operators project onto prescribed types at growing levels. Applying their theorem would require uniform dependence on these additional data. The [three trace estimates](RETAINED_PROJECTOR_COUNTS.md) and their [application on the original selected family](SELECTED_RETAINED_DEFECT.md) now prove the required scalar bound. The second-moment criterion above remains a sufficient alternative.
