# A marked type vector for the lower-row multiplicity comparison

Fix the lower source row $V=Y_0$ of rank $r$, its framed level, and the original row data satisfying `Given`, in particular the frame, lattice-genus and good-projector identifications used below. Let $\sigma$ be an admissible complex representation of the finite adelic unitary group of $V$ that is good for this level and its specified projector. There is a finite nonempty set $S$ of marked split places, an irreducible representation $\tau_v$ of $\mathrm{GL}_r(k_v)$ for each $v\in S$, and **one** nonzero vector $x\in\sigma$ with the following properties.

- Away from $S$, $x$ is fixed by the frame-lattice stabilizer $K_v$. At each $v\in S$, $x$ belongs to the span of the images of $K_v$-equivariant maps from $\tau_v$, inflated by reduction of that frame.
- For each $0<a<r$, some $v=v(a)\in S$ satisfies $\tau_v^{U_{a,r-a}(k_v)}=0$, where $U_{a,r-a}$ is the subgroup of matrices with identity diagonal blocks, arbitrary upper-right block and zero lower-left block. In a finer proper flag the radical contains the radical for any one of its cuts, so that cut's witness suffices for the flag.

These are the original `HasTypeVec` and `NoFixed` assertions. Irreducibility of $\sigma$ is needed subsequently for the multiplicity comparison. The vector and type construction itself works with the hypotheses above. ## From the good projector to the radical condition

Let $K_1$ be the principal level and let $H=\prod_{P\in P_g}\mathrm{GL}_r(k_P)$ be its marked reduction group. Level lifts give an action of $H$ on $\sigma^{K_1}$. Different lifts induce the same operator because their quotient lies in $K_1$. Goodness supplies $v_0\ne0$ in this fixed space on which the good projector $e$ acts as the identity. The $H$-span of $v_0$ is finite dimensional. By Maschke, decompose this span into irreducible subspaces. Project to a summand $U$ where $v_0$ has a nonzero component $y$. The projection is $H$-equivariant, hence $ey=y$. Write $U\simeq\bigotimes_P U_P$ for its decomposition into irreducibles of the finite factors. [Principal-level action, selection of the summand and its factors](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/ExistsTypeVectorOfGoodLevelRepresentation.lean#L109-L138)

The equality of the actual projector with the sum for good tame labels, transported through the group identification in `Given`, identifies the character of $U$ with the product attached to a good regular tuple. Indeed the central idempotent of an irreducible character acts on an irreducible summand by its character-orthogonality scalar. Since $ey=y\ne0$, a retained character is the character of $U$. At each place the good regular parameter gives Frobenius cycles, with lengths $d_1,\ldots,d_s$ summing to $r$, and the attached character is parabolic induction from the corresponding cuspidal blocks. Here Green identifies regular Frobenius orbits of characters of $\mathbf F_{q^d}^{\times}$ with the cuspidals of $\mathrm{GL}_d(\mathbf F_q)$. The project’s finite-group reconstruction gives the dictionary to Green’s original induction product and elliptic-value convention. The equality with the prescribed projector uses the row's tame-label and group-identification hypotheses. [Green, *The characters of the finite general linear groups*](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9947-1955-0072878-2) · [Precise Green input](finite_local_completion_v43/main.tex#L810-L850) · [Projector and good tuple](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/TwistedOperators/IrreducibleNotKilledByGoodProjectorSatisfiesH.lean#L159-L199)

For fixed $a\in\{1,\ldots,r-1\}$, there is a place over the first marked rational prime $g_0$ at which no subset of the $d_i$ sums to $a$. For $a\le r/2$ this is the no-common-subset condition for the good tuple. For $a>r/2$, taking complements would turn a subset of size sum $a$ at each place into one of sum $r-a$ at each place. Apply the first case to $r-a$. This proves the assertion with the place chosen after the cut. [Goodness and complements](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/TwistedOperators/IrreducibleNotKilledByGoodProjectorSatisfiesH.lean#L193-L215)

At that place, a radical-fixed vector in the induced representation would force $a$ to be a sum of whole inducing block sizes. To see this, realize induction by functions on the finite group with parabolic equivariance. A nonzero radical-invariant function has a nonzero value on some double coset for the inducing parabolic and the two-block parabolic. The relative position of their flags splits each inducing block of size $d_i$ into dimensions $b_i,d_i-b_i$, with $\sum b_i=a$. In that position the intersection with the conjugated two-block radical contains, inside each inducing diagonal block, the two-block unipotents for this split. Right invariance and left parabolic equivariance thus force the nonzero value to be invariant under those unipotents. A cuspidal block has zero invariants under every proper parabolic radical, so $b_i=0$ or $d_i$ for every $i$. This gives the forbidden subset. Equivalently, the normalized sum of the induced character over the radical, which equals the dimension of its invariant space, vanishes. The character identity transfers this zero to the selected factor $U_P$ (the remaining factors contribute their positive dimensions). Rank $r\ge2$ in the source, so taking $a=1$ also proves that the marked set is nonempty. ## One adelic change of frame

The marked primes $P$ are chosen one from each relevant conjugate pair. Restriction to the totally real field therefore gives a bijection from $P_g$ to its image $S$. The base-frame condition puts the level lattice and the reference frame lattice in the same genus. Choose a single finite adelic isometry $t$ carrying the frame lattice to the level lattice. At $v\notin S$, it conjugates $K_v$ into $K_1$. At $v\in S$, the conjugated $K_v$ acts through its indicated residue factor, with an isomorphism of residue fields and the split frame's reduction. Transport $U_P$ along this isomorphism to define $\tau_v$. It carries the two-block radical onto the corresponding radical entry by entry. [Local frames from one adelic isometry](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/TwistedOperators/ExistsSplitFramePrincipalLevelFiniteGroup.lean#L65-L109)

Use $x=\sigma(t^{-1})y$. It is nonzero since $\sigma(t)$ recovers $y$. For $k\in K_v$ one has $\sigma(k)x=\sigma(t^{-1})\sigma(tkt^{-1})y$. This proves fixedness away from $S$. At a marked place, $U$ restricted to the corresponding factor is a direct sum of copies of $U_P$ by the tensor product decomposition. Their intertwining maps, followed by inclusion into $\sigma$ and multiplication by $\sigma(t^{-1})$, are $K_v$-equivariant for $\tau_v$. Their images span the transported $U$, hence contain $x$ in their span. This proves both assertions on the same vector. [Conjugation and transport of the isotypic span](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/ExistsTypeVectorOfGoodLevelRepresentation.lean#L140-L222)

For the subsequent comparison, the prescribed isomorphism $\Phi$ to a target finite adelic row may differ from a lattice-compatible $\Phi_0$ by conjugation, $\Phi(g)=\Phi_0(bgb^{-1})$. The operator $\sigma(b)$ intertwines the two transported representations, so their multiplicities agree. The additional analytic input is the cuspidal trace comparison `TRCusp` (or its matched-parameter version) for the two actual rows, at the common odd rank, matching separated discrete-series weights, and this marked type. With it and the stated comparisons between cuspidal multiplicities and discrete-series model maps, the source deduces `MultEq`. The finite-group calculation above establishes the type hypothesis of that implication.
