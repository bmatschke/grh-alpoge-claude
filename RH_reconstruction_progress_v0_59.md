## From two period maps to one orbital sum

There are two ways to calculate a pairing of automorphic periods. On the analytic side, sum a seed function over rational translates to obtain a Poincaré series and take an inner product on the arithmetic quotient. On the geometric side, group the rational returns into orbits and integrate a rank-one operator for each orbit. The earlier chapter proved an identity equating these calculations once nine analytic and geometric conditions were supplied for the chosen pair. This edition proves those conditions on the data required by the original tied-setting theorem. [Earlier Gram argument, PGR18](period_gram_v57/main.tex)

The first difficulty is that the convolution operator acts on equivalence classes in a quotient Hilbert space, while its reproduction formula is an integral of ordinary functions. Testing against indicators of every finite-measure set identifies the two. Cauchy–Schwarz gives an absolute bound before the order of integration is exchanged. This places the actual Poincaré series in the required range of both normalized convolution operators. [Quotient proof, QPR1–QPR3](period_range_v59/main.tex)

The second difficulty is controlling all the returns at once. At finite places, the row lattice bounds denominators. At infinite places, counting number-field integers in a product of disks bounds the number of rational translates by a power of the operator norm. A compact covering set for the row unipotents lets one integrate that bound against the given wave-decay weight. The two Haar factors stay in the calculation. On the orbital side a finite Levi correction brings every return into the required support. Changing representatives preserves the amplitude because each infinite character factor cancels its own finite factor. [Folding, FLD1–FLD3](folding_v59/main.tex) · [Orbital estimates and phases, QPR4–QPR10](period_range_v59/main.tex)

The result, QPR11, applies to any ordered pair of words, including an equal pair, in one supplied setting with its original tie. Its operator series converges absolutely. Its value represents the normalized pairing of the two period maps for every pair of columns. The source also calls for summability of a second sequence of auxiliary vector norms and positivity of the identity factor. Both hold on the very same terms. This supplies the `KExpTied` and `AbsConvTied` inputs used in the diagonal and off-diagonal estimates. Producing compatible data for an entire family and proving the remaining estimates are subsequent steps. ## From Cartier curves to determinants on the given rows

The determinant-window calculation in the earlier edition takes an integral normal form as input. Passing to a new basis over the fraction field could change the valuations the calculation is supposed to measure. CRT1–CRT4 instead build an adapted chain on the two supplied saturated row families, select the required indices by residue rank, and reduce the terminal pairing by integral row operations. CRT5–CRT7 control the error in the resulting power series, including exact vanishing on the first axis and the stronger level-one estimate. These steps prove the original `LemmaM` and `LemmaMPlus`. CRT8 applies the resulting witnesses on each of the two prescribed flags to produce `BiSupply` and `RF.BiSupply`, the two odd-block inputs to the determinant calculation. The global application still needs the common geometric rows and the compatible character and monomial blocks. ## From a continuous cocycle to an integral Herr class

The local argument compares two descriptions of a Galois cohomology class. LWR1–LWR3 specify the cyclotomic group, the series variable and its evaluations, and the integral inclusion used by the comparison. LWR4 constructs the additive coordinates for the full group and its subgroup, keeping the distinct generator rules at 2. LWR5 extracts an integral pair from a continuous cocycle, proves the Herr relation, and shows that changing the lift changes the pair by a coboundary. It gives the full-group and subgroup class maps and their restriction compatibility under the original equality of generators and pairs. The next substantive theorem, `CmpS`, requires quantitative crystalline comparison on this same lattice, including coefficient bounds. ## From odd relations to rank-three components

The rank-three argument controls which components of the local deformation space can meet. On the exceptional chart, the sign action splits a presentation into even and odd variables. Writing the odd relations as a square matrix times the odd variables produces a determinant. The adjugate identity shows that this determinant annihilates the odd ideal. The incidence calculation then gives minimal components of dimension at least $3d+2$, connected through intersections of dimension at least $3d+1$, where $d$ is the sum of the local degrees. REX7 constructs the required nonsplit representation at the relevant exceptional primes. [Rank-three chapter, REX1–REX7](exceptional_v59/main.tex)

REX8–REX10 bound the opposite triangular locus by $2d+3C_0+2$. Under the original bigness hypothesis this is below $3d$, so that locus cannot contain a minimal component of the determinant section. Together these facts prove the original local rank-three `sharp3` theorem on the given chart. The next rank-changing step, `l53`, must start with the actual rank-two support and construct a separate ideal in the rank-three coefficient ring. The automorphic seed and global propagation have further premises. [Corner construction handoff](exceptional_v59/next_source_handoff.txt)
