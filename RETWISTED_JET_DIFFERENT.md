## The local estimate

Let $R$ be a complete mixed-characteristic DVR of residue characteristic $p$, and let $v$ be its additive valuation, permitting a finite extension to realize embeddings and torsion points. Fix the source's short rule, the odd auxiliary integer $N$, the squarefree level $M$, a unimodular Hermitian lattice $\Lambda$ standard at $M$, and its integral harmonic polynomial $P$ of degree $d$. Use the original geometric torsion chart of (ICL.13), with its integral Hodge directions, cubical graph line, integral theta generator, normalized translations and corrected jet. On the good-reduction locus the CM abelian scheme, its action, rigidified graph line, polarized biextension and theta section extend integrally with the specified frame. The comparison is with the chart reading this same original rule. In particular, changing its connection or using an unrelated rational power-series frame is outside the assertion.

For each level class $u$ write
$$
 X_u=\frac{1}{m_\Lambda N^d}\sum_{z\in Z_\Lambda}J_{P,N}(u,z),
 \qquad m_\Lambda=[\Lambda\cap O_E^n:NO_E^n]^r,
$$
where $r$ is the rank of the abelian period module over $O_E$. The two normalized translations take place in the order of the source's `IsUUOver`. The operator `sqN` changes the exponent's type by the identity $(N^2:\mathbb N)=(N:\mathbb Z)^2$. The multi-index formula for `hv` is exactly $J$. Thus the denominator here precedes the division by the outer cardinality of the level classes in (ICL.13). [Source short value and integral polynomial](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/ProjectiveLattices/ThShort.lean#L232-L273) · [Exponent and jet conventions](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Definitions/PlaceAdaptation/AdaptStd.lean#L94-L124)

Put $a_p=\operatorname{ord}_p|\operatorname{disc}E|/[E:\mathbb Q]$ and take $\alpha=p$ for $p\mid M$ and $\alpha=1$ otherwise. On these tests,
$$
             v(\alpha^d X_u)\ \ge\ -\lfloor d/2\rfloor\,a_p v(p).
             \tag{1}
$$
The estimate holds for every level class and every permitted $N$, even when $p\mid N$. The number $a_p$ vanishes outside the conductor of the cyclotomic host. Equation (1), divided by the normalization constant of the trait, implies (ICL.14) with its specified right hand side. It includes $d=0$ and gives integrality at every unramified nonlevel prime. ### The denominator of an adjoint mixed derivative

Let $c_{\tau i,\nu j}$ be the mixed first derivatives in the two arguments of the correction to the rigidified biextension, in integral eigen-directions of types $\tau,\nu$. The integral adjunction defect gives
$$
        (\tau(b)-\bar\nu(b))c_{\tau i,\nu j}\in R\qquad(b\in O_E),
        \quad\bar\nu(b)=\nu(\bar b).
$$
Choose an integral basis $b_i$ of $O_E$, with trace-dual basis $b_i^\vee$. Choose $c\in O_E$ generating the different after localization at every prime over $p$. Such a $c$ exists by approximation at finitely many primes, or by choosing its prescribed residues modulo the next powers of each prime. Then $c b_i^\vee$ are integral at every prime over $p$. For $\tau\ne\bar\nu$, the identity between a matrix of embeddings and the inverse transpose of its trace-dual matrix gives
$$
   \sum_i \tau(c b_i^\vee)(\tau(b_i)-\bar\nu(b_i))=\tau(c).
$$
Multiplying the adjunction tests by these integral coefficients proves $\tau(c)c_{\tau i,\nu j}\in R$. Since $E/\mathbb Q$ is Galois, the $g$ primes over $p$ have common ramification index $e$, residue degree $f$, and different exponent $a$. Therefore $\operatorname{ord}_p|\operatorname{disc}E|=gfa$ and $[E:\mathbb Q]=gfe$. Each embedding gives $v(\tau(c))=(a/e)v(p)=a_p v(p)$. This replaces the full-discriminant loss in the cited jet calculation. It is valid for wildly ramified $p$. More precisely each mixed coefficient can be bounded using the ideal generated by the individual differences $\tau(b)-\bar\nu(b)$. [Different and its trace-dual definition](https://kconrad.math.uconn.edu/blurbs/gradnumthy/different.pdf)

For completeness, derivatives here are ordinary mixed derivatives at the origin, with no division by a factorial. An integral tangent vector extends to an invariant integral derivation of the formal group. Iterating these derivations shows the raw integral function has integral Taylor values in logarithm coordinates. Leibniz's rule preserves this property for products and inverses of units. The differential identities for logarithm of a unit with value one and exponential of a series with zero constant term also preserve it, by induction on the order of derivative. For the biextension correction $h(x,y)$, each additivity defect is the logarithm of such an integral unit. Differentiating the defect in $x,x',y$, using a derivative in each of $x,x'$, gives every mixed derivative with $x$-order at least two. The $y$-defect gives the corresponding assertion for $y$-order at least two, and the two axes vanish.

After restriction to the graph of the integral CM endomorphism $w_0$, the residual ambiguity is quadratic. Terms pairing conjugate types act by the contraction operators that annihilate the harmonic $P$. In the polynomial-derivative pairing, multiplication by such a quadratic is adjoint to its contraction on $P$. This kills every term containing any unrestricted quadratic factor, also in products with the integral raw germ. Every other quadratic has mixed coefficient of valuation at least $-a_p v(p)$. In $d$ labelled derivatives of its exponential at zero each quadratic uses two labels. Induction using the derivative of the exponential gives a sum over pairings with integral multiplicities. The total loss is at most $\lfloor d/2\rfloor a_p v(p)$, including at two. Powers of the line multiply $h$ by an integer, which preserves this inequality.

### From the local jet to the level-class sum

On a locally standard $\Lambda_p$, set $N_0=\operatorname{lcm}(M,\exp(O_E^n/(\Lambda\cap O_E^n)))$. Then $v(N_0)=v(M)$. With $k=N/N_0$ the pullback of each smaller normalized translation under $[k]$ is the larger normalized translation. For odd $N$, symmetry and the $N^2$-power normalization characterize the lift, since a scalar of order dividing both 2 and $N^2$ is one. In logarithm coordinates, the corrected degree-$d$ jet of the pullback multiplies by $k^d$. After finite extension the smaller torsion sections extend by properness. Translation preserves the smaller power of the polarization line up to an integral line from the local base, which is free, by the relative Picard description. Its integral lift has a power equal to a unit, so the normalized lift is integral too. Applying the preceding jet bound gives
$$
       v(J_{P,N}(u,z))\ge d(v(N)-v(M))-\lfloor d/2\rfloor a_p v(p).
$$
For each fixed $u$ the summands are constant on cosets of $K_0=Z_\Lambda\cap A[N]^n$. Indeed the Weil pairing is trivial on $Z_\Lambda$ by self-duality, so the normalized translations there multiply, and every translation in $A[N]^n$ fixes the section pulled back by $[N]$. The subsequent level translation preserves this equality. By dual-lattice evaluation on the projective period module $\Pi$, $K_0\simeq ((\Lambda\cap O_E^n)/NO_E^n)\otimes_{O_E}\Pi$ and has cardinality $m_\Lambda$. The division by $m_\Lambda$ is therefore exact before applying the valuation. Now $v(M)=v(\alpha)$ by squarefreeness, proving (1). [Coset cancellation](https://github.com/Sidler4Hare/alpoge-claude-rh-reconstruction/blob/7d17bb991581172cebd554c33a205b1b0f3a2fb3/sections/theta-expansion.tex#L1196-L1244) · [Level reduction](https://github.com/Sidler4Hare/alpoge-claude-rh-reconstruction/blob/7d17bb991581172cebd554c33a205b1b0f3a2fb3/sections/theta-expansion.tex#L1328-L1394)

At a nonstandard lattice $p\nmid M$. Put $\Lambda_0=\Lambda\cap O_E^n$ and use the maps $a_0:O_E^n\to\Lambda_0$ (multiplication by $N$), $i:\Lambda_0\to\Lambda$ and $j:\Lambda_0\to O_E^n$ (inclusions). Write $a=ia_0$, and use the same letters for the induced isogenies of the tensor abelian schemes. They are finite flat. The tensor constructions are direct summands of abelian schemes using projectivity. The graph-line identities $j^*L=i^*L_1$ and $a^*L_1=[N]^*L$ respect rigidifications by Rosati adjunction applied to the inclusion matrices. The remaining coset sum before the last translation is $a^*\operatorname{Tr}_i(j^*s_0^{\boxtimes n})$ on the generic fibre, by the trace formula for a finite étale isogeny there. The trace of this integral section is integral on the abelian model by finite flatness and the projection formula. This step uses the unscaled class $X_u$, so no inverse determinant scalar occurs.

The last translation $t=t_u$ has order $h$ prime to $p$, by the prescribed half-level support. Take $t'$ with $h t'=t$ and $h^2t'=0$ after finite extension. The $[h]$-pullback of the last translate is, up to a root of unity, $a^*$ of the translate of $[h]^*\operatorname{Tr}_i(j^*s_0^{\boxtimes n})$ by $a(t')$. Both pullbacks lie over translation by the same $t'$. Their finite-order lifts differ by a root of unity. The translated section on the target has integral raw germ by the preceding integral-lift argument. Choose a split surjection $U:O_E^t\to\Lambda$ with section $V$. In $E^n$ we have $UV=1$, $G=U^*U$ integral, $V^*GV=1$, and $NV$ integral. Factor $a=U(NV)$. After pullback by $U$ the raw germ is integral. Differentiation of the remaining pullback gives $N^d$ times the polynomial of directions transported by $V$. Its coefficients are integral since $V\Lambda\subset O_E^t$. Its contraction for $G$ vanishes because $V^*GV=1$ carries it to the original harmonic contraction. All other mixed derivatives satisfy the different bound just proved since $G$ is integral. Division by $N^d$ and by the unit $h^d$ proves (1). ## An explicit all-place denominator on cuspidal short rules

Put
$$
 D(E,M,d)=c_0 M^d\prod_{p\mid\Delta_E}
 p^{\lceil\lfloor d/2\rfloor\,\operatorname{ord}_p(\Delta_E)/[E:\mathbb Q]\rceil},
 \qquad \Delta_E=|\operatorname{disc}E|.
$$
Here $c_0$ is the cardinality of the outer level-class index set of (ICL.13). Fix a short rule $g$ belonging to the actual small-field cusp row, admitting (ICL.13) on every good-reduction test there. Its Fourier weights are integral and its remaining common phase has valuation zero. Then
$$
                  D(E,M,d)\,\beta_H g\in M_j^F
$$
under the same marked toroidal arc and frame comparison as in the project's good-to-all criterion. The integer is uniform in the odd auxiliary integer $N$ and in the self-dual lattice for fixed row, level and degree. The specified normalization $\beta_H$ retains its lattice dependence. It also applies to a finite integral linear combination of short terms of that degree and level, each with that normalization, if the combination is cuspidal and has the indicated good-test formulas. Individual summands in that conclusion need only possess those formulas.

Indeed at each good test multiply (ICL.13) by $D(E,M,d)$. The factor $c_0$ cancels the outer division, the $M^d$ factor clears the loss at the level prime, and (1) together with the rounded local exponent makes each class integral. Hence the whole section is bounded by one at good integral framed tests at every residue characteristic. For the criterion's good generic Gauss valuation in residue characteristic $p$, this argument applies with arbitrary residue field because it uses only a finite extension for torsion, integral differentiation, a finite lattice sum and a finite flat trace. Once the marked-arc comparison applies, a cusp section in the arc frame is $h(t)/(\pi t)^m$. Archimedean cusp boundedness kills its negative $t$-powers and the good Gauss bound makes the coefficients of $h(t)/t^m$ divisible by $\pi^m$. Evaluating at $t=\pi$ and descending through the trait extension gives the assertion at every integral frame. This uses cuspidality of the entire section. If a chosen cover contributes a further nonunit scalar section, its own integral bound must also be supplied before applying this argument. [Project criterion and its precise input](https://github.com/bmatschke/grh-alpoge-claude/blob/25ad91a/RETWISTED_INTEGRAL_EVALUATIONS.md#L31-L39) · [Source extending a good-test cusp bound](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/InertiaEigenvalues/CuspFormBoundGoodPointsExtendsAllPoints.lean#L458-L501)

In particular degrees zero and one cost no discriminant factor. At a tamely ramified prime of ramification index two, $a_p=1/2$, so degree four costs a single $p$ in this rational multiplier. The logarithmic cost has a useful uniform form. Write $T$ for any cyclotomic conductor with $E\subseteq\mathbb Q(\zeta_T)$ and $m=\lfloor d/2\rfloor$. From $\lceil m a_p\rceil\le m a_p+1$ and $\sum_p a_p\log p=\log\mathrm{rd}_E$,
$$
 \log D\le\log c_0+d\log M
       +m\log\mathrm{rd}_E+\log\operatorname{rad}(\Delta_E)
 \le \log c_0+d\log M+(m+1)\log T .
$$
Indeed only primes dividing $T$ ramify. The tower discriminant formula and the cyclotomic discriminant bound give $\mathrm{rd}_E\le \mathrm{rd}_{\mathbb Q(\zeta_T)}\le T$. When $m=0$ both discriminant terms in the exact product vanish. This bounds the denominator of the normalized geometric values and leaves the other finite and archimedean costs in a global period height to be evaluated.

## Relation to the Roadmap's different and spanning estimates

The Roadmap at revision `94e6a98` already gives a quantitative spanning theorem on the minimal-level good cover used in B8.25e. Its short-generator argument fixes a nonterminal admissible point and component, rank $n=k+m-1$, a nonincreasing row weight $u$ of wall gap at least $8fn+10$, and $\log\|u\|_1\le10L_x$. It uses $N_\theta=8fn+8$ theta columns. The definite Hermitian Siegel–Weil identity in the convergent range expresses the doubled kernel by finite-genus theta products. The off-diagonal overlap relative to the diagonal is at most $\sum_{m\ge2}m^{2[E:\mathbb Q]n-N_\theta/2}<1$. The resulting inverse and a thin-slab reproducing integral express the cusp forms in the span of the theta functions, using one integral over a stabilizer domain per rational orbit. Descent to the stated coefficient field uses the rationality of the cusp space and those actual theta generators. The proof also treats its archimedean norms, the flat twists and integral generation. With $Z_0=fnL_x$ it gives at most $2[F':\mathbb Q]\operatorname{rank}$ integral frame generators and Petersson log radius at most $C_g(Z_0^3\|u\|_1+Z_0^5)$. These ingredients can be cited together where a project row is identified with that cover, weight and cusp realization. There are three separate integral estimates here. B8.24a treats the determinant twisting section on a good cover and controls its summed finite valuation width by $fr\log\mathrm{rd}_E$. B8.25e uses the coarser multiplier $Z_{\rm int}^2$, with $Z_{\rm int}=(M')^{fnN_\theta+D}|\Delta_E|^D$ at harmonic degree $D$, for its Minkowski-rescaled generators (one factor suffices at a prime with squarefree local level). B8.43m gives the closer good-frame short-value formula with multiplier $I^r|A|^n(2\Delta_E N)^D$, retaining the index $I$ and auxiliary $N$. Its proof constructs the finite extension rationalizing $N^2$-torsion and identifies the integral germs of the ordered symmetric translations on this good-reduction chart. Its argument can therefore supply the raw integral germs used above when this is the chart of the original rule. Equation (1) instead cancels the index, eliminates the auxiliary integer, and gives the root-discriminant loss in the individual level classes at squarefree level. It is for the unscaled $X_u$; the Roadmap's rescaled generators carry an additional, possibly nonintegral Minkowski scalar.
