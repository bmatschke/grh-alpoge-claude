# Nonzero matched tests on one square of the unitary grid

Fix an admissible row choice, a tuple $t$ and a square $(i,j)$. The two intermediate unitary spaces are $V_{i+1,j+1}$ and $V_{ij}$. Write $I_b$ for the integral of the product of the two edge kernels through the first space, and $I_s(z)$ for the integral through the second after a finite sum of right translations $z=\sum z_k[k]$. Write $d(z)=\sum z_k$. An admissible choice includes the equality $d(z)I_b=c_{ij,t}I_s(z)$ on every convergent pair of matched tests. The nonvanishing assertion requires an actual pair with $I_s(z)\ne0$, and separately $c_{ij,t}\ne0$. We give a local pair of zeta tests with value one, a proof that the allowed local flips reach every class of the matching quotient, and a propagation result for prescribing the finite tests *before* locating the global nonzero endpoint. The global result uses the fixed-triple comparison of theta distributions. The two rank recursions in the full matching theorem concern a later use of the square constants.

## An explicit split-place comparison

Let $k$ be a split local field with valuation ring $\mathcal O$, uniformizer $\pi$ and residue cardinality $q$. Put $d=a+a'$, where $a,a'$ are the two intermediate ranks. Identify the columns with an $a$-block and an $a'$-block, and let $X_0=(1_a,0)$, $Y_0=(0,1_{a'})$. Let $\nu=\alpha/\beta$ be the local character in the fixed splitting. The small zeta function at the identity is
$$
 Z_s(\Psi)(1)=\int_{\mathrm{GL}_{a'}(k)}
       \Psi(gY_0)\,\nu(\det g)|\det g|^{d/2}\,dg.
$$
Choose $c\ge1$ with $\nu$ trivial on $1+\pi^c\mathcal O$. Set $U_c=1+\pi^c M_{a'}(\mathcal O)$. For $Y=(Y_1,Y_2)$ let
$$
 \Psi_c(Y)=
 \frac{\mathbf1_{M_{a'\times a}(\mathcal O)}(Y_1)\,\mathbf1_{U_c}(Y_2)}
      {\operatorname{vol}(U_c)} .
$$
This is Schwartz–Bruhat, and substituting $gY_0=(0,g)$ gives **$Z_s(\Psi_c)(1)=1$**. Both the character and absolute-value factors are one on $U_c$. For Haar measure giving $\mathrm{GL}_{a'}(\mathcal O)$ volume one, its reciprocal volume is $|\mathrm{GL}_{a'}(\mathcal O/\pi^c)|$. Thus this vector is available at every marked split place, including a ramified place. [Definitions of the zeta functions](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Definitions/Divisors/RankEqCardIffExistsFrameMatrixFirstColumnBlock.lean#L701-L723)

More generally let $\Psi$ be *any* small-side Schwartz–Bruhat test. At $X=X_0h^{-T}$, the weighted complementary-frame function is
$$
 f_\Psi(X)=\nu(\det h)|\det h|^{d/2}
        \int_{\mathrm{GL}_{a'}(k)}\Psi(gY_0h)\nu(\det g)|\det g|^{d/2}\,dg .
$$
If another $h'$ gives the same first frame, the two second frames differ by some $C\in\mathrm{GL}_{a'}(k)$ and $\det h'=\det C\det h$. Changing variables $g\mapsto gC$ cancels the determinant factors. This proves independence of the completion of $X$. Left multiplication of $X$ by $G\in\mathrm{GL}_a(k)$ multiplies $f_\Psi(X)$ by $\left(\nu(\det G)|\det G|^{d/2}\right)^{-1}$. [Definition and independence of a completion](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Definitions/Divisors/RankEqCardIffExistsFrameMatrixFirstColumnBlock.lean#L753-L797) · [Determinant covariance](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/ZetaIntegralLeftTranslatePrimitiveFrames.lean#L22-L88)

Choose the lattice used in the big transfer and restrict $f_\Psi$ to the primitive frames in it, extending by zero elsewhere. The rows of a primitive frame are an $\mathcal O$-basis of the intersection of their $k$-row space with that lattice. For a given $X$ of full row rank, choose $g_1$ so that $g_1 X$ is primitive. The $g$ for which $g_0gX$ is primitive form exactly $g_0^{-1}\mathrm{GL}_{a}(\mathcal O)g_1$. On this compact open set the determinant weight in the big zeta integral cancels the left transformation of $f_\Psi$. The resulting integrand is constant, with value $f_\Psi(X)/(\nu(\det g_0)|\det g_0|^{d/2})$. It vanishes everywhere else. The Haar volume of the set is one because $\mathrm{GL}_a(k)$ is unimodular and $\mathrm{GL}_a(\mathcal O)$ has volume one. Summing over Cartan shells gives exactly this value: only finitely many shells meet the compact open set. This proves the primitive-frame integration identity, including its normalization. One must also reach the allowed finite-depth tests and the coordinates of the given square. Write $P$ for the subset of columns specified in the square's polarization. Write $\Lambda_n=\pi^{-n}\mathcal O^P\oplus\mathcal O^{P^c}$ and $D_m=\operatorname{diag}(\pi^m\text{ on }P,\pi^{-m}\text{ on }P^c)$. After the fixed column change $c_0$, there is an $m$ such that the pulled-back small test is supported on $\pi^{-m}\mathcal O$ in every coordinate and invariant under translation by $\pi^m\mathcal O$ in every coordinate. This follows from compact support and local constancy. For every $n\ge2m$, $Y\mapsto\Psi(YD_mc_0^T)$ is supported on $\Lambda_n$ row by row and invariant under $\pi^n\Lambda_n$. Indeed, multiplying by $D_m$ sends a support bound for the two blocks back to $\pi^{-2m}\mathcal O$ and $\mathcal O$. It sends the two translation lattices $\mathcal O$ and $\pi^n\mathcal O$ into $\pi^m\mathcal O$ and $\pi^{n-m}\mathcal O$, respectively. Put $N=c_0D_m$. The two-sided change $Z\mapsto g_0ZN$ in the big test brings in the nonzero factor
$$
   \kappa=\nu(\det g_0)|\det g_0|^{d/2}
                 \nu(\det N)|\det N|^{d/2}.
$$
This follows from the left calculation at $hN^{-T}$ and the identity $(hN^{-T})^{-T}=h^{-T}N$. Reindexing the columns multiplies the primitive-frame function by a fixed nonzero determinant character times an absolute-value power. Changing Haar measure multiplies the small integral by a positive scalar. Explicitly, if $\mu=c_\mu\mu_0$, $u$ is the reindexing scalar and $r$ the nonzero scalar prescribed by the coordinates, use $\Xi(Y)=\kappa\Psi(YN^T)/(r u c_\mu)$. This restores the exact equality of the big and small zeta functions for **every** $h\in\mathrm{GL}_d(k)$. Taking $n=\max(2m,\mathrm{depthAt}(w))$, partial inverse Fourier transformation in the selected block converts this lattice-depth input to an allowed natural test $\Phi_w$. Finite Fourier inversion recovers the input in the actual square flip. The diagonal factors $\pi^m$ and $\pi^{-m}$ are allowed endpoint block translations. Consequently for **every** small local test there is an allowed big test and a block translate with identical zeta function. With $\Psi=\Psi_c$ the common zeta function has value one at the identity. Under the local matching dictionary the two zeta functions agree exactly when the two images in the local quotient agree. The small quotient vector of $\Psi_c$ is nonzero: otherwise comparison with the zero big vector would force $Z_s(\Psi_c)=0$. ## Nonzero integrated kernels at the definite bottom

Consider a realized comparison triple with $p=q=0$. Its intermediate ranks are $a>a'$, and its target rank is the even integer $d=a+a'$. In each of the two Weil representations the intermediate group and the target group are definite at infinity. Write $I_\Phi(h)=\int_{[U(X)]}\theta_\Phi(g,h)\,dg$, using the Haar measure and fundamental domain of the model. There is a polynomial Schwartz test with $I_\Phi\ne0$ on each side. On the primed side one can also impose the prescribed full-rank support at the distinguished good split place. These are statements about the actual global theta integrals as functions of $h$. [Definite statement][ds-bot] · [Integral and full-rank test space][ds-int]

Run the following argument twice, using $(n,\chi)=(a,\nu^{-1})$ on the big side and $(a',\nu)$ on the small side, where $\nu=\alpha/\beta$. Put $s_0=(d-n)/2>0$. The common real quotient is nonzero, so choose a polynomial with nonzero image in it. Its compact group orbit spans a finite-dimensional space. Averaging there over $U(X)_\infty$ preserves its image, since the quotient map is invariant under this group. The average is a nonzero invariant polynomial $f_0$. At each of the finitely many ramified split places $v\in T$, take the inverse image in the split Schrödinger model of the indicator of a sufficiently small matrix ball about $(1_n,0)$. The ball can be chosen so that any translate which meets it fixes it and acts with trivial determinant character. Comparison of the two invertible first blocks forces the translating matrix into a sufficiently deep congruence subgroup. Take the model vacuum at all other finite places. These factors give the actual restricted tensor $\Phi$. [Averaging the real polynomial][ds-real] · [Ramified split test][ds-bump] · [Assembly with the unramified vacua][ds-assemble]

Let $C_\Phi(g)=\langle\omega(g,1)\Phi,\Phi\rangle$, with the oscillator $L^2$ inner product linear in the first entry. In the normalized doubling integral, let $H_T(s)$ denote the integral of $C_\Phi(g)\operatorname{ht}(e(g,1))^{s-s_0}$ over the elements integral outside $T$, where $e$ is the doubling embedding. On this set a nonzero coefficient forces the ramified components to fix the chosen vectors. This follows either from the split support calculation or from the orthogonality of different Heisenberg characters on disjoint translates. The unramified and real components also fix the test. On the support the height is one and $C_\Phi(g)=\|\Phi\|_2^2$. The support there contains a neighborhood of the identity and lies in the compact lattice stabilizer. Thus $H_T(s)$ is a positive constant for these measures. This positivity concerns a matrix coefficient on the restricted domain. The original theta integral can have oscillating summands. [Support, height and positive Haar mass][ds-support]

Use the anisotropic Siegel–Weil identity for the doubled box test $\Phi\otimes\overline\Phi$, meromorphic continuation of its flat Siegel Eisenstein section, and unfolding against the trivial representation. They give a continued zeta integral $Z_\Phi(s)$, regular at $s_0$, with $I_\Phi\equiv0\Rightarrow Z_\Phi(s_0)=0$. Indeed the box kernel restricts to a theta factor times its conjugate. At $s_0$ integration on the two compact quotients gives the squared norm of the integrated lift up to the fixed nonzero normalization. In the far right half-plane the split and inert spherical calculations and normalized Haar product give

$$
 Z_\Phi(s)D^T_n(s)=H_T(s)\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}L(s+n/2-i,\chi).
$$

Here $D^T_n$ is the unramified doubling denominator, holomorphic and nonzero on $\Re s>0$. At places in $T$ the Hecke Euler factors are one by ramification. The identity theorem extends the equality to $s_0$ through the right half-plane with the discrete exceptional set removed. This use of the doubled identity precedes the comparison of the big and small theta distributions. [Norm implication on these box tests][ds-norm] · [Spherical integrals and denominator][ds-sph] · [Euler product and continuation][ds-euler]

Write $e_0=(a-a')/2$. The arguments at $s_0$ run from $1-e_0$ to $d/2$ on the big side and from $1+e_0$ to $d/2$ on the small side. They are integers. For either character its angular exponent $k_w$ at each complex place satisfies $|k_w|=m_\alpha(w)-m_\beta(w)>d$. For an integer $j>1$, the absolutely convergent unitary Euler product is nonzero. At $j=1$, use Hecke nonvanishing on the line one. The infinity type excludes a norm character. For $j\le0$ in the required range, both $j+|k_w|/2$ and $1-j+|k_w|/2$ are positive. The functional equation therefore relates the finite $L(j,\chi)$ to the nonzero dual value at $1-j\ge1$ by finite nonzero gamma, conductor and root-number factors. Consequently the product at $s_0$ is nonzero. The doubling identity proves $Z_\Phi(s_0)\ne0$ and hence $I_\Phi\ne0$. [Hecke continuation at integral arguments][ds-hecke] · [Angular weights for both characters][ds-exp] · [Application to the two actual models][ds-both]

Finally at the distinguished good split place $v_1$ replace the primed finite factor by a test supported on the full-row-rank locus in the specified primed coordinates and having the same local quotient class. The local full-rank representative theorem supplies the replacement. The difference belongs to the span of intermediate-group translates minus their originals. Compactness of the intermediate quotient gives absolute convergence and right invariance of the integral, so every such difference integrates to zero for every $h$. The replacement leaves the nonzero function $I'_\Phi$ intact and puts its test in the source space $\mathrm{So}$. This proves the nonvanishing clause of $\mathrm{OneConst}$ at $p=q=0$. The proportionality for matched pairs, the nonzero scalar and the propagation to specified finite flip data are additional steps. ## Why nonvanishing on the two compact groups still requires a comparison

On a matched polynomial pair $y=(\Phi,\Phi')$ let $A(y)=I_\Phi$ and $B(y)=I'_{\Phi'}$, viewed as functions on the same compact automorphic quotient. Take the Petersson form linear in the first variable. Assume the two maps factor through the prescribed irreducible matched module, both projections of the match supply the needed representatives, and the reverse theta lift on each such image is constant on the primed intermediate quotient. The last assertion uses local invariance at good split places, strong approximation on the determinant-one subgroup and the remaining determinant character. These are specific inputs to the definite comparison. [Irreducibility, kernel descent and invariant forms][ds-line] · [Local invariance for the reverse lift][ds-rinv] · [Passage from good split places to the whole group][ds-allgood]

Every Petersson pairing of two equivariant image maps gives an invariant sesquilinear form on that module. The form from $B$ is nonzero by the definite calculation. Uniqueness of the invariant form gives a scalar $c$ such that
$$
 \langle A(y)-c B(y), B(y')\rangle=0
 \qquad\text{for all matched }y,y'.
$$
Set $E(y)=A(y)-c B(y)$. The same argument gives a single $\lambda\ge0$ with $\langle E(y),E(y')\rangle=\lambda\langle B(y),B(y')\rangle$. The cross-pairing identity by itself allows $\lambda>0$ if the two lifts land in different copies of the same representation. The following use of doubling resolves exactly this possibility.

Choose $y_0$ with $B(y_0)\ne0$ and use its primed test in the reverse theta lift $T$. For either input $v=E(y_0)$ or $B(y_0)$, integrate $T(v)$ over the primed intermediate quotient. Fubini on compact quotients gives $\langle v,B(y_0)\rangle$. Since $T(v)$ is constant, its value is this inner product divided by the primed quotient volume. Thus $T(E(y_0))=0$ and $T(B(y_0))\ne0$. In the right half-plane the doubling pairing is an integral of matrix coefficients of $v$ against one fixed kernel. Equivariance and the Gram identity imply that the pairing for $E(y_0)$ is $\lambda$ times that for $B(y_0)$. Continue this equality to the reverse doubling point $(a'-d)/2$. The anisotropic Siegel–Weil identity evaluates the pairing there as $2/\operatorname{vol}[U(V')]$ times $\|T(v)\|_2^2$. Both use the same nonzero Haar factor. Consequently $\lambda=0$. For every $y$ this gives $\|E(y)\|_2=0$. Continuity and full support of Haar measure imply $A(y)=c B(y)$ pointwise. The nonzero big test has a small match, so $c\ne0$. The nonzero small test and its big match prove uniqueness. [Evaluation at the reverse doubling point][ds-rnorm] · [Fubini, residual norm and pointwise equality][ds-polzero]

The [local quotient calculation](ROW_DEFINITE_LOCAL_QUOTIENTS.md) identifies the two representations at each place. The [definite descent](../theta/ROW_DEFINITE_THETA_COMPARISON.md) proves factorization through them, and the [reverse-lift argument](../theta/ROW_DEFINITE_REVERSE_LIFT.md) proves constancy on the full group. Thus on the stated theta and anisotropic doubling theorems the proportionality holds for the actual model. To pass to general matched Schwartz tests, use the defining polynomial approximants in each fixed finite component. On compact quotients the theta sum and its integral are continuous in the archimedean Schwartz topology at each endpoint: a sufficiently high Schwartz seminorm gives a summable majorant on compact sets of representatives after the denominators are cleared. The finite number of components permits termwise passage to the limit. The comparison persists with the same $c$. The local flip and global endpoint arguments concern the prescribed grid tests. The [last-column transfer](ROW_DEFINITE_GRID_TRANSFER.md) gives the precise row scalar. The [first beta step](ROW_FIRST_BETA_STEP.md) establishes the comparison on fixed compatible models in the preceding column. [Matched Schwartz limits in the source][ds-limits]

## Prescribing the finite vectors before finding the global endpoint

Let $H=U(V_{i,j+1})\times U(V_{i+1,j})$ inside the unitary group $U(W)$ of their joint block model. Suppose that on this fixed triple the big theta distribution is continuous on the relevant Schwartz space, factors through the local matched quotients on polynomial tensors, and is nonzero on one such tensor as a function on $U(W)(\mathbb A)$. Suppose also that the local quotients are irreducible with the covariance under target translation in the local matching definition. At the places outside the prescribed support choose the spherical vacuum, which has nonzero quotient image. At every marked finite place except the distinguished split place $v_0$, one may **preassign any allowed flip representative with nonzero quotient image**, including those from the preceding normalized tests. A nonzero big period at a block endpoint still exists for these choices.

To prove this, hold every component but one fixed. The local tests whose global period vanishes *as a function on the whole target* form a linear space. The factorization through the local map identifies it with the inverse image of a subspace of the local quotient. Target equivariance makes this subspace invariant, since a right translate of the zero function vanishes everywhere. It is proper if the current global function is nonzero.
