1. DIRECT ANSWER

Yes, there is a concrete opportunity to work more efficiently while
retaining the old detailed proof-writing approach.

In the branches inspected, the main obstacle is not that nobody knows
which global object could possibly satisfy all the conditions. Lean
already supplies an order of construction, shared choices, and the
theorems that justify each step. The human task is to recover that
mathematical construction, distinguish its substantive ingredients from
formal packaging, and explain those ingredients at the exact scope
used later.

Some constructions really must be simultaneous. However, there are two
different sources of difficulty:

  (a) proving a substantial theorem about the chosen objects;
  (b) making sure several uses refer to the same objects, or to objects
      linked by a sufficiently precise comparison.

A long record can contain both. Calling the entire record a single
"missing global object" conceals which kind of work remains. Conversely,
splitting it into many independent existential assertions can create an
extra compatibility problem that the source avoids.

The useful improvement is to identify one actual later use, recover
its actual upstream construction, and then assign the first substantial
missing mathematical ingredient on that route. The proof of that
ingredient should remain fully developed and human-readable.

We should also distinguish different senses of "missing":

  - Not yet located: the formal theorem proving the required result
    has not been identified precisely.
  - Located but not reconstructed: the exact producer is known, but its
    mathematical proof has not been written in human form.
  - Proved conditionally: the human argument is complete given one or
    more explicitly retained substantive inputs.
  - Proved on the wrong or unrelated data: an identification or transport
    is still needed before the actual consumer can use it.
  - Stronger than needed here: an unused part of an interface is being
    pursued even though a checked specialization would suffice.

Only the last two sometimes disappear by changing the work assignment.
The second and third usually require real mathematics. A better map
makes that work more focused; it does not make it vanish.

2. WHAT "THE SAME GLOBAL OBJECT" REALLY REQUIRES

A typical target has the shape

  for every initial datum D, there exists X such that
  P(D,X), Q(D,X), and R(D,X) all hold.

Separate proofs of "there exists X with P" and "there exists Y with Q"
do not supply that target. The efficient approach is to identify the
common construction first, and prove its properties in parallel.

The correct granularity of the common data is essential. Not every
auxiliary choice has to agree. Often one field, frame or coefficient
map is shared, while auxiliary primes, ideals or bases may differ.
Requiring unnecessary equalities makes the task harder. Omitting
required equalities leaves a gap.

There are three familiar ways to assemble compatible data.

  1. Construct all properties on one fixed object. The finite coefficient
     ring in Section 3 is an example.
  2. Pass to a common refinement, such as a coefficient extension that
     contains the data required by both representations. Prove the
     necessary properties survive that refinement.
  3. Transport along a specified comparison, proving that it preserves
     all structures used later: maps, pairings, labels, measures,
     characters and normalizations as applicable.

An abstract isomorphism of underlying spaces need not preserve all
those structures. On the other hand, once the required transport lemma
has been proved, every later use should reuse it.

Uniformity is another compatibility condition. A bound of the form

  for each family Fm, there exist C and x0 such that
  for every x >= x0 and every member i in I_Fm(x), E(Fm,x,i) <= C B(x)

does not follow from a separately chosen C for every x and i. The
dependency of a constant should be stated when it is chosen. Some
constants can depend on the family or its outer numerical data; others
must work before that family is chosen. The source and the actual
consumer determine which order is necessary.

A short working record of these choices should answer four questions:
what is chosen, what may it depend on, which later arguments reuse it,
and what comparison is available if its presentation changes. This is
an aid to constructing the proof. The exposition should explain the
choices in normal mathematical prose, rather than making the reader
navigate a large bookkeeping table.

3. GEOMETRY: THE COMMON COEFFICIENT RING IS ALREADY SPECIFIED

The section-complex route is a particularly clear example.

The required statement chooses one complex and one map to the actual
section Cech complex, and proves that the map remains a
quasi-isomorphism after every allowed coefficient-ring change. The
original ring can be arbitrary; target rings can be nonflat,
nonreduced and non-Noetherian. The model may depend on the original
geometric data. One model is not required for all schemes at once. [G1]

The formal construction proceeds as follows.

First fix the scheme, the very ample presentation, its finitely many
sections s_i, and the line bundle M. Form the graded module of the
actual twisted sections. The operators given by multiplication by
s_i are therefore fixed. The affine charts and their section modules
come from these same data.

Next choose a finite homogeneous presentation matrix Phi which gives
the correct module after the relevant chart localizations. The source
does not demand finite generation of the entire graded section module
as a preliminary step. It obtains a presentation sufficient on the
charts. [G2]

Then choose finite algebraic certificates for all chart properties:
defining equations, smoothness witnesses, splitting and annihilator
matrices, and the presentation identities. Let T be the finite union
of every coefficient occurring in these certificates and in Phi.
Set

  R0 = the subring of R generated by T.

This is the explicit choice in the source. Since it is finitely
generated over the prime subring, R0 is Noetherian. All the polynomial
data lift to R0. Injectivity of R0 -> R allows their polynomial
identities to be verified over R0. [G3]

The important point is that one includes the CERTIFICATES, not just
the entries of Phi. Flatness over R does not automatically descend
to an arbitrary coefficient subring. The descended smoothness and
projectivity certificates provide the actual flatness argument.

Finally the localization cube and its comparison maps are induced
from that same presentation. After extending scalars from R0 back
to R, division by powers of the fixed s_i and restriction identify
the resulting cube with the actual geometric sections. Compatibility
is established by these formulas. [G4]

Thus there is a definite answer to "which common coefficient ring
should we choose?" The substantial remaining human work is to prove:

  - the localization and extension statements for twisted sections;
  - finite homogeneous presentation up to chart-localization torsion;
  - descent of the finite smoothness/projectivity certificates;
  - flatness on the nonempty cube vertices;
  - finite graded Cech cohomology and the stated comparison maps.

The already reconstructed GSL algebra then consumes this model.
Separate geometric H1-vanishing, rank, polarization and translation
inputs remain separate obligations.

A useful next chapter is therefore "One finite coefficient ring
preserving every flat chart." Its output is the original homogeneous
matrix flat-chart descent theorem. Its proof should display the
certificates, the common ring, the identities, and the retract proving
flatness. This is a well-defined mathematical assignment, not a search
over arbitrary models.

Some inspected later callers use only n=1, or the identity translate
with q=1. That suggests possible specialization, but it does not show
that every consumer on the terminal proof route can use a weaker
law. No wholesale n=1 replacement has been established. Moreover,
specializing a line bundle need not simplify the finite-presentation
argument. Any narrower result must earn its place by a proved
application to all selected consumers. [G5]

4. WACH COMPARISON: CHOOSE COMPLETE DATA AT THE RIGHT LEVEL

The remaining HW/Fon/Cmp comparison also has an explicit constructor.

For each permitted layer, the source builds, in order:

  Gamma data;
  the series ring;
  the embeddings and their compatibility;
  the etale module;
  the Wach lattice;
  the Herr class maps;
  the period comparison.

Later objects are defined relative to the earlier choices. For
example, the Wach lattice belongs to the chosen etale module, and the
comparison belongs to that chosen lattice. The source packages them
as one complete Layers record. [W1]

The assembly theorem asks for a nonempty complete record for each
allowed (K,s,L). It then chooses one complete record at each such
index and constructs HW, Fon and Cmp from its corresponding fields.
Together with the stated uniformizer inputs, this is exactly the
requested output. The assembly statement does not add an unspecified
compatibility law between different indices. [W2]

This yields a precise distinction:

  - Choosing a COMPLETE Layers record independently at each allowed
    outer index is sufficient for this assembly theorem.
  - Choosing unrelated modules, lattices and comparisons inside one
    record is not sufficient.
  - The internal functoriality over lattices and their maps remains
    part of the record and must be proved.

Thus "coherent global Wach object" should not become an invitation
to invent extra transition conditions. The proof should explain the
actual internal compatibility and then use ordinary choice at the
outer level where the source permits it.

The difficult ingredients are faithful cyclotomic-kernel invariants,
the etale basis construction, the actual Wach lattice from the
integral comparison input, Herr class maps, and the quantitative
de Rham filtration comparison. The word "choose" does not supply
these arguments. The source already tells us which ones to reconstruct
and how they fit together. The v0.52 Wach handoff records their exact
entry points; its completed Selmer-signature argument remains reusable.

5. ARITHMETIC: FOLLOW THE ENDPOINT THE FINAL PROOF ACTUALLY USES

There is a concrete route clarification here.

Tracing declaration bodies backward from src/Main.lean reaches the
window-bound theorem in WindowBoundOfLiteratureLevel.lean. That proof
calls ends0_alt. It sends its GdEndAlt output through the Alt
coefficient theorem to CountI, and its ThmS6Core_s output to CountV.
This is an actual call chain, not an inference from imported files. [A1]

The distinction from the original GdEnd is transparent after unfolding
the definitions:

  - The original rank-three conclusion says that the specified
    representation is attached to an appropriate automorphic form.
  - The Alt conclusion includes that assertion and the additional
    conditional WitA property.
  - The original rank-two conclusion gives an attached newform.
  - The Alt conclusion also supplies WitAQ.
  - The common g1, Marks and FinEnd data are preserved. [A2]

Consequently GdEndAlt implies the original GdEnd by projecting
conjunctions on the SAME g1. That forgetful implication is not a deep
missing mathematical bridge. Conversely, the original weaker
statement alone does not supply WitA or WitAQ. The actual terminal
route keeps those stronger properties.

The remaining work should therefore retain the Alt payload while
reusing the original-route algebra already proved. Merely relabeling
an endpoint would be invalid; the correct relation is now explicit.
This diagnosis does not prove the Alt supplier in human form.

The construction of the common arithmetic data is also specified.

  1. Fix the given field L, frame Phi, marking g1 and link data.
  2. Choose one coefficient extension c1 and residue map f that
     accommodate both ranks.
  3. Obtain the rank-two and rank-three seeds on that same field.
  4. Construct their respective modularity realizations and ideals.
  5. For each rank, construct another realization at a different
     auxiliary place.
  6. Combine the two conclusions to remove that rank's omitted place.
  7. Return the finite-point result and both automorphic conclusions
     to the same original frame. [A3]

The two ranks need not have equal seed weights or equal modularity
ideals. There is no requirement that their auxiliary primes be one
common prime. Each rank needs two different omitted places for the
specified gluing argument. Requiring stronger equalities would create
unnecessary work.

The genuine missing mathematics includes actual automorphic seeds,
modularity/patching, component propagation, arithmetic-point
classicality, and descent preserving the additional properties.

A bounded next chapter can target the rank-two/rank-three component
propagation at the supplied realizations, through AbsMain and FinPtOf.
Its finite-point conclusion must retain the original coefficient-ring
and point quantifiers. AFE's completed FinPt-to-FinEnd argument then
applies. A more specialized modularity supplier may be sufficient on
these constructed realizations, but the application has to be written
before any general scope is discarded. Both Dat and Host entries,
including the permitted Host ell=3 case, remain in view. [A4]

6. ANALYSIS: SEPARATE DEPENDENCIES THAT DO NOT HAVE TO WAIT

The analytic trace identifies three practical distinctions.

First, constructing the core global setting does not use every field
of the full Forms0 family. The actual lattice-existence step uses only
the distinguished word, and its isotropic-vector proof uses two pieces
of that data: a Gram factorization and a norm identity for the chosen
uniformizer. The full family is used later for transport. [S1]

The mathematical construction is simple to explain once those inputs
are isolated. A Gram factorization supplies a vector x of norm one.
If pi has norm T, then its image v has norm T. The construction already
has an orthogonal vector z of norm -T. Therefore

  w = v + z

is isotropic, since its norm is T-T. It is nonzero, since its pairing
with z is -T, with T nonzero. The required orthogonality to the existing
hyperbolic pairs follows from the stated orthogonality of v and z.

This does not remove the full Forms0 requirement for later transport.
It shows why the elementary isotropic step and the full family
construction should not be assigned as one indivisible task.

Second, the source has a true-column constructor that supplies one
common family and explicit later-stage transports. Actual consumers
in LevelThreeToFour pass this family's forms into the spectral-package
construction. One should follow that route when the incoming data is
the actual ColT, instead of independently reconstructing every
alternative general column constructor. The remaining SRest/SAt
construction, common selection and spectral estimates are not
discharged by this observation. [S2]

Third, counting is a parallel branch. The count-constant theorem goes
from the Siegel-Weil premise to the volume step and counting theorem,
with constants chosen before the relevant family members. It has no
Forms0 or chosen-global-setting premise. Counting work therefore does
not have to wait for the full Forms0 construction. [S3]

For the measure branch, the next focused target is full RescaleStep3
on the actual supplied setting. Its source chooses one standard
global additive character before all words and auxiliary tuples,
then constructs the compatible measure data while retaining the
prescribed archimedean measures and bridge. Its human proof should
explain why all quotient and product normalizations coexist. The
precise Siegel-Weil premise remains explicit until justified at
its required scope. [S4]

Some analytic questions remain genuinely substantial: local densities,
the convergent Siegel-Weil formula, uniqueness, the exact archimedean
orbit-to-fiber measure scalar, counting estimates and the normalized
kernel bounds. Invariant-measure uniqueness alone does not determine
a required scalar. Bessel nonvanishing alone does not imply uniqueness.
Those distinctions are mathematical obligations, not naming issues.

7. A SMALL CHANGE TO HOW EACH PROOF ASSIGNMENT STARTS

Keep the existing detailed proof packets. Precede each new assignment
by a concise source check with the following output.

A. Write the next theorem in ordinary mathematics.

State the original data, assumptions, conclusion and quantifier order.
Make explicit which quantities are fixed, and which may vary later.
Avoid making a large Lean record name stand in for this statement.

B. Find one actual later use.

Record the theorem that will use the result and the actual arguments
at that call. Check definitions, instances, fields and normalizations.
A plausible theorem name or an import relation is not enough.

C. Find the actual producer and its construction.

Read its proof body. Identify the common choices in their real order.
Mark the first unproved human mathematical ingredients. Reuse already
proved results at matching scope. A short wrapper that invokes a large
theorem is not a proof of that theorem.

When a definition selects an object by Classical.choose, follow the
existence theorem supplied to that choice. Its proof is where the
construction or sufficient existence argument must be recovered.

D. Distinguish substantive ingredients from repackaging.

Substantive mathematics includes existence, finiteness, convergence,
uniform estimates, nonvanishing, functoriality and normalized comparison.
Projection of a conjunction or assembling already proved fields may
be immediate. Maps still need enough explanation for a reader to
understand what is being identified.

E. Test any proposed simplification at the later use.

Write the implication "specialized result -> this exact required input."
If the implication needs extra hypotheses, those become explicit
obligations. Preserve arbitrary-ring targets, family uniformity and
common-object conditions whenever the consumer uses them.

A general lemma may be clearer and easier to prove than several narrow
versions. The objective is manageable total mathematical work and a
readable argument, not the weakest syntactically possible statement.

F. Write the full human proof and its application.

Explain why the construction is natural, construct it, establish its
properties, and show how the next theorem uses it. Retain useful
intermediate lemmas. End with a precise account of remaining
substantive inputs. This is the existing approach, with a better
starting assignment.

A useful guard against unproductive expansion is to require one of
two outcomes from a substantial packet: a new original mathematical
supplier that can actually be used, or a complete bounded subtheorem
with its next use already identified. Another omnibus implication
that merely assumes the same difficult input under a new name is
not the intended endpoint.

8. WHAT TO AUTOMATE, AND WHAT TO LEAVE TO MATHEMATICS

Small navigation tools can help retrieve declarations, list their
uses, collect source excerpts and retain the correspondence to
completed human lemmas. This should be limited initially to one
active route, rather than becoming a new project to index the entire
repository before further proof writing.

Where a compatible compiled Lean environment is already available,
its elaborated declarations are preferable for dependency inspection:
elaboration resolves implicit arguments and type-class instances,
and its information trees can associate elaborated expressions and
proof states with source positions. The Lean language reference
describes these mechanisms. [T1]

The inspected checkout has no compiled environment established for
this analysis. No full semantic extractor has been built or run here.
Source-level call checks therefore remain explicitly labeled as
such. A text-search index is useful navigation, but cannot certify
all hidden premises or instance choices. Replaying the entire
accepted proof is not a prerequisite to the present reconstruction.

Even an exact formal dependency list would not automatically identify
the shortest or clearest human proof. It can expose used theorems and
arguments. Mathematical judgment must decide which small steps to
group, which conventional theorem to invoke with checked hypotheses,
which specialization really suffices, and where explanation is needed.

The best automation target is repeated navigation and bookkeeping.
The difficult proofs and the readable explanations remain the main
work. Retain reviewed source-to-lemma correspondences; revisit them
when a dependency or formulation changes. Keep routine release
administration proportionate to the mathematical change.

9. PARALLEL WORK THAT FITS THE EXISTING APPROACH

The current four-way arrangement remains appropriate, with one owner
for each shared construction.

Geometry worker:
  Prove one coefficient ring preserving all flat-chart certificates.
  Explain the retract and the comparison to the fixed actual section
  Cech complex.
  Retain arbitrary base rings and all original charts.

Arithmetic worker:
  Reconstruct component propagation and the finite-point output on
  the common rank-two/rank-three frame. Preserve the Alt properties
  in the subsequent seed, classicality and descent work. Apply the
  completed frame and finite-end transport results directly.

Analysis worker:
  Prove the exact measure-rescaling supplier on the fixed setting.
  Track one global character and all normalization identities.
  The count/volume route is independently available for a separate
  bounded packet; it need not wait for Forms0 transport.

Coordinator / Wach work:
  Reconstruct a substantive missing Layers supplier at its exact
  internal scope, then assemble the complete HW/Fon/Cmp triple.
  Coordinate shared-data joins and independently check the actual
  applications of the three workers' outputs.

This is a schedule of mathematical roles, not four unrelated searches
for existence witnesses. When two tasks need the same underlying
object, the responsible owner supplies its definition and comparison
maps before the tasks use it. Conditional sublemmas can still be
proved in parallel with that construction.

The useful review question is: "Can the next theorem now use this
result on its actual data?" Separate review of the shared choices
and normalization is particularly valuable. Source range checks and
claim inventory remain supporting evidence.

10. READABILITY AND PROGRESS

The established proof-note style is the standard for the continuation.
Each section should tell the reader what is being proved, why it is
needed, how the construction works, and why its properties follow.
Definitions should precede their use. Long but explanatory intermediate
arguments should remain. Conventional mathematical notation should
carry the main exposition; source names belong primarily in the
cross-reference material.

Readability is not measured by a lower word count, fewer lemmas or
a shorter PDF. A concise skeleton can be harder to understand than
a detailed proof. Simplification is helpful when it removes
unnecessary mathematical work or makes the reason for an argument
clearer, while retaining the required conclusion.

The current count of open whole obligations describes the selected
decomposition. It does not measure remaining difficulty or prove
that the selected route is minimal. Better progress questions are:

  - Which exact later theorem has gained a human-proved input?
  - Which substantive retained premise has disappeared?
  - Which shared-object or normalization join has been completed?
  - Which hard hypotheses remain on this route?
  - Did a proposed specialization actually apply?
  - Did the new explanation make the construction understandable
    without requiring the reader to decode its Lean record?

The diagnosis here establishes several concrete navigation gains:
the common coefficient ring is explicit; outer Wach choices do not
require invented compatibility; the terminal arithmetic route retains
the Alt payload; and counting can be pursued independently of Forms0.
It does not establish a numerical speedup or complete any of the
remaining whole proof obligations. The next progress should be the
full mathematical proofs selected using these observations.
