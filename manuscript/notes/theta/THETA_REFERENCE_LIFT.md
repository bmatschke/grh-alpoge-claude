# The definite theta integral and its field of definition

Fix a row of the theta lift from a definite unitary group $U(V)$ to $U(W)$. At the reference character there are no auxiliary twisting primes. The coefficient fields $L\hookrightarrow L'$ are embedded in $\mathbb C$. Write $\iota$ for their inclusion and $\sigma,\sigma'$ for the distinguished embeddings, with $\sigma'\iota=\sigma$. There are two maps to compare. The first evaluates a theta integral. The second applies the finite projector selecting the good types and takes the resulting vector in the chosen Hilbert model. The assertion that these operators survive coefficient enlargement includes the *values* of the operators on the original forms. [Specification of both maps](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/IwasawaDecomposition/ThetaLiftAtReferenceTupleOverEnlargedBase.lean#L28-L42)

**Reference-tuple comparison.** Suppose the original datum carries its specified analytic models and the existence and characterization of its theta maps. Assume the enlargement and the common frame and marked places of the [coefficient-enlargement theorem](THETA_COEFFICIENT_EXTENSION.md), the prescribed local types in both stages, and the enlarged datum with the transported reference objects and level characters. Then the enlarged Hilbert model has the prescribed definite automorphic inner product, the two theta maps are the integral and its prescribed projection, and the coefficients of the two good-type projectors agree under the distinguished embeddings. If the original projected map has $L$-coordinates on every $L$-rational reference input, its enlarged version has $L'$-coordinates on every $L'$-rational reference input. The original old-stage geometric hypotheses that supply this last assumption are given below. [Inputs and use on the same enlarged datum](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/IwasawaDecomposition/ExistsCharacterisedDataWeilEnlargedBase.lean#L139-L158)

## Definite forms and their norm

Gross's theory of algebraic modular forms applies the finite double-coset description to groups with finite arithmetic subgroups. Here $U(V)$ is definite at every real place. In the reference row put $G_f=U(V)(\mathbb A_{F,f})$, $\Gamma=U(V)(F)$ and let $J\subseteq G_f$ be the prescribed level group. The finite-order marked character $\chi$ incorporates the level action. In the source's convention the coefficient-valued functions satisfy $f(\gamma gk)=\chi(k)\rho(\gamma) f(g)$. Both stages have the same $J$ and $\rho$. The level characters agree since at the reference tuple their only relevant finite factors are the matching marked characters. Hence the functions satisfying this law form the same complex space. [Gross, *Algebraic modular forms*](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF02780173) · [Level and character comparison](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/IwasawaDecomposition/ReferenceTupleHilbertModelDefiniteFormsEnlarged.lean#L56-L87)

Choose representatives $y$ for $\Gamma\backslash G_f/J$, and put $\Gamma_y=\Gamma\cap yJy^{-1}$ using the embedding in $G_f$. The exact inner product is

$$
   (f_1,f_2)=\sum_{[y]}\frac{\langle f_1(y),f_2(y)\rangle}{|\Gamma_y|}.
$$

Replacing $y$ by $\gamma y k$ conjugates its stabilizer and changes both function values by the same unitary operator $\chi(k)\rho(\gamma)$. The character factors have modulus one because they come from finite residue groups. Thus the two stages may even use different representatives. The original Hilbert embedding has the same range and realizes precisely the required new norm. The old measure and fundamental domain still have their stated normalization since the group, rational subgroup and level are the same. This proves `ModVAt`. ## The target inner product and its finite character

For the target group $U(W)$ there is a comparison at **every** complex embedding $s':L'\hookrightarrow\mathbb C$. At $s'$ use the row model at $s=s'\iota$. Its archimedean CM labels agree since the two embeddings agree on the identified copy of the CM field. The compact-type weights $\kappa_{i,\tau}$ at the real places are consequently the same. This accounts for the factor $\prod_\tau\dim K_{\kappa_{i,\tau}}$ in the source normalization of the inner product of multiplicity maps, where $K_{\kappa}$ denotes the representation of the maximal compact group with the indicated highest weight. The remaining factor is the pairing on the original framed representation integrated with the row measure over its chosen fundamental domain. These are the very representation, measure and domain retained by the comparison. The archimedean realization, finite level and character defining the multiplicity subspace also agree. Thus the copied Hilbert embedding has both the required range and the full normalized inner product. [Normalization of the target metric](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/IndefiniteReal/LinksDefs.lean#L311-L323) · [Weight and multiplicity-space comparison](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/IwasawaDecomposition/FormGroupsModelsActionsMeasuresBasesCharacterise.lean#L152-L183)

At each such embedding the finite group acts on the copied model by the old action. Evaluation of an acted-on vector agrees with the corresponding level translation of its automorphic function, since the level, frame and map of the finite quotient are identified. The copied measure and domain satisfy the same Haar and fundamental-domain conditions for that level. On the definite space the old frame matrix is still orthonormal for the unchanged Gram matrix at each real place. These are exactly the remaining assertions for the target model, collected as `ModW`. There is a separate identity for the one-dimensional character $\lambda_i$ of the finite group attached to the arithmetic forms in row $i$. Its values in $L$ are read in $\mathbb C$ using the distinguished embedding. In the source's row numbering it is the identity
$$
  \sigma\bigl(\lambda_i(g)\bigr)
     =\prod_{P\in P_g}\chi_{i+1,P}(\det g_P).
$$
Here $g_P$ is the residue matrix of $g$ at the marked prime $P$. On the enlarged arithmetic form the stored character is $\iota\lambda_i$, so the left side has its old value. On the right, the primes and reduction maps are identified and the reference character comparison gives equality of each marked character. This proves the product formula for the enlarged arithmetic character at every row and group element. ## The projector and the theta integral

Write $P_{\mathrm{good}}$ for the action of the type idempotent on automorphic functions on $U(W)$. Its group-algebra coefficients are indexed by an element of the product of the marked residue groups. At the two type primes, match the factors by their underlying place of $F$. The prescribed choice above a place then gives the same place of the CM field. Under this bijection the local character labels agree, since they are the prescribed powers of the same generator. The tuples of local labels accepted by the good-type condition correspond bijectively. Indeed, the two local goodness tests use the same polynomials and the same indexed characters after relabeling. The coefficient of the idempotent at a group element is a sum over the accepted tuples of products of local series-idempotent coefficients. These local coefficients are fixed by the prescribed types. Relabeling the finite sum and its factors therefore gives the same coefficient at corresponding group elements. The placewise identifications of the residue groups carry the actual old and new marked matrices to these corresponding elements. This is the equality `IdemSame` for the idempotents in the two coefficient fields after applying $\sigma$ and $\sigma'$. Let $f$ now denote the coefficient-valued adelic function attached to an input Hilbert vector, and let $u$ be a tensor of lowest-type vectors. The prescribed polynomial contraction of $u$ with $f(h)$ supplies the archimedean test. Combining it with the fixed finite test gives a function $\Phi_{u,f(h)}$ in the source's Weil model. At $g\in U(W)(\mathbb A_F)$ the required integral is

$$
   \int_{\mathcal D_V}
      \big(\omega(h,g)\Phi_{u,f(h)}\big)(0)\,d\nu_V(h).
$$

Evaluation at zero is the kernel convention for this realization. The normalized polynomial map and test function, the old and new Weil actions, the integration domain $\mathcal D_V$ and measure $\nu_V$ agree under the preceding comparisons. The two integrands coincide for every $h$. The old defining theorem gives integrability and the displayed value, so it gives both on the enlarged side. The copied multilinear map takes values in the required automorphic multiplicity subspace since its frame, level and character are the same. This verifies the first theta map on every row in the sense specified by its defining condition. The action of $P_{\mathrm{good}}$ is the finite sum of its coefficients times the specified level translations. We have identified each coefficient, its translation, and the level character. The embedded value of the original projected map is $P_{\mathrm{good}}$ applied to the first map, where the projector acts on its adelic function values. Thus the copied map into the target Hilbert space obeys the identical projection equation. This proves `LiftAt` for both maps without introducing any freedom to multiply the integral or the projection by a scalar. ## Why the projected lift is rational on every enlarged reference form

The definite source space at the larger field is spanned by the scalar extensions of definite source forms at the smaller field. Greenberg and Voight, Lemma 2.4, identify algebraic modular forms at trivial level character with the direct sum of the fixed coefficient spaces for the finite stabilizers. Apply their lemma at the kernel of the finite level character. The original level-character subspace is cut out by the character idempotent in the finite quotient. Both finite-group invariants and this idempotent commute with a field extension in characteristic zero. The following coordinate proof checks the coefficient field and the particular polynomial reading in the present application. [Greenberg–Voight, Proposition 2.3 and Lemma 2.4](https://jvoight.github.io/articles/orthohecke-240123.pdf#page=5)

Use the equivariant functions on the original decorated points. The level on both sides has the same marked places and no extra twist primes. The coefficient action on the polynomial value space is obtained by applying $\iota$ to the old matrices. For the weight action this follows by applying $\iota$ to its polynomial coefficients. For the finite level action it follows from agreement of the marked characters after the two complex embeddings, and hence under $\iota$ by injectivity of $\sigma'$. Therefore if $(b_j)$ is an $L$-basis of $L'$, expanding an $L'$-valued form in this basis produces a finite collection of old equivariant forms. Each coefficient function satisfies the original equivariance equations because the matrices in those equations have entries in $L$. Conversely the expansion recovers the original enlarged form. Restriction of an equivariant function to representatives is onto the finite presentation used by the construction. The previously proved identity of polynomial readings on corresponding decorated points then yields, for every enlarged rational input $x'$,

$$
    j'_V(x')=\sum_j \sigma'(b_j) j_V(x_j)
$$

in the common definite Hilbert space, with old rational inputs $x_j$. Indeed both sides have the same function values, and the Hilbert embedding into functions is injective. The same identity holds for any enlarged Hilbert vector representing $x'$. This is the content of `JSpan` for the reference tuple. Let $T$ be the common projected theta map and $j_{W,\sigma}$ the old semilinear coordinate realization in the target. By the rationality assumption there is $y_j\in L^{n_i}$ with $Tj_V(x_j)=j_{W,\sigma}(y_j)$. Define

$$
      y'=\sum_j b_j\,\iota(y_j)\ \in (L')^{n_i}.
$$

Here $\iota$ acts entry by entry. Compatibility of the new target evaluations with the old ones gives $j'_{W,\sigma'}(\iota(y_j))=j_{W,\sigma}(y_j)$. Semilinearity at $\sigma'$ and complex linearity of $T$ now give $j'_{W,\sigma'}(y')=Tj'_V(x')$. This proves `RatAt` with the given embedding, target realization and projected map. In the application, old rationality comes from the geometric realization with its phase and injective evaluation at the extremal vector, the uniformity of that realization, its exact theta data, and the rank inequality $8 f r+4\le n_V$. With these hypotheses the old base row has a one-map reading. Its rational evaluation furnishes a target coordinate vector for each old rational input, and injectivity identifies that vector's Hilbert realization with the projected lift. This is the old assertion used in the calculation with $y'$. It is the geometric input to the field-of-definition argument. The result obtained here concerns each reference tuple of the stated enlargement and coordinates in its **large coefficient field** at the distinguished embedding.
