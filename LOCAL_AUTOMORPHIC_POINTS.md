# Which automorphic forms give a local Hecke point?

A local Hecke point records the characteristic-zero eigenvalues of the good Hecke operators. The same list can occur in several automorphic representations, and the chosen level can contribute several vectors from one representation. The multiplicity needed in the proof counts all these vectors. It can be recovered by decomposing ordinary algebraic modular forms at the given weight and level.

**Theorem.** Fix a local congruence class $i$ in the [local specialization](LOCAL_HECKE_SPECIALIZATION.md). Let $X_i$ be its finite set of characteristic-zero Hecke points. Decompose the complex space of algebraic forms of the prescribed type into isotypic spaces $V[\pi]$, indexed by the distinct irreducible finite adelic representations occurring in that type. For each $x\in X_i$ one has
$$
\operatorname{mult}_i(x)=
\sum_{\pi\,:\,x(\pi)=x}\dim_{\mathbb C}V[\pi] > 0 .
$$
Here $x(\pi)=x$ means that every eligible split Hecke operator has the eigenvalue specified by $x$ on $V[\pi]$, after the prescribed embedding into $\mathbb C$. A representation whose eigenvalues belong to another congruence class gets no label in $X_i$. In particular every point in $X_i$ comes from some representation. This proves the point-surjectivity and eigenspace-dimension assertions within the automorphic specialization. ## Decomposing the actual space of forms

Use the positive Petersson pairing for the definite group and fixed algebraic weight. Let $W$ be the space of smooth forms with this weight, allowing all sufficiently small finite levels. For each compact open subgroup $U$, the subspace $W^U$ is finite-dimensional: a form there is determined by its values in a finite-dimensional coefficient space on a finite double class set. For a stable subspace $S\subset W$ and $f\in W^U$, project $f$ orthogonally onto $S^U$ in $W^U$. The difference is perpendicular to every $s\in S$, since pairing against $s$ gives the same value as pairing against its $U$-average. This average is a finite sum on a smooth vector. Hence $S$ has the stable complement $S^\perp\cap W$. The elementary semisimplicity criterion for modules now gives an algebraic direct sum of irreducible representations.

Group these representations by isomorphism class. The coordinate projections are equivariant and hence preserve the condition $f(gj)=\chi(j)f(g)$ for the given subgroup $J$. Only finitely many isotypic spaces contribute to this type, which is fixed by a principal congruence subgroup in $\ker\chi$. Pulling them back by the given realization of the complex space of forms yields exactly the nonzero independent summands $V[\pi]$. In particular one label includes all copies of the corresponding representation. [Petersson pairing and the finite class set](LOCAL_HECKE_SEMISIMPLICITY.md) · [Decomposition of the given realization](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/IwasawaDecomposition/AlgebraicModularFormsIrreducibleDecomposition.lean#L44-L55)

At a good split place the supported local group is $G_v=\mathrm{GL}_n(F_v)$, with $K_v=\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathcal O_{F_v})$. The given type is fixed there by $K_v$. The operator indexed by $k$ is the double-coset sum multiplied by $q^{-k(n-1)}$, with $q$ the residue cardinality and $\operatorname{vol}(K_v)=1$. The spherical Hecke algebra is commutative. In the usual local description an irreducible spherical representation has a one-dimensional $K_v$-fixed space, so each Hecke operator has a scalar there. [Gross, *On the Satake isomorphism*, Proposition 2.10 and §6](https://people.math.harvard.edu/~gross/preprints/sat.pdf)

One can see directly why that scalar is the same on a whole isotypic space, even when its type has large dimension. In an irreducible adelic summand with $K_v$-invariants, fix a nonzero vector invariant also under some compact open $U^v$ away from $v$. The space fixed by $K_v U^v$ is finite-dimensional. For a given spherical operator $T$, take an eigenvector $a$ in this space. Its adelic translates span the irreducible summand. Average the translates over $K_v$ to span its $K_v$-invariants. The averaged local translates of $a$ are spherical double-coset operators applied to $a$, up to nonzero volume factors. They commute with $T$, as do translations away from $v$. Thus $T$ has the same eigenvalue on all the invariants. Intertwining carries this scalar to every isomorphic copy. Applying this argument to each eligible generator defines a list of scalars for $\pi$ independent of the vector chosen. ## Counting points with multiplicity

For a fixed point $x$, impose the equations $T_k(v)y=x(T_k(v))y$ on the original space after extending to the local coefficient field $K$. Restrict $v$ to the split good places of the local datum and take $1\leq k\leq n$. The point coordinates distinguish points because these operators generate the local algebra. A nonzero $V[\pi]$ can therefore have at most one label in $X_i$. The projection onto the congruence class retains the entire eigenspace for $x$, as the projected lattice spans the class over $K$. The dimension of this joint eigenspace is exactly the multiplicity defined by the action of the local algebra on that lattice. [Equality with the simultaneous eigenspace](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/IwasawaDecomposition/HeckeMultiplicityEqFinrankJointEigenspace.lean#L67-L93)

Pass through the specified embedding $K\hookrightarrow\mathbb C$. The joint equations have the same kernel dimension over both fields. Indeed, an intersection of kernels in a finite-dimensional vector space is already the intersection of a finite subfamily: starting with the whole space, add an equation only when it reduces the dimension. The rank of the resulting stacked matrix is preserved by field extension. Every omitted equation is a linear consequence at the level of rows, so it remains redundant after extension. The comparison agrees on $L$ with the given complex realization. Thus the extended equations are exactly those for the prescribed normalized operators for every allowed split-place chart and uniformizer. [Coefficient comparison and generator ranges](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/ThetaCorrespondence/ThetaPairLifts/ThirdPlaceSeries/AutomorphicPointsExist.lean#L43-L99)

Now write a complex vector as its sum of isotypic components. A component with a scalar different from $x(T)$ for any generator $T$ must vanish in the joint eigenspace. Every component with the prescribed list belongs to it. This identifies the space with the direct sum in the formula. Each $x$ was constructed from an actual nonzero characteristic-zero eigenspace, so that direct sum is nonzero. This proves both the formula and surjectivity, allowing arbitrary multiplicities in the original space.

## The polynomial at a good split prime

The same argument verifies one further local comparison with the parameter already supplied by the automorphic data. Let $q=|\mathcal O_E/v|$ and let $n$ be the dimension of the defining unitary representation. Write $c_k$ for the normalized Hecke eigenvalue at $v$ belonging to $x$, transported to $\mathbb C$. The exact polynomial is
$$
P_{v,x}(Z)=Z^n+\sum_{k=1}^n(-1)^k q^{k(k-1)/2}c_k Z^{n-k}.
$$
For any constituent $\pi$ labeled by $x$, this is the product $\prod_{\alpha}(Z-\alpha)$ for the Frobenius eigenvalues of the **given local parameter with twist $(1-n)/2$**. To verify the normalization, choose a nonzero vector of the given type in an irreducible copy of $\pi$. Such a copy exists by the type-space decomposition; an equivariant isomorphism carries the vector into the chosen representative if necessary. The vector is fixed by $K_v$. Every $T_k(v)$ acts on it by $c_k$, and the supported part of $J$ is exactly $K_v$. These are the hypotheses of the spherical polynomial identity in the supplied automorphic realization. Its coefficients give the displayed expression term by term, including the powers of $q$. This proves the comparison with that realization's unramified parameter. Constructing the global Galois representation with these characteristic polynomials is a further step.
