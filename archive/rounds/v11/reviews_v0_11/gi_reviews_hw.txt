G.kapISet, Arith.Loc.kapISet, Arith.ins, sig_eq_loc, wreq_of_wok and
weightWindowRequirement_host_of_point.

HW2: quantifier order and the complete Kit compatibility
---------------------------------------------------------
The point constructor is a supplied universal statement in the input
admissible pair, code and u0. The proof first fixes constants and all
their nested lower thresholds, then chooses hosts, an initial pair and
points at every allowed scale. Those points give a temporary family.
Only after that family is fixed is GW5's one eventual threshold chosen.
At a scale beyond it, a temporary index gives a new pair which HW1
transports to its original host. The constructor is then invoked AGAIN
for that one pair and every u0. This is not an exchange of an existential
pair with a previously fixed collection of points. It is not CoverAll
for an already constructed final family. The source HostWW and KitS
have this same order, including maxima before selecting cD and ch.

I checked the complete Pt.Same record against SamePointOfSameScale:
  * the common R,T,T2,q,d,g,pde,pde',hP and degrees follow from From;
  * underlying HostAx gives QS,artNum,rdE;
  * common scale and Val give Sc and eta;
  * CanonInst gives bs under value relabeling;
  * k,scale,Sc determine the entire kappaI function through identical
    universal deficit sets and Extra.kapI_def;
  * Extra.ep_def gives the whole ep function, including p=0,1;
  * offsets at lifted levels follow from the actual filling equation.

For the last point, a finite place exists in the number-field setting,
and the Hodge index over tau1(t) can be chosen. At 1<=j<=m, Val.hc
gives w_j=b_(j-1)+h0/2 and b_i=b1+i h0. At a top,
b1=bT(t)+floor(b'/x), while fill=bT(t)+off, so bT(t) cancels.
At a non-top, tau1(t) is thin: it misses the selected top image by
injectivity and misses the opposite thick image. Then b1=ceil(w''_n)
and fill=off. These give exactly the two cases of HW.4. Outside the
lift set, CanonInst.2 gives the prescribed unrounded values. Thus
every off(j,(false,t)) equality demanded by Pt.Same is proved; no
unneeded Boolean-coordinate equality is assumed.

Indexing by Fin(n) is nonempty because the frame has n>0. The common
code gives a single word-label type with equality of word bits. The
u0-index has that distinguished position, proving Kit.cover; the
common pair supplies cover_weights. The supplied Val, HostArith and
other point properties are retained, and HW1 gives WallPt at every
reconstructed point. Initial windows, code existence and NumAt remain
explicit supplied uniform inputs rather than hidden conclusions.

HW3: cutScale, famOfKits and a carrier already in the family
-----------------------------------------------------------
I checked cutScale and the literal famOfKits definition. Choosing an
allowed y>=x1 provides kits at all formal x. The subtype condition
x<=x1 or x in Win makes indices empty at large x outside Win and
nonempty in Win. At every existing large index the scale equals x
and has the common eps,J. The artificial small-x branch creates no
unproved large-scale assertion.

The family copies D,L,Gx,Gy, ties, fills, lattice data and Extra from
actual points. Supplied NumAt gives its parameters, sizes, rho and
the necessary numerical fields; Std gives its separate fixed/Cr data.
Kit.same and the common word code give the family equality fields.
bTopOf is independent of the kit index because scale/ranks agree
and Val.La_eq identifies LaG with the same G.La(K,s). NumAt.bT_le
gives the actual degree bound, and cover_weights gives coverS.
Nothing here manufactures the retained geometric or NumAt fields.

Because WallPt holds at EVERY point, it holds at every family index.
The sole syntactic difference between the two indicator presentations
is 2*(if E then 1 else 0) = (if E then 2 else 0), valid in both cases.
Thus the resulting statement is the literal ChosenWall, uniform over
all indices. The final setTu change does not affect these data.

Finally, at an allowed large x, Ix_win supplies i0. Its actual degrees
give admissible integer-label functions, and i0 itself is their carrier.
Every other carrier satisfies the loss assertion because ChosenWall
already applies to every index. These are all the clauses of ChosenWS.
Neither CoverAll nor even coverS is needed for this last implication.
The report correctly treats coverS only as a later way to move the
distinguished position while preserving a pair already realized.
