# Reading Alpöge’s remark against the proof

**Research update following edition 0.90, 25 November 2026.** The most productive reading of Alpöge’s four pages is to start with the costs in item 14. Repeating a theta lift forces more local divisibility, but also makes the resulting arithmetic object larger. Alpöge reports that an early estimate of those costs gave exponent $2/3$. His next two items put many periods in a common lattice and estimate how independently they sample it. This explains why a single-lift norm formula can be correct and still leave the central difficulty of the argument. The twenty-item chronology records the discovery process. Footnote 4 describes how it was assembled and cautions that the compressed descriptions vary in precision. [Alpöge, pp. 3–4](https://alpo.ge/remark.pdf#page=3) · [Overview from derivatives to primes](PROOF_IDEA.md)

## Where the remark leads to proofs

**The period scale and the prime-sum centering, item 9.** Kronecker’s first limit formula on the ideal classes of an imaginary quadratic field gives the average logarithmic area of the conjugate CM elliptic period lattices. Including the relative different of the larger field gives the exact identity $\log\Omega_E=\gamma+\log(2\pi/\mathrm{rd}_E)-L'(1,\chi)/L(1,\chi)$. This evaluates the remaining elliptic mean in the earlier Waterloo comparison and explains why the same logarithmic derivative appears on the prime-sum side. The unitary mass theorem of Gan–Hanke–Yu also gives a short derivation of the exact *successive-rank* mass ratio for the standard self-dual lattice. The Weyl dimension formula supplies a third quantity, the dimension of an archimedean representation. None of these scalar calculations alone bounds the norm of the composed theta map. In the first pass the ratio gives a lower bound for **every** word’s auxiliary prime, allowing one choice of size for all their weights. [CM periods and the mass ratio](REMARK_NORMS.md) · [Application to the starting column](FIRST_PASS_COLUMN.md)

**The seesaw and the norm formula, items 7, 8 and 12.** Interlacing governs branching of the compact representations. The theta seesaw moves a period through the tower. The Rallis formula compares inner products of lifts. At a split interior step, the shared variable in two successive tests is paired by the ordinary Fourier transform. On dual lattice boxes the two volume factors have product exactly one, at every rank and level. This calculation identifies one cancellation in the actual normalized tests. On the prescribed grid the [two inductions on the square constants](ROW_TOWER_INDUCTION.md) now justify exchanging the paths through every signature. On the untwisted column the frame primes force [one possible finite cell](ROW_MIXED_CELL_SUPPORT.md) across the catalogue. In the [evaluation of its period](ROW_MIXED_CELL_UNFOLDING.md) the archimedean branching line gives a single scalar for all the finite tests. [Local Fourier contraction and its proof](REMARK_SEESAW.md)

Item 8 describes a rank-raising lift by adjoining the parameter $\mu_j[t]$. Its real-place counterpart in an interior step of the auxiliary chain can now be read off explicitly. One exchanges two negative-weight blocks and inserts the $t$ weights of the compact $U(t)$ half-sum of roots. Li’s nonvanishing theorem and Ichino’s parameter formula explain this pattern. The auxiliary groups can be indefinite at the real places, so their signatures determine which block receives the string. [The actual interior parameters and the Hilbert-space argument](THETA_INTERIOR_DISCRETE_SERIES.md)

**The integral lattice and the angles between periods, items 15 and 16.** On the finite side one can restrict a rational period to the largest sublattice on which it is integral. The exact loss in total degree is determined by the negative elementary-divisor valuations. On the complex side the images must still sample the common target. A bound on the summed overlaps of their orthogonal projections suffices. Together these observations give a version of the mean-slope inequality with explicit errors, including a positive allowance for denominators that preserves the original frame conclusion. These are sufficient alternatives derived from the argument. The [normalized square product on the first pass](ROW_GRID_PRODUCT.md) identifies its entire fractional ideal with that of the definite-bottom ratios and critical Hecke quotients. Uniform numerical bounds on the actual periods and their summed overlaps remain open. [The two estimates with constants](PERIOD_LATTICE_LOSS.md) · [The original relative trace formula and its four types of terms](actual_gram/actual_gram_section.tex)

**The Behrend construction, items 11 and 19.** The tower supplies a long cyclic coordinate. An additive construction chooses marked positions on it, plentiful enough to retain the desired prime sum but sufficiently separated along every short Frobenius orbit. The published theorem needed here concerns sets free of nontrivial averages of up to $2L$ terms for a window of length $L$. It supplies the signed relations and density required in the tower. Ordinary three-term progression freeness alone would leave relations such as $2s_1+s_3=3s_2$. This gives a shorter literature proof for a previously reconstructed part of the host construction. ## Progress on the local Hecke obligation

For a definite unitary group, an unramified split Hecke operator is a normalized sum of translations. Its adjoint comes from the inverse double coset in the same commutative spherical Hecke algebra. Consequently the operator is normal and has no nontrivial Jordan chains. The finite class set and algebraic weight also put its eigenvalues in the integral closure of $\mathbb Z[1/q]$, where $q$ is the residue-field size. The proof handles the prescribed level character, all the primes away from the residue characteristic, and the source’s factor $q^{-k(n-1)}$. This discharges both the four-part standard Hecke input and the no-Jordan input formerly assumed in HLP3–HLP16. The original local specialization obligation now begins, on this route, with producing the common rational and integral spaces, their actual Hecke action and coefficient comparisons, and the required arithmetic and Galois data on that same family. The opening Stepanov analogy, the inert-prime calculation in item 2 and the choice of smoothing in items 5–6 explain the role of multiplicity and the sign needed at the analytic end. Item 17 gives a different clue. Classes surviving the twist can cost denominators, so their **total cohomological length** matters. The reconstruction specifies a kernel of localization in $H^1$ with coefficients $(\mathbb Q_\ell/\mathbb Z_\ell)(-e)$ and the relevant conjugation eigenspace. Its denominator theorem already gives the guarded bound, with the original finiteness and summability conditions. The reference to a weakening of Vandiver and the footnote about Lichtenbaum give historical motivation. No appeal to Vandiver’s conjecture is needed in the theorem just cited. Item 18 concerns the related indices and congruences between integral lifts. Those quantities help explain why an identity of complex periods cannot determine an integral lattice index. At the untwisted reference tuple one can now follow both signed local indices under field enlargement. Each scales by the ramification index. This determines which primes have a nonzero index, a different piece of information from a bound on the total logarithmic loss. Item 18 can also be made explicit at a fitted mixed block with two simple eigenlines.
