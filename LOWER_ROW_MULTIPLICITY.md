# The individual multiplicities on the lower rows agree

Let $Y_0$ be the bottom Hermitian space and $W_i$ the target for row $i$. Fix the discrete-series model $M_0$ allowed in the bottom row, the prescribed holomorphic model $M_i$ in the target row, and any allowed isomorphism $\Phi$ of their finite adelic groups. For every good irreducible admissible finite-adelic representation $\sigma$ of $U(Y_0)$, the original row multiplicities satisfy
$$
 m_{Y_0}(M_0,\sigma)
   = m_{W_i}(M_i,\sigma\circ\Phi^{-1}).
$$
The comparison holds for **each** row, each indicated model and each allowed identification, including multiplicity zero. On every sufficiently late pinned column it gives the assertion called `MultEq` for all data with the stated group identifications, analytic maps and geometric laws. These are quantifiers on the constructed family of hosts. [Definition of the comparison][mult] · [Pinned-column assertion][at]

## Why the same finite test suffices

A good vector supplies a single marked type and vector. For every proper block dimension $a$ there is a marked split place where the $a$-block unipotent radical has zero invariants in this type. Keep the marked type when passing to a lattice-compatible isomorphism $\Phi_0$. Use the pushforward of each finite Haar measure. Any remaining difference between the two prescribed global Haar scales can be assigned to a real factor when normalizing the individual discrete-series pseudocoefficient to trace one. The two rows have the same sorted Harish–Chandra parameter at each real place. Its adjacent gaps are at least $100r^4$ in odd rank $r$. [Marked vector and conjugation](LOWER_ROW_MARKED_TYPE.md) · [Actual parameters][weights]

At an additional split place, the test has regular semisimple support. At every real place it is cuspidal, and there are at least two real places. The invariant trace formula therefore leaves the ordinary regular elliptic orbital terms. The marked type removes all contributions arising from properly induced representations, and kills the elliptic orbital terms for which the algebra $E[\gamma]$ is a product of fields. Thus the cuspidal trace can be computed from the irreducible characteristic polynomials alone. [Proof of the trace reduction and its host-degree hypothesis](LOWER_ROW_SIMPLE_TRACE.md)

For one such polynomial, the finite isometries identify the sums of orbital integrals with their measures. The classification by a quadratic norm and global reciprocity show that the product of local sums enumerates the rational conjugacy classes. At a real place the sum over the assignments of signs to eigenlines of the regular element gives the full Weyl character. The signature sign is $(-1)^{pq}=1$ since $p+q=r$ is odd. The character and the centralizer volume are therefore identical on both sides even when the signatures differ. This gives equality of the cuspidal traces for every allowed finite test. [Termwise comparison including the global norm condition](LOWER_ROW_ELLIPTIC_TRANSFER.md)

Dalal's regular discrete-series lemma shows that the archimedean test picks out the particular representation even within the whole unitary spectrum. At fixed open level only finitely many finite parts occur on the two sides. The allowed Iwahori tests and marked projected translates separate their multiplicities: at the auxiliary place the ordered torus exponents distinguish the irreducible characters, and simultaneous density separates the remaining finite-dimensional projected subspaces. This gives the displayed equality for the lattice-compatible $\Phi_0$. The prescribed $\Phi$ differs by conjugation by a finite adele $b$ on the source. The invertible operator $\sigma(b)$ intertwines the two transports. [Spectral extraction](LOWER_ROW_SPECTRAL_EXTRACTION.md)

## Returning to the precise row datum

The row counts equivariant maps into the specified smooth discrete-series realization. To compare with the spectral count, evaluate such a map on a finite vector and an archimedean model vector to get a cuspidal automorphic function. Rapid decay puts it in the cuspidal Hilbert space. Conversely restrict the Hilbert realization to smooth vectors and to an open compact stabilizer for the finite vector. At this open stabilizer the cuspidal representative theorem gives a continuous representative for every evaluation on the real model. Two continuous representatives that agree almost everywhere agree everywhere by full support of Haar measure. Uniqueness makes the recovered map additive, complex-linear and equivariant for the finite adelic action, and makes the two constructions inverse. The linear isomorphism of Hom spaces gives equality of their ranks. Finiteness at a fixed level and infinitesimal character enters separately in the spectral extraction. [Smooth-map and Hilbert multiplicity comparison][hom] · [Use on both rows][use]

The eventual assertion on a pinned column admits later parameters that need not themselves realize the convenient numerical auxiliary model used to verify the host conditions. Use Green’s marked finite-group construction on the same column to choose an auxiliary stage with the original group labels, prescribed division scale and zero auxiliary row operators. The two valid marked stages identify their primes and the coefficients of their good idempotents. Copy the real datum using that identification. The stage size calculation supplies its weight parameters, and the copied weak characterization, analytic maps, good types, geometric guard and host gaps give the hypotheses of the comparison just proved. It applies for every $i,M_0,\Phi,\sigma$ there. The groups, models, finite action, good condition and multiplicity equations transport back to the given stage. This does not require producing another linked bundle. [Auxiliary stage and identification][bare] · [The hypotheses on the copy][given] · [Return to the prescribed stage][at]
