# Source review for the integral cyclotomic Wach comparison

The categorical equivalence fixes the integral representation and its coefficient action. The cyclotomic coordinate on the Fontaine prism includes the inverse Frobenius shift. At a lower root Q evaluates to p, whereas at a primitive root Q vanishes simply and its Frobenius pullback evaluates to p. These different behaviours are what separate the uniqueness and height arguments. Construction 6.5, including its map off the divisor, was checked against the journal version. For a jump u the modified lattice has generator t to the power −u in the ordinary frame. Testing u negative, zero and positive gives both height inclusions. The jet projection is over the coefficient field, and monic division returns the divisibility to the integral invariant module.

For the residue calculation the base field is unramified and the representation is crystalline over this field before restriction. In a fixed Wach basis reduction of the completed étale coefficient ring gives a Laurent series field. The valuation of the tame norm and the distinct linear terms of the tame substitutions identify its fixed scalars. Fontaine's theorem is used on the underlying integral representations, where it includes torsion, before taking the coefficient idempotents and averaging. Berger's erratum to the rational Wach definition adds an étaleness condition, supplied here by the actual integral étale module. The proof uses the action on the integral lattice at the origin. [Berger's errata, section 2](https://perso.ens-lyon.fr/laurent.berger/articles/errata.pdf#page=3)

For arbitrary residue components, semilinear descent gives the invariant dimension. The cyclotomic towers from the two finite base fields have the same union. An invariant vector tuple supplies a morphism commuting with the full cyclotomic group, so full faithfulness proves the fixed-vector property for this canonical realization.

For the subquotient the intersection is finitely generated over a discrete valuation ring and spans after inverting its parameter. For the Herr pair, subtracting a tuple defining a boundary produces simultaneous fixed lifts. The fixed-vector hypothesis supplies the splitting, and its value on the unit fixes the coboundary sign.
