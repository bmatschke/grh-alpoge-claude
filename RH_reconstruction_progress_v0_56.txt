2. POLARIZED BASE CHANGE: WHAT HAD TO BE CONSTRUCTED

The geometric problem was to pull back a polarized abelian record along
an arbitrary morphism S' -> S and prove every field of the result on the
actual Cartesian pullback. It was not enough to know informally that
polarizations behave well under base change. The source uses explicit
line-bundle charts, generating sections, affine nonvanishing loci and
surjective polynomial coordinate maps. Those objects must agree in the
several parts of the construction.

The first task is relative ampleness local on the base. The needed local
sections initially live on different opens and may belong to different
positive powers of the same line bundle. The proof has to produce one
power and one finite section package that works on the chosen affine
base. Quasi-compactness permits finite choices, but it does not by itself
extend a section or make its overlap equations hold.

PBC handles those two issues separately. One exponent clears the local
denominators and gives candidate extensions. A second exponent kills
the finitely many overlap defects, so that the extensions agree on the
actual overlaps and glue. The argument uses the precise vanishing
criterion in a localization; it does not assume that multiplying by a
single denominator automatically makes all chart equations exact.
The actual global construction first chooses a common positive degree
and then extends the mixed products s_b*s_a^d. The extended numerator
family is fixed before the later denominator section. This order makes
the choices visible, preserves generation by the ratio functions, and
keeps the proof valid over arbitrary bases.

Another difficulty is what happens when an additional section is added
to an existing affine generating package. A finite-module determinant
argument gives the required integral equation, and the extra-section
lemma proves affineness together with the exact generator statement.
Combining it with the extension construction yields the original
glue_pack and exists_sv. These results retain the ratio functions and
the surjective polynomial maps which the source's relative-ample
predicate requires. The resulting relAmple_of_locally theorem has the
original quasi-compact and quasi-separated hypotheses, with no added
Noetherianity or flatness condition on the base morphism.

Once that theorem is available, the polarized pullback is concrete.
Its underlying group is P.A times_S S'. Its line bundle is literally
P.L pulled back by the projection, and its dimension is defined to be
P.g. The multiplication compatibility is proved on every test scheme.
The rigidification and symmetry are transported through actual bundle
generators, so the output supplies the source's generated LB.Iso rather
than only equality of isomorphism classes.

The translation-kernel condition KTriv deserves particular attention.
For an arbitrary affine test T over S', the proof compares

  T times_(S') (P.A times_S S')    and    T times_S P.A.

These are canonically isomorphic through their actual projections.
The translation-difference bundle on the first space is identified with
the pullback of the corresponding bundle on the second. A locally
generating tuple is transported over the same open of T. The original
KTriv then forces the projected point to be the identity, and the
Cartesian point correspondence forces the original point to be the
identity. This avoids any need to descend sections along an arbitrary
nonflat morphism: the relevant comparison is an isomorphism.

PBC25 therefore proves the full original PolBaseChangeSt. PBC26 returns
it to the original all-affine-test very-ample producer. For each supplied
affine test and each specified Cartesian model, it produces a positive
power, a finite tuple of sections, affine nonzero loci and the required
coordinate-surjectivity data. The power may depend on that test. No
uniform power across all tests is asserted or needed by that endpoint.

PBC27 also supplies actual section finiteness. A Perf record presents
sections through the kernel of a finite matrix. Over the original field
K, if its degree-zero matrix has a columns, any a+1 coordinate vectors
have a nonzero linear relation. The explicit section-coordinate formula
carries that relation
to every original chart; this uses the given coordinate formula and
does not assume unstated linearity of a chosen set-theoretic bijection.
The argument then returns to the original SecM on its identity-product
model, including every integer power of the line bundle over the
original algebraically closed field K. The generic implication over an
arbitrary field retains the supplied finite-relation premise. This
section-finiteness conclusion is distinct from the arbitrary-base Perf
and polarized-pullback statements.

The geometric frontier is now more precise. Polarized base change,
the very-ample producer, Perf, both Serre conclusions and actual section
finiteness are available. The next particularly useful task is the
two-sided growth theorem for those section spaces. Its remaining
ingredients are geometric integrality, the comparison of relative
dimension with transcendence degree, and polynomial-image growth.
Multiplication geometry, invariant-function descent, the principal
rank-one law and square/translation generators remain separate.

3. THE LOCAL ARITHMETIC PRESENTATION

The arithmetic chapter retains the original F.Ok(C_0), supplied
rank-specific Space and supplied Loc. It starts with the actual local
object constructed in the preceding work. Its global rings A and B,
closed point x over m_B, coefficient extension O', local ring R and
map iota:A -> R remain fixed. The goal is a presentation of this R,
not of a newly chosen deformation problem having similar residual data.

There are two main parts. The first proves that the original normalized
representation on R has the universal property needed by deformation
theory. The second constructs an obstruction space and counts tangent
directions carefully enough to obtain the exact presentation count.
Both parts retain the ordinary characters and the marked matrix data.

For universality, the broad point_loc statement must be handled at its
original IsBar scope. A proof valid only for a restricted family of
test points would not suffice. APD constructs finitely generated graded
Laurent vertex algebras and applies the supplied Space universal property
to their
finite quotients. The values of the original degree-zero coefficients
are then recovered by compactness of the finite-residue coefficient
ring. This is a useful simplification, because it keeps the evaluation
steps on finite Laurent polynomials. It never substitutes t=1 into an
arbitrary completed Laurent series, where such an evaluation would not
be justified.

The result proves broad point realization, the extension of the actual
A-map, and the def_iso statement. Consequently the actual normalized
local representation satisfies NRep.Univ. This removes universality as
an unsupplied entry to the presentation argument. The still broader
standalone alg_closed and alg_limit statements are not claimed by this
route; they are unnecessary for this endpoint at its original arithmetic
coefficient scope.

For a small extension, arbitrary lifts of the representation produce
matrix-valued multiplicative defects. The square-zero hypothesis makes
the correction formulas linear. The proof writes down the relevant
cocycles. When the obstruction class vanishes, correcting cochains
produce the required quotient section.
The local ordinary conditions and the normalization of the marked
matrix impose their own corrections, both of which are carried out on
the same representation. This supplies the original HasObs, including
the required section for every quotient in its definition. It is more
than a statement that some cohomology group should control obstructions.

The tangent calculation likewise constructs the actual map and proves
its surjectivity at the needed strict scope. The nonzero diagonal weight
matrix has two possible contributions. If it is invariant, it supplies
a nonzero class in H_C^0; the associated tangent variation may vanish.
If it is not invariant, the variation is a nonzero vector in the kernel
of the tangent-to-cohomology map. Both cases yield the same extra +1 in
the dimension comparison. Keeping both cases preserves the next
component-chain threshold without assuming that the tangent variation
is always nonzero.

Write T=Der_(O')(R,k') and let h_i be the dimensions of the original
cone cohomology groups. The source's finite-module Euler field is
applied only after its hypotheses have been verified. APD proves open
kernel of the actual adjoint action, stability and dimensions of the
local Borel spaces, the required global triangularity, and vanishing of
the cyclotomic dual. The rank-three outer weight difference is treated
with the negative-sign element and the original delta factor; it is
not folded into the adjacent-weight argument. The permitted Host prime
ell=3 is retained.

The Euler inequality simplifies to

  h_2 + h_0 + n*d <= h_1.

The tangent and invariant-space count in these two cases gives

  h_1 + 1 <= dim(T) + h_0.

Combining them yields

  dim(T) - h_2 >= n*d + 1.

The minimal-presentation algebra turns this into a power-series
surjection. To get the exact number of listed relations, the proof may
enlarge the obstruction space by a harmless direct summand and pad a
shorter generating list with zeros. Those operations leave the zero
obstruction locus and the generated ideal unchanged. Thus APD25 gives

  O'[[X_1,...,X_N]] -> R,
  ker = (f_1,...,f_(N')),
  N = N' + n*d + 1.

This is the full original pres for either rank, with no added Big or
nontriangularity hypothesis. It does not prove every subsequent
component-dimension statement. The next algebraic producer c1ch has
two different conclusions: minimal components have dimension at least
D+1, while consecutive intersections in a linking chain have dimension
at least D. Those bounds must remain distinct. In rank two the new
equality supplies exactly the power-series dimension threshold for
D=2*d+1 and h=N'. Full sharp2 additionally needs the strict triangular
dimension bound. Rank three has a separate exceptional parity branch
requiring EPres, which is not supplied by ordinary Pres.

4. THE REFERENCE UNIPOTENT VOLUME

The volume chapter addresses the original reference measure, defined
from a Euclidean metric on a standard nilpotent Lie algebra. The actual
adelic InVolumes normalization completed earlier remains unchanged.
The purpose of the new calculation is to evaluate the reference
covolume exactly, so that the later local disintegration can compare
the actual measures with this fixed reference.

Keep one original HidaSetting X, its Hermitian lattice L0, its
hyperbolic frame e_b,f_b, and its orthogonal-complement lattice Lb.
Let r be the hyperbolic rank, k the positive middle dimension,
d=[F:Q], and Delta=abs(disc F). Let Tw(w)>0 be the original word
parameter. There are four types of arithmetic coordinate blocks:
upper unitriangular entries, rows in the complement, off-diagonal
central entries, and imaginary central diagonal entries.

The row block contains the principal arithmetic work. The supplied
decomposition and unimodularity of L0 show that Lb is a full self-dual
Hermitian lattice, even when it has no global principal basis. Its
rational trace dual is the absolute codifferent times Lb. Local ranks
compute the corresponding integer index, and a basis-change determinant
then gives the exact trace Gram determinant after multiplication by
any nonzero fractional ideal. This proves the required result in every
supplied integer basis.

The archimedean row map is conjugate-linear in its vector variable,
because the Hermitian form is conjugate-linear in its first variable.
It is correctly treated as a real linear map. The half-trace identity
has a diagonal real form whose negative coordinate contributes Tw(w)^(-1).
Its determinant converts the trace Gram calculation into the row
covolume. The complex ideal blocks follow from the embedding
discriminant formula.

For the imaginary diagonal block, the integral trace-one element is
essential. It splits the relative trace map on the fractional ideal.
The real projection of the splitting is one half of the real ideal
lattice. That factor of two cancels the realification factor in the
complex ideal covolume. This argument remains valid at residue
characteristic two and uses the actual anti-invariant kernel; replacing
it by imaginary parts of arbitrary ideal elements would change the
integral condition.

The group calculation uses explicit matrices n(Q,B,K), where Q is
upper unitriangular, B contains the row coordinates and K is
skew-Hermitian. The Hermitian part of the remaining central matrix is
forced by Q and B. Direct multiplication gives a triangular polynomial
law: each correction depends only on blocks of strictly smaller source
height. Successive translations in those blocks preserve product
Lebesgue measure, on both the left and the right.

The arithmetic subgroup must also be identified exactly. The finite
level condition is proved on the supplied finite adelic lattice by
choosing one clearing integer C coprime to the level g. After inverting
C, the actual frames become integral and congruent to the supplied
integral frames. Orthogonal projection proves all three required K2
congruences. Since C is invertible modulo g, those congruences and their
coefficients descend to the original quotient L/gL. No character
identity or PsiDd premise is introduced in this membership argument.

Rational coordinates, exact lattice preservation and reconstruction
then give the full rational dictionary and the shifted-fiber description
NOfib. The converse matters: every admissible coordinate tuple is
reconstructed as an actual rational lattice-preserving isometry. It is
not merely shown that already-known rational points satisfy necessary
coordinate conditions. The shifts use the literal source theta(D) and
tauQ, including their definitions away from rational coordinate tuples.

The triangular law now permits coordinate-by-coordinate reduction into
the product of half-open block domains. It also proves uniqueness.
Countability and translated partitions give the same volume for every
measurable strict domain. The coordinate covolume is

  C_w = Tw(w)^(r*d) * 2^(-d*(r*k+r^2)) * Delta^eN,

  eN = -(8*r^3 + 6*r^2 + 6*k*r^2 + r)/6.

The original weak supported-Haar predicate is sufficient: the earlier
AMD8 argument turns its sigma-finite, nonzero, left-invariant measure
into an actual Haar measure on this closed unimodular subgroup. Its
scalar is identified by the mass of the coordinate unit box. Therefore
the formula applies to every original Haar normalization and every
original ambient fundamental-domain witness.

It remains to compute that scalar for the prescribed reference measure.
Each independent complex Lie parameter occurs twice in the ambient
matrix norm. Its real and imaginary directions each have length sqrt(2).
Each imaginary diagonal parameter occurs once. The exact Euclidean
volume factor is consequently 2^(r^2+r*k). The exponential-to-polynomial
coordinate change is a triangular polynomial shear with determinant
one, so no further scalar is introduced.

The reference word u and lattice word w are kept independent. Their
middle frames have Gram determinants in the ratio Tw(w)/Tw(u). The
same frame-change matrix acts on each of r rows, giving real determinant
(Tw(w)/Tw(u))^r. It follows that the product reference measure nu_u has

  nu_u(Box_w) = Tw(w)^(-r*d) * 2^(d*(r*k+r^2)).

The Tw and powers-of-two factors cancel against C_w, leaving

  nu_u(Omega_w) = Delta^eN.

Applying the same reference measure nu_u to the two specified HypX
domains, whose lattice words may differ, gives the required exponent
eDF=2*eN. All cancelled factors are finite and strictly positive before
real values are taken. This completes full CovolRef. The exact local
JacGauge density and the broad product-disintegration theorem remain
the next producers for RealBracket.

5. THE WACH PRISM AND ITS COMPLETE LATTICE RETURN

The earlier WLD chapter isolated three period-ring inputs to a generic
Wach-lattice construction. WIS supplied the actual inclusion and
reflection of units. IFD supplied full inhomogeneous invariant
Frobenius-matrix descent. The remaining input was the actual prism,
including its maps and two coefficient decompositions. WPR constructs
that input and returns it to the original lattice consumer.

Let K0 be the prescribed maximal unramified subfield of K and put
W0=O_(K0). The ring is

  S = W0[[T]].

For the original layer s>=1, define

  U_s = (1+T)^(p^(s-1)) - 1,
  Q_s = sum_(j=0)^(p-1) (1+U_s)^j.

The prism ideal is (Q_s). Its Frobenius is coefficient Frobenius
combined with T -> (1+T)^p-1. Its Galois action is substitution by
(1+T)^chi(g)-1. The coefficient embeddings into the original LetA,
and the scalar map from Zp[[T]], are fixed throughout.

Several generic algebra arguments make this construction transparent.
First, Q_s is distinguished: Q_s=T^d+p*b, with
d=p^(s-1)*(p-1). To divide by it, split a series into its first d
coefficients and its tail. The quotient solves an equation q+pL(q)=a.
The series sum_j (-p)^j L^j(a) converges coefficientwise in the
complete local coefficient ring. Each output coefficient of L depends
on finitely many input coefficients, which justifies passage to the
limit. Uniqueness follows from separatedness. This proves the original
kernel theorem without adding continuity of the given ring map or
Noetherianity of the coefficient ring.

Second, the original map initially lands in O tensor Ainf. A normalized
linear retraction O -> Zp with r(1)=1 gives a retraction of pure tensors.
Truncating a formal series shows that the difference between its image
and the image obtained through that retraction is divisible by every
power of the original parameter. Parameter separation forces it to be
zero. Thus the map factors through one actual copy of Ainf, preserving
its variable and constant values. This proof requires separation,
not an unstated continuity assumption on the tensor-valued map.

The constant values are controlled by a finite Zp-basis of the actual
unramified integer ring consisting of prime-to-p roots of unity. Their
Teichmuller lifts satisfy the original IoW identity, and finite scalar
sums give every constant. No additivity of Teichmuller representatives
is assumed. The inclusion statement holds at its own broader original
scope, for every s>=0 without Good; only the later prism specialization
uses s>=1 and K contained in K_s.

The theta specialization sends T to the original zeta_s-1. Distinct
fractional valuations of its powers prove the required low-degree
polynomial independence. The same calculation shows that integral
elements of the finite cyclotomic extension have integral coefficients
in that basis. Distinguished division therefore identifies the kernel
as exactly (Q_s), and the original O_K image supplies the base structure.

Every bounded-prism field is checked. The distinguished ideal is free
of rank one, the mixed ideal (p,Q_s) is complete, p belongs to the sum
of the ideal and its Frobenius image, and the quotient has no p-power
torsion. The actual Fontaine prism is also verified using its principal
theta kernel, p-saturation and mixed completeness. The constant prism,
evaluation, actual period inclusion and all Galois prism morphisms are
then constructed on these rings. Frobenius, Galois and scalar identities
are proved as literal equalities of the prescribed maps.

The second coefficient decomposition is particularly useful. For a
finite free algebra W and a family of embeddings separated pairwise by
unit differences, explicit interpolation products produce each
coordinate idempotent in the image of O tensor W. The tensor map is
therefore surjective. A square matrix over O with a right inverse has
unit determinant and hence an inverse, proving bijectivity over an
arbitrary commutative coefficient ring. Applied to the original
unramified embeddings, this yields unique coordinates for every
original Zp-basis of O. Applying the coefficient statement degree by
degree gives the required formal-series decomposition. The first
decomposition, finite freeness of S over Zp[[T]], follows similarly.

WPR29 lists and proves all sixteen fields of the original prism endpoint.
WPR30 then uses WIS18, the homogeneous case of IFD25, and WLD26. The
result is the full original implication

  BhattScholzeCrystallineLattices and LocTied  =>  WachEx.

It holds for every original permitted layer and every supplied etale
module. The original crystalline-realization and tied-period inputs
remain hypotheses of this theorem. They have not been silently removed.
No period-ring supplier remains in this implication, while FaithH,
EtBasis, Herr class maps, quantitative comparison and the common ConclF
family still precede the complete denominator conclusion.

6. WHAT THE NEW FRONTIER SAYS ABOUT EFFICIENCY

The last four chapters confirm that the efficient unit of work is a
substantial original producer together with its next consumer. Reading
only a short wrapper tends to rename its assumptions. Reading every
remote dependency before selecting a target creates a large collection
of disconnected notes. The useful middle course is to identify the
actual object, the exact output fields and the next use, then prove a
complete construction or a precisely sufficient specialization.

The new prism illustrates this well. Once the prescribed ring and maps
are fixed, the apparent construction problem becomes a finite list of
concrete algebraic obligations: division, factorization, mixed
completeness and coordinates. The reference volume similarly becomes
a trace determinant, a literal subgroup dictionary, triangular measure
preservation and an exact Lie normalization. The arithmetic presentation
benefits from a finite-quotient point construction followed by the
actual obstruction calculation. The geometry benefits from keeping
denominator clearing and overlap gluing as two distinct operations.

Parallel work is effective because these four producers use different
constructions. It becomes less effective when separate workers choose
different realizations of what must later be one common object. The next
plan therefore keeps one owner for each ring, marked frame, measure
normalization and family. Other workers can prove independent properties
or suppliers, with the equality or transport into that common object
written explicitly.

The default next wave is: faithful cyclotomic invariants and the actual
etale basis; section growth after the now-complete polarized and Serre
inputs; component chains using the exact new presentation; and the
local JacGauge calculation. The broad ProdExact theorem can be assigned
independently when a slot becomes free. The roadmap states the exact
sources and numerical acceptance conditions for each.
