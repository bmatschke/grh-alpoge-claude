# Changing the marked frame and determining the phase

Fix a geometric realization of the original Hermitian moduli problem with its period charts, the dictionary assigning a component and frame to each adelic group element, and the original comparison laws on that dictionary. Assume the canonical volume on the arithmetic stage. The fixed-stage theta construction prescribes new orthogonal base frames. Changing to them preserves the Petersson norm's definiteness. Under the reference-row level, field and marked-action identifications, moving a section by a residue-group element introduces a single unit on each component. On a stabilized component the unit in the **raw frame reading** is exactly one. The adelic reading then has the prescribed determinant character. [Analytic stage](THETA_FIXED_STAGE_REALIZATION.md) · [Frame change on the supplied core][frame] · [Reference-row identifications][pins]

## Why zero Petersson norm still detects every section

Write $A_0,A_1$ for the old and new base frames in a given embedding. Replace a dictionary value $(c,x)$ by $(c,xA_0^{-1}A_1)$. Left multiplication by a rational isometry commutes with this change, so its original lattice and finite marking still match. The Gram identities for $A_0,A_1$ identify the two real frame models. The inverse change is right multiplication by $A_1^{-1}A_0$.

Every frame in the component domain is still represented up to its rational stabilizer and the triangular change of basis allowed in evaluating a section. Indeed, represent a frame first using the old dictionary. At each real place a unitary transformation carries the orthogonal reference frame to the one obtained by the inverse change, since their Gram matrices agree. Use these real transformations and identity at all finite places to form an adelic element. Translating the old representative by this element inserts the inverse change *before* applying the new dictionary. The two changes cancel. The rational mover on the left and triangular factor on the right give exactly the required orbit representative. Applying the inverse change gives the fiber equivalence as well. [Orbit and fiber calculation][orbit]

For the canonical volume on the stage, the earlier volume proof identifies the recorded volume with the mass of the rational quotient. Changing the level from $K$ to $K'\subset K$ and renormalizing either to mass one multiplies the adelic quotient mass by $[K:K']$. Normalized quotient probability has full support. Indeed, the inverse image of a nonempty open subset of the quotient is open, nonempty and rationally invariant. If it met a fundamental domain in measure zero, countably many rational translates would give it Haar measure zero everywhere. This contradicts positivity of Haar measure on nonempty opens. [Volume and level change](geometric_volumes/main.tex#L650-L706) · [Full support for the original measure][supp]

Let $s$ be a cusp section whose adelic reading has zero Petersson norm. This reading is continuous and rationally invariant, hence vanishes everywhere by full support. Its multiplier on each dictionary entry has modulus one, so the section reading vanishes at that frame. Orbit coverage and the two transformation laws now extend the vanishing to the whole component: the rational-character and triangular-weight factors are invertible. The original geometric separation of sections by their chart values gives $s=0$. Bounded cusp readings are square integrable since the quotient has finite mass. The same argument works after altering a determinant multiplier by a continuous unitary character with the required rational and level laws. This proves the norm comparison needed in the changed dictionary without postulating a separate comparison of chart measures. [Vanishing at every frame][read] · [Definiteness of the section norm][norm] · [Retention of the laws in the changed model][frame]

## A level move between two components

Let a lift of $g$ in the finite residue group send component $c$ to $c'$. The marked period charts and the prescribed geometric level action identify a rational isometry carrying the first lattice, marking and period frame to the second. Pulling a section along the geometric move and evaluating it in the first chart thus agrees at each point with evaluating the original section in the carried frame, up to the weight of the change of normalized generators. At a unit frame the Lie terms match and comparison of the normalized generators gives modulus one, common to every section. Triangular covariance extends the equality to all frames of the chart. The construction uses the reference row's actual marked lift and pullback on sections. [Pointwise comparison using the periods and normalized frames][point]

The unit is constant on the connected chart. To see this, write $P_s(x),Q_s(x)$ for the two holomorphic readings. If all the $Q_s$ vanish identically, take the unit one. Otherwise choose one section nonzero on a small open set. There $P_s/Q_s$ for this section is holomorphic of modulus one, so it is constant. The same constant works for every section there by the pointwise identity. Holomorphic continuation gives it on the entire chart, including the divisors of those sections. Two lifts of $g$ induce the same marking up to the principal-level equivalence, and the rational covariance identifies the readings. The scalar can thus be chosen as a function of $(c,g)$. ## Why the sign disappears on a stabilized component

Suppose now $c'=c$. Let $\delta$ be its lattice isometry. Its determinant is an algebraic integer because it preserves the full lattice (use an integral local basis at each finite place). Unitarity makes every complex absolute value of this determinant equal to one. Kronecker's theorem therefore makes it a root of unity. The determinant need not be a rational sign. [Determinant and CM degree][det]

In the integral symplectic basis adapted to the host, let $N$ be the matrix of the *inverse* isometry. The element $e=w_0^4$ acts modulo 8 as the projection on the first half of this basis. Since $N$ commutes with $e$, its two off-diagonal blocks vanish modulo 8. We need $\det N_{11}\equiv1\pmod4$ as well. Reduce the determinant of $e\delta^{-1}+(1-e)I$ on the integer lattice. It equals $\det N_{11}$ modulo 8 in the adapted basis. On restriction of scalars it equals the absolute norm of its $E$-determinant. Since $e$ is idempotent modulo 8, that determinant is congruent to $e\det_E(\delta^{-1})+1-e$: over the two idempotent summands the matrices are $\delta^{-1}$ and $I$. This calculation holds on the locally free lattice over the ring of integers.

Here $e$ comes from the imaginary quadratic subfield $K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-d})$ of the host, with $d>3$ an odd prime and $[E:K]$ even. Every root of unity in $E$ has norm one to $K$. Its odd-order part has norm one since the only roots of unity in $K$ are $\pm1$. The norm of $-1$ is one by the even degree. For a primitive root of order $2^a$, $a\ge2$, $K$ and $\mathbb Q(\zeta_{2^a})$ are disjoint because the former is ramified at $d$ and the latter only at 2. Its norm from $K(\zeta_{2^a})$ to $K$ is therefore its ordinary cyclotomic norm, which is one. The tower law proves the claim. Taking the relative norm of the idempotent expression modulo 8 gives one on both summands, hence also after taking the absolute norm. In particular the needed congruence modulo 4 follows. [Integral determinant calculation and adapted blocks][blocks]

For a symplectic change of periods the classical squared-theta transformation has a residual character $\chi_4(\det D)$, where $D=N_{11}^{t}$ in the period convention here and $\chi_4$ is the nontrivial character modulo 4. Andrianov and Andrianov give this character in the transformation of two theta constants. The congruence above makes it one. To pass to the actual section with its half-characteristic and its variable in the abelian variety, use the rigidified transformation law: the ratio of the line trivializations glues on overlaps to a nowhere-vanishing holomorphic function with value one at the origin. The Jacobi transformation accounts for the quadratic exponential in this ratio. One cancels the nonzero theta function on an open set and continues the identity before evaluating at the origin. This works even for a characteristic whose theta constant vanishes. [Andrianov–Andrianov, Lemma 2.1, (2.7)–(2.9)](https://www.impan.pl/shop/en/publication/transaction/download/product/83051) · [Transformation with the variable and rigidification](normalized_frame_laws_v51/main.tex#L142-L305) · [Transformation on this isometry][sq]

Write $h,h'$ for the period-normalizing matrices, $\kappa,\kappa'$ for the section scalars and $t_0$ for the change of theta-line generator. In these orientations the squared identity is
$$
 t_0^2 (\kappa')^2\det h=\kappa^2\det h'.
$$
The same chart normalization $C\ne0$ gives $\kappa^2=C\det h$ and $(\kappa')^2=C\det h'$. Cancellation gives $t_0^2=1$. The Lie-frame scalars agree and the section's theta exponent is even, so its total weight on this change is one. This proves the exact self-component equality. Reinstating the determinant multiplier gives the specified finite-group character at the chosen embedding, since the actual level lift reads the given residue determinant. [Section-scalar cancellation and weight][sign] · [Reinstating the character][stab]

Finally, divide the component move factors by this prescribed character and include the known determinant multipliers, as in A77G1. On components admitting a nonzero reading, evaluating successive moves on a nonzero value proves the cocycle identity. On an orbit where every reading vanishes, the correction can be chosen freely to satisfy the identity. The stabilizer calculation just made makes the corrected factor one for every stabilizer. Fix an orbit representative and transport one chosen unit to the other components. The stabilizer identity gives independence of the transporter. At the distinguished embedding use the reference solution so its phase remains one. This supplies the geometric move comparison on a core with the specified marked data. The construction of that initial core for the column still requires the actual moduli schemes, uniformizations and marked coefficient field.
