THREE-SLOT LATTICE WALK — v0.3

The complete readable proof is in walk_section.tex. This note records the exact
dictionary to the source and the few conventions suppressed in the main text.

LW1. Statements and inputs.
  Source WalkSharp is over an arbitrary commutative ring and module. The main
  note specializes to the actual DVR setup. Its six local conditions are:
    dual <= base <= ball;
    pi^a ball <= base; pi^b base <= dual; pi^c ball <= dual;
    y_v ball <= dual.
  Each condition holds with all other slot labels held fixed. The joint input
  is pi^c0 L_B <= sum_{v in B} y_v L_(B;v=ball) + span G_B.
  The operators commute; they preserve the all-base lattice as a consequence.
  The source uses indices Fin V (0 through V-1). The text uses 1 through V.

LW2. General state proof, retaining every cost.
  Fix q=beta+1. For an active q-element set B, put
    D(B) = {u < max B : u not in B}.
  Members of D(B) have completed m applications; all u>max B are waiting.
  A state has counters 0 <= i_v < m at active indices, zero off B. Its operator
  exponents are m on D(B), i_v on B, and zero on waiting indices. Define
    K_v = a_v+b_v+(m-1)c_v+m c0;
    A = sum_(u in D(B)) K_u + sum_(v in B)(b_v+i_v(c_v+c0));
    E = sum K_v + beta S.
  Let C be the union over all q-element B and all its m^q states of Y_(B,i)G_B.
  The invariant is pi^(E-A) Y_(B,i) L_B <= N+span C.

  To prove it, apply the joint decomposition and reserve c0. Each y_v term
  increments its operator exponent exactly once. If i_v+1<m, multiply the
  vector by pi^c_v to move ball to dual. The new budget is A+c0+c_v.
  If i_v+1=m and w=max B+1 exists, multiply the vector by pi^(a_v+b_w),
  move v from ball to base, move w from base to dual, and replace v by w in B.
  The new budget is A+c0+a_v+b_w. The exponents of all operators other than
  v are unchanged, including the new active w, whose exponent is zero.
  If there is no waiting index, multiplication by pi^a_v puts the vector in
  the lattice with dual slots B\{v}, hence in M. All indices outside that
  beta-element set have exponent m. Multiplying by pi^sum_(u in B\{v}) S_u
  therefore places the term in N. The remaining active powers preserve M.

  Nonnegative budgets: for active v, i_v<=m-1. Thus
    b_v+i_v(c_v+c0)+c0 <= K_v;
  if i_v=m-1, adding a_v+c0 instead gives equality with K_v.
  The terminal scalar cost is at most beta S. These inequalities justify
  every exponent subtraction in the invariant, including m=1, S=0.
  The sum of all operator exponents strictly increases at each transition
  and is at most mV; hence backwards induction terminates. Start with the
  first q indices and all counters zero. Entering their dual slots costs
  sum b_v=A, so the invariant gives pi^E M <= N+span C.
  If beta>=V, scalar generators alone give the result with C empty.

LW3. Actual specialization and elementary symmetric count.
  pi=w, w^2=p; a_v=b_v=t_v; c_v=2t_v; c0=2(D0+1);
  S_general=2S, (S_v)_general=2S_v.
  K_v = 2m(t_v+D0+1) and E=2(tau+beta S), where
    tau=sum_v m(t_v+D0+1).
  The source RestWalk supplies |G_B| <= d product_(v in B) kappa_v.
  There are m^(beta+1) active counter patterns for each B. Consequently
    |C| <= d m^(beta+1) e_(beta+1)(kappa).
  Since the left side is integral, the nonnegative floor can be inserted.
  This floor is a cardinality conversion, unrelated to the ramification-
  scaled ideal floor in LC4. It must not be confused with that rounding loss.
  All-scalar monomial gives p^sum S_v M <= N. The whole N lies in M because
  all y_v preserve M. This matches GrowthBounds.walk_clause on M=G.Mna.
  Applying LC1 and LC2 now proves the tail-to-index conclusion, conditional
  only on the slot and joint-rank inputs just listed. Transfer to G.M and the
  retained saturated lattice is handled separately by SR1 and its sequel.

LW4. What is elementary about the slot construction, and what remains.
  For a full lattice H in a finite-dimensional vector space over a DVR,
  let H^vee be its integral dual. If H <= R_e for all outgoing edges and
  H^vee <= R'_e for all incoming edges, define
    B = w^(-t)H intersect intersection_e R_e,
    T = w^(-t)H^vee intersect intersection_e R'_e,
    D = {x : phi(x) in O for all phi in T}.
  Then H <= B <= w^(-t)H. Also H^vee <= T <= w^(-t)H^vee;
  integral polarity and H=(H^vee)^vee give w^t H <= D <= H.
  These are exactly base<=ball, dual<=base, w^t ball<=base,
  and w^t base<=dual. The bound w^(2t) ball<=dual follows by composition.
  Tensor products carry each factor inclusion to a global inclusion;
  requiring equivariant integral values at lattice configurations also
  preserves inclusion. This is the human argument behind ch_locChain,
  ch_tenV_smul, ch_step, ch_chain, and restWalk_of_concrete.

  NOT reconstructed here: the detailed highest-weight representation
  argument showing H<=R_e and H^vee<=R'_e for every actual edge; the
  corresponding lattice embeddings and naturality giving y_v ball<=dual.
  Those inputs are explicit RestLocD and RestNat. Their source proofs are
  present (restLocD and restNat_proved), so this is a boundary of our human
  exposition, not an asserted gap in the released formal proof. JR proves
  the joint-rank assembly but retains its single-place polynomial inputs.
