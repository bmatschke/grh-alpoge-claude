# Conjugation on the active distinguished-character flat tests

Fix a first-pass place $w$, a row, and a coefficient automorphism $\tau$ fixing the embedded small field. Write $\ell=\operatorname{resChar}(w)$, $t$ for the old tuple attached to the active occurrence, $r=\dim_E W$, and $\xi=\delta_\ell$. The new auxiliary twist has empty conductor and trivial character. The old tuple still contains $\xi$, of order $15$, with selected set $\mathcal D=R.Q(t)$ over the distinguished rational prime $p_d\ne\ell$. Both coefficient embeddings give this same tuple. The fixed-field input fixes its character values, the base-level root and the prescribed CM curve with its endomorphism action. Use one normal coefficient model $F_*$ containing the character values, the same distinguished cover, and identical definite representatives for the two occurrences. ## The Fourier table

Let $M$ be the cover level and $M_0\mid M$ the base level. Write $\tau\zeta_M=\zeta_M^m$ and choose $m'$ inverse to $m$ modulo $M$. Then $m'\equiv1\bmod M_0$. At $P\in\mathcal D$, $p_d\mid M$, so $m,m'$ give inverse elements of $k_P^\times$. Let $\xi_P$ be the residue character and put
$$
 b_\tau=\prod_{P\in\mathcal D}\xi_P(\bar m)^r .
$$
At each such $P$ the residue function vanishes unless the required lower block is zero and the leading $r$-square block is invertible. On this support its factor is $\xi_P(\det Y_{\rm top})^{-1}$. Multiplication by $m'$ preserves the support and multiplies that determinant by $(\bar m')^r$. The marked indicator factors at the other selected primes are unchanged because $m'=1$ there. Consequently $\tau\varphi(m'X)=b_\tau\varphi(X)$, including the complement of the support. In the finite Fourier sum the substitution $X\mapsto m'X$ simultaneously undoes the $m$-th power action on the pairing. Thus every class table satisfies
$$
                   \tau w_c(y)=b_\tau w_c(y).
$$
The factors are values of the original character on idèles supported at single primes. Hence $b_\tau\in F_*$ is a root of unity of order dividing $15$, independently of any nonvanishing entry. ## The normalized cover section

On a marked component the weight-zero section $c_\delta$ has value $c_0$ times the determinant character, with one root of unity $c_0$ for all markings. On the level torsion of the fixed CM elliptic curve, the action of $\tau$, under the CM identification, is multiplication by one unit. Compare one marked component with its conjugate. For any other pair the relative change of markings differs from the first by a rational unitary element and a lattice stabilizer whose determinant is $1$ modulo $M$. Indeed the determinant congruence holds on the selected half-ideal. The unitary norm-one identity gives it also on the conjugate half, and the two coprime halves multiply to $(M)$. The determinant character is trivial on the rational element and on this stabilizer. Taking the inverse of the determinant displacement in the convention of the cover formula, choose its idèle lift $s_{\rm cov}$. All geometric component values then satisfy
$$
 \operatorname{gal}_\tau c_\delta=a_\tau c_\delta,\qquad
 a_\tau=\frac{\tau c_0}{c_0}\,\xi(s_{\rm cov})\in F_*^\times .
$$
The scalar lies in the coefficient model by descent of the common ratio of the two sections. Both factors are roots of unity. The class volume and stabilizer orders are rational. For the remaining class scalar, express its determinant ideal using an integral numerator coprime to the bad primes. Its rational norm correction is fixed and the twisted prime factors transform by the character conjugation law. With identical tuples this fixes the scalar at each representative. Conjugating the class-section formula, linear in the table and harmonic polynomial, now shows that a section $f$ for class data $x$ yields the section $(a_\tau b_\tau)^{-1}\operatorname{gal}_\tau f$ for the coefficient-conjugated data. This uses chart independence for the harmonic jet and the pinned semilinear coefficient map. ## The flat-vector scalar cancels the table scalar

For the prescribed flat vectors the model has a single central translate $z$, an idèle $s$ independent of the choice of a normalized flat vector, and a scalar $B$ fixed by $\tau$. Its formula is
$$
 f_{\rm fl}=C\,\omega_t(z,1)v_t,\qquad
 C=B\,\xi(s)\prod_{P\in\mathcal D}G_P^{-1},\qquad
 G_P=\sum_{Y\in{\rm GL}_r(k_P)}
        \xi_P(\det Y)^{-1}\psi_P(\operatorname{tr}Y).
$$
Here $\psi_P$ is a nontrivial additive character, $\xi_P$ is nontrivial, and this is the unnormalized matrix sum. The construction actually chooses exponent $-1$, rank $r=\dim_E W$, and the same $s$ even when the normalized flat vector is chosen twice. The fixed $B$ includes the dilation and square-root norm factors in the pinned formula. [Flat-vector construction and its fixed factor][flat] · [Matrix sum convention][gauss]

All values of $\psi_P$ lie in $\mu_{p_d}$, since $k_P$ has additive exponent $p_d$. Accordingly $\tau\psi_P(x)=\psi_P(\bar m x)$ for the **same** $m$ as in the Fourier table. Substituting $Y'=\bar mY$ gives $\tau G_P=\xi_P(\bar m)^rG_P$. One can check both magnitude and coefficient units from Li and Hu's Theorem 2.1, with $U=I_r$, $q=|k_P|$, and their multiplicative character $\chi=\xi_P^{-1}$. In their convention
$$
 G_P=q^{\binom r2}g(\xi_P^{-1},\psi_P)^r,\qquad
 g(\chi,\psi)=\sum_{u\in k_P^\times}\chi(u)\psi(u).
$$
Character orthogonality gives $|g|^2=q$ for this nontrivial $\chi$. Therefore $G_P\overline{G_P}=q^{r^2}$. Both factors are algebraic integers, so $G_P$ and its inverse are units at every place away from $p_d$, in particular above $\ell$. This also permits any of the rational $q$-power normalizations at these places. Applying $\tau$ to the formula for $C$ gives the exact identity $C=b_\tau\,\tau C$. [Li and Hu, Theorem 2.1, p. 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1105.4513#page=5) · [Pinned norm and integrality calculation][norm]

Write $\gamma$ for the common analytic normalization root comparing the pinned row lift with the normal model. The comparison uses the same geometric chart and the same central target translate for both occurrences. On vacuum expansions the class-section calculation therefore contributes
$\gamma/(\tau(\gamma)a_\tau b_\tau)$. A central source translate replaces the definite class argument $h$ by $hz$ on both sides. Multiplying the flat tests by $C$ introduces exactly $b_\tau$ in the comparison. The resulting multiplier, in the convention “new expansion equals the multiplier times the coefficient-conjugate expansion”, is
$$
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 \rho_\tau=\frac{\gamma}{\tau(\gamma)a_\tau}
          =\frac{\gamma c_0}{\tau(\gamma c_0)}\,\xi(s_{\rm cov})^{-1}.}
$$
It works simultaneously for every permitted source translate, every pair of coefficient-conjugated harmonic vectors, and every definite class in this row and tuple. The marked finite sum defining the final flat test preserves it, since all its character weights are fixed and right translation by each marked lift preserves the expansion law. Each $\rho_\tau$ is an algebraic unit in its field of definition. The calculation establishes the normalized expansion law on the common geometric model under its pinned row hypotheses, including the chart comparison, the reference phase identity and the rank bound for the common reading. For the pass built with the [reference scale](FIRST_PASS_REFERENCE_PHASE.md), the final change to the integral readings is computed below. ## The scalar on the actual first-pass cover

For the normal model constructed in the pin one can evaluate both finite scalars on this first pass. The covering half ideal is the product over the **distinct** prime ideals of the quadratic host below $\mathcal D$. The active selector lies over a single such prime above $p_d$, so the constructed conductor is $p_d$ and the full level is $M=g_0g_1p_d$. Taking a representative of a tuple with the same selected set preserves this calculation. The component section in this model is explicitly chosen with value $\xi(\det(\gamma h))$ at a marking, hence $c_0=1$. [Half ideal and level][half] · [Product of distinct rational primes][pd] · [Normal model and its representative][rep] · [Choice of the component section][c0]

The small-field condition contains the quadratic ray field at $g_0g_1p_\delta p'_\delta$, hence at its divisor $M$. In particular the $M$-th roots of unity lie in the embedded small field. It follows that $m=1\bmod M$, all the class tables and local Gauss sums are fixed, and $b_\tau=1$. The same ray condition puts the Weber values of the principal CM curve at level $M$ in the small field. The Weber function separates points up to the automorphisms of the CM elliptic curve. On the cyclic $\mathcal O_K/M$-module of torsion, compare the conjugate marking at a module generator and adjust its CM isomorphism by that one curve automorphism. It then fixes every marked $M$-torsion point. This is the level-$M$ curve-fixing witness, in which one may choose the CM identification anew for each $\tau$. [Divisibility of ray fields][divideray] · [Roots of unity][root] · [Quadratic CM host and level-$M$ Weber criterion][weber] · [Torsion interpretation][act]

Use that witness in the marked-component comparison. Its torsion multiplier is $1$, so its factor with signature powers $s^p\bar s^{\,q}$ is also $1$. The theorem on markings gives a lattice stabilizer $u$ with $\det u\equiv1$ on the chosen half ideal. By the norm-one identity, $\det u\equiv1$ on its conjugate as well, and the product of the two is $(M)$. The cover character is trivial on $u$. The rational unitary factor also has trivial character, so the value of the normalized component section survives conjugation unchanged. Thus $a_\tau=1$, and the actual row multiplier on this chosen normal model is
$$
                         \rho_\tau=\gamma/\tau(\gamma).
$$
The same calculation applies at the reference tuple with $M=g_0g_1$ and no distinguished conductor. The choice of $\gamma$ precedes $\tau$, so multiplying these expansions by $\gamma^{-1}$ conjugates coefficients with multiplier $1$. ## From the class-sum comparison to an integral row map

Fix the comparison field $K_w$, its DVR $O_e(w)$ and the two embeddings $\iota,\epsilon$, with $\epsilon=\tau\iota$ and $\iota e_w$ equal to the old embedding. The [character and level calculation](FIRST_PASS_ACTIVE_CHARACTERS.md) identifies the two definite groups, forms and stabilizers. The old theta integral with its specified Haar measure on the definite quotient can be unfolded on those classes. The source and target level factors are inverse, so the integrand descends to each class. The old level and the coefficient level have relative index one. The flat test is a scalar multiple of a central translate of the vacuum. Solving this identity for the vacuum gives the unfolding factor $a_0$. The translated representatives still form a class system and have the same stabilizer sizes. This yields the old lift as $a_0$ times the class sum of the centrally translated input. Both $a_0$ and $a_0^{-1}$ are integral after inverting the tuple primes. The allowed denominator $N$ is the product of the distinct residue cardinalities on the tuple conductor. Here they are powers of $p_d$, hence $N$ is a unit at $w$. [Unfolding with the same measure and level](FIRST_PASS_THETA_SERIES.md) · [Translated class representatives and relative index][unfold] · [Flat scalar and its inverse][a0] · [Allowed denominator at the active place][N]

Let $\kappa$ be the harmonic-coordinate scale in the old definite coefficient model and $\Phi$ its map from polynomial coordinates into the harmonic value space. On that space the coefficient coordinate map is faithful. Conjugation in harmonic coordinates acts pointwise by
$$
 \Phi(v)\longmapsto \Phi\!\left(\frac{\tau\kappa}{\kappa}\,\tau_{\rm coeff}v\right).
$$
Here $\tau_{\rm coeff}$ applies $\tau$ to the polynomial coefficients. Dividing the original harmonic input by $\kappa$ before applying this formula accounts for the factor $\tau\kappa/\kappa$. It thus compares the coordinates used to define the two integral lattices. The inverse formula uses $\tau^{-1}$, so the comparison is onto in both directions. More explicitly, the old integral forms are the forms whose values on rational definite classes lie in the specified coefficient lattices. The integral polynomial criterion and the rational basis of the coefficient representation identify their extended span with the integral forms in the $\iota$-reading. The central translate preserves these functions and their integral span in both directions after inverting $N$. Coefficient conjugation carries that span onto the one in the $\epsilon$-reading. Thus division by $\kappa$, translation, and harmonic conjugation composed with the original realization give a semilinear map $\Psi$ carrying the old integral span onto the prescribed new span. Their injective coordinate identifications preserve independence of an old basis. [Integral harmonic coordinates and inverse][coordinates] · [Old integral coefficient criterion][intcrit] · [Recovering the rational form][recform] · [Translation and the two spans][shift]

The harmonic comparison of the analytic class sums is for $\kappa$ times each sum with the same underlying rational stabilizer weights. The old target evaluation on the tested vectors agrees with the old geometric lift. Indeed these inputs belong to the specified finite-group summand, on which the old projection acts as the identity in its tested reading. The old evaluation of a rational target form is its cusp reading with the indicated Lie and component factors. Thus the expansion law above applies to every expression $\sum a_k f_k$ allowed for the old image. At the target, the installed reading contributes the inverse of its chosen scale $\nu$. Restoring these factors in the coefficient relation gives the single multiplier
$$
                   z_1=(\nu/\kappa)\,\rho_\tau\,(\tau a_0)^{-1}.
$$
For an explicit check of direction, if the old relation before conjugation expands a $\kappa$-scaled class sum with coefficients $a_0^{-1}a_k$, the new $\kappa$-scaled sum has coefficients $\rho_\tau(\tau a_0)^{-1}\tau(a_k)$. Reading the new sum and the transported target expression at scale $\nu$ gives exactly the multiplier displayed. [Old target evaluation][oldeval] · [Comparison with a rational cusp reading][oldread] · [Conjugation of the $\kappa$-scaled finite sum][kappa] · [Rescaling in a tested relation][z1]

In the constructed real datum the prescribed row phase is $1$. The [choice of B1 scale](FIRST_PASS_REFERENCE_PHASE.md) gives $\nu^n=c^n$ for an admissible harmonic coefficient scale $c$ and some $n>0$, also on the branch that chooses a full rationality witness. Two harmonic coefficient scales differ by a root of unity. The maps in the two compatible coefficient frames differ by an embedded root of unity, and evaluation on one nonzero coefficient polynomial cancels their common value. Thus $\nu/\kappa$ is a root of unity. The same holds for $\rho_\tau$. This shows that $z_1$ and $z_1^{-1}$ are integral after inverting $N$. To identify the installed class sums in the tested relation, use the base-generator membership established at the chosen B1 scale. For the trivial variable twist it gives membership for every definite input by finite summation. The equality of the new theta reading with that sum extends to $K_w$ on pure tensors and then by additivity. These are the two consequences of the general row rationality premise used in the place comparison. [Original phase and scale comparison][oldphase] · [Ratio of harmonic scales][ratio] · [Class sums after coefficient extension][pure]

Lift $\Psi$ through the faithful source reading, using the integral spans and then extending from an old basis. For any tested relation let $A,B$ be the two target vectors, with $B$ the transported expansion. The faithful target reading gives $r(A)=z_1 r(B)$. If some $B\ne0$, comparison in a target basis whose readings are complex-linearly independent puts $z_1=\epsilon(t)$ for one $t\in K_w^\times$, valid in every tested relation. Integral closedness of $O_e(w)$ and invertibility of $N$ give $t,t^{-1}\in O_e(w)$. Rescale the lifted source map by $t^{-1}$ to obtain equality and keep its integral span. If all $B$ vanish, all $A$ vanish by faithfulness and the unit $1$ works. Choose the resulting map at each active place and row. Together with the character, level, embedding and rank data already constructed, this gives the **first-pass character record with exact integral theta comparisons**. The quantifiers give this record over the B1 and B2 constructed with our chosen scales. The more general statement over every independently supplied B2 still has its original stronger hypothesis.
