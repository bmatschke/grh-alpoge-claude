# The first alpha step above a definite triangle

Take an upper tower triple with $(p,q)=(1,0)$, ranks $a>n$ and $d=a+n$. There are adapted hyperbolic planes $H\subset V$, $H'\subset W$ and a lower triple $(V_1,V',W_1)$ of ranks $(a-2,n,d-2)$. Its three groups are definite at infinity. Write $J_b,J_s$ for the upper big and small integrals, $J_{b,1},J_{s,1}$ for the lower ones. Assume the compatible local matching maps for these two triples, the two lower nonvanishing tests and their definite comparison $J_{b,1}=c_{1,t}J_{s,1}$ on matched tests. Set $e=(a-n)/2$, $\nu_t=\alpha_t/\beta_t$, $\mathfrak b=H.\mathfrak b$ and $\mathfrak c=H'.\mathfrak b$. The row inequalities give $e>3$ and $n>3$. For the compatible upper normalization the first alpha recurrence is
$$
 c_t=C\,\nu_t(\mathfrak b)\nu_t(\mathfrak c)
       \prod_{v\in T_t}\epsilon_v(\nu_{t,\tau_v},1-e)\,
       L^{S_0}(e,\nu_t)\,c_{1,t},\qquad C\ne0.
$$
Here $J_b=c_tJ_s$ and $C$ is fixed before the tuple varies, for these real maps, evaluations, lattices and measures. [Rank, parity and infinity-weight hypotheses][a-range] · [Pinned orientation and nonzero boundary detector][a-key]

## Rank-one convergence and the two orbits

At the big source and target let $S,T\ge0$ denote the two logarithmic Siegel parameters, and put $D=[E:\mathbb Q]$. The Poisson bound for the squared theta kernel on fixed finite factors has exponent $D[2\max(S,T)+(d-2)S+(a-2)T]$, times a continuous squared Schwartz seminorm. The four corners give $S+T+|S-T|$, the other nonzero weights give the last two sums. The big-source density is $\ll e^{-D(a-1)S}dS$. After taking the square root, the integrand on $S\le T$ is bounded by a constant times $\exp(D(aT/2-eS))$. On $S\ge T$ it is bounded by $\exp(D(nT/2-(e-1)(S-T)))$. Thus $\int_{[U(V)]}|\theta|\ll \exp(DaT/2)$ on **all** big tests, with no local support restriction. Multiplying the squared bound by the target density gives the integrable tail $e^{-D(n-1)T}dT$. On the compact small source the analogous bound is $\ll e^{DnT/2}$. These estimates give continuity in real Schwartz seminorms and individual target $L^2$ for the actual lifts. In particular one can interchange the target unipotent average and the big-source integral. [Poisson weights and integration over compact factors](ROW_BETA_SQUARE_INTEGRABILITY.md) · [General rank estimates][a-bound]

The target parabolic stabilizes $EH'.x$. In the mixed theta sum the central average along its radical selects rational source vectors of zero norm. On the small source $V'$ definiteness leaves only zero. Its ordinary mixed evaluation $E'_0$ has the law $\alpha_t(l)|l|_E^{n/2}$ and gives
$$
 (J_s(\Phi'))_\alpha(m(1,h_1)h)=C_0\,J_{s,1}(E'_0\omega'(1,h)\Phi')(h_1).
$$
On the big source the zero vector gives ordinary evaluation for the pair $(V,W_1)$ with law $\beta_t(l)|l|_E^{a/2}$. This whole term vanishes. At a real place put $\kappa=m_\alpha-m_\beta>d$. The target $W_1$ is positive definite. On the single negative source row the unsplit Fock diagonal operator on a monomial has eigenvalue $-\deg_{\rm row}-(d-2)/2$. A source-invariant functional would require eigenvalue $+\kappa/2$, since the big source splitting has exponent $m_\beta-m_\alpha$. Thus it annihilates every monomial. For the integral this functional is continuous by the unrestricted source estimate with target rank $d-2$ and fixed target point. Source Haar invariance and density of Fock polynomials give vanishing for each finite test and at each target point, including after translating the original test. [Mixed zero-orbit identity][a-zero] · [Oscillator row diagonal][a-diag] · [Invariant continuous functional][a-van]

The big nonzero null vectors form one rational orbit by Witt extension. Use the vector $H.x$ and the mixed evaluation $E_A$ with scalar direction $H.x\otimes H'.y$. Unfold to its stabilizer. Passing to the line parabolic leaves the lower source $U(V_1)$, a compact $K\subset U(V)(\mathbb A_F)$ and a multiplicative idele variable $r$. The inverse parabolic modulus is $|r|_E^{1-a}$. Moving its Levi across the mixed evaluation contributes $(\beta_t/\alpha_t)(r)|r|_E^{(d-2)/2}$, with scalar argument $r^{-1}$. Changing variable to $l=r^{-1}$ gives exponent $a-1-(d-2)/2=e$ and character $\nu_t(l)$. Consequently, with the splitting and measures fixed,
$$
 Q_A\Phi=\int_K\int_{\mathbb A_E^\times} E_A\rho(lH.x\otimes H'.y)\omega(k,1)\Phi\,
                    \nu_t(l)|l|_E^e\,d^\times l\,dk,
 \quad
 (J_b(\Phi))_\alpha(m(1,h_1)h)=C_1J_{b,1}(Q_A\omega(1,h)\Phi)(h_1).
$$
The scalar-line slices are Schwartz. Absolute integration at finite unramified places costs $\prod_w(1-Nw^{-e})^{-1}$ off finitely many widths. At exceptional places the variable lies in a fractional lattice, and at infinity rapid decay and $e>0$ suffice. The lower source is compact. These facts justify the unfolding and the lower integration on the actual tests. For target dilation $m(t,h_1)$ the scalar argument becomes $l\bar t$, while the mixed evaluation contributes $\beta_t(t)|t|_E^{a/2}$. Changing the scalar variable and using $\nu_t(\bar t)=\nu_t(t)^{-1}$ leaves $\alpha_t(t)|t|_E^{a/2-e}=\alpha_t(t)|t|_E^{n/2}$. The constants $C_0,C_1$ are nonzero because they compare compatible evaluation and quotient Haar normalizations. ## Comparison of the alpha boundary maps

At $v\notin T_t$, the scalar-line width is
$$
 \mathfrak a=\mathfrak b\,\mathfrak D_E^{-1}\,\overline{\mathfrak c}^{-1}.
$$
Indeed the line of $H.x$ has lattice ideal $\mathfrak b$ and the line of $H'.y$ has ideal $\mathfrak D_E^{-1}\overline{\mathfrak c}^{-1}$ by the skew lattice duality. No conjugation acts on $l$ in the alpha shift. The vacuum evaluation is the lower vacuum inside this lattice, and vanishes off it by the Heisenberg commutator with the dual lattice. Writing $t_w=\nu_{t,w}(\varpi_w)Nw^{-e}$ and $m_w=\operatorname{ord}_w\mathfrak a$, the local cone scalar on the vacuum is $u_v=\prod_{w\mid v}t_w^{m_w}/(1-t_w)\ne0$. The local zero evaluation on the small vacuum has scalar one. The source establishes that the codifferent has trivial $\nu_t$ ideal value under the realized unramified CM and splitting assumptions. Conjugation inverts $\nu_t$, so $\nu_t(\mathfrak a)=\nu_t(\mathfrak b)\nu_t(\mathfrak c)$. The norm $N(\mathfrak a)^{-e}$ belongs to $C$. The Euler factors outside $T_t$ give $L^{S_0}(e,\nu_t)$ since at the additionally ramified places the primitive local factor is one. [Line lattices and all ideal signs][a-ideal] · [Euler product with the norm separated][a-fac]

At $v\in T_t$, write $Q=\#k_v$, $f>0$ for the exact conductor exponent, $\pi$ for the chosen uniformizer and $\chi=\nu_{t,\tau_v}$. The small zero evaluation of the standard matrix test integrates $n$ width-$\pi^f$ entries and contributes $Q^{-nf}$. In the big cone the same $n$ narrow entries remain after removing the two big rows and the two target columns. The free designated unit and its modulated companion give $1_{\mathcal O^\times}\chi^{-1}$ and its additive Fourier transform. The latter vanishes off $\pi^{-f}\mathcal O^\times$ because additive orthogonality kills deeper shells and exact conductor kills shallower shells. Unit integration on the remaining shell, including the additive measure $Q^{-f}$ of a residue class, gives the quotient
$$
 \epsilon_v(\chi,1-e)=Q^{f(e-1)}\chi(\pi)^f
     \sum_{u\in(\mathcal O/\pi^f)^\times}\chi^{-1}(\widetilde u)\psi_v(\widetilde u/\pi^f).
$$
Its sign parameter is $+1$. It is nonzero by Fourier injectivity and primitivity. Both lower standard tests have the same nonzero quotient image. [Marked alpha coordinates and the common volume][a-mark] · [Gauss convention][a-eps]

The real cone is nonzero on the required lower projection. Here is the localization that proves this assertion in the alpha polarization. Take a nonzero continuous scalar component $D_1$ of the lower compact source projection. It extends to Schwartz vectors by averaging that compact group and projecting to the finite lower type. Put $A_f(k,l)=D_1 E_A\rho(lH.x\otimes H'.y)\omega(k,1)f$. By surjectivity of mixed evaluation and an inverse Heisenberg translate choose $f$ with $A_f(1,1)\ne0$. After replacing multiplicative by additive measure the complex weight is a constant times $\prod_w(l_w/|l_w|)^{\kappa_w}|l_w|^{2(e-1)}$. If every cone were zero, the pushforward of this weighted $A_f(k,l)\,dk\,dl$ under $\pi(k,l)=(2l_w k_w^{-1}H.x_w)_w$ would have zero Fourier transform because every character multiplier of $\pi$ is induced by a Heisenberg translate in the complementary $H'.x$ direction. The density is integrable. Over $\pi(1,1)$ the source compact element stabilizes the line with $k_w H.x_w=l_wH.x_w$. The mixed law there contributes $(\beta_t/\alpha_t)(l)$, cancelling the phase of the weight, and all remaining factors are positive multiples of $A_f(1,1)$. The map $\pi$ is proper near this nonzero point because $K$ is compact and $k_w^{-1}H.x_w$ has norm bounded away from zero. After rotation a bump supported sufficiently near this point pairs strictly positively with the pushforward, contradicting Fourier uniqueness. Schwartz continuity and polynomial density give a nonzero polynomial witness. The real small evaluation is onto by extending lower polynomials with a value-one Gaussian in the extra coordinates. [Fourier localization, fiber identity and Heisenberg multiplier][a-bump]

For a polynomial input of bounded degree the real cone has polynomial output. Under the adapted Cartan splittings the lower tensor block is orthogonal to the eliminated variables for the positive oscillator metric. On a polynomial times the vacuum, the scalar Heisenberg shift and mixed evaluation leave a polynomial of bounded degree times the lower Gaussian, with coefficients depending on the scalar variable. The real compact source translates range in a finite-dimensional space of polynomials of bounded degree. Their scalar-line integration therefore takes values in the finite-dimensional closed lower polynomial space. [Adaptation and Cartan involutions](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/BlockTheory/DivisorBase/WeilTensorModels.lean#L532-L548) · [Polynomial slice and finite-dimensional integration](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/WeilBound/ThetaLifts/LocalTheory/ArchimedeanBumps/SlicePol.lean#L290-L332)

These witnesses give descent and parabolic covariance by the one-place argument in the [finite beta comparison](ROW_BETA_LOCAL_COMPARISON.md#finite-quotient-descent-for-the-beta-coefficient). Here both upper lifts converge on all local tests, so one can descend their boundary operators directly. The same proof works in the real slot using the invariant orthogonal complements on compact types in the smooth $L^2$ image. Freeze lower projected factors all nonzero, using the calculations above and the lower definite nonvanishing. Upper local kernel vectors give zero integrated theta function by local Howe duality and $L^2$ semisimplicity. The boundary formula and detection by the nonzero lower lift then force the boundary operator to kill each such vector. Comparing its values before and after a parabolic translate yields the common law on the quotients (infinitesimally and on the compact subgroup for polynomial vectors). At unmarked places compact invariance of the vacuum, Iwasawa and irreducibility extend the scalar from the vacuum to every input. At a marked place the parabolic multiplicity argument uses the unchanged $n$-row **small** matrix model on the two levels. On full-rank middle frames the quotient is irreducible by unitary parabolic induction. A ramified unit and source relations remove boundary terms, so middle-group Schur uniqueness extends the scalar computed on the standard test. At a real place set $s=(m_\beta+a-2)/2$, $u=(\kappa-d+2)/2$, $v=(\kappa+d-2)/2$. The compact generating type of the common upper module for $U(d-1)\times U(1)$ has positive weights $((s+u)^{[a-1]},s^{[n]})$ and negative weight $(m_\beta-a+2)/2+v$. These are the joint harmonic weights of the $(a-1,1)$ source character, with the target differential shifted by $m_\beta/2$. The nonzero highest vector generates the simple upper module. The compact lower target weight is $((s+u)^{[a-2]},s^{[n]})$, by the definite rectangular calculation. Its restriction multiplicity from $U(d-1)$ equals one by interlacing. The deleted positive coordinate has weight $s+u$. Adding the negative weight gives $m_\beta+u+v=m_\alpha$, exactly the diagonal compact circle character required by the alpha law. Thus maps on this compact generating type to the required lower type form a space of dimension one. Since $\mathfrak g=\mathfrak p+\mathfrak k$, the parabolic law determines a map everywhere from its values on the type, by ordering the enveloping algebra with parabolic factors on the left. This proves proportionality, and the two real witnesses make its scalar nonzero. For fixed real maps, the Weil actions on real-supported elements agree when the splitting characters have the same real exponents. The corresponding cone and ordinary evaluations therefore use the same real operators and measures across tuples. Evaluate them on a matched polynomial pair with nonzero lower projection to see that their proportionality scalar is common. The small lower source is compact, so absolute convergence and source invariance preserve its integral as well as its quotient class. Applying the lower comparison and the factor $C_1/C_0$ gives the proposed upper scalar on the alpha coefficient. Lift a nonzero lower small test by $E'_0$ and choose its matched upper big partner by surjectivity of the quotient maps. This pair detects the scalar. ## The cuspidal difference

For matched polynomials put $F=J_b(\Phi)-c_tJ_s(\Phi')$. The calculation on $m(1,h_1)$ applies after simultaneous compact target translation, which preserves matching. The alpha law, radical invariance and parabolic Iwasawa decomposition extend the identity to every target point. We already proved the two beta terms zero independently. The target has rational Witt index one. Witt extension makes all proper rational parabolics conjugate, hence $F$ is cuspidal. The kernel seminorm estimates, polynomial compact-finiteness and the usual cuspidal rapid-decay theorem give rapidity on target Siegel sets. [Pinned passage to a cusp form and orthogonality][a-end] · [Rapidity for polynomial theta differences][a-rapid]

The definite **small** image is orthogonal to every target cusp form. Apply Wu with $Y=V'$, $X=W_1$ and the actual splitting characters, written $\chi_1,\chi_2$ in Wu's determinant convention. The source $\mathbf1$ and its nonzero lower lift $\pi=\theta^X_{Y,\psi}(\mathbf1)$ are cuspidal since both groups are anisotropic. The latter is irreducible. Wu's return-lift identity gives $\theta^Y_{X,\psi^{-1}}(\chi_1^{-1}\pi)=\chi_2\mathbf1$. His going-up orthogonality one plane above $X$ therefore applies to $\theta^W_{Y,\psi}(\chi_2^{-1}\chi_2\mathbf1)$, precisely the upper lift on the small side. [Wu, Theorems 5.1(2), 5.4(1) and 5.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1409.0770#page=15)

For the indefinite **big** side take one rational prime $\ell$ completely split in $E$ outside the fixed data. At each $v\mid\ell$ the local component of $\nu_t$ at a uniformizer has infinite order by its nonzero infinity type and complete splitting. The [two-rectangle split computation](ROW_FIRST_BETA_STEP.md#the-good-place-kernel-on-both-rectangular-blocks) makes the upper projected class of each big finite test representable by a full-rank $a\times d$ test modulo source translates minus original tests. At the potentially middle row its sieve factor is $1-\nu_{t,w}(\varpi_v)^{\pm1}\ne0$. Choose one such replacement at a fresh split place. The unrestricted absolute convergence proved above and right source invariance leave its big integrated theta function unchanged.

For a resulting full-rank big test $\Psi$, consider $T_\Psi(g)=\int_{[U(W)]}F(h)\overline{\theta_{V,W}(\Psi;g,h)}\,dh$. At each $v\mid\ell$, the individual upper kernels show that $F$ admits a finite-sum locally equivariant realization through the **opposite**, $n$-row matrix space (the matching projections are onto). Pair its local test with the $a$-row test of the reverse kernel. Stacking the two rectangles gives the split right-equivariant distribution on $d\times d$ matrices. The lower-rank strata vanish by the non-real-positive uniformizer character, and on the open orbit the unique twisted Haar density transforms on the left by the same character. Restoring the oscillator determinant factors gives right invariance of $T_\Psi$ under the big source $\mathrm{GL}_a(F_v)$ for each $v\mid\ell$. This use of the split distribution exchanges the two row blocks and inverts the corresponding character when necessary. [Distribution on the open orbit and the rank strata](../theta/ROW_DEFINITE_REVERSE_LIFT.md) · [Application to either orientation][a-dist]

Use the rational torus scaling $H.x,H.y$ by $\ell^j,\ell^{-j}$ and fixing $V_1$. Its finite components away from $\ell$ range in a compact subgroup and fix $\Psi_f$ for $j$ in an arithmetic progression of positive multiples. Rational left invariance and the right invariances just proved give $T_\Psi(1)=T_\Psi(g_{j,\infty})$ there. At fixed $h$ a nonzero minor of the full-row-rank matrix in the big test gives arbitrary decay as $j\to\infty$ in this source cusp. For dominated convergence the same full-rank estimate with zero source exponent is uniform in $g$ with polynomial target height. Rapidity of $F$ makes its product integrable on the target. Thus $T_\Psi(1)=0$. Applying the argument to every source translate of $\Psi$ gives $T_\Psi(g)=0$, since the split rank is preserved. Fubini now gives $\langle J_b(\Psi),F\rangle=0$. This also holds on each original big test by the sieve replacement. Small and big orthogonality imply $\|F\|_2^2=0$, so continuity gives $F=0$ at every point. Finally the upper seminorm bounds pass this equality to the simultaneous polynomial approximants in the definition of a matched Schwartz pair. [Source cusp limit and pairing][a-cusp] · [Full-rank minor and compact-factor estimates](ROW_SQUARE_REGULARIZATION.md)

This proves the recurrence for this single tower removal with its specified matching maps. Its non-elementary inputs include the local Howe and Fock module identifications, the normalized mixed-model evaluations, the lower definite doubling nonvanishing and comparison, cuspidal rapid decay, the split distribution theorem and Wu's global theta identities. Changing the tower frame or the real matching maps requires transporting the scalar with those choices. Further tower removals change the ranks and the rational parabolics appearing in the cuspidality argument.
