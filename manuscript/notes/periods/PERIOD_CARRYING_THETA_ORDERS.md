# The carrying section on a nonterminal period layer

Fix a nonterminal component of one of the deep covers in the geometric approach to period entry 7. Write $n=k+m-1$ for its Hermitian rank and $g=fn$ for the abelian dimension. Its level contains $15^e$ with $e\ge4$ eventually. Both $R=3$ and $R=5$ therefore give level-$R$ theta products in the **same** weight, on the **same** cover. A product $f_{R,\mathbf a}$ has $n$ single theta-null factors with labels of order dividing $R^2$ in the full marking. Fix the tuple of labels modulo $R$, generating the marking module over $\mathcal O_E$ modulo $R$. The full set of lifts of these residues gives permitted generating tuples at level $R^4$: by Nakayama they still generate modulo $R^4$. In the analytic notation each argument is a label divided by $R^2$; varying a lift by a multiple of $R$ translates the argument by an arbitrary $R$-torsion point. All translations are independent in the $n$ factors. [Deep cover and its level][level] · [Single products and their point norms][ag]

## Exact order on a fixed semistable test

Let $V$ be an original integral framed trait with the indicated level on its generic fibre. Use its split Raynaud uniformization after finite extension; normalize $v$ on the original uniformizer. The principal polarization identifies the period lattice with the torus character lattice $X$. Let $Q$ be the source's positive definite cubical quadratic period order on $X$, with the zero-dimensional case allowed. The marked residues have period images $u_i\in X/RX$. If the residue characteristic is prime to $R$, then
$$
 \min_{\mathbf a\text{ lifting the fixed residues}} v(f_{R,\mathbf a})
   = R^{-4}\sum_{i=1}^n\min_{z\in X,\ z\equiv u_i\; (R)} Q(z).             \tag{1}
$$
The $Q$ in (1) uses the actual marked theta rigidification and its unshifted toric Fourier indices. This is the principal graph-line model in [the all-row construction](../geometry/PEL_ALL_FRAME_DENOMINATORS.md). For a general translated line the label cosets and the quadratic data have to be changed. With valuations normalized instead on a ramified extension, both sides scale by its ramification index.

Here are the Fourier and torsion details. In the principal degeneration the toric Fourier coefficients of the primitive theta are a single period orbit of the generator of the abelian quotient's principal section line. In integral frames the coefficient at $\chi$ has the factor of valuation $Q(\chi)$ times a translate of a primitive theta section on the abelian quotient. The coefficient at zero is primitive: in the standard period trivialization the cubical graph characteristic is trivial on the vanishing lattice, all other terms have positive order at the identity component, and the original primitive frame has nonzero reduction on this component. Equivalently, in Mumford's description take the primitive generator on the abelian part and all its translates. This gives a primitive rigidified section on the semiabelian model, so it and the original integral generator differ by a unit. The translates of the abelian generator, tensored with the corresponding integral degree-zero lines, have nonzero reductions as sections. [Alexeev–Nakamura, §3, especially the paragraph preceding Definition 3.2](https://doi.org/10.2748/tmj/1178224770) · [Source's frame valuation and coefficient normalization][ord]

Put $m_R=R^2$. Fix a single level label and choose a lift of its *evaluated* $m_R$-torsion argument in the Raynaud extension. Its $m_R$-multiple is a period $y\in X$, defined modulo $m_RX$. The lower bound for term $\chi$ is
$$
 Q(\chi)+B(y,\chi)/m_R+Q(y)/m_R^2
       =Q(y+m_R\chi)/m_R^2,
$$
where $B$ is the polarization of $Q$. The possible additional order is that of the translated abelian theta coefficient at its evaluation point. The torus monomial and rigidified torsion-frame scalar contribute the displayed orders exactly. This follows by taking the $m_R$-th power in the period pairing and the $2m_R^2$-th power in the symmetric torsion frame; an integral torus unit contributes order zero. The coefficient series converges, and $Q$ is coercive when $X\ne0$. Therefore the minimum for the coset $y+m_RX$ is a lower bound even at a residue characteristic dividing $R$.

To attain that minimum for a fixed period class, choose a minimizing $\chi_0$. On the abelian quotient over the algebraic closure of the residue field, the reduction of its normalized coefficient is a nonzero section of a translated principal line $L$. For every point $b$ of that abelian variety, some $s\in B_{\rm ab}[R]$ makes the coefficient nonzero at $b+s$. Indeed the translates by $B_{\rm ab}[R]$ of the $R$-th power of a nonzero section span $H^0(L^R)$. Their span is stable under the theta group over $B_{\rm ab}[R]=K(L^R)$, which acts irreducibly in its weight-one representation. The line $L^R$ is globally generated for $R\ge2$. These statements apply since $R$ is invertible and $L$ is a translate of a principal ample line. They include dimension zero. Lift $s$ to integral $R$-torsion of the Raynaud extension. Such lifts exist after finite extension: multiplication by $R$ is étale on the semiabelian scheme and the map on geometric $R$-torsion onto the abelian part is surjective, by surjectivity of $[R]$ on the torus. This does not change the period class or the fixed marking residue. [Mumford, *On the equations defining abelian varieties I*, §1](https://pazuki.perso.math.cnrs.fr/index_fichiers/Mumford66.pdf)

There can be several minimizing indices. Distinct minimizers $\chi,\chi'$ have distinct images in $X/RX$: if $\chi'-\chi=R\eta\ne0$ then $\chi+\eta$ lies strictly between them and the strictly convex quadratic has a smaller value there. In the leading terms, translation by $t\in T[R]$ multiplies term $\chi$ by $\chi(t)$ up to a common unit from the choice of fibre frame. Translation leaves all three displayed valuation terms unchanged. After dividing out the common minimum (enlarge the valuation field as needed), the leading residue is thus a finite Fourier sum on $T[R]$. Its coefficient at $\chi_0$ is nonzero by the abelian translation. Orthogonality over the residue field makes this Fourier sum nonzero for some $t$, since $|T[R]|$ is invertible and its characters are distinct. The realized evaluation attains the coset minimum.

It remains to vary $y$ modulo $R^2X$ while retaining its class $u$ modulo $RX$. All lifts are available. On geometric torsion $A[R]\to X/RX$ is surjective: divide any period by $R$ in the Raynaud extension over an algebraic closure and take its image in the quotient. Adding this torsion point to the evaluated argument changes its $R^2$-period label by $R$ times that period. Consequently one may first realize the coset containing a vector minimizing $Q$ among all $z\equiv u\pmod R$, then use the two integral translations to attain its order there. This proves (1) factor by factor. Full level supplies these same translated labels among the allowed numerator rules, even though a valuation field extension was used to compute their orders.

For every generating residue tuple, whether $R$ is invertible or not, one has
$$
              v(f_{R,\mathbf a})\ge \phi_{\rm fin}/R^4              \tag{2}
$$
on *every* lift. To see this choose a minimizer for each residue coset. They span $X\otimes\mathbf Q$ over $E$, by generation modulo $R$ and finite generation over $\mathbb Z$. The project element $w=(1+\sqrt{-d})/2$ has $w+\bar w=1$. On balanced symmetric forms $C$, $\sum_i C(wz_i,z_i)=\frac12\sum_i C(z_i,z_i)$ is an integral functional strictly positive on every nonzero positive semidefinite balanced form. Its value on $B$ is $\sum_i Q(z_i)$. The original definition of $\phi_{\rm fin}$ as an infimum gives (2), including at wild primes by the termwise lower bound. This is the level-$R$ version of B8.28a. At torus rank zero (1) gives **zero common order**. Thus the union of the level-three and level-five systems has an integral unit on *every* good-reduction test, even in residue characteristics three and five. Over each geometric interior point the same theta-group argument directly gives a nonvanishing product. For individual products the value can vanish. The minimizing product may change with the trait.

## Applying the same numerators to the arithmetic height

Use the notation of [B8.25g][bg]. Write $G(y)$ for the component Bergman supremum and $a_R=\|f_{R,\mathbf a}^2(y)\|$. The general odd-level theta estimate gives $\log a_R\le(\mathcal P_R-\Phi)/R^4$, where $\mathcal P_R=\mathcal P(E,n,Q_0,m,R,1)$. In the source's thin range $\Phi=\phi$ for the admissible parameters in its numerator estimate. Outside it $\Phi\ge0$. Its explicit expression gives $0\le\mathcal P_3\le\mathcal P_5=P_0$: the positive terms $R^4$, $R^4\log R$ and $A\log(AK_*)$, $A=R^4nd_*/2$, increase on $R\ge3$. In the thin range $G\ge e^{-\phi/625}$, and in the thick range $G\ge e^{-B'_0}$. It follows for both $R=3,5$ that
$$
 a_R^3\le e^{P_0+B'_0}G,\qquad a_R\le e^{P_0+B'_0} .
$$
Indeed $3/R^4\ge1/625$ and $3P_0/R^4\le P_0$. The same source radius and error allowance work at both levels. These conclusions use the indicated thin and thick Bergman estimates on the specified uniformized row. [General numerator and Gaussian bound][ag] · [Matched thin phase and parameters][thin]

Take one product nonzero generically on the component and the exact key and flat twists of B8.28. Then $\Theta=f_{R,\mathbf a}^{24}K_{\rm key}^{3n}S_Z$ is a section of weight $8(u_1-u')$ with the original squared point-norm bound. At a coefficient place $\lambda$, divide trait valuations by its relative ramification $e_0$. Its finite order satisfies
$$
    \operatorname{ord}(\Theta)\ \ge       24\phi_{\rm fin}/(R^4 e_0)-8e_\lambda
       \ \ge\ 8\phi_{\rm fin}/(2500e_0)-8e_\lambda,
$$
when the native key and flat twists carry their stated orders. Thus the required generic nonvanishing and the pointwise numerator comparisons in the carrying construction hold on these data. The lower bound inside the project needs only one fixed numerator per component, since (2) holds for every lift. Exactness of (1) determines the relative base order of the whole numerator system; it does not imply simultaneous attainment by a fixed section on different traits. [Weights, twists and correction in B8.28][carry]

## Finitely many tests for the remaining relative finite-place cost

On one supplied normal finite-type framed chart of the good cover restrict to a component met by the marked interior. Trivialize the weight lines in framed coordinates and use the actual complete lattice of weight $u_1$ cusp rules. Let $I$ be the fractional base ideal generated by the evaluations of an integral generating list of that lattice. The original finite depth $d_y$ at a trait $y$ on this component is $\min_i v_y(s_i)$, with $s_i$ the chosen generators evaluated there. We require that the selected framed charts lift every original trait after finite extension and that a transverse trait with interior generic point represents the original frame, as on the [all-frame models](../geometry/PEL_ALL_FRAME_DENOMINATORS.md). The arguments below apply likewise on the supplied integral cover charts when they have these identifications. Bergman positivity and the rational-to-complex identification for the complete series ensure that the cusp series has no base point on the characteristic-zero interior. Work with nonzero ideals on each component.

Normalize the blowup of $I$ (clear a common rational denominator first, which leaves the blowup unchanged). The fractional ideal becomes an invertible fractional ideal. For one fixed generically nonzero carrying section $\Theta$, form the rational section $\Theta I^{-8}$ there. Local generators can be chosen on a finite affine cover. The order of this expression at a prime divisor $D$ means $\operatorname{ord}_D(\Theta)-8\min_i\operatorname{ord}_D(s_i)$. First check all its horizontal divisors have nonnegative order; there are only finitely many possible poles. When this holds, the smallest **nonnegative** loss $c_\lambda$ valid simultaneously on every original trait over a fixed finite coefficient place is
$$
 c_\lambda=\max\!\left(0,\max_{D\mid\lambda}
 \frac{8\min_i\operatorname{ord}_D(s_i)-\operatorname{ord}_D(\Theta)}
      {8\operatorname{ord}_D(\varpi_\lambda)}\right).               \tag{3}
$$
Take the union over the finitely many charts needed for the component and the chosen marking lifts. The inequality it gives is $v_y(\Theta)/8\ge d_y-v_y(\varpi_\lambda)c_\lambda$ with $v_y$ on a trait uniformizer. In (3) the orders of the *actual* carrier and the *actual* cusp base ideal are taken at the same divisor. Distinct finite places with positive correction can occur only under finitely many divisors for a fixed member and section.

Indeed any rational $c\ge0$ at least the displayed maximum makes a suitable integral power of $\varpi_\lambda^{8c}\Theta I^{-8}$ regular on every normalized blowup chart at $\lambda$, by the codimension-one criterion. Original trait maps lift by properness; a generic marked point has a lift by surjectivity and passage to a finite field extension. Evaluating the resulting inequality proves sufficiency, with orders and ramification scaling together. Conversely, choose a closed point in a dense open of any vertical component $D$ at which its reduction and the total space are regular. Avoid all other vertical components, zeros and poles of the local units involved, and closures of complementary horizontal loci. These closed subsets are proper on $D$. There $\varpi_\lambda$ equals a unit times $\xi^{e_D}$ for a local equation $\xi$ of the reduced divisor. Cutting by complementary regular parameters and completing gives a transverse trait with finite residue field and interior generic point. Its orders of both $I$ and $\Theta$ are the prescribed divisorial ones. The marking and any finite extension preserve their normalized ratio. This proves necessity. [Blowing up a base ideal](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/01OF) · [Transverse traits and membership in the original class](../geometry/PEL_ALL_FRAME_DENOMINATORS.md)

The horizontal check in fact follows from the preceding archimedean carrying estimate when the complete cusp space, its comparison with analytic sections, and the toroidal framed metric are supplied on this cover. To see this choose a generic smooth complex point on any horizontal divisor of the normalized blowup and a transverse disk with punctured disk in the interior. Write $s_i=h b_i$ on this chart, with $h$ a meromorphic generator of the pulled-back base ideal and the $b_i$ regular, at least one a unit. Write $m_w(t)$ for the point norm of the local framed generator of weight $w$, so the norm of a section with framed reading $s_i(t)$ is $|s_i(t)|m_{u_1}(t)$. In the finite-dimensional complete space at this embedding, comparison of any coefficient norm in a rational basis with the fixed Petersson norm gives
$$
 G(t)\le C\,m_{u_1}(t)^2\max_i|s_i(t)|^2.
$$
Indeed the integral generators span the complex space and one may extract a basis; the inverse of its positive Gram matrix is a constant matrix. The constant here need only be finite for this one fixed component. The key and flat point norms are constant on the component. The carrying bound and tensor compatibility of the framed metrics give
$$
 |\Theta(t)/h(t)^8|\le C' m_{u'}(t)^8 .
$$
On such a disk $m_{u'}$ and its reciprocal grow at most as powers of $1+|\log |t||$. In the semiabelian period chart the positive abelian block stays nondegenerate, all eigenvalues of $\Im Z$ lie between a positive constant and $O(1+|\log|t||)$, and the primitive theta generator's squared $L^2$ norm is a holomorphic unit squared times $2^{-g/2}\det(\Im Z)^{-1/2}$. The full tangent eigenframe over the characteristic-zero disk is related by a holomorphic invertible matrix to the invariant semiabelian Lie frame; this matrix and its inverse are bounded on a smaller disk. Every Gram–Schmidt length and its inverse are consequently bounded by powers of $1+|\log |t||$, as are the metric factors of any fixed integer weight, including negative weights. In the abelian interior all factors extend smoothly. These are the period and frame comparisons of [the all-row proof](../geometry/PEL_ALL_FRAME_DENOMINATORS.md). A rational function with a pole on the divisor would grow as an inverse power of $|t|$ on the transverse disk, contradicting this polylogarithmic upper bound after any finite base change. Thus $\operatorname{ord}_D(\Theta)\ge8\operatorname{ord}_D(I)$ at every horizontal divisor under the stated comparisons. The finite formula only involves vertical divisors on these charts.

The normalized cost of these losses on that component is $[F':\mathbf Q]^{-1}\sum_\lambda c_\lambda\log N\lambda$, with $\varpi_\lambda$ a local uniformizer. Thus in the finite part of the multiplication argument one can directly bound this sum for the actual carrier in place of bounding a separate supremum over all degenerations for the cusp space and subtracting an unrelated lower bound for the carrier. Thus (3) is an exact finite-divisor formula under the stated chart, valuation and metric identifications. The degree-weighted arithmetic intersection in B8.26, the full target-section lower bound or an estimate of the actual relative costs uniform along the family, and the remaining level and metric comparisons still enter the geometric covolume route. This calculation alone yields no bound for the raw two-run switch and does not complete period entry 7.
