# The common family and the nine-clause period theorem

The two conclusions proved here are the design-existence theorem for each admitted host request and the original conditional period-and-height theorem on the resulting class. The latter has nine clauses. Its seventh clause is the absolute upper bound for the sum of a dual row slope and a word height. The other eight were established at their selected-family uses in the preceding chapters. We use the standing geometric, automorphic and Galois theorems in their source forms. The RH Lean Roadmap supplies proofs and declaration references for these results, including the parts specific to the Alpöge–Claude construction. Kaiwen Jin gives a second treatment of the arithmetic volume and finite indices. The argument below specifies which estimates hold before making a choice, how their constants are chosen, and where they apply after that choice. ## The reference covolume for all admissible members

Fix an eligible family and height parameter in the covolume domain. For each admissible point and interior layer take the good cover of level $g_0g_1 15^e$ supplied for that point, with the original component flags, coefficient field and measures. Its existence, the bounds for its level and field, and the flat degree are uniform by B8.25a–c and B8.24a. On each component use the lower-volume estimate for the fitted weight on its own marked root tower. Apply the upper-volume estimate in the image of the integral generating sections with its measured metric. The two sides compare the *same* mixed-integral cusp sections in degrees divisible by eight, by multiplication by the nonzero carrying section and a balanced coefficient scalar. Normalization gives the extension and leaves the leading arithmetic volume unchanged. The vertical test uses horizontal arithmetic curves with finite residue field, and the archimedean norm uses the Petersson Gram in rank coordinates. These give the section comparison on the required domain, including at a bad prime of the level. With $E_p=B_0\log\log x$, B8.28 thus gives the component height inequalities with the prescribed $O(E_p)$ budget after rescaling by the theta power. B8.29 converts their mean to the core type slope, using the finite-level descent and the short sections in the type subspace. B8.30–31 remove the bad eigenspaces using the orthogonal projector. To see the uniformity explicitly, its cleared denominator is $|G|$, its lost rank fraction is at most $3\delta$, and $\delta=\exp(-x^{(\varepsilon/J)/7})$. For fixed family exponents $A,B$ the truncation cost is at most
$$
 12\delta(x^A+x^B+C_1+C_2+1) E_p\le E_p
$$
beyond one cutoff for every point and layer. Here $C_1$ is the core measure correction and $C_2$ the core slope constant, chosen to cover the terminal definite lattice as well. Comparing the good lattice with the original reference Gram therefore gives
$$
 {\operatorname{lcv}(M_j)\over D}\le M_{p,j-1}+(C_1+C_2+1)E_p.
 \tag{1}
$$
The covolume and the slope both use squared norms. In the positive-rank application $D$ is the reference rank. B8.24 specifies its convention for division by zero. The connected period family uses positive reference rank. Since $E_p\ge0$ eventually, the same cutoff works for every larger error constant. This proves exactly the geometric input of B8.24 before selecting the family. ## Why a complex spanning identity descends to the comparison field

Fix one layer, its reference tuple, the comparison field $K\subset\mathbb C$, and the space $C_K$ of algebraic cusp sections of the given weight and degree. Write $E(s)(g)$ for the value of $s$ in the comparison frame assigned to the adelic point $g$. The geometric inputs are the base-change law for sections, uniformization of complex framed points up to a Borel change of frame, and the coverage of each frame domain by arithmetic and Borel translates of the adelic comparison frames. Under precisely these hypotheses, $K$-linearly independent sections have $\mathbb C$-linearly independent functions $E(s)$. The earlier reconstruction proves this descent and applies it to the geometric class sums on the reference tuple. [Existing descent proof](../../../archive/rounds/v73/continuation_v73/kernel.tex#L108-L149) · [Application at the reference tuple](../../../archive/rounds/v76/continuation_v76/base.tex#L119-L149) · [Roadmap B8.55b](https://github.com/rh-lean-roadmap/rh-lean-roadmap/blob/c7755c1086f05d0918ee8496d95912e4f35e22f9/docs/src/book/main.tex#L59013-L59043)

To see the descent directly, suppose $\sum_i z_i E(s_i)=0$. A change from a comparison frame to any other frame in its orbit transforms every term by the same nonzero weight factor. The relation therefore holds throughout the frame domains, and all frame functions are defined to be zero outside them. Take any framed test point $y$ over a reduced finitely generated $K$-algebra $A$. Put $a_i=s_i(A,y)\in A$. For each complex specialization $\psi:A\to\mathbb C$ over $K$, base change and uniformization express $\psi(a_i)$ as a common scalar times the value in one frame. Consequently $\sum_i z_i\psi(a_i)=0$ at every complex point. The algebra $A\otimes_K\mathbb C$ is reduced because $K$ is perfect. Its complex points detect zero by the Nullstellensatz, so $\sum_i a_i\otimes z_i=0$. [Frame change and common scalar](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/UnitaryExistence/TestPointValueEqConstMulReading.lean#L20-L44) · [Adelic orbit coverage and zero-extension in the formal proof](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/ClassSumsSpan.lean#L165-L195)

Choose a $K$-basis $v_1,\ldots,v_t$ of the span of the finitely many $z_i$ and write $z_i=\sum_j c_{ij}v_j$. Independence of the $v_j$ gives $\sum_i c_{ij}s_i(A,y)=0$ for every $j$. These same coefficients work on every test. Hence $\sum_i c_{ij}s_i=0$ as section rules, forcing all $c_{ij}$ to vanish when the $s_i$ are independent. Finally, if $E(s)$ lies in the complex span of the evaluations of the allowed reference class sums, choose a finite generating list for this relation and a $K$-basis of its span from the list. Were $s$ outside that $K$-span, adjoining it would leave an independent family whose image is dependent. Thus the class sums span over the original comparison field.
