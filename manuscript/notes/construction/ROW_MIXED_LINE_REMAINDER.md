# The pure alpha line in the first mixed square

Let the upper source ranks be $a,n$, the defects $(p,q)=(1,1)$, and $b=a-2$. In the grid range $b>n$. At a fixed tuple write $\alpha,\beta$ for its unitary splitting characters and $|\cdot|_E$ for the adelic norm. Suppose that matching the **beta** coefficient has already made the difference $f$ of the two polynomial theta integrals have pure alpha line constant term. The hypothesis includes vanishing of the beta coefficient as a function on the target, hence after all right translations. The small polynomial test is primed. The claim here is that the *whole line constant term of $f$* vanishes. This is an assertion about one class of parabolics in a rational Witt-index-two group. [Definitions and exact quantifiers][spec] · [Pure-alpha induction in the pin][ind]

**Use the other plane removal.** The first beta comparison used ranks $(a,n-2)$. To dispose of the remaining character, remove a plane from the **big** source and one from the target. This gives ranks $(b,n)$ and defects $(0,1)$. Witt extension carries the initial rational isotropic line to this new adapted line $L$. Conjugation carries the unipotent quotient with its probability measure and identifies the $\mathrm{GL}_1(E)$ action on the line, preserving its character. A common right translate permits the required change of Cartan marking. Write $F_1=\mathrm{CT}_L f$, which has law $\alpha(s)|s|_E^{n/2}$. Let $j:U(W_1)\to U(W)$ be the complementary Levi embedding. For every target compact component $k$, the alpha restriction
$$
 f_k(z)=F_1(j(z)k)
$$
belongs to the lower space of differences of a big polynomial theta lift and a primed small polynomial theta lift. These lower tests need not match. On the big side the cone of nonzero isotropic vectors gives the lower big polynomial test and on the small side ordinary zero evaluation gives the lower small test. Primed replacement before evaluation handles possible loss of full rank. The other two orbit coefficients have the distinct beta law. This uses the rank-one relative orbit formulas on these actual spaces, stability under compact translation and polynomiality of the cone. No normalized lower scalar or comparison of matched lower tests occurs in this restriction. [Alpha restriction and its analytic inputs][low] · [Polynomial cone][cone] · [Change of marking][remark]

Fix an adapted rational isotropic line $M$ in $W_1$. Its constant term on the lower theta span splits into the two laws
$$
 A(s)=\alpha(s)|s|_E^{n/2},\qquad B(s)=\beta(s)|s|_E^{b/2}.
$$
For $x\in U(W)(\mathbf A_F)$, consider $z\mapsto F_1(j(z)x)$. Iwasawa writes $x=u\,m(s,1)j(y)k$. Unipotent invariance of $F_1$ and its first line law identify this function with $A(s)\,f_k(zy)$, so it has the same two-law decomposition. Let $A_x,B_x$ be the two contributions of this second constant term at the identity and put $C(x)=A_x+B_x$. They are uniquely determined by their two distinct absolute exponents. The **same second line** works for every $x$, including as the lower theta vectors vary. [Lower splitting][split] · [Iwasawa extension to all translates][two1]

Take the idele $u$ with infinite components $2$ and finite components $1$. Thus $r=|u|_E>1$. In the Levi of $L\subset L+M$ act on the two isotropic lines by $u,u^{-1}$, respectively, and on the dual plane by the contragredient. Denote the resulting element by $D$. Then
$$
 A_{Dx}=A_x,\quad B_{Dx}=\lambda B_x,\qquad
 \lambda=\alpha(u)\beta(u^{-1})r^{(n-b)/2},\quad |\lambda|<1.
 \tag{1}
$$
For the first component the two alpha characters and their powers of the norm cancel. For the second the two different ranks leave the strict negative exponent. In particular $(T_D-1)(T_D-\lambda)C=0$, where $T_D C(x)=C(Dx)$. The recurrence by itself admits the $\lambda$-term. The automorphic argument below is what excludes it. [Exact dilation, exponent and recurrence][two2]

**A contracting exponent in an adelic $\mathrm{SL}_2$ period.** Put $U=L+M$. The two successive line radicals generate the unipotent radical of the flag $L\subset U$. This flag radical can also be integrated first over the radical $N_U$ of the plane parabolic and then over the upper unipotent of $\mathrm{SL}_2$ acting on $U$. In block matrices the two quotients of the flag radical are respectively the line radical of $U(W_1)$ and the single additive entry that sends a generator of $M$ to itself plus a multiple of a generator of $L$. The splittings and quotient maps are rational. Fubini on these compact adelic unipotent quotients, with quotient probability measures, identifies the two orders of integration. Thus for any fixed $x$, along the embedded $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbf A_E)$ the function $C(gx)$ is the upper-unipotent constant term $c(g)$ of $\varphi(g)=(\mathrm{CT}_{N_U}f)(gx)$. This use of the plane average requires no vanishing statement about that average. The rational $\mathrm{SL}_2(E)$ normalizes the plane radical and preserves its normalized quotient measure. Consequently $\varphi$ is continuous, left rational-invariant and of moderate growth (compact unipotent averaging preserves the height bound). The dilation becomes $\delta=\operatorname{diag}(u,u^{-1})$. [Two orders of integration][orders] · [Restriction, invariance and growth][sl]

Adelic reduction for $\mathrm{SL}_2/E$ gives a compact $\Omega$ such that every rational class is represented by $\delta^j\kappa$, $j\ge0,\ \kappa\in\Omega$. Moderate growth gives $|\varphi(\delta^j\kappa)|\ll_\Omega 2^{mj}$ for an integer $m\ge0$, also after any fixed compact enlargement. The recurrence implies
$$
 c(\delta^j g)=c(g)+(1+\lambda+\cdots+\lambda^{j-1})[c(\delta g)-c(g)] .
$$
The forward ray values of $c$ are uniformly bounded on each compact set of $g$'s. We first prove $c(\delta g)=c(g)$ when $m=0$. By reduction $\varphi$ is bounded everywhere, which bounds its average $c$. Iterating the difference recurrence starting at $\delta^{-j}g$ bounds $|c(\delta g)-c(g)|$ by $2\|c\|_\infty|\lambda|^j$, which tends to zero. [Rank-one Siegel set][reduction] · [Borel, §4, adelic fundamental sets](https://www.numdam.org/item/PMIHES_1963__16__5_0.pdf) · [Bounded-growth argument][damp]

For $m>0$, choose a nonnegative right-convolution bump supported in an arbitrarily small neighborhood of $1$, smooth at infinity and locally constant at finite places, and normalize it to have integral one. Convolution commutes with the averaging and the recurrence. To compare $(\varphi*\eta)(\delta^j\kappa)$ with its upper-unipotent average, represent each $z\in E\backslash\mathbf A_E$ with finite parts in a sufficiently small open lattice and archimedean parts in a bounded set using additive approximation and compactness. Moving $n(z)$ past $\delta^j$ shrinks its real coordinates by $2^{-2j}$. The finite parts can be made invisible to the bump uniformly for $\kappa$ in a fixed compact set (the finite parts of $\delta$ are one). On the compact enlargement of its support, the resulting difference of translated bumps is $O_\Omega(2^{-j})$ by the real Lipschitz bound. Multiplying by the $O(2^{mj})$ growth of $\varphi$ and integrating shows that the smoothed function differs from its bounded smoothed constant term there by $O_\Omega(2^{(m-1)j})$. Its growth exponent has dropped by one. Induction gives $(c*\eta)(\delta g)=(c*\eta)(g)$. Shrink the support to $1$ and use continuity to obtain $c(\delta g)=c(g)$. These estimates also work for compact-open finite factors when the initial function is only continuous. [Convolution estimate][smooth] · [Induction and passage back to the original function][damp]

Apply this with $g=1$ for every $x$. By (1),
$(\lambda-1)B_x=C(Dx)-C(x)=0$. Consequently the second constant term of every $f_k$ has pure alpha law on the whole lower target, by taking $x=j(y)k$ for each $y$. On the lower $(0,1)$ theta span, the first-beta rank-one proof applies to **arbitrary differences** in this situation. Its alpha coefficient vanishes separately for each lift by definiteness of the big source and the forbidden real weight in the small zero term. The pure-alpha law therefore makes $f_k$ cuspidal on the lower Witt-index-one target. The rank-one orthogonality to its two theta summands makes its Petersson norm zero, hence $f_k=0$. Upper parabolic Iwasawa and the first alpha law now give $F_1(u'm(s,1)j(y)k)=A(s) f_k(y)=0$ everywhere. Thus the *line* contribution of the mixed difference vanishes. [Last step of the two-eigenvalue argument][two3] · [Lower pure-alpha vanishing and upper Iwasawa implication][finish] · [Rank-one theta orthogonality](ROW_FIRST_BETA_STEP.md#orthogonality-in-the-first-beta-step)

The hypotheses used for this conclusion are the initial pure line law, automorphy with moderate growth, the lower two-orbit splitting and alpha restriction for all compact translates, and the rank-one vanishing just described. The contraction proof is independent of the mixed square identity, a classification of the mixed automorphic spectrum, and the cuspidality of the upper difference. To deduce the full identity on the mixed target, its plane-parabolic constant term and the final orthogonality must also be handled.
