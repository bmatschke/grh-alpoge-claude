# The matrix kernel and the shifted period lattice at the word prime

Fix a word $u$, its level prime $p=T_u=\varpi\bar\varpi$ in the imaginary quadratic field, and a coefficient place $w\mid p$. Write $f=[E^+:\mathbb Q]$, $a=N_i$, $b=N_{i+1}$ and $s=(b-a)/2$. The word prime splits completely in $E$. There are $f$ selected primes $P\mid\varpi$, their completions have uniformizer $p$ and residue field $\mathbb F_p$, and the fixed row primes are away from $p$. Keep the constructed member's rational jet operators, residue markings, class coordinates and period normalization. On these inputs the seen half of the split matrix kernel has a Fourier denominator $p^{fa}$ and the unseen half has none. The valuations below are exact for the indicated scalar factors. Sums of jets may have higher valuation. [Word primes and degree-one places][split] · [The point kernel and its weights][point]

## From elliptic moments to the normalized matrix section

Use the [integral elliptic measure in the prescribed ordinary coordinate](PERIOD_LEVEL_PRIME.md). A change by an integral $p$-adic matrix in the direction list preserves the norm bound: on Mahler transforms it pushes the measure forward by the transposed change of variables. Multiplication of transforms takes a product measure followed by addition of its arguments. Both operations preserve a bound of one, uniformly in the number of directions and orders of differentiation.

Here is how the four-copy normalization in the constructed column enters this transfer. Let $P_1$ be the one-entry period lattice, let $\Lambda$ be its elliptic ideal lattice, and put $P=\Lambda^{4f}$. Diagonalize the positive $K_d$-Hermitian trace form. For any diagonal coefficient $A/B>0$ the four-square representation of $AB$ gives a rational matrix $C$ with $C^tC=(A/B)I_4$ by dividing quaternionic left multiplication by $B$. This gives the fixed isometry $g_0$ on four copies. The trace-dual property of the half-different makes $P_1$ self-dual for the imaginary part of the form. Match $g_0 P_1^4$ to $P$ above $p$ by a local unitary basis change on the split half and the inverse adjoint change on its conjugate. The matching condition is open. The Cayley chart and rational approximation give a rational unitary $u_0$ with this property and no eigenvalue one. Put $g=u_0g_0$, $Q=gP_1^4$ and $J=[Q:P\cap Q]$. Then $p\nmid J$. These are the isometries and local indices in the original normalization. [Project construction of the four copies and local matching](period_arithmetic/main.tex#L98-L308) · [Normalization of the one-entry theta][fourinit]

For commensurable self-dual lattices the normalized move $I_{P,Q}$ is $J^{-1/2}$ times the sum of the Weyl translations indexed by $Q/(P\cap Q)$. The inverse move undoes it by character orthogonality. One must account for its scalar relative to the unitary pullback when replacing $g_0$ by $g$. For clarity, on the lattice $L=g_0P_1^4$ put $A=1-u_0^{-1}$, $D=\det A$. Integrating the Gaussian kernel on a common period domain gives the scalar $(|D|/D)G$. Here $G$ is the normalized Gauss sum on $L/A(L\cap u_0L)$ with polar character $\exp(-2\pi i\,\mathrm{Im}\,H(A^{-1}x,y))$. Its radical is zero: pairing integrally with $L$ forces $A^{-1}x\in L$ and $u_0^{-1}A^{-1}x=A^{-1}x-x\in L$. The same calculation for the composition of the two moves gives another normalized finite quadratic Gauss sum after quotienting by its radical. These Gauss factors have eighth power one. Since $A^*=-u_0A$ and the dimension $4f$ is even, the eighth power of the combined scalar is $(\det u_0)^4$. The original finite product correction $\kappa_0$ was chosen so that $|\jmath(\kappa_0)|_p^2|\jmath(\det u_0)|_p=1$. Hence the resulting scalar $e$ in $\Theta^{\otimes4}=e\,(I_{P,Q}\theta_\Lambda^{\otimes4f})\circ g$ is a $p$-unit, including when $g_0$ itself has a denominator at $p$. [Gaussian integral and finite phases][gauss] · [Strömberg, §3][strom]

For a prime-to-$p$ translate $t$ of $\Theta$, put $v=g(t,t,t,t)$. The fourth tensor power of its translate is the sum over $x\in Q/(P\cap Q)$ of products of elliptic translates at the coordinates of $v+x$. Each coefficient is $e/\sqrt J$ times a root of unity from the Weyl law. If $nt$ is a period and $p\nmid n$, then $nJ(v+x)\in P$; multiplication by $J$ likewise puts every transformed period direction in $P$. Undo that multiplication by the integral substitution $1/J\in\mathbb Z_p$. All elliptic moments on the right are integral. Use independent variables in the four copies and take the coefficient of $(\nu,\nu,\nu,\nu)$: it is exactly $A_\nu(U_t\Theta)^4$. Thus the one-entry moment is integral. Products give the standard matrix moments. [Four independent blocks][fourblocks]

For self-dual endpoints $N,N'$ the prescribed matrix section multiplies the two successive normalized lattice moves by $\chi(\det(L_0,N))^{b/2}\chi(\det(L_0',N'))^{-a/2}$. We use the endpoint laws in B4.22c–e. In the finite comparison underlying these laws both endpoint lattices possess common complementary isotropic halves. At split places they are the two field factors. At unramified inert places the residue characteristic is odd, the even-dimensional unimodular spaces are hyperbolic, and a common apartment for the two self-dual lattices supplies a Witt basis. In the finite Schrödinger model the move between halves $A_0,D_0$ takes $\mathbf1_{A_0}$ to $([A_0:A_0\cap D_0]/[D_0:A_0\cap D_0])^{1/2}\mathbf1_{D_0}$ by summing characters first. The relative volume ratios multiply, so source and target moves commute. For a change at a single endpoint the unitary pullback differs by the endpoint determinant to the prescribed half rank. The finite Gauss factors on one row or column have square one: an integral scalar of norm $-1$ modulo a common multiple of four times the finite phase orders permutes each quadratic module and conjugates its phase. Such a scalar exists by the split or unramified norm map and the Chinese remainder theorem, including the split dyadic places. The even number of repeated rows or columns cancels that sign. These computations give the determinant factors in the prescribed section. [Endpoint determinants and finite norm calculation][endpoints] · [Commutation on an apartment][apartment] · [Bruhat–Tits, §§2.8–2.12][bt]

Move the two endpoints to agree with the standard lattices at $p$ by the split basis and Cayley argument. Both move indices are now prime to $p$. For a norm-one determinant ideal $\mathfrak a$ supported away from $p$, choose $h$ with $\mathfrak a^h=(z)$. Then $\mathfrak a^{2h}=(z/\bar z)$, so the principal-ideal formula for $\chi$ shows that $\chi(\mathfrak a)$ is a $p$-unit. Expanding the two averages therefore introduces only prime-to-$p$ translates and $p$-integral coefficients. Transporting back with the endpoint laws proves the same moment bound for every actual matrix section, including the companion endpoint used below. [Normalized matrix section and moved directions][movemom]

## How much of the level index survives?

Write $M=(\mathfrak c\mathcal O_E)\mathfrak b^{-1}\operatorname{Hom}(N,N')$ for the integral period module and $B_0$ for the half-lattice obtained from $M$ by multiplying its selected local components by $pg$ and its conjugate components by $(pg)^{-1}$. Here $g$ and the denominator $c_A$ are supported on row primes. The opposite half-lattice is $A(pg)$. In the class kernel the zero-coset Fourier coefficient is $1/n$, with
$$
 n=[M\cap A(pg):M\cap A(pg)\cap B_0].
$$
Only the selected $P$ contribute its $p$-part. There the quotient is $M_P/pM_P$, of dimension $ab$ over $\mathbb F_p$. At a conjugate place $M\cap A(pg)$ is already contained in $B_0$. Hence $v_p(n)=fab$. This count is independent of the exponents of $g$ and $c_A$. [Coset indicator and its Fourier normalization][coset]

At a seen place replace $N'_P$ by $\ker(l'_P)$ and $N'_{\bar P}$ by its Hermitian dual. Denote the self-dual result by $N''$, its period module by $M''$, and put $I=M\cap M''$. On $P$, after choosing a basis reading $l'_P$ as the first coordinate mod $p$, $I_P$ consists of the $b$-by-$a$ integral matrices whose first row vanishes modulo $p$. On $\bar P$, $I_{\bar P}=M_{\bar P}$. The surjectivity of every actual marking and localization of the full Hom lattice now give
$$
 [M:I]=[M'':I]=p^{fa},\qquad [I:\varpi M]=p^{fa(b-1)} .
$$
For the second index use the chain $\varpi M\subset I\subset M$. For the first equality of indices use either duality of the two relative quotients or the $a$ independent enlarged entries at each conjugate place. All these quotients are supported above $p$. Thus the exponent $\ell$ of B4.22h equals $fa$ on these marked pairs. The local conditions at the row primes leave this equality intact. [Definition and local shape of the companion][comp]

To see which factor survives the character average, write $J_0$ for the image of $c_Ag\bar\varpi I$ modulo $pg\,\operatorname{Hom}_S(N,N')$. Its kernel in $I$ is $\varpi M$, since the multiplier is a unit at every selected $P$, while at row primes the multiplier already supplies the period $g$. Thus $|J_0|=p^{fa(b-1)}$. Translation by these elements leaves the row test and its residue marking unchanged. Summing their characters cuts $B_0/(M\cap B_0)$ down at $p$ to the annihilator of $I_P$ on the conjugate half. This is $M''_{\bar P}/M_{\bar P}$, giving all $p^{fa}$ classes of $M''/I$. Away from $p$ the surviving translations are prime-to-$p$. For fixed such translation the remaining sum is the full companion move. It contributes $\sqrt{p^{fa}}\,\kappa$ with $\kappa=\chi(\det(N',N''))^{a/2}$. The determinant ideal is $(\varpi/\bar\varpi)$, so $|\jmath(\kappa)|_p=p^{fa/2}$ on the seen half. Consequently the scalar before the companion moments has exact valuation
$$
 v_p\!\left((|J_0|/n)\sqrt{p^{fa}}\,\jmath(\kappa)\right)=-fa.
$$
Here $v_p(p)=1$ and any choice of the square root has the same valuation. [Annihilator and complete companion average][ann] · [Determinant character and scalar][compchar]

On the unseen half the $p$-primary translations are represented by the image of $(\varpi/\bar\varpi)M$ modulo $M$, of order $p^{fab}$. Their character sum at a matrix $x$ is zero unless $x$ agrees with the specified residue coset at every selected $P$. In the surviving case the sum is $p^{fab}$, cancelling exactly the $p$-part of $n$. This orthogonality must apply to one measure for every character in the finite sum. Integrality of the separate translated jets alone would still allow the full denominator $p^{fab}$. [Unseen-half Fourier calculation][unseen]

For that common measure, normalize the elliptic ordinary coordinate so that connected $p$-power translation by $y$ acts on $1+Z$ by multiplication by its period-pairing root of unity $\zeta_y$. The normalization uses the perfect split Weil pairing and the primitive complementary component of the period $1$. One must also check the scalar on the theta section. For the principal even section $f$ in this coordinate, compare $S_{\zeta_y} f$ to the transform of $W_y\theta$. With $a=\jmath(\alpha)$ for a CM endomorphism $\alpha$ congruent to $1,0$ at the two primes modulo a sufficiently high power of $p$, put $m=\alpha\bar\alpha$. The quotient $\theta(\alpha z)/\theta(z)^m$ is elliptic: on the principal lattice the semicharacter is $x\mapsto(-1)^{x\bar x}$, so multiplication by $\alpha$ raises it to the power $m$. Its translated expansion cancels from the two functional equations. The ratio $R$ then satisfies $R([a]Z)=R(Z)^m$. Its constant $c$ is nonzero, $c^{m-1}=1$, and a first nonconstant coefficient in degree $j$ would require $a^j=m$, impossible since $a$ is a unit and $p\mid m$. Varying $\alpha$ a second time to make $m$ divisible by the first $m-1$ forces $c=1$. The prime-to-$p$ translates follow by applying the formal group translation to the quotient of translated and untranslated sections after clearing the prime-to-$p$ denominator. [Principal connected translation and its normalization][translation]

In orthogonal period directions integral at both halves, the matrix section is a finite sum of products of translates of powers of the principal elliptic section. Indeed one chooses vectors of unit length modulo $p$ successively using the two split bases and clears the remaining prime-to-$p$ denominators. On each resulting elliptic factor the prime-to-$p$ translates span the finite theta space by the perfect pairing of its two translation groups. The same connected translation rule therefore acts on every summand. In Mahler coordinates it multiplies a single measure by the character $c\mapsto\prod_j\zeta_j^{c_j}$. Changing from the orthogonal basis to the prescribed basis uses a dual $\mathrm{GL}_r(\mathbb Z_p)$ substitution. The bound on the unshifted matrix moments above gives norm at most one by Mahler uniqueness. In the prescribed jet coordinates a derivative in a rational matrix direction $Y$ reads $\Omega^{-1}\jmath(\operatorname{Tr}_{E/K_d}\sum_{kl}Y_{kl}\overline{x(c)_{kl}})$, with $x(c)$ the matrix read from the dual coordinates and $\Omega$ the ideal-curve period in this chart. Taking the type components and inserting the differential generators gives the entries of $x(c)^\dagger$ multiplied by the stipulated $p$-units $u_\tau$. The split local bases and their trace duals show that the differential generators are units, and the isogeny of degree $N\mathfrak c$ preserves the ordinary differential up to a unit. This keeps the character and the derivatives in the same chart. For fixed prime-to-$p$ translate $t$ the result is a measure of norm at most one for all allowed connected displacements and all jet coordinates. This is the input that permits both exact orthogonality counts. [Differential generators and chart][chartunits] · [Choice of orthogonal frame, transport of translation and derivative readings][shared]

## Passage to the constructed periods

After the sums, the seen jet table is a limit of integral combinations of $p^{-fa}$ times adjoint-matrix evaluations mapping $\ker l'_P$ into $N_P$. On the unseen half it is a limit of integral combinations of ordinary evaluations at marking-preserving matrices. To use a finite set of jets at once partition $\mathbb Z_p^r$ into small residue boxes, use the mass of each box as its coefficient and evaluate all continuous functions at one representative per box. The character sums then act on each representative simultaneously. On the seen half the residual translate $t_1$ after the companion average has $c_Ag\varpi t_1\in M''$. In particular its denominator away from $p$ can be absorbed into a prime-to-$p$ translate, and at $p$ its pole is confined to the selected component. More precisely write $A_1c_Ag\bar\varpi+B_1\varpi=1$, put $t'=B_1\varpi t_1$ and take $A_2N_0+B_2p=1$ for the prime-to-$p$ modulus $N_0$ of $t'$. For $m_*=c_Ag\varpi t_1$, subtracting $t'$ from $t_1$ gives $N_0(\bar\varpi/\varpi)A_2A_1m_*$ modulo $M''$. This is precisely an allowed companion displacement. On the unseen half each residual shift has prime-to-$p$ denominator before its connected displacement. Exactness of the counts concerns these tables before applying the test or summing the classes. [Split of the residual companion displacement][compchar]

Write $\sigma_\tau=e_{i,\tau}-a/2$ and $\sigma'_\tau=e_{i,\tau}-b/2$, so $\sigma_\tau-\sigma'_\tau=s$ and the last target weight is $h'_\tau=-\sigma_\tau$. The jet operator adds the necessary full determinant columns to the source polynomial and substitutes the indicated adjoint into the first $a$ target columns. Cauchy–Binet preserves its module of minors. In the chosen source and target frames $D_\tau,D'_\tau$, the determinant multiplier is $\prod_\tau\det(D_\tau)^{\sigma'_\tau}\det(D'_\tau)^{-\sigma_\tau}$. The bounded tables put the resulting polynomial in the dual Weyl lattice at the asserted scale, by closedness of the valuation ring on its finitely many coefficients. This is the Weyl lattice itself: the bound $2\,\mathrm{spread}(\lambda'_\tau)<p$ places each $\lambda'_\tau+\rho$ below the first affine wall. Jantzen's sum formula gives zero radical modulo $p$. The highest-vector-normalized map of the two lattices is nonzero there and hence onto, and Nakayama gives equality locally. At the seen places the frame determinants are $1,p$ up to units, while at the unseen ones both dual marking frames have determinant $p^{-1}$ up to a unit. This yields
$$
 p^{fa-\sum_\tau h'_\tau} k(d,d')S_w(L_d^\vee)\subset S_w(L_{d'}),\qquad
 k(d,d')S_w(L_d^\vee)\subset p^{fs} S_w(L_{d'}^\vee)
$$
at a seen and an unseen place, respectively. The character prefactors are units on representatives standard over $p$; their principal-ideal determinant laws cancel the frame transformations on general representatives. [Polynomial and frame calculation][weyl] · [Jantzen, II.8.19][jantzen]

For one selected $P$ the two sums over neighbours have the same indicator at a fixed rational matrix $x$. To verify it, set $N_0=\ker l_P$ locally and choose $z\in N_P$ with $l_P(z)=1$. A contributing lower source neighbour requires $xN_0\subset N'_P$ and $l'_P(pxz)=1$ with $pxz\in N'_P$; its marking on $N_0$ is forced to be $l'_P x$. Then the unique contributing upper target lattice adjoins $xz$ to $N'_P$, with the induced marking sending $xz$ to one. Changing $z$ by $N_0$ leaves these data unchanged. Conversely a contributing upper target gives the same two conditions and the same lower marking. The common coset refinement has normalized factor $p^{-a/2}$ on the target move, because adjoining one residue vector adds $a$ entries to the Hom lattice. The source move has $p^{-b/2}$. On both moves the unramified step character contributes the same factor. Thus the target sum is $p^s$ times the source sum. Reciprocal class stabilizers preserve this identity by counting incident-pair orbits. The iteration in the [level-prime calculation](PERIOD_LEVEL_PRIME.md#the-local-exponent-after-the-lifts) now gives the terminal exponents $a_0-mT^*_{u,w}$ on the seen side and $mfs$ on the unseen side. [Indicator, refinement and identical character factors][neighbors]

There are two further identifications for a period on the shifted lattice. On a **constructed good member with the stipulated class and row maps**, the terminal lowering is the tensor product of the rational interlacing maps in the local class bases. Its integral-coordinate and possible reference frame factors take local terminal vectors to the point lattice (the finite-order frame factors are units). An admissible integral row is an integral combination of the rational row generators whose point coordinates lie in the dual Weyl value lattices. The terminal period is the bilinear class sum $\sum_c\langle z_c,r_c\rangle/h_c$ on those frames. For $p>2fN_m+1$ every $h_c$ is a $p$-unit, since a nontrivial element of order $p$ in the terminal stabilizer would require a rational representation of dimension at least $p-1$. The stabilizer of the point embeds in that terminal stabilizer. Thus the same exponents hold on the terminal functional at $w$. This local argument needs only the stabilizers occurring in the sum. [Interlacing in the actual class frames][nativebranch] · [Integral coordinate test and root-of-unity frame changes][pending] · [Class sum on the rational forms][nativeforms] · [Integral evaluation on good members][terminal] · [Constructed row coordinates][row]

Finally let $\mu_w$ be the serving tuple, $A_{\mu_w}$ the image of its actual integral rows and $B_u$ the rational descended pairing. The adjacent volume indices cancel in $c^V_j/(c^I_jc^I_{j+1})$. The native comparison of the remaining factors gives a positive power of the ratio of the two nonzero total constants in $\mathbb Q^\times$ supported on the twisting primes. The serving character is unramified above $p$: the level part is unramified there and its distinguished auxiliary factor is supported at the other designated prime when necessary. If the descended pairing is nonzero, lift a rational row giving a nonzero value through the surjective row map. The ratio of the two rational evaluations then belongs to the coefficient field $K$ and realizes the comparison scalar. Its valuation at $w$ is zero by the support assertion. The zero pairing satisfies the assertion directly. This proves that the bound on the terminal evaluations transfers to $B_u$ on all local row generators. The reader of adjoint-functor gives the same construction and proposes the stronger root-of-unity conclusion on its installed tests. The prime-support version suffices here. [Precise rational-power support condition][unitspec] · [Pinned adjacent index and ratio calculation][unit] · [Serving tuple avoids its residue characteristic][avoid] · [Reader's rational scalar on the selected tuples][reader]

At $w\mid T_u$ the unshifted module is the preimage of the marked Hecke image, so the iteration applies to precisely its elements. The sharp row lattice localizes to $(A_{\mu_w})_w$, which is generated by the row images just tested. Its dual is tested by those same generators. Since $a_0\ge0$ and $S_{u,w}=-mT^*_{u,w}$ (zero on the unseen side), the unshifted content has valuation at least $e_wS_{u,w}$. Multiplication by the local shift $\mathfrak p_w^{-\lfloor e_w S_{u,w}\rfloor}$ therefore makes the functional integral. This proves the **word-prime part** of the shifted-lattice comparison on the constructed and tied column, with the normalized matrix section, rational jets, interlacing and descended-row maps specified above. The argument does not construct these arithmetic and geometric data uniformly on the family. Other finite primes and the archimedean operator height are separate requirements of the full period-height obligation.
