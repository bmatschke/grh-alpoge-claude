# The rational planes in the first beta step and the normalized definite bottom

Fix the grid square $j=m-2$, a reference tuple $t_0=\mu_0$, the grid field $K=\mathrm{LL}$, and its given embedding $\iota:K\hookrightarrow\mathbf C$. Write $a,n,a+n$ for the upper ranks. Start with an actual family of triangles $X$ at stage $(0,1)$, with its self-dual lattices, common archimedean markings, standard additive character and tuple-dependent splitting characters. The three arithmetic Hermitian spaces are $A,A',W$. The signed analytic realization uses the opposite sign on the smaller source and the host's imaginary multiple on the target. On the arithmetic spaces the negative indices are $0,1,1$. The lower ranks will be $a,n-2,a+n-2$. [Stage and rank conditions][stage] · [Two integral splittings][planespec]

## Removing the two planes over the ground field

For either $S=A'$ or $S=W$, write $h$ for its Hermitian form, linear in its first variable, and $L$ for its given self-dual lattice. Its rank is at least three, and $h$ is indefinite at every real place. The quadratic form $h(z,z)$ over $F$ has dimension $2\dim_E S\ge6$. It is isotropic at the real places by indefiniteness and at the finite places by the dimension bound for quadratic forms over nonarchimedean local number fields. The Hasse–Minkowski theorem gives $0\ne x\in S(E)$ with $h(x,x)=0$. This argument works in the arithmetic Hermitian model before the sign and scale changes. [Rank check and use at both spaces][stage] · [Pinned equivalent Hermitian isotropy argument][isot] · [Landherr, pp. 245–247][landherr]

The integral splitting requires a second choice. Let $J=h(L,x)$, a nonzero fractional ideal of $\mathcal O_E$. The surjection $L\longrightarrow J$ splits over $\mathcal O_E$ because $J$ is invertible. Extending a section to $E$ gives a vector $y_0$ with $h(y_0,x)=1$ and $Jy_0\subset L$. In this host $E/F$ is unramified at the finite places. The trace map on integers is surjective locally, hence globally, so there is $\theta\in\mathcal O_E$ with $\theta+\bar\theta=1$. Set $b=h(y_0,y_0)\in F$ and $y=y_0-\theta b x$. Then $h(y,y)=b-(\theta+\bar\theta)b=0$ and $h(x,y)=1$. For $z,w\in L$, putting $J_z=h(z,x)$, integrality of the form on $L$ gives
$$
   h(z,y_0)\overline{J_w}\in\mathcal O_E,\qquad
   bJ_z\overline{J_w}=h(J_z y_0,J_w y_0)\in\mathcal O_E .
$$
The first is $h(z,J_w y_0)$. By self-duality these identities give $h(z,y_0)x\in L$ and $bJ_z x\in L$. Hence $h(z,y)x\in L$ and $J_z y\in L$. Subtract these two line components from $z$ to obtain its component in $L_1=L\cap (Ex+Ey)^\perp$. This proves the integral direct sum
$$
 L=(L\cap Ex)\ \oplus\ L_1\ \oplus\ (L\cap Ey).
$$
The two line lattices can be fractional ideals. A vector in the orthogonal complement pairing integrally with $L_1$ pairs integrally with all of $L$, so belongs to $L_1$. Thus $L_1$ is self-dual. Removing a hyperbolic plane removes one positive and one negative direction everywhere. [Fractional-ideal section][section] · [Trace-one modification and the integral projections][partner] · [Self-duality and the resulting signatures][compl]

Apply the construction twice to the lattices already in $X$. Fix both inclusions, both hyperbolic planes, and both orthogonal complements before choosing a tuple. Keep the original marking on $A$. Mark the two self-dual complements, giving one lower triangle $Y$ for the whole family. Passing from this geometry to a triangle uses the standard Weil representation with the prescribed splitting characters, the two common normalized vacua, and the standard tuple-indexed finite tests. The restrictions of the characters to $\mathbf A_F^\times$ remain admissible because the dimensions have changed by two. The source explicitly retains the upper idempotents and conductor data. Its construction of $Y$ uses the unitary-pair Weil existence input. It establishes a valid stage-zero triangle. Common polynomial witnesses for its theta integrals require a separate argument. [Choice before the tuple][stage] · [Weil actions, vacua and actual assigned tests][weily]

## The analytic coordinates for a pair of twists

For a fixed pair $\{t,t_0\}$, fit one analytic base $B$ to both twists, with the same additive character, auxiliary split places and archimedean embeddings required by the base conditions. The multiplicative characters $\nu_t,\nu_{t_0}$ may differ. The same base fits the lower ranks. Transport the two rational decompositions into the analytic coordinates. On the smaller source the coordinate map conjugates the entries and multiplies the form by $-1$. On the target it uses the actual imaginary generator $\xi_0$ and the fixed nonzero lattice ideal $\mathfrak A$. Multiplication of the lattice and its complement by the same invertible ideal preserves the direct sum. More explicitly, if the form in coordinates is $c\,\epsilon(h)$, use $x_{\rm an}=\epsilon(x)$, $y_{\rm an}=\bar c^{-1}\epsilon(y)$, with the frame-coordinate map understood. Their pairing is again one. [Actual sign, target lattice and change of coordinates][coords] · [Construction on the same fitted base][fit]

The marked split components need particular columns. At each marked $v$, prescribe a valuation of $u\in E^\times$ at the selected prime over $v$ that makes $u x_{\rm an}$ primitive in the local lattice on its line. Prescribe independently at the conjugate prime the valuation that makes $\bar u^{-1}y_{\rm an}$ primitive at the selected prime. All these primes are distinct, so weak approximation realizes the prescriptions simultaneously. The replacement $x_{\rm an}\mapsto ux_{\rm an}$, $y_{\rm an}\mapsto\bar u^{-1}y_{\rm an}$ preserves the pairing, the two lines and their complement. The primitive columns and the image of the given basis of the lower space give a lattice basis since the localized sum is direct. The two distinguished columns, numbered from zero, are $0,n-1$ on the smaller source and $a,a+n-1$ on the target, with the complement in the other columns in its inherited order. At the real places extend the Cartan involution of the lower space by the hyperbolic swap, with the unit scalar required by the signed form. On the Hermitian plane one can take the swap $x\leftrightarrow y$. The skew plane uses the Cartan involution for $iJ$. The resulting upper marking retains its rational form and lattice. This constructs an adapted marking for the same decomposition even when the original Cartan involution moves each of the chosen planes. [Valuation prescriptions][prim] · [Columns and Cartan extensions][adapt] · [Precisely the fields changed in a marking][reS]

For each adapted plane $H$, the fractional ideal $\mathfrak b_H=\{u\in E:ux_H\in L_{\rm an}\}$ is nonzero and has valuation zero at **both** primes over every marked $v$. At the chosen prime this follows from the $x_H$ column. At the conjugate prime use the primitive $y_H$ column, self-duality and the unit pairing. For the trace-dual skew form one uses that the different is a unit at the marked places. This assertion concerns just the marked primes. The ideal need not be principal. The two planes are fixed before $t$, while choices of an adapted generator, the resulting ideals, fitted base and markings can depend on the pair being compared. [Hermitian plane ideal][hcop] · [Trace-dual skew plane ideal][scop] · [Order of the quantifiers][fit]

## The descended bottom and the specified standard bottom

Let $X_{\mathrm b}$ be the prescribed standard bottom triangle for the same square. Assume that $Y$ has a family $c=(c_t)$ satisfying $\mathrm{IsConst}$. This means there exist two polynomials common to all tuples, all the required integrals converge, each small integral is nonzero at some target point, and the big integral equals $c_t$ times the small integral as a function of the target. Some $c_t$ is nonzero. For this assertion alone one can transport the witnesses so that
$$
 X_{\mathrm b}.\mathrm{IsConst}\bigl(t\mapsto\iota(\zeta_t)c_t\bigr),
 \qquad \zeta_t\in\mu(K),\qquad \zeta_{t_0}=1 .
 \tag{1}
$$
Here $\mu(K)$ is the group of all roots of unity in $K$. The input on coefficient fields is the grid's $\mathrm{NumbersIn}(\iota)$ and the arithmetic character assumptions $\mathrm{LevCh.Is}(t_0)$. Three changes account for (1).

1. **Rational spaces.** The rank relations identify the three corresponding ranks. The arithmetic spaces have the same all-positive real signatures. At a nonsplit finite place, a self-dual lattice puts the determinant in the unramified norm class, since it is a unit in a lattice basis and units are norms for an unramified quadratic extension. The determinant condition is automatic at split places. Thus the determinants of corresponding spaces have the same local norm classes. Landherr's theorem gives rational isometries. Conjugation by the three isometries transports the actions, theta test functions, polynomials, measures and fundamental domains. Changing variables in the integrals preserves $c_t$ exactly. [Determinant and isometry on this host][land] · [Exact transport of the witnesses][moveiso] · [Landherr's local-global theorem][landherr]

2. **Self-dual lattices.** On each fixed rational space the two lattices are locally isometric. At a split place the two halves are dual lattices, and at a nonsplit place this is the local classification of unimodular Hermitian lattices for an unramified quadratic extension. The two lattices agree at almost all finite places in common coordinates. Choose finite adelic isometries $g,g',h$ carrying the three lattices, with identity archimedean component. Write $x,x',y$ for idelic Hilbert–90 lifts of their respective determinants, in the signed convention of the two Weil actions. The normalized vacuum transported with the $t_0$-action differs from transport with the $t$-action by the quotient of their splitting factors. Thus the big and small integrated kernels are multiplied by $r_t^{-1}$ and $(r'_t)^{-1}$, with the same right translation of the target. The multiplier on $c_t$ is $v_{1,t}=r'_t/r_t$ and the splitting-factor calculation gives
$$
 \frac{v_{1,t}}{v_{1,t_0}}=\xi_t\bigl((x')^{-1}y x^{-1}\bigr),\qquad
 \xi_t=\nu_t/\nu_{t_0}.
$$
All these factors are nonzero. The level character law gives $\xi_t^{N_t}=1$ for some $N_t>0$, and the connected archimedean ideles contribute one. Each nontrivial finite local evaluation lies in $\iota(K)$ by the character-value clause of $\mathrm{NumbersIn}$. There are finitely many such factors on each idele, so their product is $\iota(z)$ for $z\in K$, and $z^{N_t}=1$ by injectivity of $\iota$. This proves the root-of-unity strengthening even when a determinant lift is a nonunit idele. [Finite adelic transport][latbase] · [Explicit determinant product and orientations][latsplit] · [Global finite order from the level condition][finord] · [Change of variables in both integrals][latmove] · [Wall, local integral classification][wall]

3. **Marked finite frames and archimedean normalization.** Once lattices agree, let $k_t$ be a unitary stabilizer element carrying the target frame at the marked idempotent. Both integrals acquire the same right translate by $k_t$. The two standard finite tests also acquire the scalars
$$
 b_{1,t}=\nu_t(x_{1,t})/\beta_t(y_t),\qquad
 b_{2,t}=\nu_t(x_{2,t})/\alpha_t(y_t),\qquad
 \nu_t=\alpha_t/\beta_t .
$$
Here $x_{1,t},x_{2,t}$ are the determinants from the two source-frame comparisons and $y_t$ is the split-component determinant of $k_t$. All three are finite unit ideles, with trivial archimedean component. Thus $v_{2,t}=b_{1,t}/b_{2,t}=\nu_t(x_{1,t}y_t/x_{2,t})$ is itself in $\iota(\mu(K))$. Indeed the restrictions of continuous circle-valued characters to the profinite integral unit group have finite image because their images are compact quotients of the profinite group and closed subgroups of the circle. The field assertion uses the local value clauses for the fixed and varying characters composing $\nu_t$. In particular it does not require $\nu_t$ itself to have finite order. The normalized frame multiplier $v_{2,t}/v_{2,t_0}$ has the same property. [Two finite tests and their exact scalars][frame1] · [Translation, frame quotient and order][frame2] · [Membership in the actual grid field][frameK]

With the three lattices and three finite frames aligned, the definite Cartan involutions on the unsigned standard positive spaces are the identity, and the sign on the first oscillator factor is the one prescribed by its model. Real orthonormal frame changes act by substitution on the polynomials, independent of $t$. Uniqueness of the oriented vacuum line and uniqueness of the Weil action on theta functions give two nonzero scalars $a_1,a_2$, common to all $t$. Positive Haar-measure comparison gives two further nonzero scalars $m_1,m_2$, also common to all tuples. In the direction from the transported witnesses to $X_{\mathrm b}$, the common ratio is
$$
  \kappa=\frac{m_1/a_1}{m_2/a_2}\ne0.
$$
It follows that $X_{\mathrm b}.\mathrm{IsConst}(v_t c_t)$ with $v_t=\kappa v_{1,t}v_{2,t}$. Multiplying the first common witnessing polynomial by $v_{t_0}^{-1}$ proves (1). This argument never divides by $c_t$. Passing the complex scalar into the polynomial uses exactly the freedom in the definition of $\mathrm{IsConst}$. For polynomials or measures fixed in advance, the argument instead gives the multiplier $v_t$ and locates $v_t/v_{t_0}$ in the embedded roots of unity. [Real vacuum and polynomial comparison][norm1] · [Haar scalar and normalization][norm2] · [Common rescaling][rescale] · [Composed public away-unit statement][ratio]

Finally, if a separately prescribed family $c^{\mathrm b}$ on $X_{\mathrm b}$ must be compared to $c$, the exact additional assertion used by the pin is factorization for $X_{\mathrm b}$. Under that assertion any two $\mathrm{IsConst}$ families for it are scalar multiples by a single nonzero complex number. Consequently, for nonzero reference constants,
$$
  \frac{c_t^{\mathrm b}}{c_{t_0}^{\mathrm b}}
     =\iota(\zeta_t)\frac{c_t}{c_{t_0}} .
$$
The factorization supplies nonvanishing at every tuple as well. The unnormalized constants can carry the common archimedean and Haar period. The [standard-test factorization](ROW_STAGE_STANDARD_TESTS.md) supplies this hypothesis on the definite bottom. The separate theorem comparing two *standard* bottom triples gives a $K^\times$-multiple at each tuple. Its hypotheses differ because the stage-zero complement just constructed need only be a stage-zero triangle. The geometry above supplies the lower triangle and its adapted coordinates. The [upper-to-lower recurrence on the grid](ROW_FIRST_BETA_GRID_RATIOS.md) combines this geometry with the polynomial integral identity and the change of analytic markings at both levels.
