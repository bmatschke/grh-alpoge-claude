# A uniform branching coefficient for the core Poincaré series

Let $n=r_D$, $N=n+1$, $f=[F:\mathbb Q]$ and $b$ be the scale of the grid. At each real place there is a translate of a unit vector of lowest compact type on the rank-$N$ core whose restricted matrix coefficient reproduces a unit vector on the rank-$n$ row, with scalar $c_\tau>0$. The integral of its absolute value transverse to that row, and the integral of its first derivative along the row, are bounded **relative to that same scalar**. The constants in the estimates below give the admissible global loss
$$Q=10872 f N^4\log b.$$
Consequently every nonzero bottom row and every prescribed nonzero vector of the algebraic tensor product of the local smooth models admit a nonzero bottom–core period, in the large-scale range of the grid. This is the period at the *start* of the mixed-model ascent. The transfer to a top Bessel test is a further assertion. [Local statement][rb-input] · [Period with the prescribed vector][rb-period]

## The two actual real representations

Use signed orthonormal frames. Write $G=U(p,q)$, $H=U(p-1,q)$, and let $z$ be the added positive unit vector, so $H=G_z$. Let $\pi$ be the discrete series of the core and $\sigma$ the **conjugate of the stored bottom discrete series**. Write $\lambda,\mu$ for their decreasing signed parameters. The core has the extra positive sign. The smaller group has at least one negative sign, with at most as many negative as positive signs. On the actual grid $n\ge3$, all parameter coordinates have absolute value at most $B=b^2$, the spacings in each list are at least two, and each opposite-sign gap within either list is at least $1000N^3$. In the combined decreasing list, adjacent entries have equal signs exactly when they come from different lists. This interlacing also persists after subtracting one from any single $\lambda_i$. The source obtains these assertions by counting the negative signs in the two even blocks, applying the prescribed gaps of the grid to successive entries and observing that lowering one entry preserves the allowed adjacent signs. It applies this calculation to the enumeration of the **dual** bottom parameter. It gives $\log(8NB)\le6\log b$. He's real discrete-series branching theorem for this sign word gives an isometric intertwiner $j:\sigma\hookrightarrow\pi|_H$, unique up to phase. Write $P=j^*$. [Grid verification][rb-grid] · [He, *Gan–Gross–Prasad Conjecture for U(p,q)*, §§6–7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1508.02032#page=26)

## Finding a translate with appreciable projection

Put $T=8NB$, fix unit lowest-compact-type vectors $v_0$ in $\pi$ and $w_0$ in $\sigma$, and write $F(g)=|\langle jw_0,\pi(g)v_0\rangle|^2$. Formal degrees $d_\pi,d_\sigma$ use the actual two Haar measures. Schur orthogonality gives $\int_G F=1/d_\pi$. We claim
$$\int_G\|g^{-1}z\|^2F(g)\,dg\le T^{8N}/d_\pi. \tag{1}$$
The weight measures distance in the quotient $H\backslash G$. A bound on the unweighted integral alone would allow its entire mass to move out along that quotient.

Here is the coefficient calculation behind (1). On $\pi^\infty\otimes(\mathbb C^N)^*$ the coordinate action of $g$ is $x_c\mapsto\sum_l(g^{-1})_{lc}\pi(g)x_l$. Translation of an infinitesimal character by the dual defining representation decomposes this smooth module into its distinct regular summands $\pi_s$ with parameters $\lambda-e_s$. In this range the shift stays in the same discrete-series chamber. This is the translation principle with minuscule weights of multiplicity one. The invariant form inherited from the defining representation is indefinite. Its restriction to each summand is a nonzero scalar $a_s$ times the unitary form. Let $p_s$ be the corresponding algebraic projection and $q_s$ the coordinate in the unitary model of $\pi_s$. Thus $[y,p_s x]=a_s\langle q_s y,q_s x\rangle$ on smooth vectors. No Hilbert orthogonality of the algebraic projections is required. [Zuckerman's translation theorem](https://doi.org/10.2307/1971097) · [Shifted summands and forms][rb-trans]

Two elementary bounds for these projections are used. For $e_l v_0$ supported in coordinate $l$, every component of $p_s(e_l v_0)$ has norm at most $T^{4N}$. Also
$$P\big(p_s(e_z\eta)_z\big)=\gamma_sP\eta,\qquad |\gamma_s|\le T^N$$
for every smooth $\eta$, where $e_z$ denotes the coordinate of the added line. To see the constants, set $m_i=\lambda_i-(N-1)/2$. The quantum characteristic polynomial of the matrix of derived matrix units has roots $-m_i$. Evaluation of its comatrix at a root gives the projection times $\prod_{j\ne i}(m_j-m_i)$, up to the ordering sign. Each factor in that denominator has modulus at least one. A cofactor is a signed sum of $n!$ ordered products with scalar entries bounded by $4B$. On a lowest-compact-type vector, a word of length $k\le n$ in matrix units has norm at most $[8\sqrt N(B+n)]^k$. Indeed after $j$ differentiations its compact weights lie in the lowest type tensored with $j$ copies of the adjoint representation. The sum of squared matrix-unit norms on this layer is the quadratic form of $2C_K-C_G$. The compact highest-weight formula bounds each successive action by $8\sqrt N(B+n)$ for these layers. Expanding the cofactor therefore gives $n!(4B+8\sqrt N(B+n))^n\le T^{4N}$. Deleting the $z$ row and column gives the Capelli determinant of $H$. Applying the intertwiner evaluates it at the infinitesimal character of $\sigma$, so the numerator of $\gamma_s$ is $\prod_{j=1}^n(\mu_j-\lambda_i+1/2)$. This gives the second bound with the same denominator. [Comatrix and its denominator][rb-cof] · [Norm and smaller determinant][rb-cof2] · [Compact Casimir bound][rb-word]

For clarity about the possible signs of $a_s$, put $\xi=jw_0$. The coefficient $d_\sigma\overline{\langle w_0,\sigma(h)w_0\rangle}$ is an integrable self-adjoint convolution idempotent. Integrability follows from the lowest-compact-type bound below for the smaller rank. In any unitary representation its range consists of the $w_0$ lines in its copies of $\sigma$. Indeed convolution on the two sides of a group translate by that idempotent gives the same idempotent times the corresponding $\sigma$ coefficient, by Schur orthogonality. The cyclic subrepresentation of each vector in its range is consequently a copy of $\sigma$, by equality of the Gram matrices of its translates. In $\pi|_H$ it projects onto $\mathbb C\xi$. Choose a smooth $\eta$ with $\langle\eta,\xi\rangle\ne0$. Apply this idempotent to the $\pi_s|_H$ vector $q_s(e_z\eta)$. Pair the result with $q_s x$ in the preceding form identity. The $z$ coordinate is fixed by $H$, so the resulting functional represents $x\mapsto \langle\xi,(p_s x)_z\rangle$, up to the common factor $\langle\eta,\xi\rangle$. Pairing once with $x=e_z\eta$ and using the formula for $\gamma_s$ cancels that factor. Schur orthogonality on $G$ now gives exactly
$$\int_G |\langle\xi,(p_s(g\cdot(e_l v_0)))_z\rangle|^2\,dg
=|\gamma_s|\, |a_s|\,\|q_s(e_l v_0)\|^2/d_{\pi_s}.$$
The form identity gives $|a_s|\|q_s(e_l v_0)\|^2=|[e_l v_0,p_s(e_l v_0)]|\le T^{4N}$. In the Weyl product for formal degrees only the $N-1$ differences involving the shifted coordinate change. The old spacing is at least two, so each old-to-new absolute ratio is at most two. Thus $1/d_{\pi_s}\le T^N/d_\pi$. Each displayed integral is at most $T^{6N}/d_\pi$. Summing the $p_s$ recovers the identity. Cauchy–Schwarz on that sum and the sum over $l$ cost at most $N^3$, which proves (1). [Projected-form identity, cancellation and Schur calculation][rb-moment] · [Summing the components][rb-sum]

Set $Y=2T^{8N}$. By (1), at least $1/(2d_\pi)$ of the mass lies where $\|g^{-1}z\|^2\le Y$. Every left $H$-coset there has a representative of squared Hilbert–Schmidt norm at most $16(N+Y)^2$. Indeed a rotation in the two compact signature blocks and a two-dimensional hyperbolic rotation send $z$ to $g^{-1}z$. Thickening by the unit ball $U=\{h:\|h-I\|_{\rm HS}\le1\}$ of $H$ gives a subset of the ball $\mathcal C=\{g:\|g\|_{\rm HS}^2\le64N(N+Y)^2\}$. Weil integration thus bounds the quotient measure of these cosets by $\mathrm{vol}(\mathcal C)/\mathrm{vol}(U)$. On an entire fibre the integral of $F$ equals $\|P\pi(g)v_0\|^2/d_\sigma$ by Schur orthogonality on $H$, and this projection norm is constant on the fibre. [Mass, cosets, and fibre integral][rb-local]

The two Haar comparisons used here are $d_\pi\mathrm{vol}(\mathcal C)\le4T^{24N^3}$ and $1/d_\sigma\le T^{67N^2}\mathrm{vol}(U)$. For the first, on a ball of radius $(100N(B+1))^{-2}$ about the identity the unit lowest coefficient has modulus at least $1/2$, by integrating its first Lie derivative in logarithmic coordinates. Its squared integral on the group is $1/d_\pi$. Packing translates of this small ball into a matrix ball of Hilbert–Schmidt radius $R$ costs at most $(4N(\sqrt N+1)R/\delta)^{2N^2}$ for small radius $\delta$. For this exponent, thicken each group element by its left translate of the *ambient matrix* box of coordinate radius $\delta/(4N)$ around $I$. Left multiplication preserves ambient real volume because the determinant of a unitary-group element has absolute value one. The union fits in the box of radius $(\sqrt N+1)R$. For a fixed ambient matrix in the union, the group elements whose boxes contain it lie in one translate of the group neighborhood of radius $\delta$, by multiplying the two near-identity factors. Fubini and the ratio of these two ambient box volumes give the bound. The projection source uses $R\le T^{10N}$, and its elementary powers give the stated $24N^3$. For the second inequality, sum the squared lowest coefficient of $H$ on dyadic matrix balls, using its large-gap decay and ball volumes relative to $U$. This bounds its full integral $1/d_\sigma$. These comparisons hold for arbitrary scalings of the two Haar measures. Combining them with the mass lower bound produces $g_0$ such that
$$\|g_0\|_{\rm HS}\le T^{10N},\qquad \|P\pi(g_0)v_0\|\ge T^{-100N^3}.$$
For example the source's mass comparison gives $1\le16T^{67N^2+24N^3}\|P\pi(g_0)v_0\|^2$, which implies the displayed bound. [Ambient matrix volume argument][rb-box] · [Both measured ball estimates][rb-balls] · [Selection and final exponents][rb-choose]

## Reproducing the row and estimating the derivative

Put $v=\pi(g_0)v_0$. The vector $Pv$ is nonzero and smooth on $H$ by equivariance. With $w'=Pv/\|Pv\|$, the restricting coefficient reproduces $w'$ with scalar $c=\|Pv\|^2/d_\sigma$. To check this, first use inputs which are finite linear combinations of translates of $w_0$. The integrals of their matrix coefficients against the restriction on $\pi$ are the corresponding translates of the rank-one idempotent calculated above, divided by $d_\sigma$. The resulting identity for arbitrary $b,w$ extends by density once the restricted coefficient of $v$ is in $L^1(H)$, as proved below. With inner products linear in the second argument it reads
$$\int_H\overline{\langle v,\pi(h)v\rangle}\langle b,\sigma(h)w\rangle\,dh
=d_\sigma^{-1}\langle b,Pv\rangle\langle Pv,w\rangle.$$
Taking $w=w'$ proves the reproduction identity. In particular the weaker inequality $c\ge\|Pv\|^2/(2d_\sigma)$ used for the source's constants holds. [Source version of the eigenvector bound][rb-eigen]

Write $V_H=\mathrm{vol}\{h:\|h-I\|_{\rm HS}\le1/4\}$, and $L(g)=\|gz\|$. Here are the two relative estimates. For a rank-$m$ lowest-compact-type coefficient with opposite-sign gap $h\ge1000m^3$, its absolute value for unit inputs is bounded by $e^{100m^3}\|g\|_{\rm HS}^{-40m^2}$. The source derives this from the compact integral for the lowest-type compression. In its polar decomposition $g=k\exp X$, the integrand's radial factor is a product of leading minors of $k'^{-1}\exp X k'$ to exponents $-(\lambda_i-\lambda_{i+1}+1)$. Cauchy–Binet writes each minor as the expectation of $e^y$ over exterior-power weights, with mean exponent zero and second moment the squared norm of the block crossing that minor's cut. The weighted sum of the latter counts every opposite-sign entry of $X$ with weight at least the gap. Bounding the exponential remainder gives the radial majorant $\exp[-(h/8)\min(\|X\|_{\rm HS}^2,\|X\|_{\rm HS}/\sqrt{2\min(p,q)})]$. Compact Schur orthogonality cancels the normalization of the compact integral. Using $\|g\|_{\rm HS}\le\sqrt m\exp\|X\|_{\rm HS}$ gives the coarser power bound above. [Compression formula][rb-compress] · [Leading minors and the gap][rb-minor] · [Power bound][rb-dec]

Conjugating $g$ by $g_0$ costs at most $\|g_0\|_{\rm HS}^{80N^2}$ in that power bound. The same two-dimensional rotation supplies a representative of $gH$ with norm at most $\sqrt{3N}L(g)$. After a left translation of the $H$ integration variable, use $L(g)\le\|gh\|_{\rm HS}$ and $\|h\|_{\rm HS}\le\sqrt{3N}L(g)\|gh\|_{\rm HS}$ in this frame. Keeping $4n^2+1$ powers for integration on $H$ leaves at least $2n+7$ powers of $L(g)$ after the second inequality. Dyadic ball packing bounds $\int_H\|h\|_{\rm HS}^{-(4n^2+1)}dh\le e^{10n^2}V_H$. With $\|g_0\|\le T^{10N}$ the resulting bound is
$$\int_H |\langle v,\pi(gh)v\rangle|\,dh\le V_H\, T^{1000 N^4} L(g)^{-(2n+7)}.$$
It also proves the claimed $L^1$ bound at $g=1$. The power estimate on all of $G$ implies the separately requested exponent $20N^2$ with a finite constant. [Transverse bound including the exponent 1000][rb-far] · [Dyadic sum][rb-pow]

For $X\in\mathfrak h$ of Hilbert–Schmidt norm at most one, consider $u_h(s)=\langle\pi(\exp sX)v,\pi(h)v\rangle$ on $0\le s\le1$. Writing $R=\|g_0\|_{\rm HS}$, the two-letter Lie bound gives $|u_h''(s)|\le [(8NB)^2R^2]^2=:M_2$. The conjugated coefficient estimate also gives $|u_h(s)|\le E^2$ uniformly in this interval, with
$$E=e^{50N^3}\left(\frac{\|h\|_{\rm HS}}{(\sqrt N+3)R^2}\right)^{-20N^2}.$$
Apply Taylor's formula at step $\min(1,E)$, using also $|u_h|\le1$. It gives $|u_h'(0)|\le(2+M_2)E$. The same dyadic integral now gives $\int_H|u_h'(0)|dh\le V_H T^{600 N^4}$. Finally $d_\sigma V_H\le T^{11N^4}$ by the small identity ball and formal-degree argument, with the comparison radius $1/(4(8nB)^2)$ on $H$. Squaring $\|Pv\|\ge T^{-100N^3}$ and weakening $N^3$ to $N^4$ gives the two losses of $100$. Absorbing the factor $2$ in $c\ge\|Pv\|^2/(2d_\sigma)$ costs at most one further power $T^{N^4}$. Together these give $V_H\le cT^{(11+100+100+1)N^4}$. Both estimates have the required form $c T^{C N^4}$, for instance with the source's $C=1000+600+11+201=1812$. The extra $L(g)$ factor remains in the transverse estimate. [Second-derivative interpolation][rb-der] · [Quarter-ball and formal degree][rb-vol] · [Combination of constants][rb-const]

Tensor the selected vectors over the real places. The conversion from the conjugate bottom representation to the original bottom representation conjugates the coefficient equation and preserves its positive scalar. The change to each actual Haar measure multiplies the equation and both absolute estimates by the same positive factor. Since $\log T\le6\log b$, the tensor gives the loss $Q$ stated above, with one absolute constant before the choice of a large grid. The short-vector and lattice-counting estimates for the original congruence level now give a nonzero core Poincaré period by strict dominance of its identity term. This first calculation uses a product of the local vectors. A prescribed nonzero vector $w$ may be a sum of such tensors. In the irreducible smooth product representation the closed invariant subspace generated by $w$ is the whole representation. Equivalently, projecting its translates onto compact types gives a nonzero submodule of the irreducible Harish-Chandra module, which is dense in the smooth model. The pairing against the core cusp form is continuous there, so it cannot vanish on all the translates of $w$. Moving a successful translate to the core factor, then approximating by compact-finite vectors there, gives a nonzero period for the prescribed tensor.
