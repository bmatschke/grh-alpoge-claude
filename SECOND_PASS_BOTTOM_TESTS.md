# The bottom tests on the restricted retwisted datum

Let $w'$ be the [enlarged pinned column](SECOND_PASS_RETWIST.md), with its enlarged full field $L'$ and its transported small and fixed subfields. Let $z^+$ be its fine extension, put $W=W_1(z^+,F'_0)$ and restrict $z^+$ to $W$. Write $z_W$ for the result. This restriction exists before a row-lift family or fitted state has been selected. Under the global theta, period-separation and arithmetic field inputs specified below, the bottom theta maps on **this $z_W$** have a common family of separating complex-linear functionals with values in the given coefficient embedding on every rational source image. Together with the lower-row multiplicity theorem and the weight comparison, this gives the preliminary package $P'$ used in [the construction of the row state](SECOND_PASS_ROW_STATE.md). ## Obtain the tests without a prior fitted row

The size theorem used to construct a complete choice applies to a stage with specified division parameter and zero auxiliary row operators. The retwisted fine stage is not required to have those numerical values. On the *same column* take the temporary stage with division parameter $6x^{-C_F}$ and zero auxiliary operators. Green's construction makes its marked group true for the same frame. The two true groups identify the marked places, group elements and good idempotents. Copy $z_W$ to that stage using the identification. The exact analytic maps survive the copy. This temporary stage supplies the required size data in the same scale. The copied pins, exact maps, host frames, finite tuple indexing and marked group give the Hida realization on which B8.17 operates. Take $C_F$ above the field and separation thresholds and then take the common eventual size range. [Hida setting from the actual pins][hida] · [Temporary stage, sizes, and return][at]

For a nonzero smooth tensor, B8.15 provides one real pair working for **every** nonzero bottom multiplicity map. Choose finitely many of the column tests at that pair which separate the bottom Hilbert space. Such a subfamily exists because the space is finite dimensional and the common kernel of all tests is zero. The matching calculations use the prescribed finite data at each tuple. Their product of comparison scalars is $c_t\ne0$, with $c_t/c_{\mu_0}$ in the coefficient field. Projection of a rational source vector to its good summand leaves its theta image unchanged. The normalized finite sum on that projection is in the same embedded field. The [common scalar calculation](COMPOSITE_ROW_RATIONALITY.md) rescales every test by $c_{\mu_0}/\kappa$ if the common real factor $\kappa$ is nonzero. If $\kappa=0$, each test value on these images already vanishes. The stage sign transports the Hilbert coefficient embedding to the one specified for $z_W$. Returning through the marked-group identification transports the row tests to the original restricted realization. This works for all its tuples, including those with nonempty conductor. ## A literature calculation inside the separation theorem

The reference core chain has ranks $n_i=r+1+i(r+5)$ with $r$ odd. At an inert $v$ outside its exceptional set, write $K=F_v$, $L=E_w$, $Q=Nv$, $2a=n_i$ and $2b=n_{i+1}$. Both splitting characters in that step are the unramified $\widetilde\chi_i=\chi_{m-i}^{-1}$. The restriction condition gives $\chi(\varpi)=(-1)^{\mathrm{rank}}$ for a uniformizer $\varpi$ of $K$, also a uniformizer of $L$. Each rank is even, so the value at $\varpi$ is one. Unramifiedness supplies the value one on $\mathcal O_L^\times$, proving triviality on all of $L^\times$. An odd rank would give $-1$ at $\varpi$. The residue characteristic here is odd because the host parameter $d_{\rm CM}\equiv7\bmod16$ makes $-d_{\rm CM}$ a square in $\mathbb Q_2$. Every dyadic place of $F$ therefore splits in $E=F(\sqrt{-d_{\rm CM}})$. [Host congruence][host] · [Core ranks and exceptional set][ranks] · [Character value and norm segment][charval]

The two integral frames use the same field involution and uniformizer. They identify both lattices with unimodular lattices in the split spaces of these even dimensions. An anti-invariant unit converts the source form to a skew-Hermitian form with the same stabilizer and keeps the target Hermitian. For the oscillator character, the original localization first normalizes the local trace pairing by a lattice generator. It then recovers an additive character on the fixed field which is trivial on $\mathcal O_K$ and nontrivial on $\varpi^{-1}\mathcal O_K$, with the half-trace identity of the local Heisenberg action. This verifies Liu's conductor hypothesis for the *localized* character. [Two charts][chart] · [Construction on the actual vacuum][locmodel] · [Local trace duality][cond] · [Anti-invariant unit and half-trace identity][delta]

In the original localization a nonzero vacuum generates its local subspace by Heisenberg translations. It is fixed by the self-dual tensor lattice $\Lambda$ and by both lattice stabilizers. The translates indexed by cosets modulo $\Lambda$ span that subspace. They are linearly independent because their eigencharacters under integral Heisenberg translations are distinct by self-duality. The same calculation applies in the Schrödinger model to the vacuum $1_{\Lambda_V^a}$ for the target lattice $\Lambda_V$. Sending each translated vacuum to the corresponding translate, with the common Heisenberg multiplication rule, intertwines the two models. An operator commuting with the Heisenberg action preserves the one-dimensional $\Lambda$-fixed line. Its scalar on that line determines the operator on all translates. Thus the two actions of the unitary pair can differ only by a character. This character is one on the two compact stabilizers because they fix both vacua. In either split unitary group the Cartan decomposition has representatives diagonal by inverse uniformizer powers on paired hyperbolic lines. Each such pair of diagonal entries belongs to the special unitary group of its hyperbolic plane, isomorphic to $\mathrm{SL}_2(K)$. This group is perfect, so the character is one on these representatives as well. This proves equivariance on both factors. [Localized covariance, spanning and compact invariance][modelacts] · [Liu's Schrödinger action and vacuum](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.14223#page=8)

For the self-dual target Liu's sign is $+$ and the Witt index is $b$. Normalize both maximal compact subgroups to have volume one. Liu's Lemma 2.7 accounts for the change from Iwahori normalization. In his Definition 2.8 the source homomorphism from its spherical algebra to the ring of Weyl-invariant Laurent polynomials in $a$ variables is an isomorphism. Inversion of the variables preserves each such polynomial. The target homomorphism inserts $Q,Q^3,\ldots,Q^{2(b-a)-1}$. For an arbitrary target operator $h'$ choose the unique source operator $h$ with that substituted polynomial. Then $h\otimes1-1\otimes h'$ belongs to the kernel in Definition 2.8. By Liu's Theorem 1.1(1) this kernel annihilates the spherical Weil module, so the two convolutions agree on the prescribed lattice vacuum. Conversely, if they agree on that vacuum, Theorem 1.1(2) says it generates the module, so their difference is in the same kernel. This proves both directions of B8.15j on these even-rank charts. In base change $Nw=Q^2$ and the newly inserted eigenvalues occur with their inverses, giving precisely the segment $(Nw)^{(n_{i+1}-n_i-1)/2-l}$ for $0\le l<n_{i+1}-n_i$. At split places the transfer law keeps the factor $\chi_S(w)/\chi_T(w)$ on the old roots and $\chi_S(w)$ on the new segment. The ramified twisted-square comparison has its own hypotheses in B8.16. [Liu, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.14223#page=2) · [Liu, Lemma 2.7 and Definition 2.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.14223#page=7) · [B8.15j][local] · [Split and inert transfer][chain]

## Which global inputs are used

* **B8.11.** The Roadmap starts from doubling continuation and the definite Siegel–Weil value of B8.12. Langlands's continuation theorem and the definite specialization of Gan–Qiu–Takeda provide its classical inputs. B8.11 adds the common matching quotient, decay on the specified full-rank primed tests, and a single nonzero proportionality scalar for matches. It is this global matching assertion that the row calculation uses. [B8.11][b11] · [B8.12 and its references][b12] · [Gan–Qiu–Takeda](https://arxiv.org/abs/1207.4709)
* **B8.15.** Besides the local theta package, the separation proof uses convergent Siegel–Weil for $b>2a$, the first convergent formula of B8.13 in its separate range $a<b\le2a,\ a+\operatorname{Witt}(B)<b$, split and inert unramified doubling, Harish–Chandra finiteness and the vacuum result B8.14. The inert Hecke identity above supplies B8.15j. The remaining separation also needs the global period and column-chain arguments, with $C_F\ge6510$ and the asserted eventual rank, gap and level conditions. [B8.15 and quantifier order][b15] · [Column-chain hypotheses][col] · [Eischen–Harris–Li–Skinner, §4.2.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.01776) · [Period ascent on the row](ROW_PERIOD_ASCENT.md)
* **B8.16–18.** B8.16 uses the local theta package, B8.11, and the unitary Siegel-set estimate. Its matching assertion compares the two *twisted* squares after passing to representatives with the required convergence and a common real/finite quotient. B8.17 further requires the Hecke–Tate functional equation and the five toric assertions of B1.14k. The Roadmap derives these via elliptic CM, partial-zeta rationality and integrality, and functional equations. B8.18 adds the unitary Siegel-set and Green inputs. These are theorem-level dependencies of the comparison, in addition to the geometric data on a particular stage. [Twisted-square theorem][b16] · [Toric inputs and their derivation][toric] · [Published versus derived local theta inputs][localinputs]

On $z_W$ the [individual lower-row multiplicity result](LOWER_ROW_MULTIPLICITY.md) and the bottom rational tests now hold together under these hypotheses. Apply the lower-row theta and Hecke comparison of [the weight construction](LOWER_ROW_ASSEMBLY.md). Its other guards are exactly the true group and its marked-place comparison, the division and volume identities, the geometric and core data, $W=W_1(z_W,F'_0)$ and support above the allowed primes. Fine extension and restriction supply them. This gives the intersection law on $z_W$. The row-family constructor uses that law and conjugation stability of $W$ and returns $P'$ with this $W$ and an extension of $z^+$. Thus the remaining analytic dependence is explicit. The same-column application and the inert spherical identity are proved above. A fully independent verification of the global matching and separation arguments is a further task.
