RESULT AND BOUNDARY
-------------------
For fixed eps>0 and real Co,Lb, this component proves the actual finite
orbit_terms/not_good_le/orbit_sum branch for independent point inclusion
probabilities q in [3delta/4,delta]. It gives one explicit x threshold and
the source-compatible positive constant
  Cb=(|Co|+1)*((max(Lb,1))^K+2),
where K=ceil_Nat(8(h+2)/eps), h=2+8eps. At every allowed orbit length
  x^(eps/4)<=n<=Co*x^h,  L2<=Lb,
the probability of a bad orbit, conditional on ANY one of its coordinates
being selected, is <=Cb*x^-2. The orbit base is arbitrary. The exact
ambient selected-and-bad probability is the conditional probability times
the inclusion probability of that selected point, hence <=delta*Cb*x^-2.
Weighted summation gives the input needed by the parent's finite coset
selection, including the literal factor delta and no extra order factor.

This closes a finite probability estimate. It does not construct the final
top set, its biased Frame.Shares property, arithmetic hosts, geometric
points or a global RH/Dirichlet-GRH proof. No Lean source was edited or
compiled. Each declaration/range/file correspondence is in provenance.json.

The uniform-law GII9 argument explains the general mechanism, but is NOT
invoked to infer this biased result. The complete badness event is not
monotone in the selected subset: a marked tile requires empty cells as
well as singleton cells. We therefore prove the nonconstant product
estimates directly. The count-tail part alone uses monotone domination.

LITERAL PARAMETERS AND EVENTS
-----------------------------
For eps>0 put
  theta=eps/8, h=2+8eps, K=ceil_Nat((h+2)/theta), r=K+1,
  delta=x^-theta, u=ceil_Nat(delta^-1), ell=(6r+1)u,
  pi0=10^(-(6r+1)),
  M=ceil_Nat((h+3)log(x)/pi0),
  G=(M+5)ell,
  N=ceil_Nat(max(6eGdelta,(h+3)log_2(x)))+1.
These equal dE, goodU, KE, rE, orbitSmallMultiplicity, goodG' and
orbitWindowCount in the source; in particular N has its final +1.
The source's hE/rE choices do not require eps<1/52 for this lemma.
The global application uses that smaller range, which is a specialization.

On the n coordinates {0,...,n-1}, define
  W(i,L)={(i+j) mod n : 0<=j<L}.
Its cardinality is at most L even if L>n. A complete tile of index s is
[s ell,(s+1)ell), for 0<=s<t=floor(n/ell). Cell c, 1<=c<=6r+1,
is [s ell+(c-1)u,s ell+c u). A tile is Marked exactly when its odd-numbered
cells contain no selected points, and each even-numbered cell contains
one selected point whose absolute orbit coordinate is even. Thus there
are 3r+1 empty cells and 3r singleton-even cells. Cell-number parity and
coordinate parity are distinct conditions; both are preserved here.

The literal GoodOrbitIntervalTileCondition is the conjunction:
  A: every L2-window has at most K selected points;
  Tiles: for every s0<t, some j in {0,...,M} makes tile (s0+j) mod t marked;
  Intervals: every G-window has at most N selected points.
The remaining tail [t ell,n) is not a complete tile. The proofs below
keep it; there is no assumption that ell divides n.

BO1. FINITE PRODUCT LAWS, CONDITIONING AND COUNT-TAIL DOMINATION
-------------------------------------------------------------
For a finite coordinate set S and probabilities 0<=q_i<=1, set
  mu_S(U)=prod_(i in U) q_i * prod_(i in S\U)(1-q_i), U subset S.
Expanding prod_(i in S)(q_i+(1-q_i)) gives sum_U mu_S(U)=1.
Every weight is nonnegative. Finite-sum expansion gives linearity and
monotonicity of expectation immediately.

If i is not in S, split subsets of S union {i} according to whether
they contain i. This gives the exact insertion identity
  E_(S+i) F = (1-q_i) E_S F + q_i E_S[U -> F(U union {i})].
If S1 and S2 are disjoint, subset union identifies their powersets
product with the powerset of S1 union S2 and their mu weights multiply.
Summing unused coordinates therefore gives a factor 1. Consequently:
  E_S F(U intersect A)=E_A F for A subset S;
  E_S prod_j F_j(U intersect A_j)=prod_j E_(A_j) F_j
for pairwise disjoint A_j subset S. These are all the source's
expectation_spec clauses, including its nonconstant probabilities.

For i0 in S and any event E of a selected subset, the same expansion gives
  P(i0 in U and E(U))
    =q_i0 * E_(S\{i0})[1_E(V union {i0})].              (BO1.1)
This identity remains valid even if q_i0=0. When q_i0>0, the second
factor is exactly the conditional probability given i0 selected. In the
actual application q_i0>=3delta/4>0. Thus conditioning does not change
the other probabilities; in particular it does not replace them by their
upper or lower endpoint.

Count-tail domination can also be proved with finite sample spaces.
For each coordinate take a pair (X_i,Y_i) with possible states
  (0,0), (0,1), (1,1)
and probabilities 1-delta, delta-q_i, q_i. They are nonnegative and
sum to one if 0<=q_i<=delta<=1. Make these pairs independent. Their
marginals are Bernoulli(q_i) and Bernoulli(delta), and X_i<=Y_i in
every state. Pad with g-|S| further Bernoulli(delta) variables if |S|<=g.
For every natural b this proves
  P(sum_(i in S) X_i>=b) <= binUpper(g,delta,b).        (BO1.2)
Here binUpper=sum_(j=b)^g binom(g,j)delta^j(1-delta)^(g-j) is the exact
binomial count probability. It is zero for b>g and one for b=0.

If b>0, any set with at least b selected coordinates contains some
selected b-subset. The union bound gives
  P(sum X_i>=b) <= binom(|S|,b)delta^b
                <= binom(g,b)delta^b.                 (BO1.3)
The same inequality holds for b=0, with delta^0=1, and for b>g,
where both sides are zero. This proves both clauses of the literal
tail_le, rather than importing a general probability theorem. The source
uses coordinate induction to prove the same monotone comparison.

BO2. THE EXACT BIASED MARKED-TILE LOWER BOUND
-------------------------------------------
Suppose 0<=delta<=1, u>=1, r>=0 and 3delta/4<=q_i<=delta.
For any cell C of length u,
  P(C empty)=prod_(i in C)(1-q_i)>=(1-delta)^u=:A0.
For a fixed even coordinate j in C, the event that precisely j is
selected has probability
  q_j prod_(i in C\{j})(1-q_i)
    >=(3delta/4)(1-delta)^(u-1).
Every interval of u consecutive integers contains at least floor(u/2)
even integers: pair successive integers, or inject i -> a+(a mod 2)+2i
for 0<=i<floor(u/2) into the even coordinates of [a,a+u).
The singleton events for different j are disjoint, so their union has
probability at least
  A1=floor(u/2)*(3delta/4)*(1-delta)^(u-1).
All 6r+1 cells are disjoint. BO1 therefore gives
  P(tile s marked) >= A0^(3r+1) A1^(3r)
                    =markedLow(delta,r,u)=:pi.        (BO2.1)
This holds for every complete tile, irrespective of the probability
profile or parity of its starting coordinate. Nonnegativity is explicit,
and pi<=1 follows already from (BO2.1) and probability normalization.

Now specialize to u=ceil(delta^-1), 0<delta<=1/100. The ceilings imply
  1<=u delta<=1+delta,
  floor(u/2)delta >= (u-1)delta/2 >=(1-delta)/2>=99/200.
The integral identity
  -log(1-delta)=integral_0^delta 1/(1-t) dt<=delta/(1-delta)
gives
  A0>=exp(-u delta/(1-delta))>=exp(-103/100)>3/10.       (BO2.2)
Indeed (1+delta)/(1-delta)<=101/99<103/100. For an elementary check
of the numerical exponential comparison, the power series gives
  e<=5/2+(1/6)sum_(j>=0)4^-j=49/18<11/4,
  exp(3/100)<=sum_(j>=0)(3/100)^j=100/97.
Hence exp(103/100)<(11/4)(100/97)=275/97<10/3.
The factorial estimate used in the first bound is
(j+3)!>=6*4^j for j>=0.

Since 0<1-delta<=1, its (u-1)st power is at least A0. Thus
  A1 >= (99/200)*(3/4)*(3/10)=891/8000>1/10.
Both factors are at most one; for instance
  A1<=floor(u/2)*(3delta/4)<=3(1+delta)/8<=303/800.
It follows that
  0<=10^(-(6r+1))=pi0<=pi<=1.                          (BO2.3)
This proves the literal markedLow_bounds. The lower factor 3/4 is the
only change to the singleton probability from GII9, and the stronger
delta<=1/100 preserves the source's exact pi0 without increasing r or M.

BO3. FORCED-POINT FAILURE BOUND, INCLUDING THE CYCLIC REMAINDER
------------------------------------------------------------
For this finite lemma take arbitrary natural n,L2,K,r,u,M,G,N and
0<=delta<=1 with u>=1 and (M+1)ell<=n, ell=(6r+1)u. Select coordinates
in {0,...,n-1}\{i0} independently with probabilities in [3delta/4,delta]
and adjoin i0. This is the forced-point law. When i0<n and q_i0>0 it is
the actual conditional law from BO1. It also makes sense at delta=0
without dividing by a zero probability.

Short windows: if a window of length L2 has more than K points after
i0 is inserted, its unforced part has at least K selected points.
Its underlying coordinate set has cardinality at most L2. BO1.3 and
the union bound over all n starts give
  P(not A) <= n binom(L2,K)delta^K.                    (BO3.1)
No assumption L2<n is needed. Here the actual n upper bound is Co*x^h,
so this coarse short-window bound has sufficient exponent; the sharper
split used by GII9 for the larger x^(h+eps/8) ambient range is unnecessary.

Tiles: put t=floor(n/ell). The length hypothesis gives t>=M+1>=1.
For a fixed start s0<t, the tile indices
  (s0+j) mod t, 0<=j<=M,
are distinct, because two congruent values would differ by a multiple of
t of absolute value at most M<t. Remove the index floor(i0/ell), if it
occurs in this set. At least M indices remain. Their complete tiles are
pairwise disjoint, lie in {0,...,n-1}, and do not contain i0. Under the
forced law their coordinates have their original probabilities. Their
marking events are therefore independent, even though their marking
probabilities may differ. Each is at least pi by BO2.1.

If all M+1 original tiles are unmarked, then these remaining tiles are
unmarked. Consequently its probability is at most (1-pi)^M. There are
t<=n starting tiles, so
  P(not Tiles) <= n(1-pi)^M.                           (BO3.2)
If i0 lies in the remainder [t ell,n), its index floor(i0/ell) equals t
and is not among the complete tile indices. No tile is then removed;
weakening M+1 unaffected factors to M preserves the bound. The argument
also works algebraically if i0>=n, although that case is not needed for
conditioning. In particular there is no hidden periodic continuation
of the coordinate parity through the incomplete remainder.

Long windows: if its selected count exceeds N, deleting the possible
forced point leaves at least N selected coordinates. This implies the
weaker source threshold max(N-1,0); source N-1 is natural subtraction.
The unforced underlying set has cardinality at most G. BO1.2 gives
  P(not Intervals) <= n binUpper(G,delta,N-1).         (BO3.3)

The complement of the conjunction is contained in the union of these
three failure events. Summing (BO3.1)--(BO3.3) proves exactly
  P_forced(not GoodOrbitIntervalTileCondition)
    <= n binom(L2,K)delta^K + n(1-pi)^M
         + n binUpper(G,delta,N-1).                   (BO3.4)
This is the literal not_good_le under its supplied finite hypotheses.
No badness-event monotonicity or rotation invariance was used.

BO4. EXPLICIT UNIFORM NUMERICS AND SOURCE-COMPATIBLE CONSTANT
-----------------------------------------------------------
Return to the actual parameters at the start. Let
  A=|Co|+1>0, B=(max(Lb,1))^K>=1,
  C1=(h+3)/pi0+2>0, Cstar=2(6r+1)C1,
  y0=max(1,4Cstar/theta^2,log(100)/theta), x0=exp(y0),
  Cb=A(B+2)>0.
This explicit threshold is a human simplification of the source's
ot_exists_log_le_rpow. It depends only on eps; allowing dependence on
Co,Lb is harmless but unnecessary. Fix any real x>=x0, any naturals
n,L2 with x^(eps/4)<=n<=Co*x^h and L2<=Lb, any allowed probability
profile, and any forced coordinate. If Co or Lb make these inequalities
impossible, the assertion is simply vacuous; the positive constants
remain defined.

Write y=log x. Then y>=y0>=1 and delta<=1/100. From y>=4Cstar/theta^2,
  Cstar(y+1)<=2Cstar y<=theta^2 y^2/2<=exp(theta y)=x^theta.
The last inequality is the quadratic term of the exponential series.
Also u<=delta^-1+1<=2x^theta and
  M+1<=(h+3)y/pi0+2<=C1(y+1).
It follows that
  (M+1)ell<=Cstar(y+1)x^theta<=x^(2theta)
              =x^(eps/4)<=n.                          (BO4.1)
Thus the exact finite tile-fit premise of BO3 is proved, with all
ceilings included. This alone does not assert 3G<=n; the separate local
geometry uses its own already stated sufficiently-large-scale bound.

For the short-window term, theta K>=h+2 and x>=1 give
  delta^K=x^(-theta K)<=x^(-(h+2)),
  binom(L2,K)<=L2^K<=(max(Lb,1))^K=B.
Since n<=Co*x^h<=A*x^h,
  n binom(L2,K)delta^K<=AB*x^-2.                       (BO4.2)

For the marked-tile term, BO2 gives pi>=pi0 and pi<=1. Using
1-z<=exp(-z) for z>=0 and pi0 M>=(h+3)log x,
  (1-pi)^M<=exp(-pi0 M)<=x^(-(h+3)),
  n(1-pi)^M<=A*x^-3.                                 (BO4.3)

For the long-window term, first prove the source helper in a shorter way.
For natural g,b and 0<=delta<=1, if b>=6e g delta, then
  binUpper(g,delta,b)<=exp(-b).                        (BO4.4)
If b=0 both sides are one. If b>g, the tail is zero. Otherwise b>=1,
and the union bound in BO1.3 applied to g Bernoulli(delta) trials gives
  binUpper(g,delta,b)<=binom(g,b)delta^b
    <=g^b delta^b/b!<=(e g delta/b)^b<=6^-b<=e^-b.
The factorial inequality follows from
  log(b!)=sum_(j=1)^b log j>=integral_1^b log t dt
          =b log b-b+1>=b log b-b.
The elementary e<3 already proved in BO2 implies 6^-b<=e^-b.
This establishes the full binomial-tail bound, not merely a point-mass
bound. The source sums a geometric estimate for each point mass instead.

At the actual parameters b=N-1 is exactly the natural ceiling in (BO.1),
so b>=6eGdelta and b>=(h+3)log_2x>=(h+3)log x. Here 0<log 2<1,
because 1<2<e, and log x>=0. Applying (BO4.4) gives
  n binUpper(G,delta,N-1)<=A*x^-3.                    (BO4.5)
Adding (BO4.2), (BO4.3), (BO4.5), and using x>=1, proves
  n binom(L2,K)delta^K+n(1-pi)^M+n binUpper(G,delta,N-1)
    <=A(B+2/x)x^-2<=Cb*x^-2.                          (BO4.6)
Together with (BO4.1), this is the exact conclusion of orbit_terms.
Combining it with BO3 gives the claimed conditional probability bound.
The constants precede x,n,L2, the profile, the base, and the forced point.

BO5. ACTUAL GROUP ORBIT MARGINAL AND WEIGHTED EXPECTATION
-------------------------------------------------------
Let A be a finite abelian group with independent inclusion probabilities
q:A->[3delta/4,delta]. Fix a step v of finite positive order n, a point
a, and a base b satisfying a=b+jv for some natural j. Reduce j modulo
n to obtain i0<n with a=b+i0v. The map
  phi:{0,...,n-1}->A, phi(i)=b+iv,
is injective: phi(i)=phi(j) implies (i-j)v=0; the order n divides the
integer i-j, whose absolute value is less than n, so i=j.

For a prescribed subset U of the orbit coordinates, sum the ambient
product weight over every T with phi^-1(T)=U. Each point outside im(phi)
contributes q(z)+(1-q(z))=1. Hence the resulting weight is exactly
  prod_(i in U) q(phi(i)) * prod_(i notin U)(1-q(phi(i))).
This is the source's fibre_sum/marginal identity. It is an equality of
finite products and sums, not an approximation or a uniform-orbit law.

Let E(U) be any event of the coordinate subset. Marginalization and
(BO1.1) now give the precise source orbit_sum identity
  sum_(T subset A) pw(q,T) 1_{a in T and E(phi^-1(T))}
   =q(a) * E_(q composed phi, [0,n)\{i0})
                   [1_E(V union {i0})].               (BO5.1)
This identity is valid for arbitrary real q as a polynomial identity;
the probabilistic interpretation uses 0<=q<=1. In our application the
conditional expression has no zero-probability ambiguity since q(a)>0.

Take E to be the literal complement of IsGoodOrbit, after identifying
Fin(n) with its natural value labels. Suppose x>=x0 and the order n is
in the BO4 range. BO3--BO4 apply to q composed phi without changing any
parameter. Therefore
  P(a in T and E(phi^-1(T)))
    <=q(a) Cb*x^-2<=delta Cb*x^-2.                    (BO5.2)
The base b may depend on v and a. In the actual topsGroup application,
b=bs(v,a); its only required property is existence of j with b+jv=a.
We do not claim invariance of the parity-sensitive tile event under
rotation, nor that two points on the same orbit necessarily use the
same based event. Uniformity in b and a makes either claim unnecessary.

Finally fix a finite index set P of steps v_p of orders in the same range,
nonnegative real weights omega_p, and a prescribed allowed base b_(p,a)
for each p,a. For
  Z(T)=sum_(p in P) omega_p *
        #{a in T : its prescribed based v_p-orbit is bad},
write the cardinality as a sum of indicators and interchange the two
finite sums with expectation. Equation (BO5.2) gives
  E Z<=delta |A| Cb*x^-2 * sum_(p in P) omega_p.        (BO5.3)
No independence among different primes, steps, orbit events, or points'
badness events is used. If P is empty or all weights are zero, both
sides vanish. The stronger pointwise hypothesis needed by the source
select theorem is precisely (BO5.2), with beta=Cb*x^-2. The parent's
selection proves the final factor-4 bound together with coset counts.

DEPENDENCIES AND WHAT CHANGES FROM GII9
-------------------------------------
All five BO claims are proved here using finite product expansions,
elementary order arithmetic, elementary inequalities for exp/log, and
the exact displayed definitions. There is no retained representation,
cohomological, or host-construction premise in these probability claims.
The actual group/profile/order inequalities are hypotheses of their
application and are supplied separately.

Compared with GII9, the singleton lower bound receives the factor 3/4;
delta<=1/100 restores pi0 unchanged. Unlike GII9's larger ambient upper
exponent h+eps/8, this source branch assumes n<=Co*x^h, so its original
coarse n*binom(L2,K)delta^K bound suffices. BO4 gives an explicit threshold
instead of an asymptotic existence wrapper and shortens the long-tail
proof, but keeps the exact source constant Cb and all parameter ceilings.
BO5 identifies the actual t-dependent based orbit and keeps its exact
selected-point factor q(t). The host identification and simultaneous
biased coset selection are not silently imported into these results.
