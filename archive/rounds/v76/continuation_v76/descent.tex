\section{Descent on the identity cover}

% BEGIN A76T1
\paragraph{A76T1. A value for every base algebra with its lattice normalization.}
Fix the given theta generator, connection and framed point $y$
over a $K=c.F$-algebra $S$. Use the exact odd $N$, allowed
unimodular $\Lambda$, polynomial $P$ and residue function $w$
of the short rule. Write $r=p+q$, $S_0=\mathcal O_E^n$,
$d=[\Lambda\cap S_0:NS_0]^r$, $\mathcal Q=(\mathrm{LevQ})^n$
and $\beta=\beta_H(\Lambda)\ne0$. The section value on an arbitrary
such $S$ is determined by its pullback to any faithfully flat
algebra $B$ carrying the auxiliary data. In $B$ it is
\[
 V_B=(\beta d\,|\mathcal Q|\,N^{\deg P})^{-1}
 \sum_{q\in\mathcal Q}w(q)\sum_i
 \sum_{m\in\operatorname{supp}P}P_m
       \prod_{b=1}^n\nabla_{m_b}F_{qib}.
\]
Here all displayed field coefficients map to $B$. Existence of
one common $B$ for all $q,i,b$ follows from the finite division
and splitting constructions of T73C1. The harmonic evaluations
are independent of their jet charts by G76J3.

There is a point worth checking when comparing witnesses over
different algebras. After mapping both to a common algebra, localize
at a maximal ideal. For fixed $q$, the two tuples of division
points differ by $u$ in the enumerated isotropic group: each
component is $N$-torsion and killed by a power of the designated
half-level ideal. Translation by $u$ reindexes the two complete
enumerations bijectively. In a matched pair, changing the chosen
lifts scales each column by an $N^2$-th root of unity $r_b$.
Its square is the theta-group commutator with that isotropic
element. The product of these commutators is one by isotropy.
Thus the product of the $r_b$ has square one and odd order dividing
$N^2$, hence is one. Since every summand uses one germ per column,
its value survives reindexing. This is the local calculation of
T73C1 with the factor $\beta^{-1}$ carried along. Equality in
all localizations at maximal ideals implies equality in the common
algebra, also for algebras with nilpotents.

Apply the calculation to the two maps
$B\rightrightarrows B\otimes_S B$. Faithfully flat descent for
ring elements gives a unique $V_S$ pulling back to $V_B$. For a
map $S\to T$ use $T\otimes_S B$ and compare there after a further
faithfully flat extension if a second witness was used over $T$.
Injectivity of faithfully flat maps gives $V_T$ equal to the image
of $V_S$ on the transported frame. This proves both uniqueness
and naturality for the normalized value on the actual coefficient
field.
% END A76T1

\paragraph{A76T2. The actual rule and its uniqueness on the test rings.}
Take precisely $d=\mathrm{idCover}(\nabla,n)$ of ICL11.
Its coefficient map and level map are identities, its character
is one, and $c_\delta=1$. Put $w$ equal to the residue function
of the chosen admissible lower tuple. Restrict the natural value of
A76T1 to the framed points over the original reduced test algebras.
This defines a rule $f$. Under triangular reframing with diagonal
$t$, the row-highest equations give $\prod_{b,i}t_{b i}^{a(b,i)}$
in the source convention. The scalar change of the theta generator
occurs once in each of the $n$ columns. These are exactly the two
weight factors of $\mathrm{sec}(X_o,c,a,n)$, by the triangular
frame calculation of T73C2. Thus $f$ belongs to this space.
For a geometric point choose its own frame as its lift. Any
admissible lift over the identity gives the same evaluation by
transport of the rigidified generators and the framed tangent
vectors. The prescribed product of the cover coefficient with the
short value is $1\cdot V$. Consequently $f$ satisfies
$\mathrm{IsShortRule}$ with the given $\Lambda,N,x_0,P$; every
tuple allowed in $\mathrm{shortGen}$ produces its own such
section.

Conversely let $f'$ be any section satisfying this rule on
geometric points. If $y$ is over an original test ring $A$,
then for every homomorphism $A\to k$ to an algebraically closed
field, transport $y$ to $k$ and take its identity lift. Evaluations
of $f'$ and $V_A$ have the same image there. The intersection of
the kernels of these maps is the nilradical (use the algebraic
closure of the fraction field of each prime quotient). Each such
$A$ is reduced, so $f'(y)=V_A$. This also identifies
$\beta_H(\Lambda) f'$ on every test ring with the raw lattice
value having its $\beta$-factor removed. The argument for the
unique natural $V$ on general algebras used the faithfully flat
equalizer; reducedness enters only in this converse for a section
specified by geometric values.
% END A76T2
