# The theta datum on the fourth-stage first-pass column

Fix a [fourth-stage column](FIRST_PASS_FOURTH_STAGE.md) $l$ for the normalized extension with origin, at the chosen depth, and write $D=w.D(Z)$. Its row slot is $\mathrm{RX}$. There is a theta object $X$ on $D$ with the full comparison $\mathrm{ThT}(l.n,X)$, under the standing adelic unitary Weil representation, quotient rapid decay and discrete-series inputs specified below. The resulting row maps are isometries on every used tuple through the top index $m$. The theta intertwining identity holds at *every* tuple and every $0\le j<m$. This construction is pointwise in $l$. [Native row slot][slots] · [Level-four requirement][level] · [Fields of the theta comparison][tt]

Put $U=\{\mu_0\}\cup\{\mu_{\rm At}(v):v\text{ a coefficient place}\}$. A tuple lying in several parts of this union has a single stored row. For $\mu\in U$, the weak geometric reading and the pinned characters give the level characters, group models and real theta chain of the actual realization. The tuple comparison identifies its character with $l.n.\mathrm{tp}.\xi(\mu)$. The sharp row gives isometric multiplicity-space readings up to positive Petersson factors $c_j$, together with the adjoint pairing against the specified edge operator $i_{\mu,j}$. At the top use the range of the given projected $X'$-space and transport it to $Y_m$ by its adelic isometry. [Used tuples][used] · [Reading on the chosen column][read] · [Both endpoints of the weak chain][weak]

Here is how the measure and test normalizations give the two identities on a used tuple. Evaluate the multiplicity maps $e_j$ on pure unit vectors $d_j$ of the archimedean models. The vectors can be chosen together across the entire chain. Each nonzero polynomial intertwiner has a nonzero matrix coefficient on pure tensors. For each edge fix a polynomial for which this happens. The finite list of coefficients, with vectors at adjacent vertices shared, consists of functions polynomial on real lines, each nonzero somewhere. Successively take lines from a vector where the preceding coefficients are nonzero to one where the next is nonzero. Avoiding finitely many roots gives one choice where every coefficient is nonzero. Normalize each archimedean factor to unit norm and rescale each edge polynomial to make its coefficient one. This also works for the final $X'$-edge. [Common polynomial tests and unit vectors][frame]

Write $k_j=m_Y(\mu,j)\,\mathrm{petC}(Y_j)>0$ for the required quotient factor. Comparison of the transported Haar integral with the normalized quotient integral supplies $r_j>0$ at each row. With its row Haar comparison use $s_j=(c_j r_j/k_j)^{1/2}$ and the cusp form $s_j e_j(x)(d_j)$, pulled back under the group identification. Schur's pairing at unit norm gives its normalized Petersson integral against the corresponding form for $y$ as $\langle x,y\rangle$, since $s_j^2 k_j/r_j=c_j$. For an edge from $W=Y_{j+1}$ to $V=Y_j$, let $r_W,r_V$ be the Haar comparisons in the edge integral and the target pairing, and write $k_W,k_V,s_W,s_V$ for their row factors. Orient and transport the original nonzero Schwartz test, then multiply it by
$$
              a=\frac{c_V r_V r_W}{k_V k_W s_Vs_W}>0 .
$$
The source integral evaluates to the lift multiplied by $(k_W/r_W)a s_W$. Pair it with the target row. The total real scalar is $(k_W/r_W)a s_Ws_V(k_V/r_V)=c_V$, and the chosen archimedean coefficient is one. Conjugate symmetry and the weak edge identity therefore give $\langle i_{\mu,j}y,x\rangle$ in the source convention for the integral of the conjugate lift times the target form. Rational left invariance and Haar transport justify the changes of domain, and integrability follows from the fitted chain and the Petersson pairing on cuspidals. The edge uses its own source Haar comparison if the stored chain uses a different source measure there. [Haar and row normalization][haar] · [Edge scalar and integral][edge] · [Terminal edge][top]

For $\mu\notin U$, the sharp intertwiner $i_{\mu,j}$ is zero by the extra row witness. Take all cusp-form maps to be zero and retain a nonzero theta test at each required edge from the character datum. Both the lift integral and the subsequent pairing are integrals of zero, and $\langle i_{\mu,j}y,x\rangle=0$. The Petersson isometry is only required on $U$. Thus no assertion that the abstract unused multiplicity spaces vanish is needed. Choose the two constructions tuple by tuple in the same character frame. Uniqueness of the level Hecke characters and of the standard additive character fixes the splittings used in the edges. [Vanishing supplied by the row][off] · [Assembly on and off the used set][both]

Choose the normalized infinite family, the standard additive character of sign $-1$, the adelic Weil action with its equal-rank compatibility, and the tower. The automorphic realization with its Petersson, top, Bessel and lift integrals takes the preceding concrete row maps and tests as its input. The installed real comparison is $l.\mathrm{real}$. The resulting $X$ satisfies the row intertwining law and retains the used-row pairings. The construction gives compatible character comparisons for every tuple-character record with the indicated $\xi$. Specializing this comparison to $l.nch,l.n.\mathrm{tp}$ gives the required finite character agreement on the actual column. Together with the full Weil equivariance, the provision on real models, the sign of the additive character and the row intertwining law, these are the fields of $\mathrm{ThT}$. The analytical inputs used by this construction are the adelic unitary Weil theorem, cusp rapid decay, existence and unitary uniqueness for the prescribed signed real discrete-series models, and existence for the rational-parameter models. The published wrapper also accepts Casselman–Wallach globalization and the Harish–Chandra module theorem, which do not occur in its displayed proof. The reconstruction supplies the exact quotient-height version of rapid decay. **Passage to the built core.** The two ambient lattices and the divisor with its universal object condition already have first-pass constructions. In addition to the theta comparison above, the original core assembly uses the host's compatible Haar gauge, the split column's layer comparison, and the integral lattice and kernel certificate evaluated on the *chosen* divisor $Y$. The pin constructs the last from a uniform bound on the column, adapted bases for its jets, an intrinsic lattice layer and compatible local levels and maps at the served places. Its input $Q_p$ comparison is the one proved for $Y$. The resulting kernel is used in the object itself, so its entry certificate refers to precisely that object. On the common eventual range of these producers the built record takes its character-and-origin slot verbatim from $l.nx$. The same origin is available in the resulting core. A full human proof of the named kernel and measure producers requires their underlying constructions beyond the theta calculation here.
