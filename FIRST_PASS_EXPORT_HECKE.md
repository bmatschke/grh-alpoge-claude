# The Hecke operator on the indefinite row's lattice of bounded forms

Fix the real-side realization used for export, with its original rational forms, moduli interpretation, marked level actions and chosen comparison with cuspidal sections. Here “real side” refers to the realization of the indefinite automorphic row. The bound is at a **finite** place $\lambda$ of its coefficient field. Write $B_\lambda$ for the rational forms whose evaluation has norm at most one at every integral framed test above $\lambda$. Let $P$ be an admitted split Hecke prime of the CM field, of residue characteristic $p$, off the marked level on both conjugate sides, with $\lambda\nmid p$. For each permitted $0\le k\le r$ the rational Hecke operator in the prescribed reading satisfies
$$
 H_{P,k} B_\lambda\subseteq B_\lambda .
$$
It commutes with the finite type-group action. Both assertions hold at every such prime for every row of a real-side realization. Transport to any compatible series realization gives the two export clauses called $\mathrm{SupHk}$ and $\mathrm{CommG}$, at every nonzero index and for both coefficient embeddings. The statements require the standing rapid decay of cuspidal forms. [Definition of the bound and its level][ball] · [Scope of the two export clauses][sup] · [Finite group actions in export][comm] · [Use in the export theorem][export]

**The prescribed normalization.** Put $q=|k(P)|$ and let $\mu_{0,j}$ be the row's base character. In its indefinite automorphic reading the operator is the right-coset sum with multiplier
$$
 \mu_{0,j}(P)^{-k}\,q^{-k n_V/2},
$$
where $n_V$ is even. On the moduli side take the transformation of the cusp section by the split $P$-isogeny correspondence. For each allowed kernel the trace construction transports the theta generator. The finite kernel sum is a section of the specified weight and is cuspidal. The pinned split chart and fixed local vector give a bijection between the kernels at a test point and the coset representatives. Under this bijection the automorphic character factor is the same in each term. Reindexing the finite sum therefore identifies the geometric transform with this normalized automorphic operator. The chosen reading is injective, so the resulting section represents the specified rational operator on the row. The local norm in the next step is taken on this very section. **Evaluation at a test above $\lambda$.** Take an integral framed object over a complete discrete valuation ring $A$ above $\lambda$, with fraction field $K_A$. Its Hecke kernels can be evaluated over an algebraic closure of $K_A$. The isogeny and its inverse up to multiplication by $p^2$ descend to a finite extension there. Extend the valuation and pass to a further finite extension on which the target has an integral frame. Because $p$ is a unit in $A$, the induced maps on the relevant integral differential modules are inverse up to a unit. The adapted-frame comparison at the target differs by a permitted triangular frame change with unit diagonal. The weight character on this change is a unit. Thus evaluating a section from $B_\lambda$ at that geometric Hecke target gives an element integral over $A$. This uses the full class of integral tests, including those outside the good-reduction subclass. The construction separately preserves good reduction for a test in that subclass. [Integral frames and descent of the isogeny][frames] · [Change of frame and values][values] · [Test points and lattices on the fitted family](FIRST_PASS_MODULI_POINTS.md)

The kernel set is finite. Its evaluation sum is integral over $A$, and the geometric scalar multiplying it is a $\lambda$-unit since its norm factors are powers of $q=p^f$. The value of the transformed rational section at the original framed point belongs to $K_A$. Since $A$ is integrally closed, it lies in $A$. Pulling this inequality back by the row's point and evaluation identifications proves the asserted unit-ball bound. In this calculation the character factor in the complex formula was already accounted for by the term-by-term frame comparison. No estimate of an arbitrary complex scale at $\lambda$ is involved. **Type restrictions and transport.** The double coset is supported away from the marked places where the finite group acts. These actions commute in the automorphic model, and injectivity of the row reading transfers commutation to the rational operator. It therefore preserves each isotypic character condition and commutes with every finite-group projector used for good forms. After extension of coefficients the lattice generated by the unit ball is stable by linearity. Intersecting it with the extended type or good space preserves stability of the intersection. These arguments work over both the small coefficient field and the full row field, for every valuation ring lying over $\lambda$. The fitted real-side theorem supplies the common frame for these calculations, using cuspidal rapid decay for spanning and the pinned evaluations to identify each operator. Finally the series reading identifies the stored Hecke maps with the transported operators up to the specified local unit. This proves the two export clauses on the actual series datum. There is a useful consequence at $\lambda\mid p$ with a different quantifier. The original rational integral form lattice is generated over the integers of the small field by finitely many forms which span the rational row. For each generator the image under $H_{P,k}$ has a fixed denominator in this field. At the finitely many primes above $p$, a sufficiently high power of $q$ clears it. Away from $p$ the bound already proved applies and $q$ is a unit. Take the largest exponent needed by the finitely many generators. Then $q^a H_{P,k}$ preserves the integral lattice and its span over the integers of the full coefficient field. The exponent can depend on this operator and the fitted row. In the export argument the other valuation estimate concerns the theta-series coefficients as the auxiliary character varies and has its own quantifiers.
