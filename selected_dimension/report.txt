Purpose and status
==================
The selected-symbol theorem is SelectionLemmas.Conclusion2, defined in
GrowthBounds/ValuationLowestDegreeRename.lean. It has both a quantitative
rank conclusion and a normal-degree conclusion. The rank concerns an
integral direct summand whose basis consists of actual allowed generators.
The degree assertion concerns the entire span of those generators.

This note proves the integral selection, determinant nonvanishing, and
normal-degree assertions. It also gives elementary proofs of several
finite-dimensional and counting steps used in the rank argument. It does
not claim the entire quantitative selected-symbol rank theorem proved.
In particular, a primitive selection lemma cannot establish the number
of generators left after the path restrictions. All remaining hypotheses
are identified below and in provenance.json.

SD1. Selecting a primitive lattice without enlarging the generator set
=====================================================================
Let O be a DVR with uniformizer pi and residue field f. Let S be a finite
set of monomials, M=O^S the corresponding coefficient module, and G a
set of polynomials supported on S. Let d be the dimension of the span of
their reductions in f^S. Choose g_1,...,g_d from G whose reductions form
a basis of that span. This is possible by elementary vector-space basis
selection from a spanning set; G itself need not be finite.

Write the g_i as the columns of an |S|-by-d matrix C. Reduction has column
rank d, so some d coefficient rows form a square matrix A with determinant
nonzero modulo pi. Therefore det A is a unit of O, and A^{-1} has entries
in O. Let pr be the map that reads those d coefficients from ANY polynomial
in the ambient polynomial module. Set R=A^{-1} pr and T=ker R. If
i:O^d -> O[X] sends e_i to g_i, then R i=id. Thus

             O[X] = i(O^d) direct-sum T.

The g_i are a basis of i(O^d), all belong to G, and rank i(O^d)=d. The zero
rank case uses the empty matrix and the zero summand. This is precisely
the source RestSel. It also shows saturation directly: if a x lies in the
summand and a != 0, the T-coordinate of x is zero because O[X] is torsion
free. No completion of O, characteristic-zero argument, or integral
isotypic decomposition is involved.

Source route: SelIsComplRestrictSupport.sel_key, sel_li, sel_sat, restSel.
The source divides relations by pi, proves independence/saturation, and
then obtains a complement. The unit-minor proof above is our shorter
replacement; it proves the same conclusion with the same generator set.

SD2. Residue independence and product assembly
=============================================
First suppose L is a finite free saturated submodule of a polynomial
module and b_1,...,b_h is an O-basis of L. If a relation among the reduced
b_i vanishes, lift its coefficients to c_i in O. Then sum c_i b_i=pi P,
because each coefficient of that polynomial is divisible by pi. Saturation
gives P in L; expressing it in the b_i basis shows c_i is divisible by pi
for every i. Hence the reductions of the b_i are independent. This is
also the familiar equality L intersect pi M = pi L.

A support restriction is saturated over any domain: a nonzero scalar
cannot erase a nonzero coefficient. Intersections of saturated modules
are saturated. Therefore an already saturated segment module remains
saturated after imposing its fixed multidegree and digit support bounds.
The source uses this in LamSatRestrict.restLamA_of. Its input is the
specific saturation assertion in NestedTypeBound for XX'. The general
argument here does not silently replace that identification: the actual
XX' identification must either be proved from its integral path basis
or retained explicitly.

Here is the precise tensor-product argument used next. For each family
index f and segment j, let V_fj be a finite-dimensional polynomial space
over f with allowed monomial support S_fj and basis v_fji. A substitution
maps a monomial m to X^{E_j m}. Suppose

     (f,(m_j)_j) |-> ell_f + sum_j E_j m_j

is injective when m_j belongs to S_fj. The direct sum over f of tensor
products of the free monomial spaces f^{S_fj} has a monomial basis indexed
by those tuples. The indicated substitution/product map takes that basis
injectively into distinct monomials of the target ring. It is therefore
injective. Its restriction to the tensor products of the V_fj is injective
as well, giving a family of sum_f product_j dim(V_fj) independent products.
This is the content of lam_li_fixed and lam_li_all. It is a linear
statement; no assertion that products of arbitrary polynomial bases are
automatically independent is being made.

The actual source exponent map is lamL(la)+sum_j imgMon(lamG_j,lamE_j,m_j),
where lamE_j(x,c)=p^(dist(j,x)+R_j). Its local recovery equation is

                  n = ell + P(u+Q v).

Here 0<=ell<P; at a terminal-frame plane coordinate 0<=u<Q. Reading n
modulo P recovers ell, then dividing and reducing modulo Q recovers u,
and division recovers v. A non-frame late letter has no old-frame copy,
so v=0 and no upper bound on u is needed. At normal coordinates old-frame
variables are absent, again v=0. The source LamGeo conditions guarantee
one late occurrence, at most one old-frame occurrence, and the factor
P_old=P_late*Q at an overlap. Row sums then recover the family parameter e.
These conditions explain why simple tensor multiplication is legitimate.
The full verification of LamGeo, the row-degree matching RestLamDeg, and
the specific XX' saturation input have not all been expanded here. SD2
is a proved product-assembly lemma under these explicit data, not a claim
that the entire source RestLam has already been discharged.

SD3. A nonzero determinant multiplier in every residue characteristic
=====================================================================
We spell out the geometric input. A valid periodic frame F has r ordered
even frame letters fr(j,0)<...<fr(j,r-1), consecutive frames are disjoint
and ordered, and fr(j+J,i)=fr(j,i)+n', with J>=1. All frame letters belong
to the periodically lifted selected set TT. Segment j consists of the
selected letters from the first letter of frame j-1 to the last of frame
j. Its late letters lie after the last letter of frame j-1.

For a late letter x, take the last r even selected letters before x in
that segment; call them y_1<...<y_r. At least r exist, namely frame j-1.
The r entries of any frame occupy distinct residue positions modulo n':
the whole frame lies strictly before the next frame, and hence before
the start of its translate J frames later, which is n' further on.
Consequently every interval [u,u+n') contains r distinct periodic
translates of the entries of a fixed frame, all even and selected.

Suppose y_r >= y_1+n'. Choose those r frame translates in [y_1,y_1+n').
They all lie in the segment and before x, and are distinct from y_r.
Together with y_r they give at least r+1 even selected letters in
[y_1,x). But by construction y_1,...,y_r are exactly the last r such
letters. This contradiction proves y_r-y_1<n', and hence the y_i occupy
distinct residue positions modulo n'. This is the mathematical content
of late_vectors_distinct_tops.

Consider det(X_{y_i,c})_{i,c=1}^r after embedding in plane coordinates
and substituting X_(y_i,c) -> Z_(y_i mod n',c)^{p^{dist(j,y_i)}}. Distinct
residue rows make it possible to assign diagonal variables 1 and all
off-diagonal variables 0. All exponents are positive. The resulting
matrix is the identity, so the substituted determinant is not zero over
any nonzero field. This proof does not require that its total degree be
less than the field size: it proves a formal polynomial is nonzero by
one explicit evaluation.

The source multiplier is

 Delta = product_j product_(late x in j)
         ph_j(det(X_(row(j,x,i),c))^{s(j,x)}),
 s(j,x)=floor(Bo(j,x)/(k^2 Y)).

Every reduced factor is nonzero. The residue polynomial ring is a domain,
so their finite product is nonzero. The result is exactly
DiscriminantNonzeroModUniformiser. Different determinant factors need not
be simultaneously made 1 by a single evaluation; their product is
nonzero because the polynomial ring is a domain.

SD4. The normal-degree estimate, with a slightly sharper intermediate bound
============================================================================
Let k=r+K, N=|TT|, and Lcal=CLa Ns^2 log a. The source degree vsDeg counts
the final K coordinates in even rows and the first r coordinates in odd
rows. As n' is even, parity is preserved on reduction of a row modulo n'.
For a segment monomial m, define ordDeg_x by the same parity convention.

Every monomial in XX'_j is supported on segment letters, and its normal
variables are supported only on late letters. The old-frame letters are
even: the validity condition excludes selected letters between adjacent
frame letters, so the selected letters in the old-frame interval are
exactly those even frame letters. Old-frame plane variables therefore
have normal weight zero. For every support monomial m,

 vsDeg(ph_j m) <= sum_(late x) p^{dist(j,x)} ordDeg_x(m).

In fact the nonzero-weight terms give the displayed sum directly; the
inequality formulation is sufficient and matches the source. By the
definition of XX', ordDeg_x(m)<=o(j,x), where

 o(j,x) = Lcal*a/(p*k*p^{dist(j,x)})
          + (10 if x is even, 1 if x is odd)*a*p^{-(L1+dist(j,x))}.

It follows term by term that

 p^{dist(j,x)} o(j,x) <= A+10B,
 A=Lcal*a/(p*k),  B=a*p^{-L1}.

Let e_j=fr(j,r-1). The late letters of segment j are exactly the selected
letters in (e_{j-1},e_j]. These intervals partition (e_{-1},e_{-1}+n']
as j runs over one period 0,...,J-1. Reduction modulo n' is injective on
this interval and covers the selected positions, so each member of TT
is counted once. The source only needs the weaker total count <=N.
Thus the product of all segment factors has normal degree <=N(A+10B).

The full generator is that product times a permitted label monomial X^ell,
and has fixed row degree d_t=deg_t-E'_t on TT and zero degree outside TT.
For a nonzero generator, every surviving monomial of the full generator
contains X^ell, so ell is bounded coordinatewise by that monomial. The
zero-generator case is vacuous. Hence ell vanishes outside TT. At a selected row t,
choose its late representative x in some segment. The label definition
gives ell_(t,c)<p^{dist(j,x)}<=Q. Counting at most k coordinates per row
therefore gives vsDeg(ell)<=NkQ.

Consequently every generator, and hence every polynomial in its O-span,
has support of normal degree at most

             N(A+10B+kQ) <= 10N(A+B+kQ).

The last step uses nonnegativity of A,B,Q, which follows from the source
hypotheses (in particular a>=1 and CLa>=0). Multiplication adds the weighted
degree; taking a sum or multiplying by a scalar can only remove support.
This proves RestDeg and the degree clause of Conclusion2 with Cdeg=10.
The sharper first expression is our retained simplification; none of the
downstream source constants is changed.

SD5. Rank transfer through a nonzero multiplier
==============================================
Let Nbar be the residue-field span of the actual integral generators and
Lambdabar the residue-field span of the source coarse generators. Suppose
that for each coarse generator u there exists an actual generator g with

                         overline(Delta*u)=overline(g).

Then multiplication by overline(Delta) maps Lambdabar into Nbar. SD3 shows
that this multiplication is injective, so dim(Nbar)>=dim(Lambdabar). SD1
produces a direct summand Sg in the actual integral span with

                 rank_O Sg = dim_f Nbar >= dim_f Lambdabar.

All these spaces lie in finite monomial-degree blocks where needed, so
their dimensions are finite. The congruence is precisely the separate
source GeneratorModuloUniformiser; it is not automatic from a nonzero
Delta or from characteristic-zero generation.

The coarse generators have the form X^ell product_j phc_j(y_j), where
y_j belongs to a segment space M_j with fixed row degrees and bounded
terminal-frame plane exponents. phc_j raises exponents by
p^(dist(j,x)+R_j), and labels have the corresponding enlarged digit bound.
The source proves the congruence by splitting a coarse label into its low
digits and the next R_j digits, then replacing the segments modulo the
uniformizer by integral allowed path combinations after multiplication
by the determinant powers. The latter step uses ColumnWordReductionOfFour,
SegmentReductionLiftsMultihomogeneous, and the exact NestedTypeBound lift.
Those statements require a further human expansion.

If dim(Lambdabar)>=q^{-rK} B0, then the same is true for rank Sg. For the
alternative bound B0/nPl, there are at least q^{rK} r-planes in f_q^k:
the graphs of all linear maps f_q^r -> f_q^K are distinct. If B0>=0 this
implies B0/nPl<=q^{-rK}B0; if B0<0 the desired lower bound is automatic
from nonnegativity of rank. This is the final finite-field-plane conversion
in second_estimate_of_rank_bound, and uses no exact Grassmannian formula.

SD6. Residue classes in a truncated cube, and terminal path degrees
=================================================================
Fix integers r>=2, Q>2b>=0, and c,rho. There are Q^{r-1} tuples
(u_1,...,u_r) in {0,...,Q-1}^r satisfying c+sum_i u_i=rho mod Q: freely
choose r-1 entries and the remaining entry is uniquely determined.
For any one coordinate, imposing a specified value leaves Q^{r-2}
solutions, again by solving for a different coordinate. There are 2b
forbidden values in [Q-2b,Q). The union bound therefore leaves at least

                  Q^{r-1}-2rb Q^{r-2}
                = Q^{r-1}(1-2rb/Q)

solutions in the truncated cube. The bound is valid even when its right
side is negative. This is exactly ResidueClassCount. The source derives
it through the longer LDdigits recursion; the union-bound proof is our
replacement. The restriction r>=2 matters when counting a fixed
coordinate; Q need not be prime and the residue ring need not be a field.

For a positive horizontal-strip step mu -> mu' of total degree d,
interlacing gives mu'_(i+1)<=mu_i, so summing and telescoping yields

                  d=sum_i(mu'_i-mu_i)<=mu'_1-mu_k.

In the source terminal-frame construction, mu'_1=b=beS. All preceding
negative steps have total degree at most b: the terminal frame steps
are all positive, and the sum defining beS includes all earlier degrees.
Starting from zero, no coordinate can be less than minus the total
negative degree, so mu_k>=-b. Hence d<=2b. This proves the exact
TauDegreeOnPathBound independently of any quantitative good-path count.

Now assume 4Yb<=Q and Y>=1. For each terminal-frame row, append an r-tuple
of auxiliary exponents u_ic<Q-2b and prescribe
d_i+sum_c u_ic=rho_i mod Q. The count above is at least
Q^{r-1}(1-r/(2Y)). The rows can be chosen independently. The resulting
e_i=d_i+sum_c u_ic is less than rQ, and u_ic+2b<Q as required by Idx.
The original path and its standard terminal normal index are retained,
so this map to the disjoint union over e of Idx is injective. Thus the
source RestMon factor is

             [Q^{r-1} max(0,1-r/(2Y))]^r

per good path. If the displayed inner factor is nonpositive, the source
bound is simply zero. The good-path definition and its threshold depend
only on the earlier degrees, so changing the terminal-frame row degrees
to e_i preserves it. This is why the congruence count can be made for each
good path separately. The actual parameter estimate 4Y beS<=Qc, called
BetaRatioBound, remains separate; the preceding proof does not supply it.

SD7. Compressing the final multiplicative losses
===============================================
Let integers J,N,r and real k,Y,alpha satisfy J<=N, N>=1, r>=2, k>=5,
Y>=1, and 1-1/(32Y)<=alpha<=1. Set

                eta=(1-5/Y)(1-1/Y)(1-r/(2Y)).

Suppose 1-Nr^2 k/Y>0. Then Y>Nr^2 k>=r^2 k, so every factor of eta and
the lower bound on alpha is in [0,1]. The elementary inequality
product_i(1-x_i)>=1-sum_i x_i for 0<=x_i<=1 gives

 alpha eta >= 1-(1/32+5+1+r/2)/Y >= 1-(7+r/2)/Y.

Let b0=7+r/2. The hypotheses imply b0<=r^2 k, hence b0/Y<1. As alpha<=1
and J<=N,

 alpha^J eta^N >= (alpha eta)^N
                 >= (1-b0/Y)^N
                 >= 1-Nb0/Y
                 >= 1-Nr^2 k/Y.

The third inequality is Bernoulli's inequality. This is sum_total_loss.
If the desired final rank lower bound has nonpositive coefficient, it
follows from rank>=0 without using this product estimate. One should not
apply the displayed product estimate unconditionally when eta could have
negative factors: the positive-target case is part of the argument.

The source alpha is 1-a^{-Ns^2}. The step alpha>=1-1/(32Y), together with
the individual multiplicative bounds 1-5/Y,1-1/Y,1-r/(2Y), is established
by the separate SumFacts/RestSum computation. Our proof compresses their
final combination and explains Crk=1, but does not assume those preceding
estimates have thereby been proved for the actual data.

SD8. Exact theorem and remaining dependencies
============================================
The literal numerical hypotheses of Dat.Hyp / Conclusion2 include:

* prime p>=13, even n', r>=2, k=r+K and a valid periodic frame;
* a<=deg_t<=2a for t in TT;
* at most Ns letters in each segment, Ns<=N1 and Ns<=2|TT|,
  and Ns+r<=K;
* p^{dist(j,x)}<=Q, Y>=1;
* Ck Ns^2 log a<=k;
* 128 k^2 Ns Y^2<=p^{L1-1};
* E'_t<=E0 and 32 p Y k^2 Ns Q^2(E0+mStar+1)<=a.

The source SegmentDegree.secondPartClosed chooses the uniform threshold
C0=1088 and gives Crk=1, Cdeg=10 once CLa,Ck>=1088, under the stated AdmB
interfaces. In the unramified quadratic setting of Conclusion2, it yields
a complemented Sg with a basis in the actual generator set, satisfying

 rank_O Sg >= q^{-rK}(1-|TT|r^2 k/Y)
                  product_(t in TT) choose(deg_t+k-1,k-1),
 q=p^{n'},

and the corresponding denominator nPl(f_q,k,r) lower bound. SD4 proves
the degree conclusion; SD1 supplies the exact primitive selection once
the residue rank has been bounded; SD3 supplies multiplier nonvanishing.

The remaining rank construction has the following explicit frontier.

1. GeneratorModuloUniformiser: multiply coarse generators by Delta and
   lift their reductions into the allowed integral generator set. Its
   current source route is generator_modulo_uniformiser_of_adm ->
   column_word_reduction_of_four -> segment_reduction_of_column_words,
   with numerical column bounds and the specific NestedTypeBound lift.

2. RestLam: the actual segment spaces have the claimed saturated residue
   bases, and the allowed label/degree families give injective mixed-radix
   monomial assembly and exact total row degree. SD2 proves the abstract
   linear algebra and explains the two division steps. The complete
   geometric and degree-identification verification is still retained.

3. RestSeg: construct enough independent famEl in M_j. It uses RestMem,
   RestInd, the GoodPathWeight abundance estimate, and RestMon. SD6 proves
   the residue count and path-head bound, and the deduction of RestMon
   once BetaRatioBound is supplied. The quantitative abundance estimate
   is the major remaining combinatorial part: its admissible paths retain
   at least 1-a^{-Ns^2} of the indicated product of binomial dimensions.

4. RestPad and RestSum: the selected digit families obey the row-padding
   hypotheses, and summing their products yields the claimed global
   factor q^{-rK} and binomial product. SD7 proves the final error
   combination; it does not prove the preceding factor estimates or
   the degree-family summation identities.

RestCountOf.restCount_of combines items 2--4 by finite sums and products;
Coarse.restRank_of then combines them with item 1 and SD3; finally
NonemptyDvrSettingOfStandard.second_estimate_of_rank_bound invokes SD1
and SD4. This dependency description narrows the next local target without
presenting the source theorem as an already reconstructed human proof.

Scope and validation
====================
All arguments concern the pointwise local symbol construction under the
exact supplied frame/size data. They do not construct the global frames,
prove the Type-I alternative, or discharge the global theta/cohomology
and spectral estimates. They also do not enlarge Dirichlet GRH to general
automorphic or Dedekind GRH. The source locators and hashes are generated
from unchanged bytes at the pinned commit. No Lean build or independent
kernel replay is claimed.
