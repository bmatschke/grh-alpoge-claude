# The square Selmer matrix at a given auxiliary conductor

Fix a good signature of the fitted untwisted column at an eligible index. Let $O_0$ be its base coefficient DVR, $k$ its finite residue field, $T$ the integral row representation after the specified scalar twist, and $r$ its rank. Take any prescribed finite list of the anticyclotomic ray coordinates, trivial above $\ell$, and write $Q$ for their conductor pairs, $\Delta$ for their product of cyclic $\ell$-groups and $\Lambda=O_0[\Delta]$. On $T\otimes_{O_0}B$, for a discrete $\ell$-primary $\Lambda$-module $B$, let $G_E$ act with the inverse universal character. Write $S(B)$ for its Selmer group with the specified local conditions. There exists one square matrix $\Phi_Q$ over the maximal ideal of $\Lambda$ such that, naturally in all $B$,
$$
 S(B)\simeq\ker(\Phi_Q:B^a\longrightarrow B^a),\qquad a=\dim_k S(k).
$$
This allows $a=0$. The construction holds for each such family of primes, before imposing any condition on a coefficient of the determinant. The Roadmap book gives the representation argument in Lemma B8.07g and verifies its local hypotheses in Lemma B8.07k. **Local dimensions and exactness.** The ring $\Lambda$ is finite free over $O_0$, complete and local. Its reduction is a group ring of a finite $\ell$-group over $k$, whose augmentation ideal is nilpotent. Thus every simple coefficient is $k$ and the universal character is trivial on it. Put $W=T\otimes k$. The [residual irreducibility on the fitted column](FIRST_PASS_POINT_COMPARISON.md) and $r\ge2$ give $H^0(E,W)=H^0(E,W^\vee(1))=0$. Local unramifiedness outside the specified exceptional places comes from the row and its twisting character. At $v\mid\ell$ use the [labelled jumps on the original column](FIRST_PASS_FILTERED_DIMENSIONS.md). After the scalar twist the degree lists at conjugate embeddings pair by $a_\tau\mapsto t-1-a_\tau$. The gap in the grid puts every degree at most $-2$ or at least $t+1$. Hence both the representation and its Tate dual have no local rational invariants. Each conjugate pair contributes $r$ negative degrees in total. For $d_v=\dim_{\operatorname{Frac}(O_0)} H_f^1(E_v,T\otimes\operatorname{Frac}(O_0))$, the Bloch–Kato local dimension formula gives $\sum_{v\mid\ell}d_v=r[E:\mathbb Q]/2$. [Bloch and Kato, Corollary 3.8.4][bk] · [Pinned jump computation][jump]

Use the coefficients of the actual integral cocycles whose rational classes belong to $H_f^1$. Their lattice has rank $r+d_v$. Rational coboundaries contribute $r$, and continuity lets one clear denominators on each basis vector since the decomposition group is compact. Conversely finitely many point and coordinate evaluations separate rational cocycles, so the module of integral ones is bounded. It is saturated in the functions with integral values by pointwise division. A basis therefore remains independent as functions modulo the uniformizer. Finitely many evaluations give a unit minor. Over any $B$ that minor recovers the coordinates of a finite cocycle, so its span pulls back under injections and lifts under surjections. This argument uses triviality of the universal character above $\ell$. If $L_v$ is the image of the resulting residual cocycles in $H^1(E_v,W)$, quotienting by residual coboundaries gives
$$
 |L_v|=|H^0(E_v,W)|\,|k|^{d_v}.
$$
At unrestricted places away from $\ell$ there are no extra equations. Unramifiedness elsewhere is built into the global Galois group. Global invariant vanishing extends from simple coefficients by filtration and union. The long exact cohomology sequence and the local pullback just proved therefore make $S$ left exact. It is additive and commutes with directed unions of finite submodules, because continuous cocycles have finite image and use finitely many local witnesses. [Local saturation, count and functoriality][one]

**Bounding the number of relations.** Write $G_\Sigma$ for the group allowing ramification above $\ell$ and at the specified exceptional and auxiliary primes. Put
$$
 D_2=\ker\!\left(H^2(G_\Sigma,W)\to\prod_{v\mid\ell}H^2(E_v,W)\right),\qquad
 C_1=\operatorname{coker}\!\left(H^1(G_\Sigma,W)\to\prod_{v\mid\ell} H^1(E_v,W)/L_v\right).
$$
For $0\to k\to B\to B'\to0$ a Selmer class in $S(B')$ has a global lifting obstruction in $D_2$, since its local conditions lift above $\ell$. Among classes with the same obstruction, differences admit global lifts. Their local defects lie in the quotient defining $C_1$. Correcting by a global $W$-class whenever possible gives at most $|C_1|$ distinct cosets of $\operatorname{im}S(B)$ in one obstruction fibre. Poitou–Tate makes the map out of global $H^2$ onto the indicated local product surjective, since the global Tate dual has zero invariants. The local and global Euler characteristic formulas over this totally complex field give
$$
 |H^1(G_\Sigma,W)|=|H^2(G_\Sigma,W)|\,|W|^{[E:\mathbb Q]/2},\quad
 |H^1(E_v,W)|=|H^0(E_v,W)|\,|H^2(E_v,W)|\,|W|^{[E_v:\mathbb Q_\ell]} .
$$
Substitute the formula for $|L_v|$ and sum the local degrees. The result is $|D_2|\,|C_1|=|S(k)|$, so the quotient $S(B')/\operatorname{im}S(B)$ has order at most $|S(k)|$. Finite coefficients suffice for this bound. Arbitrarily many distinct cosets in an infinite quotient would be detected on one finite submodule of $B'$ and its finite preimage. [Milne, *Arithmetic Duality Theorems*, I.2, I.4.10 and I.5.1][milne] · [Pinned obstruction inequality][obs]

Let $I=\Lambda^\vee$ be its continuous additive dual into $\mathbb Q_\ell/\mathbb Z_\ell$ and $M=S(I)^\vee$. Duality identifies the endomorphisms of $I$ as a $\Lambda$-module with $\Lambda$. By left exactness, $S(k)$ is the socle of $S(I)$ after identifying the socle of $I$ with $k$. Thus $M$ has exactly $a$ minimal generators by compact Nakayama. For finite $B$, a finite free presentation of $B^\vee$ dualizes to a kernel between finite sums of $I$. Left exactness identifies $S(B)$ with continuous $\operatorname{Hom}_\Lambda(M,B)$ by that kernel calculation. Directed unions extend the natural identification to all $B$.

Choose a minimal surjection $\Lambda^a\to M$ and call its kernel $N$. Then $N\subset\mathfrak m_\Lambda\Lambda^a$. Any continuous $N\to k\subset I$ extends to $\Lambda^a\to I$. Evaluation at $1$ identifies these maps with continuous additive characters, which extend on finite quotients by divisibility of $\mathbb Q_\ell/\mathbb Z_\ell$. Modulo the socle the extension gives a map $M\to I/k$. This construction embeds $\operatorname{Hom}_\Lambda(N,k)$ into $S(I/k)/\operatorname{im}S(I)$, since a class mapping to zero would extend into the socle already and would vanish on $N\subset\mathfrak m_\Lambda\Lambda^a$. The obstruction inequality bounds the dimension of $N/\mathfrak m_\Lambda N$ by $a$. Nakayama over the noetherian ring $\Lambda$ yields at most $a$ relation generators. Use them as the rows of $\Phi_Q$, padding by zero rows as necessary. Applying $\operatorname{Hom}$ gives the asserted kernel. The [specialized length formula](FIRST_PASS_COPRIME_SPECIALIZATION.md#what-the-galois-determinant-measures) applies whenever the specialized determinant is nonzero. Establishing a bounded-degree coefficient for simultaneous good signatures uses the [additional Frobenius tests and integral cofactor argument](FIRST_PASS_GALOIS_PRIMES.md).
