# Automorphic types and the local good-point projector

Fix an active twisted first-pass row of index $1\le j\le m$, a place $w$, and one residual block $i$ over $(j,w)$. Write $V_L$ for the stored number-field form space, $V_\mathbf C$ for the complex form space, $V_P$ for its isotypic subspace labeled by $P$, $O_i$ for the block valuation ring, $K_i=\operatorname{Frac}(O_i)$, and $p_i:V_L\to V_i$ for the stored semilinear projection. For $x\in X_i$, let $H_x\subset V_i$ be the whole common eigenspace for evaluation at $x$. Over $K_i$ these spaces are independent and span. Write $H_Y=\bigoplus_{x\in Y}H_x$ for any $Y\subset X_i$. The compatible embedding $\iota_i:K_i\to\mathbf C$ extends the stored embedding of $L$ along the map $L\to K_i$. The first-pass construction obtains Hecke equivariance, rationality and the automorphic labeling together for the same fitted row. [Local splitting and lattice](FIRST_PASS_HECKE_LATTICE.md) · [Embeddings and old and local models][model] · [First-pass suppliers][supplier]

**Projection of an automorphic type.** Scalar extension gives a complex-linear surjection $\widetilde p_i:V_\mathbf C\to V_i\otimes_{K_i,\iota_i}\mathbf C$. Indeed the rational basis gives a complex basis, and the range of $p_i$ spans $V_i$ over $K_i$. For every allowed split place, chart and uniformizer and every $1\le k\le n$, the spherical operator is the right-coset sum with multiplier $q_v^{-k(n-1)}$. Its action preserves $V_L$ and $p_i$ intertwines it with the stored $hT_i(v,k)$. Extending from the rational basis proves the same intertwining on $V_\mathbf C$. The type vectors at this place are fixed by the local maximal compact subgroup because the level character is trivial there. The split spherical operator acts by one scalar on that fixed space in the irreducible type, and intertwining carries the same scalar to every isomorphic copy. Thus it acts by one scalar on the whole $V_P$, including all multiplicities. [Spanning property][pspan] · [Normalization][normalization] · [Hecke comparison][intertwine] · [Spherical scalar on isotypic vectors][scalar]

Let $\pi_x$ project onto $H_x\otimes\mathbf C$. If $\pi_x\widetilde p_i(z)\ne0$ for some $z\in V_P$, apply each operator to $z$ and then apply $\pi_x\widetilde p_i$. The two calculations give the same nonzero vector multiplied by the type scalar and by $\iota_i(hT_i(v,k)(x))$, respectively. The scalars agree for every allowed test. The defining equivalence for the stored point concludes $\operatorname{pt}_i(P)=x$. Consequently a type labeled at $x$ projects into $H_x\otimes\mathbf C$, and a type with no point in this block projects to zero. For any set $S$ of types, sum these inclusions and descend the containment along the field embedding for rational $y$ with $j_Vy\in\sum_{P\in S}V_P$. This proves the full $\mathrm{TypeTie}$ statement, including sets $S$ that omit some types with the same point. [Point criterion][criterion] · [Exact statement and proof][tie]

There is an equality when all types over a given set of points are retained. Put $W_Y=\sum_{\operatorname{pt}_i(P)\in Y}V_P$, where the sum includes only defined labels. Then
$$
 \widetilde p_i(W_Y)=H_Y\otimes_{K_i,\iota_i}\mathbf C .
$$
Indeed, the images of $W_Y$ and $W_{X_i\setminus Y}$ lie in complementary coordinate spaces. The sum of the images is the whole target by surjectivity, since the old types span and the remaining unlabeled types have zero image. This proves equality for both sets. In particular the statement applies to the stored good-point subset of a mixed block, without a multiplicity-one or rational splitting hypothesis for the individual types. [Exhaustion of the old row by types][types]

On the constructed automorphic side this surjection is an isomorphism on $W_Y$. Indeed, the multiplicity formula gives
$$
 \dim_{K_i} H_x=\sum_{\operatorname{pt}_i(P)=x}\dim_{\mathbf C} V_P.
$$
The old isotypic spaces are independent. Summing over $x\in Y$ proves injectivity on $W_Y$. Thus the kernel on the whole old complex row is precisely the sum of its unlabeled type spaces in this block. This also excludes loss of a proper part of a labeled isotypic space in the projection. [Automorphic multiplicity formula](LOCAL_AUTOMORPHIC_POINTS.md) · [Construction on the same local specification][multiplicity]

**Which projector is the first-pass projector?** Let $U,W\subset V_L$ be the rational preimages of the prescribed $Z_0,Z_0^\perp$. The rational presentation of the fitted form, Gram matrix, lattice and columns spanning $Z_0$ gives $U\oplus W=V_L$. Put $Y_i=Z_{\mathrm{good},i}$, $G_i=H_{Y_i}$, $B_i=H_{X_i\setminus Y_i}$. On this rational decomposition the identity
$$
 p_i P_U=P_{G_i\parallel B_i}\,p_i
$$
is equivalent to $p_i(U)\subseteq G_i$ and $p_i(W)\subseteq B_i$. Those two containments are exactly $\mathrm{SpecPr}$. A sufficient type-level verification is that every $V_P$ lies wholly in $Z_0$ or wholly in $Z_0^\perp$, and every defined point places $V_P$ on the corresponding side. Indeed, the old types span and the two sides are disjoint, so each side is spanned by the types it contains. Apply $\mathrm{TypeTie}$ to those two sets. This argument also explains the boundary of the preceding equality for $W_Y$. The labeling by spherical tests alone supplies no identification of the prescribed theta subspace with the good types. In the pin, the separate classification uses the class-sum inner product, the fitted theta comparison, the internal isotypic decomposition and the good-row criterion, with named inputs $\mathrm{Conclusion42}$, $\mathrm{ConclusionZ}$ and $\mathrm{GoodFTAll}$. In particular, the original eventual theorem obtains the former two under its stated $\operatorname{hypO}$ and point statement, and obtains the latter from Level A. The [pointwise comparison](FIRST_PASS_POINT_COMPARISON.md) supplies these hypotheses together on a finite request in the common eventual range. The [theta-image argument](FIRST_PASS_THETA_IMAGE.md) then establishes the classification with its orientation on these same rows. **Integral consequence.** Identify the blocks by their semilinear maps with subspaces of the common fraction-field model at $(j,w)$. The rational vector $y$ maps there to $\sum_i\Psi_i p_i(y)$. The preceding two containments, for every $i$, put the scalar extensions of $U,W$ inside $\bigoplus_iG_i,\bigoplus_iB_i$, respectively. Since each pair spans the whole space and the latter pair is disjoint, both inclusions are equalities. Thus the extension of the actual rational projector equals the direct sum of the indicated local projectors. The integral Hecke idempotents for distinct residual blocks split the common transported lattice $\Lambda_c=\bigoplus_i\Lambda_i$, with the ring identifications understood. Inside each block projection induces a canonical isomorphism
$$
 \frac{\Lambda_i}{(\Lambda_i\cap G_i)+(\Lambda_i\cap B_i)}
 \ \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;}\
 \frac{P_{G_i\parallel B_i}\Lambda_i}{\Lambda_i\cap G_i}.
$$
Surjectivity follows from the definition. A vector maps to zero precisely when its good projection is integral, in which case subtracting that projection leaves an integral vector in $B_i$. The direct sum of these quotients is the common-ring quotient for the rational cut. A pure block contributes zero; a mixed block can contribute a nonzero index even though the rational projector identity holds exactly. Flat base change preserves the two lattices defining the rational quotient, and dividing lengths by $\operatorname{length} O/(\ell)$ on both sides accounts for ramification at $w$. For $T_{j,t},C_{j,t}$ the original projected and cut lattices at the tuple $t$ belonging to $w$, the result is
$$
 \sum_{i\in I_{j,w}}\frac{\operatorname{length}_{O_i}\bigl(\Lambda_i/
       ((\Lambda_i\cap G_i)+(\Lambda_i\cap B_i))\bigr)}{v_i(\ell)}
 =\frac{\operatorname{length}_{\mathcal O_{K,w}}((T_{j,t}/C_{j,t})_w)}{v_w(\ell)} .
$$
Here valuations assign one to a uniformizer. This treats every active pair in every dimension, including multiple isotypic copies in one eigenspace. The [support argument](FIRST_PASS_CONGRUENCE_LENGTHS.md) gives the equality off the active set as well. [Sum of residual projections][projections] · [Identification of the two field subspaces][spaces] · [Base change and normalization][normal] · [Additivity over the actual lattices][sum]

For example, let $O$ be a DVR with uniformizer $\varpi$ and fraction field $F$. Take $\Lambda=O^3$, $G=Fe_1+Fe_2$, and $B=F(e_1+\varpi^d e_3)$ with $d\ge1$. The operator that is zero on $G$ and $\varpi^d$ on $B$ preserves $\Lambda$. Its two characteristic-zero eigenvalues give a single residual block, with good eigenvalue zero of multiplicity two. The two saturated intersections are $Oe_1+Oe_2$ and $O(e_1+\varpi^d e_3)$. Their sum has quotient $O/(\varpi^d)$. Equivalently, projection of $e_3$ along $B$ gives $-\varpi^{-d}e_1$, so the projected good lattice gains exactly $d$ powers of the uniformizer. This is the integral index appearing on both sides once the original theta projector has been identified.
