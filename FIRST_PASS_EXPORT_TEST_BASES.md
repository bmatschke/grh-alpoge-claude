# Finite test bases and the rank of the projected theta space

Fix a first-pass origin, its arithmetic datum $D$, and the fitted real and series data $X,Y$ for its prescribed tests $E$. Write $O_i$ for the coefficient discrete valuation ring and $K_i$ for its fraction field. For a nonzero place index $i$, row $1\le j\le m$, and sign $b$, put
$$
 \Theta_{ijb}=\sum_{a\in E.\mathrm{Dat0}_{ijb}} O_i\theta_a,
 \qquad L_{ijb}=A_O\Theta_{ijb},\qquad A_O=m_G e_G .
$$
Here $e_G$ is the finite-group projector and $m_G$ is the positive integer called `restRowsFiniteGroupCard`. The target is $V_0=e_G(Fm_0)$, where $Fm_0$ consists of the forms on which the type subgroup $U$ acts through the prescribed character. The fitted coefficient-field condition `FieldOk X` yields a finite set of actual labels $S_{ijb}\subset E.\mathrm{Dat0}_{ijb}$ such that the vectors $A_O\theta_a$ for $a\in S_{ijb}$ form an $O_i$-basis of $L_{ijb}$. At each active index with the fitted base tuple, standing cuspidal rapid decay and rationality of the installed base readings further give
$$
 K_i L_{ijb}=V_0,\qquad |S_{ijb}|=\dim_{K_i}V_0.
$$
These are respectively the `BasisOf` and `FullRk` assertions in the export. The latter gives rank over the fraction field. Computing the index in a particular larger integral lattice would be a further assertion. [Type space and averaging][def-space] · [Basis and rank][def-basis]

## A basis consisting of prescribed tests

The fitted series comparison identifies the theta generators with concrete series and identifies the integral source module with definite forms satisfying the coefficient-lattice condition. The level character takes integral values. The type character has finite order and its values lie in the coefficient field by the comparison. At each of the two distinguished type primes $g$, the residue-field unit character is covered by the $g(g-1)$-th roots of unity in `FieldOk`. The fixed twist and the varying twist have unit values in their specified coefficient rings. Multiplying these factors gives the level character over $O_{i,k}$. [Character calculation and fitted comparison][integral-comparison]

For an admissible twist, translate the polynomial datum to the shifted finite representative and form its trace over the finite stabilizer. The coefficient lattice is preserved under this translation. The integral trace form, multiplied by the determinant-character unit, has class sum equal to the given generator. The class-sum identity includes the stabilizer normalization, so no division by the stabilizer order enters the construction of the integral source vector. The fitted comparison carries this identity back to the original row. It proves the containment called `admT4`. [Integral trace and its class sum][trace]

Apply this containment with $k=0$, $Q=\varnothing$ and trivial varying character. In this case the twisted generator is $1\otimes\theta_a$. The ring $O_{i,0}$ is finite and faithfully flat over $O_i$, so $v\mapsto1\otimes v$ is injective. The extended generators lie in the theta image of a finitely generated $O_{i,0}$-module. Its image remains finite over $O_i$ by restriction of scalars. Since $O_i$ is Noetherian, the embedded span of the generators is finitely generated. This proves finite generation of $\Theta_{ijb}$ and hence of $L_{ijb}$. [Trivial-twist specialization and descent][finite]

Choose a generating subfamily of the $A_O\theta_a$ of smallest finite cardinality. In any nonzero relation over $O_i$, select a nonzero coefficient of least valuation. It divides every coefficient. The ambient $K_i$-vector space is torsion-free over $O_i$, so cancelling the selected coefficient gives an integral relation with its coefficient equal to one. The corresponding generator could then be removed, a contradiction. Thus the chosen subfamily is an integral basis, including the empty basis when the module is zero. Make this choice for every nonzero index and in-range row of each sign, and use the empty set outside that range. Row rationality and a bound on toric denominators are unnecessary for this choice. [Minimal-generators proof][dvr] · [Choice for the exported family][family]

## When the chosen tests have full rank

Every prescribed $\theta_a$ transforms under $U$ by the required character, so $L_{ijb}\subset V_0$. For the reverse inclusion, the active base tuple identifies the geometric theta range with the one in this row. The fitted good-range theorem, using cuspidal rapid decay, followed by the definite class-sum expansion, puts the complex reading of each $x\in V_0$ in the complex span of allowed base theta sections. [Good range and passage to class sums][span]

The additional arithmetic premise is local to this argument. Every allowed base section must belong to the range of the **installed** reading $r_{ijb}$. This is its `RatBase` property. The fitted comparison then supplies an abstract label with that section as reading, and the label belongs to $E.\mathrm{Dat0}_{ijb}$ by the fullness of the prescribed test set. A `RatRow` for the row supplies precisely this instance through the scalar compatibility of $Y$. The [rationality argument on the original row](FIRST_PASS_EXPORT_ROW_COEFFICIENTS.md) supplies this premise for the fitted data. [Use of the installed normalization][rank] · [Passage from a section to its label][read]

The reading preserves linear independence from $K_i$ to $\mathbb C$. Thus a $K_i$-basis of the span of the $\theta_a$, enlarged by $x$ if $x$ were outside that span, would read as a complex linearly independent list. This contradicts the complex span just obtained. Applying $e_G$ to the resulting $K_i$-linear expression for $x$ fixes $x$. Since $m_G$ is nonzero in $K_i$, we obtain the expression for $x$ through the $A_O\theta_a$ claimed above. This proof uses only the base part of row rationality. [Faithfulness and rank for fitted data][rank] · [Descent and projection][read] · [Averaging factor][average]

## The uniform input for all coprime powers

The remaining all-powers clause has additional quantifiers. For every positive tolerance, every family of valid finite bases, and every finite set of places to avoid, it asks for a single choice of conductor, coefficient level, and character whose every integer power prime to $\ell_i$ satisfies the required estimates. The finite basis and full rank established above furnish the separate test-set clauses at active indices. [All-powers statement][powers] · [Assembly of the three conclusions][export]

On the toric side, it suffices for each nonzero $i$ and each label $\lambda$ admitting a permitted critical integer to bound the denominators of $\theta_{i,Q,\lambda,\delta}$ in $O_i$ by a single $\ell_i^{n(i,\lambda)}$, uniformly over all admissible $Q,\delta$. Taking the maximum over the finite set of labels gives $N_0(i)$. One sufficient input allows a fixed nonzero period $\Omega$ for each host datum and a bound uniform over the individual order classes. After summing the classes over a fibre, the fixed scalar relating that period to the installed toric element can be absorbed by a further power of $\ell_i$. Distribution and the nonvanishing assertion for the empty conductor are obtained separately. The pinned criterion invokes the rationality part of the real partial-zeta input and the two normalized CM lattice estimates in the cases selected by the local conductor and discriminant. The [order-lattice proof](FIRST_PASS_ORDER_CLASS_BOUND.md) derives this bound with the actual period and transfers it to the installed coefficient. The [simultaneous specialization](FIRST_PASS_COPRIME_SPECIALIZATION.md) explains how bounded coefficients and determinant congruences at high levels are used for every coprime power. The implication from a uniform order-class bound to the toric input for export uses no specific period formula from that paper.
