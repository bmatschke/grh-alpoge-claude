# Detecting a vertical pole of a cusp section on the normalized image

In period entry 7 the finite estimates for theta derivatives apply to integral frames over complete discrete valuation rings with finite residue field. A vertical prime divisor of a positive-dimensional arithmetic variety can have residue field of positive transcendence degree. The following argument proves that the available frame tests detect every vertical pole of the section transferred to the projective image. It applies on each good marked cover, with its own level, component and section lattice, and introduces no height cost. Kaiwen Jin gives the arithmetic curve selection in Appendix J.A.6 and the finite order estimates in J. The RH Lean Roadmap states the normalized-image comparison in the proof of B8.25. Here we identify the generic flag and the frame on the curve that detects a pole. [Jin's centered-trait criterion][curve] · [Roadmap v40, normalized-image comparison][road] · [Earlier section transfer](PERIOD_JIN_FULL_THETA_TRANSFER.md#the-small-section-estimate-on-the-fitted-component)

## An arithmetic curve through a pole

Let $S$ be a normal integral scheme of finite type over the ring of integers of a number field $F$, dominating its base. Let $W$ be a dense open of the generic fibre. Suppose a nonzero rational function $h$ has negative order along a prime divisor $D$ over a finite place $w$. On a sufficiently small open neighbourhood of the generic point of $D$, $h^{-1}$ is regular and vanishes on $D$. This follows because the local ring at that point is a DVR. Choose a closed point $x$ of the surviving open in $D$. Its residue field is finite. One may also require this neighbourhood to lie in any prescribed chart containing the generic point of $D$.

There is a map $\operatorname{Spec}V\to S$ taking its closed point to $x$, with $V$ a mixed-characteristic complete DVR of finite residue field, and its generic point in $W$ where $h$ and $h^{-1}$ are defined and nonzero. To construct it, use the excellent local domain $B=\mathcal O_{S,x}$, of dimension $n\ge1$. After localizing the base at $w$, take a uniformizer $\pi$ there and choose $b\ne0$ so that $D(\pi b)$ in $\operatorname{Spec}B$ lies in the required generic open. Complete $\pi b$ to a system of parameters $\pi b,t_2,\ldots,t_n$. This is possible since $B$ is a catenary local domain and $\pi b$ is a nonzero nonunit. A minimal prime $P$ over $(t_2,\ldots,t_n)$ has height at most $n-1$. If $\pi b$ belonged to $P$, the parameter ideal would force $P$ to be the maximal ideal. Thus $B/P$ is a local domain in which $\pi b$ is nonzero and cuts out the closed point. The principal ideal theorem makes it one-dimensional. Normalize, localize at a point over the closed point, and complete. Finite normalization and finite residue extension follow from excellence. The resulting $V$ has fraction field of characteristic zero and lies over $w$. In fact it is finite over $\mathbb Z_p$ for the rational prime $p$ below $w$: $V/pV$ is finite and lifts of its generators span $V$ over $\mathbb Z_p$ by completeness. The value of $h^{-1}$ is in the maximal ideal of $V$ and is generically nonzero. Hence $\operatorname{ord}_V(h)<0$. This proves detection even if the initial pole is met only where the flag map fails to extend integrally. [Jin's parameter argument][curve] · [Roadmap v40, good-point test on a chart][road-curve]

## Keeping the flag on the detecting curve

Fix a fitted nonterminal good cover and a component $Z$. Let $s_1,\ldots,s_N$ generate its original integral weight-$u$ cusp lattice over $\mathcal O_F$. Let $X$ be the integral projective image in these coordinates and $S=X^\nu$ its finite normalization. The ratios of the original series generate the function field of the flag component: on the interior the adjoint cusp sections separate points and generate the fibre. In characteristic zero a generically finite map that separates geometric points has generic degree one. More generally any positive-dimensional geometric generic fibre would also contradict point separation. Thus the flag component and $S_F$ have isomorphic dense opens. Shrink these opens to the marked interior and trivialize the generic flag and theta lines there. This is the open $W$ used above. Every fraction-field point supplied by the curve now has a marked PEL point, its ordered Lie flags, and a framed representative $y_0$. [Jin, separation and equality of the ratio fields][ratios] · [Construction on the given cover](PERIOD_SELECTED_MARKED_ORIGIN.md#identifying-components-flags-and-valuations)

It matters that the integral frame preserve those flags. At $p$ use the proper lower model of this level with invertible direct image of the rigidified theta line. At a prime dividing the level it is constructed by keeping the generic marking, removing the $p$-part of the lower level and normalizing in the fixed generic cover. The rank and good-level conditions are those of the fitted cover. The map of the generic marked point extends to this model by properness. In each Lie eigenspace intersect its generic flag with the integral eigenlattice of the pulled-back semiabelian group. Each such intersection is saturated; over a DVR its successive quotients are free and one can lift their bases successively. This gives an integral basis adapted to the original flag even at ramified primes, working separately in each saturated eigenspace. The invertible theta direct image, with its section base-change law, gives a primitive theta generator on the trait. These choices produce an integral frame $y$ related to $y_0$ by an invertible triangular change and a scalar in the theta line. The native statement constructs this frame for **each** prescribed generic frame $y_0$. It defines the extra algebraicity condition on $y$ to be finiteness of the residue field of its trait, which the constructed $V$ satisfies. [Jin's compatible local and global models][models] · [Native model at the specified level][model] · [Integral frame for a given generic frame][frame] · [Base-change and twist hypotheses][laws] · [Definitions of the triangular change][tri] and [integral and algebraic frames][alg]

If the local theta calculation needs split period data, make its finite extension of $V$. The residue field stays finite, the given normalized evaluations are unchanged and all unnormalized orders are multiplied by the relative ramification index. In particular a strict negative order remains negative. This extension has no effect on a quotient of two evaluations of the same weight: all terms are read with the given component, flag and marking. [Uniformised chart after finite extension][split] · [Ramification and phase in the carrying bound][carry]

## The chart generator attains the minimum

Take $m=8k$ with $k>0$ and a mixed-integral cusp section $f$ of weight $mu'$. Use the carrying section $\Theta_Z$ of weight $8(u-u')$. For a finite place $w\mid p$, retain the previous normalization $v=\operatorname{ord}_V/e_0$ where $\operatorname{ord}_V|_F=e_0v_w$. The previously established finite inputs on the good cover read
$$
 v(\Theta_Z)/8\ge \phi_C/(2500e_0)-e_w,\qquad
 d_Z(v)\le \phi_C/(2500e_0)+v_w(p)a_p,\qquad
 v(f)\ge-m\delta_w .
$$
Here $d_Z(v)$ is the infimum of the orders of the integral sections at the frame, $e_w$ is the correction for the parallel factor and $\delta_w=v_w(p)\mathbf1_{p\in S_B}e_p\|u'\|_1$. The first two bounds apply on the same split trait. In particular they imply $v(\Theta_Z)/8+e_w+v_w(p)a_p\ge d_Z(v)$ before applying the mixed estimate. Choose the same nonzero scalar $b_m$ as in the [finite and infinite balancing argument](PERIOD_JIN_FULL_THETA_TRANSFER.md#the-small-section-estimate-on-the-fitted-component), with $v_w(b_m)\ge\lceil m(e_w+v_w(p)a_p+\delta_w)\rceil$. These inequalities give
$$
 v(b_m\Theta_Z^kf)\ge m d_Z(v).
 \tag{1}
$$
A zero numerator already satisfies (1). [Jin's multiplier on the component][mult] · [Reference and mixed estimates][depth] · [Local reference-depth estimate and its jet hypotheses](PERIOD_REFERENCE_DEPTH_BUDGET.md)

Suppose that the rational section $b_m\Theta_Z^kf$ of $\nu^*\mathcal O_X(m)$ has a vertical pole $D$. Choose any standard projective chart $X_i\ne0$ meeting the generic point of $D$. In its trivialization the rational coordinate of this section is
$$
 h_i=b_m\Theta_Z^kf/s_i^m .
$$
Its negative order on $D$ gives the finite-residue trait above with $\operatorname{ord}_V(h_i)<0$. The trait lies in the chart $X_i\ne0$, so the generic values of *all* $s_j/s_i$ belong to $V$. The upper triangular change from $y_0$ to $y$ multiplies all the $s_j$ by the same weight character. The numerator of $h_i$ and $s_i^m$ also have the same character, of weight $mu$. Thus these equalities of ratios hold when evaluated in the integral frame $y$, with no valuation cost. In particular $v(s_i(y))=\min_j v(s_j(y))$. Every integral cusp section is an $\mathcal O_F$-linear combination of this finite list, and the component summand has the same attainable orders on $Z$. Therefore the minimum is $d_Z(v)$. Equation (1) gives $v(h_i)\ge0$, a contradiction. Only the common weight character is needed; the individual diagonal entries of the triangular change can have nonzero valuations.

The local inequalities have the required order of quantifiers. Under the arithmetic and Hodge packages, deep-goodness, flat-degree, size and cover conditions of B8.28t, that proposition supplies a finitely supported $a_p$ for the quotient weight on any given good cover beyond the common threshold, with $T\sum_p a_p\log p\le C_RE_p$. Under the spacing and geometric hypotheses for the carrying section, the level-five construction chooses a generically nonzero tuple for the given component before testing its finite places. Its finite-order inequality then holds on every split test at that component. Thus the trait detecting a hypothesized pole can depend on the section and chart without changing either input. [Roadmap v40, family-wide reference bound][road-depth] · [Level-five hypotheses][road-level] · [Construction of the multiplier and its finite order][road-mult]

This proves the vertical assertion for every component of every supplied good cover satisfying the stated local estimates. Together with horizontal extension and normality, it yields the same-degree section on $X^\nu$ used by the small-section injection. The arithmetic curve, choice of chart and possible splitting extension add zero to its finite or infinite budget. The input inequalities on the fitted family, including the depth computation on algebraic frames, retain their original hypotheses. Period entry 7 also uses the source-volume lower bound, the arithmetic indices and the quantitative family estimates. [Horizontal extension][horizontal] · [Normalized image and arithmetic volume](PERIOD_JIN_FULL_THETA_TRANSFER.md#the-small-section-estimate-on-the-fitted-component)

## The metric at all embeddings and the dimension of the Chow sphere

The upper volume estimate uses the very same Hermitian line as the component height $h_Z$ appearing in the determinant calculation. Two dimensions occur. Let $r$ be the dimension over $F$ of the component cusp space in weight $u$, and let $n$ be the length of the generating list for its integral lattice. Take a rational basis $b_1,\dots,b_r$, write $g\in M_{r,n}(F)$ for the matrix whose columns are the generators in that basis, and choose $C\in M_{n,r}(F)$ with $gC=I_r$. At an embedding $\sigma$, choose $A_\sigma\in\mathrm{GL}_r(\mathbb C)$ such that the Petersson norm of a section with basis coordinates $x$ is $\|A_\sigma^{-1}x\|_2$. Thus the columns of $A_\sigma$ represent a Petersson-orthonormal basis. Positive definiteness here comes from the supplied good component. The native definition of the component height uses precisely this norm, integral lattice and generic evaluation. The tuple entering the determinant calculation takes $h_Z$ from that definition. [Component and pointwise norms][pet] · [Component height and conjugation][ht] · [Height used in the determinant tuple][tuple] · [Frame and normalized image height][hframe]

In a local frame $e$ of the image line put $Y=(s_1/e,\ldots,s_n/e)$ at a complex point. The squared Fubini–Study denominator in that frame is
$$
 D_{\sigma,e}=\|Y\,\sigma(C) A_\sigma\|_2^2 .
$$
On the dense flag open write $z$ for the row of the basis evaluations divided by $e$. Then $Y=z\sigma(g)$ and $Y\sigma(C)=z$. This equality is independent of the right inverse. The linear relations among the projective coordinates persist on the closed image, and the resulting smooth metric restricts to its normalization. The formula gives precisely the sum of squared evaluations of a Petersson-orthonormal basis. In a unit frame for the original point metric it is $G_y$. For a section of degree $m$, passage to its squared image norm divides its squared point norm by $G_y^m$, just as required in the archimedean multiplier bound. This holds for an overcomplete integral list as well as for a basis. For example duplicating one generator doubles its contribution to the unweighted coordinate sum $\sum_j|Y_j|^2$. The formula above preserves the original metric. [Native metric in the generating coordinates][fs] · [Jin's construction from the integral list and the Petersson Gram][jgram] · [Roadmap v40, cancellation in degree $8k$][gcancel]

There is an explicit convention at the other embeddings. For each conjugate pair $(\sigma,\bar\sigma)$ the formal construction evaluates the Petersson integral at the unique member reading the distinguished quadratic root, using the restriction of the global probability measure to the component. It uses this positive norm at both embeddings on rational sections. Extending by conjugation makes the two complex normed spaces anti-isometric: in the rational basis one may choose $A_{\bar\sigma}=\overline{A_\sigma}$. On conjugate points their denominators then agree, and sums of log norms count both embeddings with divisor $[F:\mathbb Q]$. Transporting the good-embedding estimate this way accounts for every embedding without a volume renormalization on each component. [Choice of conjugate representative][good] · [Component norm and measure][pet] · [Petersson norm under conjugation][ht] · [Jin, measure convention][jmeasure]

For precision let $P$ be the Chow form in the rational basis, $B=d+1$ its number of blocks, and $\delta=\deg X$. The native $B\delta h_Z$ is the normalized sum of finite Gauss logarithms of $P$ after the substitution $g$, and of archimedean block-sphere logarithmic means of $P$ after $A_\sigma$, with $B\delta H_{r-1}/2$ added at each embedding. Here $H_0=0$. The Petersson sphere in this formula has complex ambient dimension $r$, even when $n>r$. To see the equivalent convention with a redundant ambient sphere, equip the coefficient space $\mathbb C^n$ with a Hermitian norm for which its quotient by the kernel of $\sigma(g)$ is the given Petersson space. In orthonormal bases the quotient map becomes the projection onto $r$ coordinates. A uniform unit vector there projects to $RU$, where $U$ is uniform on the unit sphere in $\mathbb C^r$ and independent of $R$. The mean of $\log R$ equals $(H_{r-1}-H_{n-1})/2$: one can compare the expected log modulus of the first coordinate in the two sphere dimensions, using $\mathbb E\log|U_1|=-H_{r-1}/2$ (integrate the first squared coordinate, with beta density $(r-1)(1-x)^{r-2}$ for $r>1$). Since the Chow form is homogeneous of degree $\delta$ in each of $B$ independent blocks, this change in sphere mean cancels the change in harmonic term exactly. Thus either isometric presentation gives the same normalized height. The unweighted Euclidean metric on a freely chosen redundant list has no such identification in general. [Native finite and archimedean formula][chow] · [Roadmap v40, Fubini–Study and generating-frame comparisons][road-chow]

Consequently a constant shift $t$ changes the normalized arithmetic self-intersection by $\delta t$. With the normalization of the component counts the resulting upper bound is $\delta(h_Z+t)$; there is no extra Gram-determinant, redundancy or conjugation term in that bound. The finite lattice still supplies the generators and the threshold $c_s$ ensuring nefness, and the spherical harmonic term is already included in $h_Z$.
