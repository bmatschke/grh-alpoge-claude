## What the detailed edition includes

Edition 5 assembled 65 texts into eight chapters. They comprise the overview, 42 mathematical notes, one guide to the formal source, 13 research modules, three TeX texts from an earlier round and five texts prepared for publication. The repository at that revision contained 313 Markdown notes under `manuscript/notes/` and 252 files named `main.tex` under `research/modules/`. **Where does infinitesimal information enter?** At level invertible on the base the argument factors the classifying map as $U\to M\to\mathcal A$. The ordinary marked action space $M$ is finite and formally unramified over Siegel space $\mathcal A$. On the projective theta model the abelian open $U$ is finite over $M$ and agrees with it near the generic coefficient fibre. These assertions require different arguments. For example $\mathbb Z_p[pt,t^2,t^3]\subset\mathbb Z_p[t]$ is a finite birational normalization and becomes an isomorphism after inverting $p$. At the closed origin its relative differentials still have a nonzero class $dt$, since $p\,dt$, $2t\,dt$ and $3t^2\,dt$ all vanish in the cotangent fibre. Finiteness and generic agreement alone therefore leave the tangent test undecided. GRT73 proves it on $M$ by rigidity of the action. GRT74 uses the actual common dense marked open, homomorphism extension and the ample determinant to compare $U$ with $M$. For the normalization in the actual component field GRT77 constructs the common marked open, using GRT75 for the complex periods and GRT76 for the normalized abelian sections. A basis over that open may acquire an index in an integral boundary lattice. The remaining input here is the identification of the smooth semiabelian group and of both ample section lattices on the completed boundary charts.
