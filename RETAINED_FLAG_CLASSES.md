# Integral coordinates for a short isotropic flag

The flag-volume argument sums over the isomorphism classes of the short graded lattices and an isometry class of a residual lattice. Here is the integral splitting that makes these indices sufficient. It applies to each of our three projectors since they use the same class of point lattices. The outside sources are Adriazola's estimate for **one** adapted class and the proof of Lemma 37.17 in *A guide to the Alpöge–Claude proof*. The calculation below identifies the integral class to which that estimate applies. [One-class estimate][ad] · [Normalization in (40.572)–(40.574)][book]

Let $E/F$ be the CM extension, unramified at all finite primes, $A=\mathcal O_E$, and $(L,h)$ a self-dual Hermitian $A$-lattice. Let $0=S_0\subsetneq S_1\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq S_a=S\subset L$ be a saturated isotropic flag. Then $L$ admits an orthogonal decomposition
$$
 L\simeq H(P_1)\perp\cdots\perp H(P_a)\perp R,\qquad
 P_i\simeq S_i/S_{i-1},\qquad H(P)=P\oplus P^\# ,
$$
in which $S_k$ corresponds to the sum of the first $k$ isotropic summands $P_i$. Here $P^\#$ is the conjugate $A$-linear dual, and the two summands in $H(P)$ pair by evaluation. The complement $R$ is self-dual, isometric to $S^\perp/S$, and has signature $(p-J,q-J)$ at every real place for $J=\operatorname{rk}_A S$. This permits nonprincipal $P_i$ and residue characteristic two.

Since $A$ is Dedekind, each finite torsion-free quotient in the flag is projective. Successive splittings give $S=\bigoplus_i P_i$ compatibly with the steps, and $S$ is a direct summand of $L$. Restriction $L^\#\to S^\#$ is onto. Via self-duality choose a section $j:S^\#\to L$ of the pairing map. In the coordinates $S\oplus j(S^\#)$ the pairing has blocks $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\1&B\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, with $B=B^*$ an integral map $S^\#\to S$ by biduality.

There is a single $u\in A$ with $u+\bar u=1$. Indeed the integral trace image is an ideal of $\mathcal O_F$. Its localization at every finite place is the full local ring. At a split place the trace is addition. At an inert unramified place the residue trace is onto by separability, giving an integral element of unit trace. Consequently the ideal is $\mathcal O_F$. Replacing $j(y)$ by $j(y)-uB(y)$ in these coordinates subtracts $uB+\bar u B=B$ from the lower diagonal block. The resulting summand is the unimodular hyperbolic module on $S$. Pairing with it gives an integral orthogonal projection from $L$. Its kernel $R$ gives the decomposition, and $S^\perp=S\perp R$ inside it. This proves the claim with the original lattice. [Pinned construction][split]

Two flags with isomorphic $P_i$ and isometric integral residual $R$ are isometric as flagged lattices. An isometry is given by the direct sum of the maps on $P_i$, their inverse conjugate duals, and the residual isometry. Rational extension gives the global isometry. If the lattices come from finite adelic translates of the reference lattice, this isometry identifies the translates up to its finite stabilizer, because their localizations agree at each finite place. Thus repeated realizations of the same list require only one flag-class bound. This argument uses an upper sum over the realizable lists, which one may enlarge to all lists of the appropriate genus because every term is nonnegative. [Changing models][move] · [Conjugacy of flags][conj] · [Nonnegative adelic summation][sum]

The residual lattice belongs to the designated genus as well. At a split finite place a unimodular Hermitian lattice is a pair of mutually dual lattices over the two factors, classified by rank. At an inert place reduction gives a nondegenerate Hermitian form over the quadratic extension of residue fields. It has a vector of nonzero norm. Indeed, if every vector were isotropic, polarization after multiplying one argument by arbitrary residue scalars would force the form to vanish by nondegeneracy of the trace pairing. Any integral lift of that vector has unit norm. Its orthogonal complement splits off this line integrally. Every unit of the base is a norm on units of the unramified extension, including at two. This follows by the residue-field norm and successive lifting through the principal-unit quotients by the residue-field trace. Inductively this gives a unit orthonormal basis over the local extension. It applies to both $R$ and the reference self-dual lattice. Landherr's classification now identifies their rational Hermitian spaces, since they have equal localizations at finite places and equal signatures at infinity. After this identification the two integral lattices agree at all but finitely many places. The local isometries at the remaining places give a finite adelic translate. Conversely, integral residual isometry classes in the resulting genus are the double cosets of the rational unitary group and the compact open lattice stabilizer. [Jacobowitz, unramified Hermitian lattices](https://doi.org/10.2307/2372982) · [Landherr's theorem as stated in Gross, Theorem 3.1](https://people.math.harvard.edu/~gross/preprints/incoherent.pdf#page=7)

The [normalized flag-volume calculation](RETAINED_FLAG_MEASURE.md) applies the one-class estimate in the actual gauges, sums the determinant slabs and divides by the unitary quotient volume. The determinant-one Levi volume in rank $j$ follows from Weil’s Tamagawa number of the special linear group. The $2J$ consecutive unitary volume ratios follow from the one-vector convergent Siegel–Weil formula and telescope. [Adelic genus and full measure comparison][vol] · [Levi volume][gl] · [Consecutive unitary volumes][sw]

The resulting inequality and our numerical bound give C83N5–6’s weighted moderate-near estimate for each projector.
