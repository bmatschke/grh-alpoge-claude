# Almost every row is retained on the selected family

Let $\mathcal A$ be the common selected class of the ordinary and Dirichlet arguments. Use its original range of small positive $\varepsilon$, the source's ordered large-parameter choices, and a family $F$ in $D_3(3,\mathcal A)$ meeting those choices. Write $\vartheta=F.\mathrm{ez}=\varepsilon/J$, and let $\delta(x,i)$ be the discarded dimension fraction stored on its actual member at scale $x$ and index $i$. Under the standing published local representation-theoretic and Siegel–Weil inputs of the reconstruction, there is an $x_0$ such that
$$
       0\le\delta(x,i)\le \exp(-x^{\vartheta/7})
          \qquad(x\ge x_0,\ i\in F.I_x).
$$
This proves the retained-dimension assertion consumed by both endpoints. More precisely the closed family assertion first chooses $\varepsilon_1>0$, then for each $0<\varepsilon\le\varepsilon_1$ the source's ordered size thresholds, and then $x_0$ for the admitted family and its constants. No threshold in the displayed conclusion depends on $i$. The construction of enough families meeting the thresholds remains the common design obligation. ## Which finite-dimensional spaces are counted?

Fix the output of the actual selector. Its linkage identifies the member, its object, and its bundle with a single weighted point. That point already carries a common automorphic realization $X$, an operator defining its good space and an isometry from the spectral model to the model of $X$. The same record carries its global and local measure comparisons and transport at every word of its family. The rank, marked levels, signatures, formal degrees and the two measures giving the main term are the ones in this realization. We use these data to form the three projectors. [Spectral record, all-word transports and group][sp] · [How the selected point retains the record][sat] · [Same-point comparison][goodties]

There is a finite-label condition in the definition of the class used here. Its origin matters. Green's characters of the finite general linear groups and the Zelevinsky support rule relate a depth-zero type to its cuspidal-support labels. The normalized local correspondence identifies the tame inertia of a depth-zero cuspidal representation with its prescribed residue-field character orbit. On the selected normalized datum the inertia generator is *the* generator used to define its polynomial labels. This follows by forgetting the additional fields of the strengthened extension down to the first normalized extension and projecting its generator law. For each prescribed pattern the finite-side comparison supplies an auxiliary representation and a nonzero vector fixed by the projector onto good types. This assertion also covers a pattern whose summand in one particular row vanishes. Expand that projector into orthogonal products of the local series idempotents. At least one product has a nonzero component, and its tuple of labels is good by construction. In the conjugate realization the idempotents carry the dual labels. At each marked place the nonzero component identifies the Teichmüller lifts of the roots of this dual label with the tame-inertia roots of the prescribed pattern. Injectivity of the Teichmüller lift identifies the monic polynomials. Duality inverts the roots. It preserves factor degrees, distinctness of prime-to-$\ell$ components for every tested $\ell$, and avoidance of the specified tame values together with their inverses. The resulting prescribed tuple is therefore good at both primes. This argument uses the original local row representation and the specified tame generator at both primes. It need only establish goodness of the patterns assigned to the retained data. The ambient row space may have other types. For clarity, on this $X$ let $e$ denote the level projector and $e_t$ its component of finite label $t$, at all places over the two distinguished primes. Orthogonal central type projectors give an orthogonal sum of their images on the full space. On the real side the normalized coefficient of the tensor of unit lowest-type vectors $w$ acts as a matrix unit $P$. The identity $P R(g)P=\langle w,\sigma(g)w\rangle P$ identifies $\operatorname{im}(R_{e_t}P)$ by evaluation at $w$ with the projected intertwiner space. A vector $z$ in the image gives the equivariant map $\sigma(g)w\mapsto R(g)z$, and the matrix-unit identity makes its extension well-defined on the cyclic span and continuous. Count $\dim\operatorname{im}(R_{e_t}P)$ indices of type $t$. On the good space the same isometry transports the given row basis onto a basis with the prescribed labels. Projecting a basis expansion to a single label shows that these are exactly the good indices, with every multiplicity. The construction identifies the total ambient dimension as well. ## From the three counts to the discarded fraction

Let $M=D^\star(e)>0$ be the main term of the full space and $p_j$ the finite local share for the bad types at the $j$th distinguished prime $g_j$, $j=0,1$. The main term of its cylinder is $p_jM$ because the finite convolution is taken on precisely the same Haar measure and commuting type projectors. The selected type data give the normalized Green weight law for its own labels. Relabelling permutes the irreducible factors within each degree, with the tame generators in the label comparison fixed by the normalized datum. The lower comparison on the complementary event and the upper squarefree comparison apply to the local weights. ARO29 gives $0\le p_j\le W_{g_j}$ with the explicit finite-group envelope of GQT3. [Original finite law and its comparison][weights] · [Weight bound on this row](arithmetic_rows_v46/main.tex#L1163-L1201) · [Family's own type data][final]

The three counts have relative error $\alpha=e^{-nf\log n/16}\le1/2$ eventually. Their estimates apply when $p_j=0$ as well. There are at most $(1+\alpha)M(p_0+p_1)$ bad indices, by the union bound, and at least $(1-\alpha)M$ full indices. This proves $0\le\delta\le3(W_{g_0}+W_{g_1})$. The original family size law gives GQT3 with $C_s=6$ for $x>F.x_1$, $x\ge4$. Indeed $g_j\ge e^{8x^\vartheta}$, $x^2\le f\le x^6$, $m\le x^6$, $100\le n\le x^6$ and $\min(q_{\rm host},d_{\rm host})\ge e^{x^{\vartheta/6}}$. Here $n$ is the spectral rank, and $q_{\rm host}$ is the host prime. GQT3 therefore gives
$$
        0\le\delta \le 6x^{21}e^{-x^{\vartheta/6}}
                      \le e^{-x^{\vartheta/7}}
$$
after one final enlargement of the common cutoff. The last inequality follows since $x^{\vartheta/6}-x^{\vartheta/7}-21\log x\longrightarrow+\infty$. This completes the scalar estimate on the selected family. [Size comparison][sizes] · [GQT1–5](global_quantitative_v42/main.tex#L159-L378) · [Source endpoint][final]

## Scope of the original producer register

The edition 0.90 entry also tracks a stronger construction of transport for *arbitrary* supplied automorphic form families. What is proved above uses the transports for all words in the family of each selected point. It covers the original scalar assertion with its actual indices and threshold order. The universal transport theorem is a separate remaining reconstruction target. BQS23 already gives Bessel nonvanishing and GMR19 gives measure rescaling. The outstanding general construction includes the integral genus and level comparisons and the compatible representation intertwiner on arbitrary supplied input.
