PURPOSE AND SCOPE

Complete a human-readable proof of RH and unrestricted Dirichlet GRH by
reconstructing the Alpoge–Claude Lean proof, allowing simplifications when
they reduce the remaining proof burden and preserve everything used by
the final argument. Throughout this roadmap, GRH means the Dirichlet
L-function generalization covered by the source.

The underlying Lean proof is accepted as fully checked and correct.
Outstanding obligations concern the human reconstruction. The milestones
below are proposed work; this roadmap establishes no additional proof
claim and changes no v0.41 completion status.

There are two completion goals:

1. Mathematical closure: every essential step follows from a written
   argument or an explicitly stated published theorem with its hypotheses
   checked.
2. Readable exposition: the argument has a natural order, consistent
   notation, and sufficient explanation for an appropriately trained
   reader to verify it without consulting Lean.

The organizing principle is to work backward from the final theorems and
forward from completed inputs, prioritizing whole mathematical theorems
and shared dependencies. The most recently reconstructed branch does
not automatically receive the next allocation of effort.

Sources [S1]–[S3] are linked in Section 12. Statements about present proof
status refer to that frozen baseline; scheduling and editorial decisions
are recommendations for subsequent work.


1. THE MATHEMATICAL OUTLINE

The cumulative manuscript describes the following top-level route. [S2,
“The route and the present boundary”]

1. Construct a centered, smoothed prime sum detecting a fixed target
   L-function.
2. Construct iterated theta-period maps with a common integral target.
   Compare finite-place divisibility with archimedean sizes and overlaps.
3. Obtain the required one-sided square-root bound on arbitrarily late
   intervals [Y, Y^(3/2)].
4. Use the analytic argument that a zero to the right of 1/2 would force
   failure on every sufficiently late such interval.

The prime-sum setup, final zero-exclusion argument, numerical end of the
comparison, and several algebraic and slope arguments already have
compact reconstructions. The substantial remaining work is concentrated
in producing the arithmetic and geometric inputs and proving the
quantitative estimates linking them. [S2, opening outline]

The exposition should make the central mechanism visible: what objects
are constructed, why their arithmetic divisibility and analytic size
can be controlled simultaneously, and how the resulting comparison
forces the prime-sum estimate. Each supporting chapter should explain
which part of that mechanism it supplies.

3. MILESTONES AND COMPLETION CRITERIA

The numbering below organizes the work; it is not a strictly serial
schedule. Global quantitative assessment, uncertain theorem matching,
and manuscript integration should run alongside the main reconstruction.

M0 — WRITE THE CONDITIONAL MAIN PROOF

Turn the existing outline into a short, readable derivation of RH and
Dirichlet GRH from a small list of precisely stated major theorems.
Reuse the already reconstructed analytic argument.

For each major theorem, state its conclusion, purpose, hypotheses,
objects, normalizations, quantitative requirements, and choice order.
Map it to completed proofs, checked published applications, or the open
register. Explain where each input is used in the final deduction.

Completion criterion: the entire final argument can be read without
following Lean declarations, and every remaining input has a precise
mathematical statement and a known use. The proof is explicitly
conditional until those input theorems are completed.

M1 — COMPLETE FINITE LEVELS AND ORDINARY STRUCTURE

This is the best immediate bounded construction sequence because the
new neatness and weight theorems supply inputs it can use now. [S1, §V]

First prove FuCmpMem.finU on the actual group, level, local sections,
auxiliary congruence condition, and Iwahori factors. Obtain the finite
representative set with factorization in the source's order:

    g = glob(gamma) · s · (u,b).

Then reconstruct OrdFree.ordStruct and ordFree. Organize the human proof
around finite coefficient quotients, compatible projectors, completeness,
and identification of the actual ordinary submodule. The required
factorial-limit condition is:

    for every f and precision k, there exists J such that for all j ≥ J,
        U_z^(j!) f − e(f) belongs to valIn(k).

One projector e is chosen before f and k. The convergence threshold may
depend on them. Establish finite freeness, the original filtration
assertions, and invertibility of all required positive-monoid operators.

Next prove OrdBCMapOrd.ordBC for every allowed local coefficient map
A → B, using the original coefficientwise map and the mapped
representation and character. Preserve the full complete-local-ring
scope, including potentially nonreduced coefficient rings.

Completion criterion: finite-level representatives, the ordinary
projector, ordinary freeness, and base change are proved for the actual
modules and maps. The completed chapter can feed control theory without
an unexpanded module-existence premise.

M2 — PRODUCE THE COMPLETE SAME-IDEAL ARITHMETIC INPUT

Continue from M1 through classical control, the Hecke algebra, arithmetic
fibers, and their required separation/density properties. Reconstruct the
full integral TypeForAlt in parallel; ordinary structure can start before
the type theorem is complete, but the final arithmetic package needs it.

The four exact Hida endpoints are:

• PointTheoremBaseObject;
• ArithmeticPointDecomposition, including its cofinite strict
  Frobenius-ratio bound;
• IrrArithPointsAutomorphicAway / AutOffN;
• TwistOk.

Do not strengthen the strict ratio conclusion to exact purity. All four
must hold on the same ideal J within each original construction. The
all-level HasAut producer is a separate requirement on that same J.
Both original rank-four Links, the fixed complex embedding, ell = 3,
and one common coefficient threshold before later data remain in scope.
[S1, §V and entry 13]

The selected Raw implication is already available. The task is to
produce its original arithmetic antecedents; its completed patching,
normalization, and descent arguments should be reused.

Completion criterion: the support theorem applies to an actual
constructed arithmetic object, with all its required Hida and all-level
automorphy properties simultaneously established.

M3 — PRODUCE THE COMMON GEOMETRIC AND LOCAL FAMILY

Coordinate the geometric and local work around one eventual existence
theorem. Many transfers and constructors are already proved on their
inputs; the next milestone produces those inputs together.

Geometric work:

• Construct the prescribed higher covers over the required field.
• Prove the actual class-sum statements, cusp membership, and uniqueness,
  including the records needed after phase rescaling.
• Establish one common point identity with its parameters fixed before
  the later tuple records and normalization constants.
• Prove base class-sum generation with its original size inequality.
• Supply the geometric full-lattice assertion where required and not
  already given.

Reuse the completed scalar, phase, good-projector, and both-level test
transfers. The full-lattice assertion is needed for MLat; it must not be
added as an unnecessary premise to the already proved rationality and
finite-test implications. [S1, §III]

Local work:

• Complete F3: the local bridges matching the stored valuation ring,
  local norm, decomposition-group action, topology, GoodCoef condition,
  and original coefficient maps.
• Complete F4: the twist-ring map and common-overfield reading of the
  entire original coefficient field, with the prescribed scalar maps.
• Supply actual common MtInput and choice data, complex Hecke standard
  facts and the no-Jordan input, arithmetic character and reciprocity
  conditions, and the remaining automorphic/Galois specialization data.

The F3/F4 tasks are useful bounded parallel assignments. Their completion
does not by itself produce all the other inputs of ndSpec_exists or
NBuilt. [S1, §IV and entry 29]

Integration work:

Establish the compatible readings, exact eta agreement, required division
identities, second-layer selection, and actual base row identities on
the same family. Retain the metrics, measures, phase, coefficient maps,
lattices, and eventual-family quantifiers. [S1, entries 28–33]

The logical target is:

    there exists X such that P1(X), P2(X), ..., Pm(X) all hold.

Separate statements providing Xi with Pi(Xi) do not establish that
target. Compatibility is part of the mathematics.

Completion criterion: one common geometric/local family is available
to all the downstream rationality, reading, spectral, and row arguments,
with the required uniformity and original identifications.

M4 — COMPLETE THE OTHER ARITHMETIC INPUTS

Reconstruct the one-character/Kato-input/congruence branch and the
rank-three host/counting branch, following their exact downstream uses.
Schedule their finite assembly steps when their substantive inputs are
ready. Keep the additional Dirichlet-GRH seed/tensor/chain/promodularity

Give the two D entries an early, bounded investigation:

• HOST_RIGID_S: match the original Count-V host/frame, marked point,

For each, write the actual mathematical objects beside the proposed
published theorem. Either certify the complete application or identify
the source argument requiring direct reconstruction. Failure of a
proposed literature shortcut concerns that shortcut, not the standing
assumption of formal correctness. [S1, entries 10 and 24]

Completion criterion: the required arithmetic cases are proved on their
original inputs; no essential representation, marked-point, or
local-condition identification remains unresolved.

M5 — CLOSE THE GLOBAL QUANTITATIVE COMPARISON

Begin a substantive assessment of this milestone immediately, alongside
M1. The five global C obligations may dominate total remaining effort;
the audit does not establish ordinary theory as the unique longest path.

The five targets are denominator control, retained defect, period/height
comparison, local gain, and normalized kernel/Gram estimates. [S1, 1–5]

For each target:

1. State the exact inequality used by the final proof.
2. Identify all variables, normalizations, exceptional cases, and guards.
3. Record the permitted dependencies of every constant and threshold.
4. Determine the arithmetic and geometric inputs actually used.
5. Separate checked published applications from substantial source
   reconstruction.
6. Test whether a weaker estimate would still close the final numerical
   argument, and prove that implication before adopting a replacement.

Maintain one quantitative calculation showing parameter order, constant
dependencies, error contributions, and simultaneous eventual bounds.
After fixing the initial datum, a single constant working for all later
choices must not be replaced by constants chosen separately afterward.

Completion criterion: all five estimates are proved at the required
strength and apply simultaneously to the constructed data. The complete
parameter calculation yields the required prime-sum bound.

M6 — ASSEMBLE RH AND THEN THE ADDITIONAL DIRICHLET-GRH CASES

The audited RH route uses Cases I, II, and V. Dirichlet GRH additionally
uses III, Seed IV, and rank-four chain propagation/descent. [S1, §V]

Aim first to close the 26 shared open obligations and the full RH
dependency graph, then present a complete RH proof. Complete the seven
additional entries and assemble unrestricted Dirichlet GRH. Preserve
the character and conductor dependence in shared chapters so that an
RH-first exposition does not force duplicate reconstruction later.

The seven additional entries include substantial C arguments; their
number does not imply a short final appendix.

Completion criterion: every step from the constructed data to the
specified theorem is written, with no remaining conditional input,
unproved existence claim, or unmatched published-theorem hypothesis.

M7 — COMPLETE THE HUMAN EXPOSITION

Develop the manuscript during M0–M6, then integrate the finished proof.
Use three levels:

• Main argument: construction, arithmetic/analytic comparison, major
  theorems, and deduction of RH and Dirichlet GRH.
• Supporting mathematical chapters: analytic criterion; quantitative
  comparison; theta geometry and rationality; local/integral structures;
  ordinary and automorphic theory; rigidity, counting, and additional
  character cases.
• Source correspondence and technical records: exact Lean locations,
  notation dictionary, checked published applications, replacement
  arguments, and detailed compatibility calculations.

Each mathematical chapter begins with its purpose and principal theorem.
Use consistent notation and explain how it contributes to the main
comparison. Source labels remain traceable without dominating the prose.

Completion criterion: an appropriately trained reader can verify the
argument from the manuscript and its stated references. “The Lean
source supplies this” is not used as mathematical justification for an
essential step.


4. SIMPLIFICATIONS THAT CAN REDUCE THE WORK

The test for a replacement is whether it reduces the remaining proof
burden while supplying everything consumed downstream.

• Specialize a general source theorem. Prove only the scope needed by
  the selected final route, checking every downstream invocation.

• Apply a published theorem. Check its precise hypotheses, conventions,
  coefficient scope, and conclusion. A familiar theorem name alone
  does not discharge an obligation.

• Package repeated arguments into a general lemma. Finite algebra,
  completeness, descent, and elementary linear algebra are natural
  candidates, provided the lemma retains the required conclusions.

• Use canonical identifications. The main exposition can work up to
  canonical isomorphism once the maps, actions, integral lattices, and
  normalizations are shown to commute under the identifications.

• Prove a weaker sufficient estimate. Recheck the entire final parameter
  argument before replacing a stronger source bound.

• Remove an unused branch. First verify that every use on the selected
  RH and GRH paths has been accounted for.

Working with the original objects controls the correspondence to Lean;
the finished exposition need not reproduce implementation-specific
coercions or record fields. Equal dimensions, abstractly isomorphic
fields, or unrelated free lattices nevertheless do not supply a required
map, operator, embedding, or metric.

A shorter phrase can conceal the original difficulty. Replacing several
records by “a compatible automorphic object exists” is useful only when
that existence theorem is proved.

For every substantial replacement, record:

1. The original output used downstream.
2. The proposed replacement statement.
3. Its proof and the connection to every required output.
4. The dependencies removed and those retained.
5. Any remaining compatibility, uniformity, or choice-order obligation.

Avoid replacing finite-level information by an unjustified assertion over
the complete ring; replacing a common field or ideal by separately chosen
witnesses; losing the prescribed complex embedding or normalizations;
or excluding small primes or character cases still needed by the target.


5. PARALLEL ORGANIZATION AND REPRIORITIZATION

Use four complementary roles when capacity permits:

1. Main reconstruction: finite levels, ordinary projector, freeness,
   base change, then control and arithmetic fibers.
2. Independent substantial branch: integral types or geometric covers,
   class sums, and the common point identity.
3. Global assessment/reconstruction: the five quantitative estimates,
   with early attention to the two D matches.
4. Integration/review: exact statements, shared objects, quantifier
   order, and applications of completed results to their consumers.

Assign F3/F4 or similarly bounded tasks when they fit an independent
workstream. Independent work must have a precise input/output statement
and an owner for the subsequent compatibility check.

Reprioritize after a major endpoint closes or a C/D obligation becomes
substantially better understood. Ask:

• Which final-theorem step is now available?
• Which hypotheses still lack a construction?
• Which available task would next complete a major theorem?
• Which uncertain argument could dominate the remaining effort?
• Did a simplification remove dependencies or only rename them?
• Is the next assembly task ready, or are its substantive inputs open?

Do not use claim count, source-file completion, or recency of work as the
primary scheduling measure.


6. PROGRESS RECORD FOR EACH WORK PACKAGE

Use the following compact record in subsequent rounds:

• Mathematical target and purpose in the final proof.
• Exact original source endpoint(s).
• RH/shared/Dirichlet-GRH-only scope.
• Classification A, B, C, or D, separate from completion status.
• Known inputs and remaining input-producing theorems.
• Objects, maps, normalizations, and order of choices to preserve.
• Proposed simplification, if any, and its required connection proof.
• Completed proof blocks and their source references.
• Review findings and unresolved issues.
• Downstream theorem that becomes usable on completion.

Report progress primarily by major theorems completed, whole obligations
closed, unresolved input producers, certified theorem matches, and
branches that can now be invoked end to end. New claim counts remain
useful inventory but do not support a completion percentage or a reliable
calendar estimate.

Refining a C obligation may expose additional sub-obligations. Record
that refinement transparently; changing the granularity of the register
is not itself mathematical progress or regression.

10. CONTINUATION AND UPDATE RULES

At each future checkpoint, retain the baseline used, identify completed
major endpoints, and update the open register with additive explanations.
Keep historical proof and source records traceable. The latest validated
audit governs current completion status when earlier prose is superseded.

Separate three kinds of change:

• Mathematical progress: an input theorem, implication, or compatibility
  argument has been proved.
• Improved understanding: a dependency has been expanded, a matching
  question resolved, or a more efficient route identified.
• Expository progress: notation, organization, or readability improves
  without changing the set of proved mathematical statements.

Preserve full RH and Dirichlet-GRH scope unless a restricted statement is
explicitly labeled as an intermediate result. Saving a roadmap, writing
a conditional main proof, or renaming a work package closes no obligation.
