# Matching the Galois determinant on the actual first pass

Fix one built untwisted column with origin in the common eventual range, a nonreference index $i$, its base coefficient DVR $O_0$ and the finite set of its good signatures. Write $r$ for the rank of the row representation and $t$ for the Tate twist in its conjugate polarization. Put $T_s=\rho_s\chi_s^{-1}$ on the **stored** lattice, $T'_s=T_s^\vee(1)$, and
$$
 \nu=\min_{g\, :\, \epsilon_\ell(g)^{tr}\ne1}v_\ell(\epsilon_\ell(g)^{tr}-1),\qquad d=v_{O_0}(\ell^\nu).
$$
The construction supplies $R,D,M_0$ depending on the fixed column, index and signatures. For every $M\ge M_0$, for the prescribed admissible ray family at that level, an allowed scalar inertia element $z$, any extra open normal subgroup $H'$ and any finite avoidance set, it supplies $R$ first and $R$ second representatives of disjoint conjugate pairs. The first Frobenii are conjugate to $z$ modulo $H'$. At the second representatives the first ray characters have coordinate vectors $e_j$. Every prime has degree and ramification index one over $\mathbb Q$, and norm $1\bmod 2\ell^M$. Together with their conjugates they form $Q$. For each good $s$, there is a Selmer polynomial for **this** $Q$ with a coefficient of valuation at most $d$ in degree at most $D$. This is the Galois half of the two-list specialization, on the standing first-pass local-Galois and cohomological inputs. One may impose any finite list of Frobenius congruences to the same $z$ by intersecting their kernels in the original Galois group. The constants may depend on $i$ and the original member.

The inherited compatibility is mathematical. The actual triple, the residual full matrix span, the integral polarization $(T_s^\vee(1))^c\simeq T_s(t)$, the conjugate de Rham filtration counts and the inequalities on $r,t,g_0,g_1$ are attached to the column in [the first-pass local proof](FIRST_PASS_EXPORT_LOCAL_CONDITIONS.md). The marked degree-$r$ tame types and the two incompatible exceptional sets give irreducibility and, by the good-row root exclusion, vanishing of residual inertia invariants at the marked primes away from $\ell$. At the other designated exceptional prime the scalar frame character has order fifteen. Use an inertia value of order three for $\ell\ne3$, and of order five for $\ell=3$, so both the scalar $d_s-1$ and $d_s^2-1$ are units, including at two. The designated set uses $p'_{\rm de}$ when $\ell=p_{\rm de}$. Local Tate duality and the local Euler characteristic then give zero residual $H^1$ at these exceptional places. The Galois attachments give unramifiedness elsewhere away from $\ell$. The de Rham jumps miss $[-1,t]$ and pair by $a\mapsto t-1-a$, so the local rational $H^0$ for $T_s$ and its Tate dual vanishes above $\ell$ and the two conjugate tangent dimensions sum to $r$. Residual local duality uses the image of the **integral finite cocycles** above $\ell$, not a choice of an arbitrary residual local condition.

On the group ring $\Lambda=O_0[(\mathbb Z/\ell^M)^{2R}]$, the universal inverse ray character acts on $T_s$. Above $\ell$ the ray coordinates are trivial and the finite local condition is the coefficient span of the original integral Bloch–Kato cocycles. At $Q$ and the designated exceptional places away from $\ell$ the condition is unrestricted, and elsewhere it is unramified. The [proof of B8.07g on this lattice](FIRST_PASS_SELMER_PRESENTATION.md) produces a square matrix over $\Lambda$ whose kernel on each discrete torsion coefficient module gives precisely this Selmer group. The finite-coefficient order argument uses Poitou–Tate, local and global Euler characteristics and the Bloch–Kato duality and dimension formula. The equality of the residual primal and dual dimensions at empty auxiliary level uses the conjugate ranks to cancel the complex-place contribution in Greenberg–Wiles.

At first order the differences of local pairings at conjugate primes span all bilinear forms if the residual representation is inequivalent to its conjugated Tate dual. Equivalence implies $\nu>0$ by determinants. In the equivalent case one gets all alternating forms on the sum of two residual Selmer spaces. Odd $r$ forces the intertwining pairing to be symmetric, even in residue characteristic two, and an alternating form of maximal rank leaves at most one dimension unmatched. For $\nu=0$ full rank detects a unit coefficient in degree at most $4rR_0$, with $R_0$ the initial number of paired tests. For $\nu>0$ the same degree bound initially detects a cofactor. Modulo $(\ell^\nu)\mathfrak m_{O_0}$, the two new local inertia vectors contribute $2r$ generators killed by the Frobenius difference. Multiplying old Fitting minors by its $2r$th power preserves old detections. If the determinant for the next signature still fails, its nonzero cofactor detects a dual class and a nonzero local pairing. Chebotarev selects one further second prime with this pairing and the required first-coordinate vector. The tame relation promotes the old cofactor to a detectable determinant term. Reserving one extra coordinate per signature gives $R=R_0+|\mathcal S|$ and the recursion $w_0=4rR_0$, $w_{j+1}=2(d+1)w_j+2+2r$, $D=w_{|\mathcal S|}$. Thus $R,D$ are fixed before $M,H'$ or the avoidance set. The positive-loss branch includes $\ell=2$ and can also include odd primes.

B8.07f first constructs *one* ray family via class field theory. In applying the selection paragraph of its proof to a ray family already tied to the installed Artin maps, keep its $H,S,d_P$. The Chebotarev correction uses only the defining properties of an admissible family. Intersect the open normal subgroup for the prescribed finite tests with $H$ and the subgroup fixing $\mu_{2\ell^M}$. Exclude $S$ and its conjugates. Inertia at each first prime surjects onto its own $d_P$ coordinate and vanishes on the others and in the imposed finite quotient. It can therefore correct a proposed second Frobenius to the desired coordinate vector without changing any of its prescribed images. This verifies the quantifier on *any supplied admissible coordinates* in the pinned input, and explains why one can use the coordinates of the toric and automorphic tests.

## When the simpler Galois construction applies

This test can be made on the host before choosing any auxiliary prime. Let $n=rt$, odd for the fitted ranks. For odd $\ell$ put $m_\ell=[\mathbb Q(\mu_\ell):E\cap\mathbb Q(\mu_\ell)]$. Then
$$
                 \nu=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad m_\ell\nmid n.
$$
Indeed the residual cyclotomic image of $G_E$ is cyclic of order $m_\ell$, and $\nu=0$ means that its $n$th-power character is nontrivial. In particular every odd $\ell$ unramified in $E$ gives $\nu=0$. Total ramification of $\ell$ in the cyclotomic field makes the intersection trivial, so the image contains $-1$. It is already enough for the intersection to be totally real, since complex conjugation then fixes it. This verifies the unit-coefficient branch at those primes without shrinking the family or changing its parameters.

For $\ell=2$ one has exactly
$$
 \nu=\max\{h\ge1:\mu_{2^h}\subset E\}.
$$
For every $w\in\mathbb Z_2^\times$ the cofactor $1+w+\cdots+w^{n-1}$ is odd. Thus $w^n-1$ and $w-1$ have the same valuation, with both differences zero when $w=1$. The entire cyclotomic image is one modulo $2^h$ precisely when $G_E$ fixes $\mu_{2^h}$. The host has finite degree, so there is a largest such $h$ and an element of the image detecting failure at $h+1$. In particular $\nu=1$ if $\sqrt{-1}\notin E$. This calculates the allowed valuation loss $v_{O_0}(2^\nu)$ for the cofactor calculation, while leaving every dyadic index in the construction.
