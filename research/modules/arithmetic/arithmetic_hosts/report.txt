1. SCOPE AND READING CONVENTIONS
------------------------------

This component supplies the actual arithmetic construction that earlier
HC4/HC10 could use only as a hypothesis. It reconstructs:

  * the finite quotient of a prescribed character group, the minimal
    modulus and the mixed finite frame;
  * the three-coordinate CRT Artin map, including the sign correction;
  * its actual fixed field in a cyclotomic field, the specified Galois
    isomorphism, and the square roots of -d and -q;
  * conductor exactness, eventual rank multiplier, kept characters,
    the exact kernel, and the full quadratic-twist description;
  * every one of the 30 fields of HostDer for an arbitrary HostRaw;
  * the selected half-different ideal and trace self-duality of its inverse;
  * all eight FieldRows fields and the literal fieldChi conclusion.

The source is not changed and no Lean compilation is claimed. The proofs
are mathematical reconstructions, sometimes shorter than the pinned Lean
proof. The source ledger identifies both the endpoint and its substantive
suppliers; a range hash does not itself certify a mathematical proof.

The ambient foundations used are finite abelian groups and their complex
characters, CRT, elementary quadratic residues, finite Galois theory,
Dedekind ideal arithmetic, and local number-field ramification. The
cyclotomic integral-generator and residue-order arguments are explained in
AH9. The root-discriminant formula is derived in AH15, rather than treated
as an unexplained input. These are algebraic foundations, not assumptions
about zeros, prime distributions, period comparison, or reciprocity.

The exact prime hypothesis is HostHyp(q,d):

  q,d prime; q = 3 mod 8; d = 7 mod 16; (-d/q) = 1.

Thus q and d are odd and distinct. AH constructs data once these supplied
primes also meet the stated coprimality and splitting conditions. It does
not prove that such primes can be found in the simultaneous analytic
windows. FieldChi's constants depend only on the prescribed character;
they precede all choices of e,q,d,b.

Notation: U_a is the unit group modulo the positive integer a; T_e=2^(e+2);
H_e=U_Te/{±1}; Q=QS; N=Q*q*d. A is written multiplicatively here, while
the literal Frame transports it to an additive group on Fin(n). Its
distinguished subgroup A0 is cyclic; it need not be the whole group.
G={±1}×{±1}×A; c=(-1,-1,1); iod=(1,-1,1); Psi is the kernel of the
second sign. No TwoGroup hypothesis is silently imposed.

2. AH1 — FINITE DUALITY AND THE TWO-ADIC FACTOR
---------------------------------------------

For j=0, v2(5-1)=2; for j=1, v2(25-1)=3. Thereafter
5^(2^j)=1 mod 8, so the factor 5^(2^j)+1 has valuation 1 and each
doubling of the exponent raises the valuation by exactly one. Thus 5 has
order 2^e modulo 2^(e+2). Its powers have residue 1 mod 4, whereas -1
has residue 3. Their product has 2^(e+1) elements, all units modulo T_e,
and hence is the whole unit group. The quotient by {-1,1} is cyclic of
order 2^e, including e=0 when it is trivial. This is the same elementary
calculation used in the existing SZ13 cyclic-frame discussion.

Let Xi be any subgroup of the complex character group of U_M, M>0.
Define ann(Xi) by simultaneous evaluation 1, and B=U_M/ann(Xi).
For any finite character lambda, the sum of lambda over its group is
zero unless lambda=1, by translation of the sum. Applying this twice to
sum_{u in U_M, chi in Xi} chi(u) gives

  |ann(Xi)| |Xi| = |U_M|,

so |B|=|Xi|. Every member of Xi descends to B. A finite abelian group
written as a product of cyclic groups has exactly its order many complex
characters; choosing the image of a cyclic generator gives the count.
Consequently there are no further characters of B: the double annihilator
is Xi. This proves all three source facts mem_ann, mem_iff and card_B.

Surjectivity U_b -> U_a for a|b is also explicit. Choose an integer lift
of a unit mod a; CRT permits it to be 1 at every prime of b absent from
a. It is already prime to the primes of a, so the resulting integer is
prime to b. This reduction fact is used in the frame, conductor, and
order-stabilization arguments; no divisibility between consecutive Q_e
is assumed.

3. AH2 — MINIMAL MODULUS AND FRAME
--------------------------------

Put W_e=lcm(T_e,M). The map xiW:U_We -> B is reduction mod M followed
by the quotient. It is onto. A divisor Q of W_e is good if T_e|Q and
ker(U_We -> U_Q) lies in ker(xiW). The integer W_e is good, so the
least good Q exists. Since Q divides a positive integer, it is positive.
The kernel inclusion and the preceding surjectivity give a unique
surjection xi:U_Q -> B with xi(reduce_Q(u))=xiW(u).

Take eta=(cl,xi), where cl is reduction to H_e, and A=image(eta).
Both projections p1:A->H_e and p2:A->B are onto. On ker(p2), p1 is
injective: the two coordinates determine an element of A. Hence ker(p2)
is isomorphic to a subgroup of the cyclic H_e. Choose a generator a0.
The designated subgroup A0=<a0> is exactly ker(p2), and the map to H_e
is injective on A0. Thus the literal Frame generator axiom holds for
every integer exponent, not only for nonnegative exponents.

To obtain the actual Frame carrier, choose a bijection between the finite
set A and Fin(|A|), transport the group operation along it, and transport
p1 and a0. The source's Frame.ofGroup and ofGroupIso perform exactly
this operation. The quotient and the Artin map below are transported
through this isomorphism, so later equalities concern one common frame.

The exact sequence A0 -> A -> B gives n=n0*|B|, whence o=|Xi|.
The surjection onto H_e gives 2^e|n. If B has 2-power exponent, A is a
2-group as a subgroup of H_e×B. In the general character construction
B can have odd-order factors; none of the arguments discard them.

4. AH3 — THE ACTUAL CRT ARTIN MAP
-------------------------------

The construction applies more generally to any ModData(Q,e,B,xi) with
T_e|Q, not only to the minimal modulus. Assume HostHyp(q,d), gcd(Q,qd)=1,
e>=2 and splitting of -d at all odd prime divisors of Q.

For u in U_N, write alpha(u)=(u/q), beta(u)=(u/d) and let a:U_Q->A
be eta with its frame transport. Define w(s)=a(s) for s=±1. Then

  art(u)=(alpha(u), beta(u), a(u mod Q)*w(alpha(u))).

It is a homomorphism because A is abelian and all displayed maps are
homomorphisms. For a desired (s,t,b), choose v with a(v)=b*w(s).
Choose 1 or -1 modulo q and d to obtain signs s and t, since both primes
are 3 mod 4. CRT independently prescribes these two residues and v mod Q.
The A coordinate then equals b because w(s)^2=1. This is full
surjectivity, including both signs and every element of A.

At -1 both signs are -1, while the A coordinate is a(-1)^2=1.
Thus art(-1)=c, even when xi(-1) is nontrivial. Removing the w factor
would invalidate this identity for odd characters. Projection to H_e
kills w(s), so the exact art_h identity follows. If u=1 mod Qq, the
first sign and A coordinate are 1, leaving only 1 or iod. If u=1 mod Qd,
the second sign is 1 and the A coordinate is w(alpha), so art(u)^2=1.
The second-sign Jacobi formula is part of its definition. These facts
fill every one of CharData's 16 fields, along with its explicitly supplied
prime, coprimality, positivity and splitting data.

5. AH4 — FIXED FIELD AND GAUSS ROOTS
---------------------------------

Let L=Q(zeta_N), identify Gal(L/Q) with U_N by exponent on zeta_N,
and put E=L^ker(art). Galois theory makes E/Q finite Galois and abelian,
and identifies Gal(E/Q) with U_N/ker(art), then with the literal G.
Its degree is 4n. Restriction of tau_u is, by construction, the
automorphism labeled art(u). This proves the exact compatibility of
the inclusion E->L, the primitive Nth root, gal and art; it does not
merely prove existence of an abstract field with the same degree.

For p|N, p=3 mod 4, define the quadratic Gauss sum g_p in L.
The change of variable a -> u^{-1}a proves tau_u(g_p)=(u/p)g_p.
Complex conjugation is tau_-1, and (-1/p)=-1, so bar(g_p)=-g_p.
The elementary norm calculation is

  g_p*bar(g_p)
   = sum_{a,b !=0} (a/p)(b/p) zeta_p^(a-b)
   = sum_{t !=0} (t/p) sum_{b !=0} zeta_p^((t-1)b)
   = (p-1)-sum_{t !=0,1}(t/p)=p.

Thus g_p^2=-p. All terms belong to the algebraic cyclotomic field, so
checking this at its complex embedding proves the algebraic identity.
If the Legendre character is a coordinate of art, every element of
ker(art) fixes g_p; it therefore belongs to E with the desired action.
The second coordinate provides s_d, and the constructed first coordinate
provides s_q. The former is part of HostRaw; the latter proves the
separate stronger FieldRows.sqq clause. A general arbitrary HostRaw is
not assumed to have PsQ or a chosen square root of -q.

This constructs every one of the 26 HostRaw fields, including its two
typeclass fields: the finite intermediate field is a number field, and
the extension is Galois because the kernel is normal in the abelian
cyclotomic Galois group.

6. AH5 — EXACTNESS AND PRIMITIVE CONDUCTOR
---------------------------------------

For every prime l|Q, one must produce a unit congruent to 1 modulo N/l
whose Artin image is nontrivial. This is stronger than saying Q divides
the original modulus and is proved constructively as follows.

If T_e|Q/l, failure to find v=1 mod Q/l with eta(v)!=1 would force
xiW to vanish on the reduction kernel for Q/l. That smaller modulus
would be good, contradicting minimality. If T_e does not divide Q/l,
then l=2 and v2(Q)=e+2. Write Q=T_e*c with c odd, and take
v=1+Q/2. It is a unit because gcd(1+Q/2,Q)=gcd(1+Q/2,2)=1;
Q/2 is even. Modulo T_e it equals 1+2^(e+1), neither 1 nor -1 for
e>=1. Thus its cl coordinate is nontrivial. In both cases CRT lifts v
to a unit u with residue 1 modulo q,d. The congruences combine to
u=1 mod (Q/l)*q*d, and art(u)'s A coordinate is the nontrivial a(v).

For primitive psi of conductor M, its cyclic subgroup has annihilator
ker(psi). For any v=1 mod gcd(Q,M), generalized CRT chooses z=1 mod Q
and z=v mod M. These congruences make z a unit modulo W_e, because
T_e divides Q. Goodness gives psi(v)=1. Hence psi factors through
gcd(Q,M), forcing gcd(Q,M)=M by primitivity. This proves M|Q, and the
already known T_e|Q|lcm(T_e,M) then shows Q=lcm(T_e,M). Only M|Q is
needed in the literal KerChi/TwistForm suppliers; the equality is a
valid extra simplification under the stated primitive cyclic hypothesis.

7. AH6 — EVENTUAL ORDER WITH THE CORRECT QUANTIFIERS
-------------------------------------------------

Let J_e=xi(ker(cl)) as a subgroup of the fixed finite B. The kernel of
p1:A->H_e is {(1,b):b in J_e}. Hence |A_e|=2^e*|J_e|, and
1<=|J_e|<=|B|=|Xi|.

To compare consecutive e, start with a representative in U_Q(e+1), lift
it to U_W(e+1), reduce modulo W_e and then modulo Q_e. Its two-adic
residue remains ±1 and its B value remains the same. This proves
J_(e+1) subset J_e without claiming Q_e divides Q_(e+1). The sequence
of positive subgroup orders decreases, so the set of its values has an
attained minimum. Choose e1 at a minimum c0. Every later value equals
c0, and |A_e|=c0*2^e for all e>=e1. The construction of c0,e1 uses only
M,Xi. It has no dependence on q,d,b, an analytic scale, or a prime window.
This matches the literal ordAny quantifier order and its bound c0<=|Xi|.

8. AH7 — KEPT CHARACTERS AND THE EXCEPTIONAL SIGN
----------------------------------------------

For chi in Xi, descend chi to B and define theta_A=chi_B composed with
xi_A. Put theta_chi(s,t,a)=theta_A(a*w(s)). It kills A0 and iod, hence
belongs to Xp. It need not kill c; no evenness is claimed. Composing
with art cancels the two copies of w(alpha), yielding the original chi
on units of a common multiple of N,M. The unique primitive characters
induced by these two imprimitive characters therefore agree.

For clarity, uniqueness of a primitive inducing character is finite
conductor theory. If two primitive characters become equal on units of
a common multiple, kernel factorization and the same generalized CRT
argument used in AH5 make the common character factor through the gcd
of their conductors. Primitivity forces both conductors to equal that
gcd; equality after reduction then gives equality at that conductor.
No assertion about prime values or an analytic identity is required.

Level.ofSub collects exactly these primitive inducing characters. This
proves KeptInXp. Its psK is none. To prove its exceptional predicate,
choose u=1 mod Q,1 mod d with first Legendre sign -1. Every theta_chi
has value 1 on art(u), while psC, the first-sign character, has value -1.
If psC were kept, equality of primitive inducing characters would imply
equality on this unit. Thus it is not kept. The branch of the predicate
for psK=some psi is vacuous, and the psK=none branch is proved. No claim
of KeptEqXp is made for this general mixed character construction.

9. AH8 — EXACT KERNEL AND ALL TWISTS
----------------------------------

Take Xi=<psi> with psi primitive. For a coprime to N, art(a)=1 implies
both Legendre signs are 1. Its A coordinate is then just a(a mod Q).
The latter is trivial iff cl(a)=1 and xi(a)=1. These say a=±1 mod T_e
and psi(a)=1. Conversely those four conditions make all coordinates
trivial. Together with M|Q from AH5 this is the literal KerChi.

For a character theta of G trivial on A0, its restriction to A descends
through B, hence corresponds by AH1 to one chi in Xi. The restrictions
to the subgroups s -> (s,1,w(s)) and t -> (1,t,1) have the form s^i,t^j
with i,j in {0,1}. The product of these two factors and
(1,1,a(a mod Q)) is exactly art(a). Evaluation gives

  dirAt(theta,a)=chi(a)*(a/q)^i*(a/d)^j,
  theta(iod)=(-1)^j.

The sign coordinate of iod is retained exactly, and every character
trivial on A0 is covered. A pure 2-primary quotient or an even-only
construction would not establish this literal theorem for arbitrary psi.

10. AH9 — INTEGRAL CYCLOTOMIC REDUCTION AND LOCAL GROUPS
-----------------------------------------------------

This is the substantive ramification supplier, not an appeal to the
HostCore record. Write N=p^v*m with p prime and p not dividing m.

First, O_L=Z[zeta_N]. Locally at p, the algebra obtained from the
prime-to-p part is finite etale: the polynomial X^m-1 has invertible
derivative at its roots. Its completed factors are unramified discrete
valuation rings. On each factor, adjoining zeta_(p^v)-1 is governed by
Phi_(p^v)(1+X), an Eisenstein polynomial at p when v>0. Its root is a
uniformizer, and its power-basis ring is a DVR and thus integrally closed.
The p-primary and prime-to-p generators together generate zeta_N, and
conversely are powers of it. Hence the completed tensor of Z[zeta_N]
with Z_p is the full integral closure at every prime. The finite-index
quotient O_L/Z[zeta_N] has no p-primary part for any p and is zero.
The v=0 case uses just the finite-etale part. This explains the
integral-generator fact invoked by ringHom_ext_zO in the pinned source.

A root of order coprime to p cannot become 1 nontrivially upon reduction:
the geometric sum would reduce to its nonzero order. Consequently the
reduction of zeta_N has order exactly m. Its powers are equal modulo a
prime precisely when their exponents agree modulo m. Since integral
ring maps are determined by zeta_N, tau_u is inertia iff u=1 mod m,
and it is arithmetic Frobenius iff u=p mod m. A unit of the latter
congruence class exists by CRT; there is no need to select a new prime.

To pass to a Galois subfield E, one must justify the reverse inertia
direction. A lift of an automorphism of E is first adjusted by Gal(L/E)
to stabilize the chosen prime. The required adjustment exists by
transitivity on primes above the contraction. If the original
automorphism acts trivially on the residue field of E, this lift has
residue action in Gal(kappa(P_L)/kappa(P_E)). Surjectivity of the local
decomposition action permits a second adjustment by an element over E
to cancel that residue action. It becomes an inertia lift.

The standard local Galois fact used in that cancellation is the exact
sequence I -> D -> Gal(residue extension) -> 1. One proof passes to
completions, takes the maximal unramified subextension (obtained by
lifting the finite residue field's separable polynomial), and extends
its residue automorphisms using normality. Thus all residue field
automorphisms lift and the kernel is exactly inertia. This is the
standard algebraic prime theorem underlying CycLift.inertia_lift, not
an additional global genus, class-number, or analytic hypothesis.

Restriction of the cyclotomic congruence groups now gives the literal
inertia and decomposition equivalences in AH.22. Frobenius lifts form
one coset of inertia; this proves the Frobenius equivalence even when
an arbitrary initial lift is not itself Frobenius. At p not dividing N,
m=N, so inertia is trivial and the Frobenius is exactly art(p).

11. AH10 — CM STRUCTURE FOR EVERY RAW HOST
----------------------------------------

This argument starts from an arbitrary HostRaw, not just AH4's choice.
Its cyclotomic embedding and art_spec identify restriction of exponent
automorphisms with art. Each automorphism of E extends to the normal
cyclotomic field, so art is onto; the image of E is exactly the fixed
field of ker(art). The local conclusions of AH9 therefore apply.

The exponent -1 is complex conjugation on the cyclotomic field, and
art(-1)=c. Every complex embedding of E extends to the cyclotomic field,
so gal^{-1}(c) is complex conjugation at every embedding. The element
s_d^2=-d rules out a real embedding. Its fixed field is totally real
and has index two, so E is CM. An element is real at every embedding
iff it is fixed by this conjugation. The Galois cardinality is 4n and
the fixed-field degree is 2n. Injectivity of any embedding proves the
conj_iff equivalence with an arbitrary automorphism.

The exact CM-type chooses the embeddings sending s_d to i*sqrt(d).
The only two possible images are ±i*sqrt(d); conjugation exchanges them.
Its cardinality is therefore 2n. Precomposition by sigma preserves the
chosen sign exactly when the second sign of gal(sigma) is 1. This
gives the full three-part cmType field and identifies K as the fixed
field of Psi.

For ps_root, take the fixed field of the kernel of the first sign.
Its degree is two and conjugation acts nontrivially. Choose x there
not fixed by conjugation; x-bar(x) is a nonzero first-sign eigenvector.
This proves exactly the weak ps_root required for every raw host.
For the specially constructed host AH4 also proves the stronger s_q^2=-q.

The raw s_d and the quadratic Gauss sum in the containing cyclotomic
field have the same square and differ by sign. The Gauss transformation
therefore forces art_ch=(u/d) even though HostRaw does not list art_ch
as a field. At unramified p, quadratic reciprocity gives (p/d)=(-d/p)
because p is odd and d=3 mod 4. With the definition of psAt and art_h,
this establishes all coordinate clauses of the source frob theorem.

12. AH11 — THE TWO DISTINCT RAMIFICATION ARGUMENTS
-----------------------------------------------

AC8's half-integral quadratic calculation yields
O_K=Z[(1+sqrt(-d))/2] for the squarefree prime d=3 mod 4. The basis has
discriminant -d. Its generator has polynomial X^2-X+(d+1)/4. At p=2,
d=7 mod 16 makes the constant coefficient even and gives distinct roots
0,1 modulo 2. At odd p not equal d the polynomial has distinct roots
iff (-d/p)=1, and otherwise is irreducible. At d the discriminant has
valuation one and the prime ramifies. Thus every p|Qq splits in K.
The decomposition group of E at such a prime restricts to the trivial
decomposition group in K, proving decomp_typeI including p=2.

At d, the prime-to-d complement of N is Qq. Hence inertia is art(K_Qq).
The art_d axiom bounds that image by {1,iod}. CRT chooses a unit equal
to 1 mod Qq and -1 mod d; art_ch excludes identity for that unit. Thus
the inertia image is exactly {1,iod}.

The first-sign statement ps_d for an arbitrary raw host is not obtained
by assuming PsQ. First lift the prescribed square roots of -d through
all prime powers dividing Qq. At odd primes the derivative 2s is a
unit. At 2, d=7 mod 8 starts the induction and changing s by 2^(a-1)
corrects the square modulo 2^(a+1), for a>=3. CRT gives a square root
mod Qq. The first-sign character factors through U_Qq by art_d, so on
the residue d=-s^2 its value is the first sign of art(-1), namely -1.

Conjugation cannot stabilize a prime above Qq because its restriction
to K exchanges the split primes. At d there is a different argument.
If c were in decomposition, write it as rho*F^j with rho in {1,iod}
and F a Frobenius lift. F is represented by a unit congruent to d mod Qq.
The A coordinate and art_h would imply d^j=±1 mod T_e. Since e>=2,
reduce modulo 16: 7^j is 7 for odd j, so j must be even. Its first sign,
however, is (-1)^j by ps_d, whereas the first sign of c is -1. This
contradiction proves decompConj at d. The argument retains the exact
d=7 mod 16 and e>=2 assumptions.

At p not dividing N the inertia group is trivial; at p dividing N the
conjugation is absent from decomposition. Hence it is absent from every
finite inertia group. In the quadratic E/F extension it is the only
possible nontrivial inertia element, so E/F is everywhere unramified
at finite primes. This is the complete unramEF field.

13. AH12 — ORDINARY AND ETA ARE EXACTLY THE SOURCE PREDICATES
----------------------------------------------------------

The quadratic factorization above yields the exact splitK criterion,
including p=2 and the ramified prime d. The ordinary predicate's
universal equivalence sigma*tau in Psi iff sigma in Psi, for tau in
decomposition, reduces to D(P) subset Psi by taking sigma=1; the
converse is the subgroup cancellation law. Restriction maps this
decomposition group onto the decomposition group in K. It is trivial
there exactly when the rational prime splits into two primes of K.
This proves the whole ordinary equivalence at all primes.

For E/F, finite unramified quadratic splitting leaves only (g,f)=(2,1)
or (1,2). The second alternative means conjugation fixes P. At a
rational prime p not dividing N, D(P) is generated by the Frobenius,
so this says c lies in its cyclic subgroup. At p dividing N, AH11
excludes c from D(P), so every contracted prime is split and etaF=1.
These statements fill the three exact eta clauses; they do not assert
that all rational primes have a prescribed splitting behavior.

14. AH13 — ACTUAL HALF-DIFFERENT, WITHOUT PRINCIPALITY
---------------------------------------------------

The relative inertia in E/K is I(P) intersect Psi. At d this is trivial
because iod is not in Psi; outside N it is trivial by AH9. Hence the
support of the relative different is above Qq. The local equivalence
between a different prime and ramification is valid in number fields
because residue extensions are separable; it follows from the trace
dual in an unramified DVR extension and the ramification filtration.

Every rational prime in this support splits in K. Thus P and bar(P)
contract to distinct conjugate K-primes. The different is invariant
under every automorphism of E/Q: such an automorphism preserves K, and
trace is covariant under its restriction to K. This preserves the
integrality condition defining the codifferent and hence the different.
Its exponents at P and bar(P) agree.

Order the finite set of K-primes below the support. Select P exactly
when its K-contraction precedes the contraction of bar(P), and take the
product with the original different exponents. Exactly one of each
conjugate pair is selected. Therefore b*bar(b)=D_E/K, and their
supports are disjoint, so b+bar(b)=O_E. An automorphism over K preserves
the contractions and exponents and commutes with conjugation, hence
preserves the selection and fixes b. The empty-support case gives b=O_E
and all conclusions still hold.

The Lean theorem is named principal. Its statement does not say b=(x)
for one element x. It says that this integral ideal exists with the
factorization, coprimality and Psi-invariance properties. No class-number
one statement, principal ideal theorem, or unproved choice of generator
is used anywhere in this reconstruction.

15. AH14 — TRACE DUALITY IN BOTH DIRECTIONS
----------------------------------------

For a nonzero fractional O_E-ideal J, define

  J*={x in E: Tr_E/K(x*y) in O_K for every y in J}.

The trace condition is equivalent to x*J subset D^-1, because for every
a in O_E, a*y still belongs to J. Conversely that inclusion certainly
implies the trace condition by testing a=1. Since J is invertible,
{x:xJ subset D^-1}=D^-1 J^-1. This is the source's general dual formula.

If D=b*bar(b), both ideals are nonzero. Conjugation maps b^-1 onto
bar(b)^-1, since y*b subset O_E is equivalent to bar(y)*bar(b) subset
O_E; conjugating again supplies the reverse implication. Therefore
testing Tr(x*bar(y)) for all y in b^-1 is exactly testing membership
in the trace dual of J=bar(b)^-1. Its dual is

  D^-1 J^-1=(b*bar(b))^-1*bar(b)=b^-1.

This proves the full iff in unimodular, for any ideal satisfying the
factorization. Its pairing is O_K-valued and uses conjugation on y.
It is not a claim about an arbitrary Hermitian metric or an independently
chosen lattice. The root PA construction can use this actual inverse
ideal under its supplied Host hypothesis, without any dependency cycle.

16. AH15 — DERIVING THE ROOT-DISCRIMINANT FORMULA
----------------------------------------------

Let L=Q(zeta_N), E=L^U. The integral-generator result gives
O_L=O_E[zeta_N]. The minimal polynomial f_E is monic with coefficients
in O_E, since its roots are integral and O_E is integrally closed.
Its powers provide an O_E-basis. Lagrange interpolation gives the
trace-dual basis with denominator f_E'(zeta_N). Explicitly,
sum_{f_E(z)=0} z^j/f_E'(z) is 0 for 0<=j<a-1 and 1 for j=a-1;
reduction of higher j using the monic polynomial gives a triangular
integral transformation with diagonal ±1. Thus the codifferent is
(f_E'(zeta_N))^-1 and the different is (f_E'(zeta_N)).

The derivative equals the product over u in U, u!=1, of
zeta_N-zeta_N^u. The discriminant tower identity follows by taking the
determinant of the trace matrix using an integral Z-basis of O_E and
the relative power basis. Subtracting its logarithm from the analogous
identity for E=Q leaves exactly the factors u not in U.

For such u, h=additive order of u-1 mod N is greater than one.
The difference zeta_N-zeta_N^u is a root of unity times one minus a
primitive hth root. Its absolute norm has logarithm

  phi(N)*Lambda(h)/phi(h).

Indeed, the product of one minus all primitive hth roots is Phi_h(1).
From product_{a|h,a>1} Phi_a(1)=h, induction gives p for h=p^j and
1 otherwise. Norm transitivity supplies the degree phi(N)/phi(h).

The finite summation identity AH.39 is checked by prime-power cases.
If h has at least two distinct prime factors, no multiple h|a can be
a prime power and both sides vanish. If h=p^j and N=p^v*m, the relevant
totients sum to p^(v-j), giving exactly 1/phi(p^j). The divisibility
h|a is equivalent to u=1 mod N/a. Hence exchanging the finite sums
and using |K_f|=phi(N)/phi(f) proves

  log(rd(E))=sum_{a|N} Lambda(a)*rho_U(N/a),
  rho_U(f)=|K_f outside U|/|K_f|.

This is a finite algebraic identity. It does not use prime number
theorems, a zero-free region, a zeta-function formula, or an assumed
root-discriminant bound. The pinned source proves the same identity
through RdDiff.log_rd and RdArith.abel.

17. AH16 — THE THREE BOUNDS, SUPPORT, AND SQUARED DISCRIMINANT
-----------------------------------------------------------

(a) Index bound. Choose the least divisor c1 of N such that K_c1 is
inside U. The identity K_gcd(a,b)=K_a K_b follows from generalized CRT,
with lifts to U_N. Minimality then forces c1|f whenever K_f subset U.
If c1 does not divide f, U intersects K_f in a proper subgroup, so at
least half of K_f is outside U. For each p^v||N and p^b||c1, precisely
the last b powers p^j have c1 not dividing N/p^j. Their von Mangoldt
weights sum to b*log(p). Thus 2S(U)>=log(c1). The quotient U_N/U
is a quotient of U_c1 and has size <=phi(c1)<=c1. This proves
|U_N/U|<=exp(2S(U)), giving 4n<=rd(E)^2 for the actual host.

(b) Upper bound. In every K_f, identity belongs to U, so
rho_U(f)<=1-phi(f)/phi(N). At f=N/d, the Artin image has order two,
giving rho=1/2. At f=N/q, the reduction kernel is cyclic (it is U_q
by CRT); art_q makes its image exponent at most two, hence size at
most two, giving rho<=1/2. Start from sum Lambda(a)=log N. The q,d
terms save half their logarithms. Among the other terms retain the
identity correction at every power 2^j dividing N. Their total is

  log(2)*sum_{j=1}^v phi(2^(v-j))/phi(2^v)=log(2).

The terms are disjoint because q,d are distinct odd primes. Thus
log rd <= log N - log 2 - (log q+log d)/2, which is precisely
rd <= Q/2*sqrt(qd).

(c) Lower bound. Write N=2^v*m, m odd, v>=e+2. Because art_h is the
quotient by ±1, every element of ker art reduces to ±1 modulo T_e.
For 0<=i<=e+1, reduction of K_(2^i m) onto the kernel in U_Te has
equal fibers and image order 2^(e+1)/phi(2^i). The signs ±1 both lie
in that image only for i=0,1. For i>=2 just +1 does. Hence the fraction
inside U is bounded by

  [phi(2^i)+1_{i<2}]/2^(e+1).

The corresponding a=2^(v-i) are distinct positive powers of two.
Summing their lower bounds gives (e+1-2^-e)*log(2), since
sum_{i=0}^{e+1}phi(2^i)=2^(e+1) and there are two exceptional indices.
The separate d term supplies log(d)/2. This gives
rd >= 2^(e+1-2^-e)*sqrt(d), with exactly the source exponent.

(d) Support. Outside N inertia is trivial. At d it has order two.
At 2, CRT lets the inertia congruence group map onto H_e via art_h;
e>=2 makes H_e nontrivial. Ramification and discriminant support are
equivalent, so all discriminant primes divide N and both 2 and d occur.
Since E/F is unramified at every finite prime, its relative different
is the unit ideal. The discriminant tower identity yields the square
of disc(F) in absolute value. E has 2n conjugate pairs, an even number,
so its signed discriminant is positive. Therefore disc(E)=disc(F)^2
as the literal signed integer identity.
