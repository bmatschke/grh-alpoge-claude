MATHEMATICAL REVIEW

QR1: The inverse ray-class ideal and its congruence-one coset are the actual
objects from IG1. The map alpha -> (alpha)a^(-1) preserves integrality, the
coprimality-to-M condition and the original class. Units congruent to one are
trivial for M>=3 by M|t|<=|u-1|<=2, since a nonzero imaginary-quadratic integer
has squared absolute value a positive integer. Hence no hidden unit factor
enters either the norm count or the covolume M^2 N(a) sqrt(d)/2.

QR2: A shortest primitive vector extends to a Z-basis; subtracting the nearest
multiple makes the parallel component at most half its length. The perpendicular
height is then at least sqrt(3)/2 times its length, giving |v|^2<=2Delta.
The ideal-norm lower bound gives |v|^2>=M^2 N(a). Expanding in parallel and
perpendicular coordinates gives exactly the stated theta1, theta2 and shear,
with a,b>=1 and ab=dM^4/4. Changing the orientation changes the shear and
perpendicular sign consistently. Every represented value is a positive integer
norm of the original ideal fiber.

QR3: The integer count of each row differs from the interval length by at most
one, including endpoints. The layer-cake argument for the full shifted sum has
error at most f(0), because each centered superlevel interval has discrepancy
at most one. There are at most 2sqrt(x/b)+3 nonempty rows. The displayed bound
2sqrt(x/b)+3+2sqrt(x/a)<=10sqrt(x) is valid for x>=1 and a,b>=1. Neither shift
nor shear changes this argument.

QR4–QR5: The Gaussian periodization and its Fourier series are absolutely and
uniformly convergent on a period. The Fourier coefficient and sign convention
are correct (reversing the Fourier sign gives the same symmetric sum).
The estimate e^(-y)<=2/y^2 and sum j^(-4)<=2 give a uniform relative error at
most s^2. Applying it to the inner shifted sum and then the outer sum gives
(a t)^2+(b t)^2+(a t)^2(b t)^2. For t<=1 and a,b>=1 this is bounded after the
main prefactor by 3pi(ab)^2 t. Substitution ab=dM^4/4 is safely below B^10 t
for B=4dM^2. Finite norm fibers and nonnegativity justify both the finite count
and Tonelli regrouping. The first count retains the source floor endpoint.

QR6: The ideal von Mangoldt identity is a finite sum over ideal divisors.
Factoring an ideal prime to M is equivalent to factoring it into two ideals
prime to M; norms multiply and ray classes add. This gives the exact additive
class argument g-h in LFam.conv. At n=0 the source's divisor-antidiagonal set
is empty and the a coefficient is zero; n=1 has zero ideal weight.

QR7: At norm p^r a prime ideal of residue degree f contributes f log(p) only
when f divides r. The sum of those f is at most the total sum e f=2, so the
bound 2Lambda(n) includes ramified and inert primes. At a rational prime with
nonzero identity-class coefficient the actual ray relation gives a generator
pi congruent to one modulo M, not merely a principal ideal. The cancellation
b=a pi yields pi-1=(b-1)-pi(a-1) in M O_K. Writing pi=1+M(r+s omega) gives
4p=A^2+dD^2, M|D and 2M|(A-D-2), with the original d,M,p unchanged.

QR8: The real-character divisor factor is e+1 at sign +1 and 0 or 1 according
to odd or even exponent at sign -1. Thus all coefficients are nonnegative,
and a squared ideal contributes at least one. Squaring ideals is injective by
unique factorization. This proves both clauses of LFam.sq for every n, with
the zero case covered, and with the same finite ideal sets used by convolution.

QR9: One precision request was made and resolved before PASS. TL14 alone had
only an unspecified C_E short-vector constant. The final QR9 now gives the
explicit plane calculation: an ideal of covolume Delta=N(I)sqrt(d)/2 has a
translated radius-sqrt(Delta) disk containing two distinct points, because its
average count is pi>1. Their difference has squared length <=4Delta, so the
corresponding ideal in the inverse class has norm <=2sqrt(d). Taking I in the
inverse desired class supplies every class. Degree-two ideal counts at each
rational prime power are bounded by r+1, hence the count up to X is <=floor(X)^2.
This gives h_K<=4d and |G|<=4dM^2 exactly. The inequalities 1/B<=kappa<=4 are
immediate for d>=100, M>=3 and kappa=2pi/(sqrt(d)M^2).
