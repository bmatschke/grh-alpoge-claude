RESULT AND SCOPE

This report proves the precise integral standard-monomial basis, complementary
array count, quantitative dimension comparison, and integral Cartan product
used by CL1--CL5. The actual matrix/determinant/edge conventions are included.
The main argument works over every integral domain and uses no field-level
irreducibility theorem, integral standard-monomial theorem, hook-length
division, or residue-characteristic restriction. The determinant count is
proved as an integer counting identity using rational polynomials; that does
not introduce denominators into a lattice.

The argument is reconstructed directly from the pinned source. It is not a
Lean build or an independent verification of the entire RH/Dirichlet-GRH
release. The primary route is FinalC -> FinalD -> TestSideCounting ->
TheoremIIClosedOfTestBound.primStep_of, together with Cartan.cartanOnto_proved
-> RestCloudBOfTwo.ontoAll. The signed path basis IP and Gaussian Fock duality
IW are compatible but unnecessary for this proof. Exact declaration ranges,
full-file/range SHA-256 hashes and pinned URLs are in provenance.json.

1. CB1: THE ACTUAL INVARIANT POLYNOMIAL MODULE

Let R be an integral domain, and let X=(X_at) be an r by n matrix of independent
variables, indexed from 1 here. For a nonnegative tuple nu of length n, define

  nabla_nu(R) = {f in R[X]: every monomial has column degree nu_t,
    and f(...,C_j+t C_l,...) = f(...,C_j,...) in R[X,t] for all l<j}.

This is exactly StatementA.dualWeyl. In particular the invariance condition
is a polynomial identity with an extra independent parameter. Over a finite
field, invariance at its finitely many scalar points would be a weaker and
insufficient definition. No passage to pointwise invariants is made below.

Use column-lexicographic order: compare exponents at (a,t) by t first, then a,
and a larger exponent at the earliest differing variable gives the larger
monomial. Thus X_11 > X_21 > ... > X_r1 > X_12 > ... . This is StatementC.colLex.
Let e be the exponent array of the leading monomial of a nonzero f. The column
degree condition immediately gives sum_a e_at=nu_t. We prove

  sum_(b<=a) e_bj <= sum_(b<a) e_b,j-1.                 (CB1.1)

The genuinely needed lemma is slightly more general. Fix columns l<j and
write x_a=X_al, y_a=X_aj. Let f be nonzero with

  deg_t f(x,y+t x) <= s.

First project f to the component containing its leading monomial for the
grading that fixes x_a-degree plus y_a-degree for each a and fixes each other
variable's exponent. Column addition preserves this grading if t has degree
zero, so projection retains the degree bound; its leading monomial is still
that of f. The exponents of all other columns are now fixed. We may factor
their common monomial and regard the coefficients as lying in a domain.
Thus we may assume f homogeneous of degree omega_a in each pair (x_a,y_a).

If (a_i,b_i) are its leading exponents, then

  sum_(i<=q)b_i <= s + sum_(i<q)a_i,  1<=q<=r.       (CB1.2)

Proof by induction on rows. At row 1, write

  f = sum_(u=0)^K x_1^u y_1^(e+K-u) f_u,
  f_K != 0, e=omega_1-K>=0,

where K is maximal and every f_u is independent of x_1,y_1. The leading
monomial is x_1^K y_1^e times the leading monomial of f_K: x_1 is the earliest
variable that is not fixed, and all earlier-column variables are fixed.
Put f_u^+(t)=f_u(x',y'+t x') and

  Q=sum_(u=0)^K x_1^u (y_1+t x_1)^(K-u) f_u^+(t).

Then f(x,y+t x)=(y_1+t x_1)^e Q. The substitution is injective (its inverse
replaces t by -t), so f_K^+ is nonzero. Also Q evaluated at x_1=1,y_1=-t is
exactly f_K^+, so Q is nonzero. Degrees in t add in a polynomial ring over a
domain, whence e<=s and deg_t Q<=s-e. Since deg_(y_1)Q<=K, substitution
x_1=1,y_1=-t gives deg_t f_K^+<=s-e+K. Apply induction to f_K with the new
bound s-e+K. For q=1 this gives e<=s; for q>1 it gives

  e+sum_(2<=i<=q)b_i <= e+(s-e+K)+sum_(2<=i<q)a_i
                            = s+sum_(i<q)a_i.

This proves (CB1.2). The generic invariance of nabla gives s=0. Apply it to
each adjacent column pair to obtain (CB1.1). Adjacent inequalities also imply
the weaker all-l<j version used in the earlier cloud description.

Source match: PeelDegBound.peelDegBound_of uses exactly the specialization
x=1,y=-t; TwoColumn.lctwo_main iterates the row peeling; LeadInterlaced takes
the graded component with the original leading monomial. The present proof
combines those steps but makes no stronger integral assumption.

2. CB2: EXPLICIT TABLEAUX, MONICITY, AND INTEGRAL SPANNING

Let SS(r,n,nu) be the finite set of nonnegative arrays e with the fixed column
sums nu and (CB1.1). It is finite because 0<=e_at<=nu_t. If it is nonempty,
nu is decreasing: take a=r in (CB1.1). Make a diagram with row t of length
nu_t and fill that row with e_at copies of a, in weakly increasing order.
The inequalities (CB1.1) are equivalent to strict increase down columns.
Indeed, if the qth entry in a lower row is a, the lower row has at least q
entries <=a; the upper row then has at least q entries <a. Conversely, strict
column increase implies the same counting inequalities.

For every tableau column c, of height h_c, let I_c be its strictly increasing
list of entries. Define the integer polynomial

  b_e(X)=product_c det X_(I_c,{1,...,h_c}).             (CB2.1)

Each initial-column minor is invariant under every C_j -> C_j+t C_l, l<j:
if j exceeds its height nothing changes, while otherwise both columns occur
and the added determinant has two equal columns. Its column degree is the
indicator of {1,...,h_c}. Thus b_e belongs to nabla_nu(R).

For an increasing list I=(i_1<...<i_h), the diagonal monomial of its minor
leads with coefficient +1. In a nonidentity permutation, at the first column
where it differs, the previously used rows coincide, so the selected row is
larger than the diagonal row. That monomial is smaller in column-lex order.
Products preserve monic leading terms; consequently LT(b_e)=X^e. Row weight
is also homogeneous, with a-th coordinate sum_t e_at.

Distinct e give linearly independent b_e over R: a nonzero finite relation
has a largest leading exponent, whose coefficient cannot be canceled. To
span, start with any nonzero f in nabla_nu(R). CB1 puts its leading exponent
e in SS. Subtract lc(f)b_e; the result remains in nabla and has a strictly
smaller leading monomial, unless zero. Since all polynomials have fixed
column degree nu, only finitely many monomials can occur. Repetition ends.
No coefficient is divided at any step. Thus

  nabla_nu(R)=direct_sum_(e in SS) R b_e.              (CB2.2)

The empty shape gives b=1 and a rank-one module. If SS is empty the same
argument shows nabla=0. For a square k by k matrix and a partition, SS is
nonempty: use the highest tableau with row t filled by t.

The same integer polynomials (CB2.1) give a basis over every domain. This
includes the fraction field and any residue field, with unchanged monic
leading terms. This assertion is proved directly for those domains; it
does not assume that taking invariants generally commutes with reduction.
For disjoint matrix blocks, products of basis elements span by expansion,
and their distinct blockwise leading exponents prove independence. This is
the source's TensorBasis without any extra tensor-flatness hypothesis.

The source SSBasis existentially chooses a monic minor product for each e;
the sorted tableau gives one explicit allowed choice. Downstream results
use only its leading exponent, coefficient one, weight homogeneity and
spanning. They do not depend on a different canonical tableau normalization.

3. CB3: EXACT DIMENSION BY A FINITE DETERMINANT IDENTITY

For a decreasing integer tuple lambda of length k set

  D_k(lambda)=product_(1<=i<j<=k)
                    (lambda_i-lambda_j+j-i)/(j-i),
  D_0=D_1=1.

For a partition nu of length k, SS(k,k,nu) counts tableaux of shape nu with
entries 1,...,k. Removing entries k leaves a shape mu of length k-1 satisfying

  nu_i >= mu_i >= nu_(i+1), 1<=i<k.                  (CB3.1)

This is a bijection with the union of tableaux of the smaller shapes: the
removed k's form a horizontal strip, because columns are strictly increasing,
and reinserting k in that strip preserves weak rows and strict columns.
Thus the count satisfies the usual interlacing recurrence, which is exactly
the source's dGT. The following direct identity evaluates the recurrence.

For strictly decreasing integers x_1>...>x_k, let Delta(x)=product_(i<j)
(x_i-x_j). Then

  sum_(x_(i+1)<=y_i<x_i, 1<=i<k) Delta(y)
       = Delta(x)/(k-1)!.                            (CB3.2)

Proof. Write Delta(y)=det[y_i^(k-1-j)]_(1<=i,j<k).
For each j choose a rational polynomial Q_j with

  Q_j(t+1)-Q_j(t)=t^(k-1-j).

Such a polynomial is obtained by successively canceling the leading term;
its degree is k-j and its leading coefficient 1/(k-j). Multilinearity of
the determinant sums each row independently, giving

  det[Q_j(x_i)-Q_j(x_(i+1))]_(1<=i,j<k).

This equals the determinant with k rows (Q_1(x_i),...,Q_(k-1)(x_i),1): apply
the determinant-one row transformation taking successive differences in
the first k-1 rows. The last column becomes (0,...,0,1). The column degrees
are k-1,k-2,...,1,0. Triangular changes to monomial columns show that its
determinant is Delta(x) times the product of leading coefficients, namely
1/(k-1)!. This proves (CB3.2), including its sign and endpoints.

Now set x_i=nu_i+k-i and y_i=mu_i+(k-1)-i. Condition (CB3.1) becomes
x_(i+1)<=y_i<x_i. Induction and (CB3.2) give

  #SS(k,k,nu)=Delta(x)/product_(j=1)^(k-1) j!=D_k(nu).

Subtracting lambda_k from all entries preserves D, so normalized dominant
integer weights have the asserted count. Negative reversal also preserves
D: the pair (i,j) corresponds to (k+1-j,k+1-i), preserving each positive
factor. These facts require no representation-theoretic dimension formula.

By CB2 the rank/field dimension of the actual square polynomial lattice is
D_k(lambda). Tensoring gives

  dim_K Vsp(K,lambda0)=product_s D_k(lambda0_s),       (CB3.3)

because the effective odd weight is -rev(lambda0_s). This proves exactly
tableLineDegreeFive.FewBadOrbits, whose name alone does not describe its
mathematical content. It does not count the source's separate selected-symbol
submodule Gen or its admissible frame data.

4. CB4: THE SELECTED COMPLEMENTARY FAMILY

Write k=m+1; the source assumes m>=2 in this application. Let T be its selected
set TT and lambda0_s a dominant integer tuple at each position. Put

  d_s = lambda0_(s,1)-lambda0_(s,2)  if s in T, otherwise 0,
  ell_s = lambda0_s - d_s(1,0,...,0).

At selected positions ell_s=(lambda0_(s,2),lambda0_(s,2),lambda0_(s,3),...,
lambda0_(s,k)); at unselected positions ell_s=lambda0_s. This is restW.
The effective weight is ell_s for an even/vector position and -rev(ell_s)
for an odd/covector position. Subtract its last entry to get nu_s.

The exact source index Xs is a subset of SS(k,k,nu_s): no further restriction
off T; e_11=nu_1 at a selected even position; e_1t=0 for all t at a selected
odd position. The source uses indices starting at zero, so these are its
e(0,0)=nu(0) and e(0,t)=0.

Even case. The first column sum and e_11=nu_1 force e_a1=0 for a>1, and
interlacing forces e_1t=0 for t>1. Delete row 1 and column 1. This gives SS of
size k-1 and shape (nu_2,...,nu_k). Conversely insert nu_1 at the top left
and zeros elsewhere in the new row/column. Interlacing against the new first
column follows because every relevant cumulative count is at most nu_2<=nu_1.

Odd case. The imposed first row is zero. Normalization gives nu_k=0, hence
the last column is zero. Deleting that row and column is a bijection with
SS of size k-1 and shape (nu_1,...,nu_(k-1)); the inverse inserts zeros.
Because ell_1=ell_2, in fact nu_(k-1)=nu_k=0. This stronger fact is exactly
what the separate CL leading-term product-independence lemma requires.

For even parity the reduced shape is the original tail minus lambda0_k.
For odd parity it is lambda0_2 minus the reverse of the original tail.
CB3 therefore gives the same count D_(k-1)(lambda0_2,...,lambda0_k) for both.
At unselected positions the count is D_k(lambda0). Thus the product index I
has exactly

  n2=product_(s in T)D_(k-1)(lambda0_(s,2),...,lambda0_(s,k))
        product_(s notin T)D_k(lambda0_s).             (CB4.1)

Choose at each block the b_e from CB2 and form B_i=product_s b_(i_s), i in I.
This is a subset of the integral tensor basis; its span is a direct summand,
and its reductions remain independent over every residue field. It supplies
the actual bX and sO in TheoremIIClosedOfTestBound.primStep_of. The additional
independence of products Th(Q_i)B_i is the explicit leading-term injection
already proved in CL2--CL3; CB2 supplies exactly the missing monic basis
hypothesis there. We do not infer that stronger statement merely from
independence of the B_i.

5. CB5: QUANTITATIVE DIMENSION COMPARISON

For one selected weight let d=lambda_1-lambda_2 and delta_j=lambda_2-lambda_j.
The product definition gives the exact ratio

  binom(d+k-1,k-1)D_(k-1)(lambda_2,...,lambda_k)/D_k(lambda)
       =product_(j=2)^k (d+j-1)/(d+delta_j+j-1).       (CB5.1)

Assume d>=a>0 and 0<=delta_j<=c0. Write each factor as 1-u_j, where
u_j=delta_j/(d+delta_j+j-1) lies in [0,1). The elementary finite product
inequality product(1-u_j)>=1-sum u_j yields

  ratio >= 1-(k-1)c0/a >= 1-kc0/a.                 (CB5.2)

This proof remains valid if the right side is negative. To multiply the
weaker source lower bounds use its additional kc0/a<=1. Applying (CB5.2) at
every selected position, CB3.3 at all positions, and CB4.1 gives

  (1-kc0/a)^|T| dim_K Vsp(K,lambda0)
      <= product_(s in T)binom(d_s+k-1,k-1) n2.      (CB5.3)

The source WeightHyp supplies the assumptions: lambda_1=aT, lambda_k=-bT,
all intervening entries have absolute value <=cpp, aT>=a+cpp, bT>=0, and
bT+2cpp<=c0. Hence d>=aT-cpp>=a, and every tail entry plus bT lies between
0 and bT+cpp<=c0. In particular 0<=delta_j<=c0. The source also has an upper
bound for d and many frame inequalities; they are not needed for this count.
The factor (k-1) above is slightly sharper than the source's k, but we retain
the source factor downstream and claim no improvement to the RH exponent.

6. CB6: INTEGRAL CARTAN MULTIPLICATION AND PRIMITIVITY

Let nu and mu be partitions of length n, and set nu_(n+1)=mu_(n+1)=0.
Then multiplication has exact integral span

  nabla_(nu+mu)(R)=span_R{fg:f in nabla_nu(R), g in nabla_mu(R)}.  (CB6.1)

The inclusion from right to left follows immediately from column degrees
and multiplicative generic invariance. For the reverse inclusion, use CB2
to write the target in minor products. In any such product of shape nu+mu,
the number of factors of height j is

  (nu_j+mu_j)-(nu_(j+1)+mu_(j+1)).

Choose nu_j-nu_(j+1) of them for the first group, and all remaining factors
of that height for the second. The groups have shapes nu and mu respectively.
Every group is an allowed product of initial-column minors; it need not be
standard. Thus each basis element itself factors into a permissible product,
which proves (CB6.1) without any scalar division. Empty groups give the unit.
The same argument works with rectangular matrices and with empty modules.

Over a DVR O with uniformizer pi, coefficient divisibility is exact lattice
divisibility: if f in nabla_nu(O) has all coefficients divisible by pi, then
f/pi again satisfies its column degrees and generic invariance. Equivalently
nabla is saturated inside the ambient multidegree polynomial module. Thus
f is primitive iff its reduction is a nonzero coefficient polynomial.
The residue polynomial ring is a domain, so primitive factors have primitive
product. This proves the source's CartanDomain as well as CartanOnto. Its
primitive-product assertion requires the designated element to generate the
maximal ideal (or be irreducible in the DVR); for an arbitrary nonzero w it
is not claimed as a residue-field argument. Span (CB6.1) needs no DVR.

7. CB7: EXACT BASIS, DETERMINANT, TENSOR, AND EDGE TRANSPORT

Let O embed in its fraction field K and M in GL_k(K). For dominant integer
lambda, put c=lambda_k and nu=lambda-c. Write R(M)f(X)=f(MX). Then

  R(M)R(N)=R(NM),
  L_lambda(M)=det(M)^(-c) R(M^(-1)) nabla_nu(O).     (CB7.1)

This agrees literally with StatementD.nablaL (a COMAP under R(M)) followed
by nablaLTw (scalar det(M)^(-c)). The equivalent membership condition is
det(M)^c R(M)F in nabla_nu(O), not its inverse. This distinction already
matters for the one-dimensional shape.

If N O^k=M O^k, then N=MU for U in GL_k(O). Row substitution by U and U^-1
preserves nabla_nu(O), because it has integral coefficients, preserves column
degrees, and commutes with all column additions; det U is a unit. Hence
(CB7.1) is basis independent. Since the source H0 is the intersection of
nablaLTw over all bases of the same lattice, it is exactly L_lambda(M).
This is also EL2's elementary basis-independence proof, included to make the
Cartan transport explicit rather than treating it as an abstract identity.

For a finite set of positions s, take the effective weight lambda^eff_s=
lambda0_s at even s and -rev(lambda0_s) at odd s. The tensor lattice has basis

  product_s det(M_s)^(-lambda^eff_(s,k))
            R(M_s^-1)b_(e_s),

with block embeddings understood. Tensor products are products of polynomials
in disjoint variables. The dimensions and selected-family indices proved
above therefore refer to the actual source Vsp/Hten, not only to isomorphic
abstract representations.

At an actual edge, the EL midpoint calculation supplies matrices M_s with
LeAt(s,e)=M_s O^k. The source edge exponent is the signed additive integer

  A_r(lambda0)=sum_s sum_(a=1)^r lambda^eff_(s,a).

No assertion that A_r>=0 is needed. For nonzero w define

  C_lambda(F)=w^(A_r(lambda))
      (product_s det(M_s)^(lambda^eff_(s,k))) blockAct(M_s)F.  (CB7.2)

By (CB7.1) this is an O-linear isomorphism from the source
Re=w^(-Ar)HLe onto the integral tensor coefficient lattice. Its inverse is
w^(-Ar) times the product determinant factor with negative exponents and
blockAct(M_s^-1). In particular the complementary vectors are explicitly

  sh_i=C_rest^-1(B_i).                               (CB7.3)

Their integrality, indexing, linear independence and direct-summand property
follow at once. This agrees with TransStep.transStep: the existential
tr_tens_lift witness is unique after applying the invertible coordinate map.

Both lamAt and Ar are additive, and normalized partitions satisfy
nu(lambda+mu)=nu(lambda)+nu(mu) for dominant weights. Thus

  C_(lambda+mu)(FG)=C_lambda(F) C_mu(G).              (CB7.4)

Tensor (CB6.1) over positions and transport by (CB7.4). This proves the actual
source OntoAll: Re(lambda+mu) is spanned integrally by products from
Re(lambda) and Re(mu). Setting lambda=flW(d), mu=restW gives precisely the
Cartan span used for contraction detection in CL5. Primitive multiplication
and coefficient reductions transport as well. Signed determinant twists and
the edge's w^(-Ar) scalar introduce no additional denominators into the
integral coordinate argument.

8. WHAT HAS BEEN CLOSED, AND WHAT HAS NOT

Closed here:
  * The exact monic array-indexed integral basis in the actual invariant
    polynomial module, including reduction to the residue field.
  * The selected even/odd complementary family and its exact cardinality.
  * The actual Vsp dimension formula (source FewBadOrbits), and the
    quantitative source top_count/count_of_exact comparison.
  * Integral Cartan onto and primitive-product properties, with the source
    H0/Hten/Re identifications and complementary vector construction.

Already handled elsewhere and used only at their stated boundaries:
  * CL2--CL3 prove the additional product-family leading-term injection and
    primitive-combination argument. CB supplies its former basis input.
  * EL proves existence of the actual midpoint matrices and the local
    polynomial-lattice transport. CB7 rechecks the convention and additivity.
  * IW proves normalized Fock duality and the denominator sandwich;
    neither follows merely from the basis or dimension formula here.

Separate from CB:
  * Incoming membership of the initial/word-closed selected symbol family
    is handled by the distinct IN/WT companions in this version. The actual
    initial route is Lemma5aAt via blocked MemAtB/PlanePairsOnBlocksAt and
    rest5aMono_of_cut; the word route is WordConditionAt via OneOpStep and
    WordBoundStep. Lemma5aGeneratorProductsAt is a stronger, uninstantiated
    interface on this call path. CB's basis is a different polynomial family.
  * Quantitative dimension/saturation of that selected symbol space and the
    simultaneous frame/size hypotheses. The exact complementary count n2
    is not a substitute for those selected-symbol estimates.
  * The global arithmetic and cohomological window assertions, or the
    geometric/spectral estimates listed separately in the main reconstruction.
