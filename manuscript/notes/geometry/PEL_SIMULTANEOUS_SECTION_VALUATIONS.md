# Simultaneous realization of valuation bounds on the marked section charts

For the marked PEL model, a single algebraic integral frame can reproduce **every member of a prescribed finite list of section valuations at once**. There is a second statement at a frame whose coefficient valuation is trivial. Its vector of orders is the slope of an entire sequence of algebraic frames at any one chosen finite place. These statements prove the transfer left open in the closing paragraph of the [marked section laws](PEL_SECTION_LAWS.md), conditional on the marked-model chart comparison described below.

The [direct semiabelian argument](PEL_SEMIABELIAN_SECTION_TRANSFER.md) also proves the native regularity law from the generic marked data. The chart-centre condition here gives the stronger freedom to prescribe the horizontal place.

## Input and result

Fix a finite place $\lambda$ of the small coefficient field. Write $O$ for the chart's complete DVR, $K=\operatorname{Frac}(O)$, $\pi$ for a uniformizer and $e_O>0$ for its ramification factor. For any mixed-characteristic chart test $V$, the normalized value is $\operatorname{ord}_V(x)/(e_O\operatorname{ord}_V(\pi))$. The marked good-level comparison supplies a finite family of finite-type $O$-domains with the following three properties. Every mixed-characteristic integral frame is covered. Every coefficient-trivial frame is covered with centre $\mathfrak p$ satisfying $(\pi)+\mathfrak p\ne A$. And any point of a chart over the ring of integers of a finite extension of $K$ which meets the generic frame locus gives a native algebraic test. In each comparison, all section readings are computed at the same point. These are the section-independent parts of the cited chart supplier. The cited good-level supplier assumes positive rank, good level at every rational prime and the geometric packages PartI and N1ab. Equivalently for this argument, one can directly supply ModelAt at the chosen place, the section base-change law, the evaluation laws and invariance under integral changes of frame. A single semistable extension at one algebraic point supplies only that point. Let $f_1,\ldots,f_m$ be sections, allowing different weights.

1. For each mixed-characteristic integral frame $v$ above $\lambda$, there is an algebraic test $y$ above $\lambda$ with $v_y(f_i)=v(f_i)$ for every $i$.
2. For each coefficient-trivial frame $q$, there are constants $c_i\in\mathbb Q$ for the finite readings $d_i=\operatorname{ord}_q(f_i)$. For **every sufficiently large integer** $N$, there is one algebraic test $y_N$ above the fixed $\lambda$ such that
   $$
     v_{y_N}(f_i)=N d_i+c_i\quad(d_i<\infty),\qquad
     v_{y_N}(f_i)=+\infty\quad(d_i=+\infty).
   $$
   The constants and threshold may depend on the frame, place and list.

## Proof

In the chart containing the given frame, choose $u$ in its specified ideal with nonzero image. The regularity clause presents each reading as $a_i/(\pi u)^{m_i}$. The cross-multiplication identity in the definition of a chart reading makes this presentation give the required value even if the chart map has a kernel. Present $A$ by finitely many generators and relations over $O$, which is Noetherian. Integral coordinates, the relations, $u\ne0$, and vanishing or prescribed valuation comparisons for these fractions are a finite list of conditions in the language of valued fields.

For the first assertion, if $r_i=v(f_i)$ is finite, write $e_O r_i=p_i/h_i$ with $h_i>0$. Require the reading to be nonzero and $f_i^{h_i}/\pi^{p_i}$ to be a unit. For an infinite reading require $f_i=0$. The covered frame solves the resulting formula in a valued extension of $K$. Extend its valuation to an algebraic closure of that extension and take $\overline K$ inside it. Algebraically closed valued fields admit quantifier elimination in the language of divisibility, so the formula has a solution in $\overline K$. Its finite list of coordinates lies in one finite extension $L/K$. Integrality puts these coordinates in $O_L$, a DVR finite over the complete DVR $O$. The chart's algebraic-point clause gives the required native test and the normalization above. [Haskell–Hrushovski–Macpherson, Theorem 2.1.1](https://math.mcmaster.ca/~haskell/papers/ACVF-elimimag.pdf) · [Marker, Theorem 4.4 and the language of divisibility](https://homepages.math.uic.edu/~marker/math512-f18/vf-4.pdf)

For the second assertion, use the covering map $A\to V$, with $V$ discretely valued and $\pi$ a unit. Let $\mathfrak p$ be its centre and $B=A/\mathfrak p\subset\kappa(V)$. The coefficient map embeds $O$ in $\kappa(V)$, since every nonzero element of $O$ maps to a unit in $V$. Choose a maximal ideal $\mathfrak m\subset B$ containing $\pi$, using the centre condition. Fix a uniformizer $t$ of $V$. For the nonzero section readings $x_i$, adjoin the residues of $x_i/t^{d_i}$ to $\operatorname{Frac}B$ inside $\kappa(V)$. The resulting field $D$ is finitely generated over $\operatorname{Frac}B$, and $B_{\mathfrak m}$ is a Noetherian local domain with a nonzero nonunit $\pi$. The DVR domination theorem therefore supplies a discrete valuation $w$ of $D$ dominating $B_{\mathfrak m}$. Its proof blows up the maximal ideal to reduce to dimension one and takes a localization of the integral closure. In particular $w(\pi)>0$ and all the finitely many adjoined residues have integer $w$-value. Extend this valuation to $\kappa(V)$. All required ratios of secondary values remain rational, independently of the value group of the extension. [Stacks Project, DVR domination, Lemma 10.119.13](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/00PH) · [Stacks Project, valuation extension](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/00I8)

Compose the discrete valuation of $V$ with this valuation on the residue field. For $x\ne0$, this composite value is
$$
  \bigl(d(x),\,w(\overline{x/t^{d(x)}})\bigr),\qquad d(x)=\operatorname{ord}_V(x),
$$
in lexicographic order. The images of all chart coordinates remain integral because the residue of each primary unit from $A$ belongs to $B\subset O_w$. For each nonzero reading $x_i$, put $b_i=w(\overline{x_i/t^{d_i}})/w(\pi)\in\mathbb Q$. In a variable $T\ne0$, prescribe integral chart coordinates as above, the same vanishing readings, and
$$
       (f_i/T^{d_i})^{h_i}/\pi^{p_i}\ \text{a unit},
       \qquad  p_i/h_i=b_i,\quad h_i>0 .
$$
Call the resulting existential formula $P(T)$. The composite field admits a solution at $T=t$ with $v(t)>M v(\pi)$ for every integer $M$. The valuation restricts on $K$ to the given place. Thus for each $M$, quantifier elimination transfers existence of such a solution with $v(T)>M v(\pi)$ to $\overline K$.

In one variable quantifier elimination makes $P(T)$ a finite Boolean combination of polynomial equalities and valuation comparisons. For sufficiently large $v(T)$, each nonzero polynomial has valuation $v(a)+j\,v(T)$, where $aT^j$ is its lowest nonzero monomial. Since $\overline K$ has rank one, each comparison eventually has constant truth value. Hence $P(T)$ holds for every sufficiently small nonzero $T$. Set $T=\pi^{e_O N}$. Taking the one finite extension containing the coordinates and applying the algebraic-point clause gives the second assertion with $c_i=b_i/e_O$.

## Consequence for the outstanding transfer

Apply the first assertion to $\eta,s_1,\ldots,s_n$ on one frame. Both integrality and the asserted bound $\min_i v(s_i)\le\nu_\lambda+c'v(\eta)$ carry over with the original real constants. At a coefficient-trivial frame with finite $d_\eta$, apply the bounds at $y_N$, divide by $N$, and let $N$ grow. The finite minimum commutes with this limit, the term $\nu_\lambda/N$ tends to zero, and the result is $\min_i d_i\le c'd_\eta$. Infinite readings are preserved exactly. In particular, if every $s_i$ vanishes and $\eta$ does not, the alleged algebraic-test bound would already fail at every $y_N$. For $\eta=0$, only its integrality is required and follows immediately. The same limit proves integrality of any section. This verifies both clauses of the native regularity law, including the uniform minimum at mixed-characteristic frames when required. The transfer uses no family-dependent refinement of the chart. It does require the inequality at all native algebraic tests.
