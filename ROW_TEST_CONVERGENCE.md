# Convergence of the bottom tests and nonvanishing of the core period

Fix an arithmetic stage, its Hida setting, and an allowed marked choice. Its bottom Hilbert space identifies with the actual space of multiplicity maps at the trivial finite character. Write $w$ for the chosen nonzero smooth tensor. A test applies the $m$ theta lifts on the row $(i,0)$ to $t(w)$, then pairs the resulting automorphic function with a column on the definite top group in a Bessel period. Both the real data and the finite coset in every arrow are inputs of this integral. [Definitions and quantifiers][formula]

## Absolute convergence for every real substitution

**Theorem.** All these iterated tests converge on every bottom vector. The assertion holds for every replacement of the real data, with the finite tests, measures and models of the original choice. In particular it gives `LinK` on its stated domain.

At the initial node the input is a smooth cuspidal automorphic function of the given discrete-series type. In the Schrödinger realization the kernel is a rational lattice sum of a Schwartz function. At fixed open finite level its absolute value is bounded by a Schwartz seminorm times fixed powers of the two adelic heights. The same exponent can be used for all derivatives in the output variable, by applying the differentiated oscillator representation to the test vector first. A smooth cuspidal vector in the prescribed automorphic realization at fixed finite level decays on Siegel sets faster than any prescribed power. Integrating the product gives absolute convergence at every output point and a smooth automorphic function of uniform moderate growth. The constants can depend on the chosen row and test. The statement requires the eventual scale to work for all tests, while allowing each convergent integral its own majorant. [Lattice-sum majorant](THETA_CUSPIDAL_FACTORIZATION.md#convergence-and-the-growth-bound-on-the-prescribed-integral) · [Miller–Schmid, Theorem 2.4](https://arxiv.org/abs/1106.2149)

To iterate this estimate we need cuspidality on the *increasing* rank arrows. In signed orthonormal frames one such arrow goes from rank $n$ to $n+h$, where $h=r_D+6$ is odd. With the determinant shift removed from the contragredient of the integrated representation, its parameter is $(\alpha,\beta\,;\,\gamma,\delta)$. The lengths are $x,y,z,v$ respectively. The first and third lists are positive, the other two negative, with $|a|\ge(h+1)/2$ on all four. The larger partner has signature $(x+v+h,z+y)$ or $(x+v,z+y+h)$, according to which compact block receives the new string. On the actual row this follows by subtracting the center $c$ of the new string. The old grid entries $a$ satisfy $a+(h+1)/2\le c$. The common half-integer parity shifts cancel in this subtraction. The partner moves negative coordinates to the opposite block and adds the new string entirely in one block. Negating the output Hermitian space interchanges the two signature labels, as in the signed grid comparison. [Grid entries and shifts][addpar] · [Two signature patterns][signed] · [Pinning the sign and the oscillator character on this arrow][arrow]

A proper rational isotropic subspace of dimension $d\ge1$ reduces both target signatures by $d$. If the new rank $n+h-2d$ is larger than $n$, Ichino's necessary inequalities for a nonzero lift are $r'\ge x+v$ and $s'\ge z+y$. One of these fails because that particular target coordinate started at equality. If the new rank is smaller than $n$, a nonzero lift requires a centered consecutive string of length $2d-h$ in the input parameter. This length is positive and odd, so the string contains zero, which is absent. Equality of the two ranks is impossible by parity. For target rank zero the oscillator is the character giving the centered string of length $n$, with the same obstruction. Thus every lower Witt member arising from a proper constant term has zero lift. The tower constant-term formula expresses the constant term of the original lift as a lower lift after taking the partial Fourier coefficient of its test. Lower local vanishing kills this expression. Applying compact averaging to smooth inputs and using the Schwartz majorant to pass from polynomials to smooth tests gives the same conclusion for the integral originally specified. [Ichino, Theorem 4.1(i),(ii), §§4.1–4.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.09148#page=7) · [Constant term and passage to smooth tests](THETA_INTERIOR_CUSPIDALITY.md#from-local-vanishing-to-a-global-constant-term)

The surviving archimedean type is also the prescribed one. The same formula of Ichino gives its cohomologically induced representation with the $h$ new entries in a single compact block. The gap just displayed is the sufficient regular range in Ichino’s Remark 4.2. The only nontoral Levi is compact $U(h)$, and its half-sum of roots gives the consecutive string in that block. The other entries are the four old lists in partner order. Equivalently the local Howe quotient has exactly the partner type. For a compact-finite test, projecting to each irreducible component of the cuspidal spectrum gives an invariant local pairing, hence only this type survives. The smooth assertion follows by the compact-projection and globalization argument for the multiplicity maps. This proof works for either location of the single compact block. Induction therefore takes each partial integral to a smooth cuspidal function of the required type, permitting the same estimate at the next arrow. In the initial identification with the grid one uses the original Haar fundamental domains, self-dual frame and kernels on each coset; changing only the real polynomials preserves all three. [Local parameter and spectral projection](THETA_CUSPIDAL_FACTORIZATION.md) · [Actual grid, measures, and kernels][match] · [Induction on the row][ind]

At the top, the reductive group in the **outer** Bessel integration is definite, hence has compact adelic quotient. The inner group is a unipotent radical; its rational quotient is compact and has mass one for the specified measure. The integrand is continuous. For a simultaneous bound choose compact representatives for both quotients. Moving an outer representative by a rational element conjugates the radical and preserves its rational character, and moving the inner representative by a rational unipotent leaves the integrand invariant. This bounds it everywhere by its supremum on the pair of compact sets. Both integrations are absolutely convergent. Finite level makes the column locally constant in the finite argument and its representation at infinity is finite dimensional, so the same proof works for every coefficient functional. This completes convergence on all original catalogues and all real substitutions. [Quotient estimates and rational invariance][bcomp] · [All substituted tests][conv]

## Detecting a row by a Poincaré series

A separate, quantitative assertion produces a nonzero detector. Here is the exact estimate on the actual row. Let $Y$ be the bottom unitary space of rank $r_D$, $V$ its prescribed one-line extension of rank $r_D+1$, $f=[F:\mathbb Q]$, and $\mathfrak m$ the core principal-congruence modulus. Let $\phi=t(w')$ be the smooth cusp form obtained from the bottom real vectors of the local branching construction for a nonzero row $t$. Its selected tensor is nonzero, and cyclicity at each archimedean factor implies that $\phi$ is nonzero. Rapid decay gives a finite positive supremum $b_0$. Choose $x$ with $|\phi(x)|>b_0/2$. Write $Q=C f(r_D+1)^4\log b_{\rm scale}$ for the allowed archimedean loss. The real coefficient on $U(V)$ must restrict on $U(Y)$ to convolution that returns the selected vector times a scalar $c\ne0$ after finite level volume factors. Its decay and derivative estimates are also required with this $Q$. These clauses, with one $C$ before the choice of stage, are stronger than the existence of a local branching functional. [Actual real estimate][real] · [Passage to the given adelic row][poin]

For $0<s\le1/2$, the short-vector estimate, obtained from the vanishing of the cusp form's constant term and the derivative bound along a short unipotent direction, would give
$$
 |\phi(x)|\le b_0 e^Q\, r_D^2 f g_0g_1\,\operatorname{rd}_E(2s)^{1/m}
$$
if the bottom lattice at $x$ had a short line in the source's product norm. The depth inequalities make its right hand side at most $b_0/2$. Thus this lattice satisfies the required lower bound. Put $R=s^f\sqrt{\mathrm N\mathfrak m}$, $K=(6\dim V+9)f$. The same depth estimate gives $1<R$ and $2e^{Q+K}\le R$. In the integral of the core Poincaré series against $\phi$, unfolding by the bottom rational subgroup gives identity term $c\phi(x)$. The other terms supported by the level inject into the nonzero vectors of the translated congruence lattice via the difference from the distinguished vector of the added line. Its stabilizer is exactly the embedded bottom group, which is why the difference determines the coset. For each such vector $z$ the majorant is $b_0|c|e^Q\prod_\tau((1+\|z_\tau\|)/3)^{-(2\dim V+5)}$. The minimum of the product norm is at least $R$. Summing the majorant gives $b_0|c| e^{Q+K}/R\le b_0|c|/2$. This proves absolute unfolding and strict dominance of the identity. Positive mass of the identity term alone would not give this conclusion. [Depth inequalities][pays] · [Short-vector estimate][short] · [Sum on the actual congruence lattice][sum] · [Dominance and summable unfolding][tail]

The Poincaré series here uses a translated integrable discrete-series coefficient, with finite test the indicated principal level. Its cuspidality and real type place it in the required space of core forms. The resulting nonzero core period pairs some core vector with $t(w')$. For the *prescribed* $0\ne w$, the translates of $w$ span a dense subspace of the smooth irreducible module in the sense needed for this continuous period. If all its translates paired to zero with this core vector, $w'$ would also pair to zero. Change variables by a successful real translate and move its inverse to the core vector. Approximation there by compact-finite vectors supplies one such core vector $a$. All finite levels remain fixed. This proves the passage from the indicated quantitative branching and depth estimates to a nonzero core period for each allowed $w$. ## Why the core period reaches the finite catalogue

There are two further inputs to this last implication. First, a compact-finite value of the core multiplicity space must lie in the span of values descending along the prescribed chain from allowed definite columns. In finite dimension it suffices that the adjoint of the downward composite be injective. Resolve a vector into simultaneous good-prime Hecke eigenspaces by their finite-level Petersson metric. Along an upward step the new local parameter is the old parameter, with the fixed twist and the prescribed string adjoined. This operation is injective on the labels since the same multiset can be cancelled. A successful upward test on each nonzero vector of a single label therefore gives a nonzero adjoint there by Petersson adjunction. Different transferred labels are linearly independent, so cancellation cannot destroy injectivity on their sum. Repeating this on every step proves surjectivity of the downward composite. This argument covers multiplicities within a label because the test must succeed on each nonzero vector there. At the positive-definite endpoint the finite double-class model and its coefficient dual identify the admissible top columns. Measuring the core with its actual measures identifies these values with the reachable chains. [Chain adjunction and distinct eigenlabels][adj] · [Endpoint and level identifications][core]

For the core chain, put $n$ for its initial rank and $h$ for its step size. The source gives $h\ge n+2$. At the upward norm point $(h+1)/2$ the original rank-$n$ partial Euler factor converges absolutely by the trivial Satake bound $(n-1)/2$. Each previously adjoined string contributes character factors $L^S(h-j,\chi_i\chi_k^{-1})$, $0\le j<h$. They are nonzero at these positive integers: for arguments larger than one use the Euler product, and at one use Hecke nonvanishing for a nontrivial unitary character. The characters have distinct infinity exponents so the quotients are nontrivial and are not pure norm powers. This proves the required partial $L$-value is nonzero. To use it in adjunction the chosen tests must also have a nonzero semilocal zeta integral. The finite coefficient has prescribed support and invariance. If it kills the complement of the real group times the level, integrating on the level gives the positive Petersson scalar times the real oscillator–discrete-series coefficient integral. One nonzero real polynomial coefficient suffices. The doubling inner-product formula applied to those tests then gives a nonzero upward lift. An unrestricted existence assertion for an arbitrary Schwartz test would leave this fixed-level step open. [Partial-product calculation at the norm point][chainl] · [Finite support and real integral in the prescribed variables][semi] · [Gan–Qiu–Takeda, §1.7 and Part III](https://arxiv.org/abs/1207.4709) · [Fixed-data construction][all]

The finite support assertion in this argument can be checked on each selected split half. Write $A=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal O_v}(L',L)$ for the homomorphisms of the two local core lattices and $C=X_0+\mathfrak p^e A$ for the prescribed coset. Here $e\ge1$ and $X_0$ is onto modulo $\mathfrak p^e$. Such a simultaneous choice exists because the larger lattice has at least the rank of the smaller: take projections and sections over each factor of $\mathcal O_E/\mathfrak m$ and lift them to homomorphisms of the projective lattices. At a split half the Heisenberg characters identifying $C$ make a nonzero oscillator autocoefficient at $g$ possible only when $C\cap gC\ne\varnothing$. Indeed translation in the complementary polarization detects the characters of $X_0$ and $gX_0$ on the joint annihilator of $\mathfrak p^eA$ and $g\mathfrak p^e A$. Distinct characters are orthogonal. Equality says $X_0-gX_0\in\mathfrak p^e A+g\mathfrak p^e A$ by double annihilation for compact open lattices, exactly the claimed intersection.

If $Y=gX$ lies in that intersection with $X,Y\in C$, both $X$ and $Y$ are onto $L$ by reduction modulo $\mathfrak p$ and Nakayama's lemma. Thus $gL=L$. Reducing $gX=Y$ modulo $\mathfrak p^e$ now gives $gX_0=X_0$ there. Composing with a section gives $g\equiv 1\pmod{\mathfrak p^e}$. The conjugate half is forced by the inverse-adjoint relation. Away from the selected places the domain of the semilocal integral already imposes the lattice stabilizer. Real polynomial operators commute with all these finite translations. Hence the off-level part of the semilocal integral really does vanish for the chosen congruence coset, including for every real polynomial used in it. ## The real zeta integral on the prescribed core coset

Let $n$ be the smaller rank at this core arrow and $m=n+t$ the larger rank. On the actual core $t=\operatorname{sLen}(D)-1$. Both splitting characters on the exchanged arrow equal $\chi_{m_D-k}^{-1}$, with common infinity exponent $a=-\operatorname{cen}_{m_D-k}$, where $m_D$ denotes the number of arrows. The core uses its own even ranks and exponents. For every fixed nonzero core vacuum $f$ and every covolume-one pairing there are a polynomial in the real creation operators and a tensor of compact-finite discrete-series vectors for which the real zeta integral is nonzero. The same choices satisfy the height estimate at $s_1=0<t/2$. [Core parameters and characters][rz-core] · [Exact assertion][rz-target]

At a real place the smaller parameter after removing its shift and undoing the dual is $(\alpha,\beta;\gamma,\delta)$. Write $x,y,z,w$ for the lengths. The larger signature is $(x+w+t,z+y)$. Hence Li's inequalities $x+w\le r$ and $z+y\le s$ hold, and $|u|\ge(t+3)/2$ for every old entry exceeds the local threshold $(m-n+1)/2$. Ichino's Lemma 8.2 gives a nonzero small-to-large lift at this signature. For the actual core both splitting exponents in Ichino's integer convention are $2a$; the old entries belong to $(t+1)/2+\mathbb Z$. To compare in the stored row, pull both smaller-group actions back by $g\mapsto\bar g$ in its signed orthonormal frame. The linear isometry of the smaller discrete-series model identifies its pulled-back action with the covering discrete series times the same unitary determinant factor as the oscillator. These factors cancel in the oscillator coefficient times the conjugate discrete-series coefficient. The exchange producing this action and the frame comparison are the ones used in the core construction. [Exchange and actual four-list assertion][rz-core] · [Smaller linear frame comparison][rz-frame] · [Archimedean oscillator identification](THETA_INTERIOR_DISCRETE_SERIES.md) · [Ichino, Lemma 8.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2008.06174#page=35)

By Ichino's Proposition 7.1 the four-vector pairing of these two matrix coefficients is nonzero. Remark 7.2 gives nonzero inputs with both oscillator vectors polynomial and both discrete-series vectors compact-finite. Polarize separately in the oscillator pair and in the discrete-series pair. If every value at $(P,P,v,v)$ vanished, the two complex polarization identities would force all four-vector values to vanish. Choose a successful $P_\tau,v_\tau$ at each real place. On the prescribed global vacuum, the real vacuum space is a line at each place, tensored with a multiplicity space containing the fixed finite vector. Creation operators at infinity and the real group act on the real factors. Consequently the product polynomial $P=\prod_\tau P_\tau$ has global real coefficient equal to the product of its local coefficients, for normalized local vacua, times $I_0=\langle f,f\rangle>0$. This also follows by dividing each one-place coefficient computed on $f$ by $I_0$ and multiplying their product by $I_0$. The polynomial leaves the prescribed finite coset intact. A real Haar measure is a positive scalar multiple of product Haar measure. Absolute integration place by place therefore gives a nonzero product for every Haar measure. [Ichino, Proposition 7.1 and Remark 7.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2008.06174#page=25) · [Global vacuum and coefficient normalization][rz-product]

For the weight, at one real place set $G=U(p,q)$ with $p+q=n$. For any allowed majorant, in a majorant-orthonormal frame the Cartan coordinates $x_1\ge\cdots\ge x_\ell\ge 0$, $\ell=\min(p,q)$, give $H(g)=\prod_j\cosh(x_j)^{-2}$. Indeed the eigenvalues of $gg^*$ off the unit eigenvalues occur as $e^{2x_j},e^{-2x_j}$, and the inverse of $\det((1+gg^*)/2)$ is the doubled height. The oscillator coefficient of a polynomial is bounded by $C H^{m/2}$. To see the exponent, the doubled section formed from the two polynomial vectors transforms under the Siegel Levi by the character of absolute value $|\det|_{\mathbb C}^{m/2}$ at the Siegel–Weil point and is bounded on the compact factor in Iwasawa decomposition. Evaluating that section at $(g,1)$ gives the oscillator coefficient. [Doubled determinant and height][rz-height] · [Polynomial bound and its exponent][rz-bound]

Let $b(g)$ be the selected smaller discrete-series coefficient. It is square integrable since $G$ has compact center. The absolute value of the weighted integrand at $s_1=0$ is bounded by $C H^{n/2}|b(g)|$. In the positive chamber the Haar Jacobian is bounded by $C'\exp(2\sum_j(n-2j+1)x_j)$, using the multiplicities $2$ for $e_i\pm e_j$, $2|p-q|$ for $e_i$, and $1$ for $2e_i$. Since $H^n\ll\exp(-2n\sum_jx_j)$, their product is bounded by $C''\exp(-2\sum_jx_j)$, which is integrable. This includes the compact case by the empty-product convention. Cauchy–Schwarz now gives the weighted absolute integral, and $0<H\le1$ gives the unweighted one as well. The same argument at every place and Tonelli's theorem give the required adelic real estimate. This proof requires only the existential real polynomial and vector asked for by `RealZeta`. The stronger source assertion specifying a particular joint highest polynomial, a separate $L^1$ bound for its discrete-series coefficient, and a formal-degree evaluation in a compact case has additional content. The independent quantitative branching and derivative estimates for the bottom Poincaré series also have separate hypotheses. [Source comparison][rz-source]

## From a detected core period to a catalogue entry

A nonzero core value in that span pairs nontrivially with $t(w)$ on at least one reachable chain, by linearity of the absolutely convergent core pairing. To ascend that chain the source uses a bottom seesaw for the orthogonal one-line extension, then alternates Fourier–Jacobi and Bessel periods. Here is the argument from the mixed-model identities. If all next-period tests vanish, unfolding in the isotropic coordinates implies vanishing of the previous functional on the partial Fourier transforms of every admissible next test. These transformed tests include a nonzero lattice vacuum, together with its translates by the permitted Heisenberg operators. Their span is dense in the prescribed fixed-lattice space, and the period is continuous there by rapid decay. In the Fourier–Jacobi-to-Bessel direction an allowed product test evaluates to the preceding nonzero Fourier–Jacobi period. In the reverse direction a product evaluates to the preceding Bessel period times the self-pairing of a nonzero auxiliary Schwartz vector. This pairing is positive for the selected Haar measure. Either evaluation contradicts total vanishing. Polynomial density on a fixed finite type then allows a finite coset and a Fock polynomial for the new arrow while keeping all previously chosen coordinates. Starting with the bottom seesaw and finishing at even index $m$ yields a nonzero top Bessel period for this very row, possibly with a row-dependent real pair. The marked kernel property and the spanning catalogue in each datum fibre transfer this to injectivity for the original pair. [Two product calculations and vacuum argument][upb] · [Reverse direction][upj] · [Even-length ascent][asc] · [Split Fourier pairing and its support conditions](REMARK_SEESAW.md) · [Marking and fibre span][marked]

**Separation on the given choice.** On the large-scale Hida range the [uniform branching calculation](ROW_CORE_BRANCHING.md) gives the relative coefficient and derivative bounds for the core Poincaré series, with $Q=10872[F:\mathbf Q]\,(r_D+1)^4\log b$. Use the core adjunction above on the same finite level. The core function that pairs nontrivially belongs to the span of reachable functions. Every such function is bounded on restriction by rapid decay, and the bottom cusp form is integrable. If every reachable function paired to zero, linearity of the integral on this span would contradict the chosen core period. One reachable chain therefore has nonzero initial period. On its standard depth frame the [alternating period argument](ROW_PERIOD_ASCENT.md) gives an allowed top column, a finite coset at each arrow and a real pair detecting this particular bottom row. Fibrewise spanning replaces the column by a catalogue entry. On any marked choice this proves injectivity: a row killed by the marked catalogue would be killed at every substituted pair, in particular at its own detecting pair. For constructing the mark itself the [common-real-test theorem](ROW_COMMON_REAL_TEST.md) applies before proving detection. These conclusions use the stated real and cuspidal models, the core doubling and unramified transfer inputs to adjunction, and the mixed Weil model on the specified lattices. Arithmetic values on the joint tuple images require the [matched period identity](ROW_PERIOD_JET_FORMULA.md). [Formal assembly on the original row][asmb] · [Real estimates in the source][realproof]
