# The mixed grid ratio and cancellation along a tower

On the third column from the right, both theta sources are indefinite. Fix a row $i$ and write $g_t$ for its nonzero square constant at twist $t$. Let $b_t$ be the constant on the standard arithmetic bottom selected for this square, with its period sums. Under the local theta-matching, definite doubling and arithmetic inputs of the reconstruction, the normalized constants satisfy
$$
 \frac{g_t/g_0}{b_t/b_0}\in\mu(K),\qquad \frac{g_t}{g_0}\in K^\times .
$$
Here $0$ is the reference twist, $K$ is the prescribed coefficient field in its chosen complex embedding, and $\mu(K)$ is its group of roots of unity. We first put the two removals on compatible rational spaces and show that their function identities reach the grid endpoints.

## One fitted base and compatible planes for the two removals

Write $a,n,d=a+n$ for the upper ranks and $e=(a-n)/2$. Fix an actual grid stage at $(p,q)=(1,1)$. On the arithmetic Hermitian spaces $A,A',W$ the negative indices are $1,1,2$. Remove a hyperbolic plane from $A'$ and $W$. This gives the intermediate triple $A,B',W_1$, of ranks $a,n-2,d-2$ and negative indices $1,0,1$. Remove a hyperbolic plane from $A$ and from this same $W_1$. The resulting $B,B',W_2$ have ranks $a-2,n-2,d-4$ and are definite. In particular the two target planes are orthogonal rational planes in the original $W$.

All four splittings take place on the actual self-dual lattices. For an indefinite space of rank $r\ge3$ with $h+1$ negative directions and $r\ge h+2$, the isotropic-vector and integral dual-partner argument gives a rational pair, an isometric inclusion of its orthogonal complement, and a direct sum of its two fractional line lattices and the self-dual complement lattice. Here $n>5$ and $e>5$, more than is required in the four applications. Choose the four decompositions before varying the tuple. The intermediate arithmetic family can retain the big-source rig and the original idempotents and conductor data. On the resulting bottom lattices the usual unitary Weil construction gives a stage family with its own vacua and assigned tests. Its small-source rig need only have the prescribed lattice. Analytic markings of the unchanged spaces can subsequently be chosen equal. [Integral splitting proof](ROW_BETA_RATIONAL_PLANES.md#removing-the-two-planes-over-the-ground-field) · [Integral complement and range][mg-split] · [Intermediate family][mg-family] · [Bottom family on prescribed lattices][mg-bottom]

Fix the pair $t,0$, where $0$ denotes the reference tuple. Use the fitted base $B_*$ for the upper stage. Its construction fixes the archimedean embeddings across tuples, takes the grid's additive character, takes $P=T_t\supseteq T_0$, chooses the split branch at each $v\in P$ from the grid's marked primes, and fixes an auxiliary completely split $v_1$ outside $P$. Both tuples are admissible on this base. The restriction of each splitting character to the ground-field ideles is a power of a quadratic character. Reducing either rank by two preserves the required restriction, and the lower bound on $m_\alpha-m_\beta$ becomes weaker. All other fit data agree. Thus the *same* $B_*$ fits $(a,n)$, $(a,n-2)$, and $(a-2,n-2)$, in that order. A different pair may require different marked places. [Fit and both rank changes][mg-fit] · [Construction and reference inclusion][mg-choose]

For analytic coordinates, on the sources the change conjugates entries, with form multiplier $+1$ for the big and $-1$ for the small source. The target uses the imaginary scalar $\xi_0$ and the fixed fractional lattice ideal of the host. That ideal scales both a lattice and its complement, preserving the integral splitting. If the coordinate-change multiplier is $c$, multiply the coordinate image of the second plane generator by $\bar c^{-1}$ to recover a unit pairing. [Signed coordinates and target lattice][mg-coords]

Choose the analytic Cartan involutions and marked bases **from the bottom upward**. Start with valid bottom markings. At the intermediate level copy the bottom small-source marking, and extend the bottom big-source and target markings across the alpha planes. At the upper level copy the intermediate big-source marking, and extend the intermediate small-source and target markings across the beta planes. On each copied space, the rational form and lattice agree in the coordinate realizations. At a marked place, extend the given lower split basis by two primitive plane columns, inserting all lower columns in their existing order. A rational rescaling $x\mapsto ux,\ y\mapsto\bar u^{-1}y$ makes both columns primitive at all marked branches, since the conditions prescribe valuations of $u$ at disjoint pairs of conjugate primes. At infinity extend the given lower Cartan involution by the hyperbolic interchange for the signed form. This extension accepts any two prescribed ordered positions. ## From the mixed comparison to the grid endpoints

Use the intermediate Weil realizations and quotient maps in both comparisons, with one real pair shared by $t,0$. The [mixed beta comparison](ROW_FIRST_MIXED_SQUARE.md) gives an identity on the upper polynomial model and the [first alpha comparison](ROW_FIRST_ALPHA_STEP.md) gives the identity on the intermediate model. The [stage tests](ROW_STAGE_STANDARD_TESTS.md) realize their scalars on the assigned finite vectors. The change to the grid's Darboux readings is invertible. At finite places the vacuum fixes the coordinate-change scalar, and the quotient of the small and big zeta multipliers is the splitting character on the three frame determinants. At infinity the coordinate substitutions are invertible, carry the primed supports and common real relation, and introduce a comparison scalar shared by $t,0$. The source Haar measures on the model are held fixed in this comparison. [Real and finite changes of presentation](ROW_FIRST_BETA_GRID_RATIOS.md#from-the-fixed-model-to-the-prescribed-grid)

The big integral converges on every grid input by the [mixed bound](ROW_MIXED_INTEGRABILITY.md). For a given finite flip of the small test, take a representative of the same quotient class with full row rank at the designated split place. The difference is a finite sum of source-translation differences. Choose a fresh split place at which all these vectors are spherical and that lies outside the supports of all the translations. The same regularizer there, of nonzero degree, makes all integrals absolutely convergent and commutes with the translations. Each translated pair then has equal integrals by source invariance. Dividing the regularized identity by that degree identifies the fresh-place regularized value with the ordinary integral of the full-rank representative. Applying the regularizer identity to every allowed convergent $z$ gives $d(z)I_b=g_t I_s(z)$, including $d(z)=0$. The finite-flip and Haar factors are the ones computed on the preceding column. [Regularizer identity on convergent tests](ROW_SQUARE_REGULARIZATION.md) · [Transfer of the model identity](ROW_FIRST_BETA_STEP.md#from-the-first-beta-model-to-the-grid-square)

The mixed beta coefficient detects a polynomial with nonzero big integral on the full target. Local quotient descent lets us fix the unmarked vacua and any reachable nonzero flip classes at the other marked places, keeping one split place free. The two endpoint groups realize every target determinant on a nondegenerate line. Strong approximation on the special unitary group at the free place then gives a nonzero value at a block endpoint after varying that one local class. Local flip reachability at this last place absorbs the remaining block translate into the endpoint. Surjectivity of the common real relation supplies the small partner. A fresh regularizer of nonzero degree at this endpoint detects the small period as well. This proves the nonzero witness on the specified grid. Evaluating on a fixed nonzero stage image identifies the two real normalizations up to a single scalar shared by the twists. Thus the top-stage calibration applies to this particular square on the function identity just proved. ## Arithmetic cancellation on the third column from the right

Fix an original square $(i,j)$ with $m\ge3$, $j=m-3$. Write $a=\operatorname{rank}V_{i+1,j+1}$, $n=\operatorname{rank}V_{i,j}$ and $e=(a-n)/2$. Its two signature defects are $p=\lfloor(m-j-1)/2\rfloor=1$, $q=\lfloor(m-j)/2\rfloor=1$. The beta step removes a plane from the small source and target, giving $(a,n-2;1,0)$. The alpha step removes a plane from the big source and smaller target, giving $(a-2,n-2;0,0)$. The alpha recurrence requires $q=0$ on both of its stages, which explains this order. The square indices $(i,j)$ stay fixed. In particular the two steps use exactly the same characters $\alpha_u,\beta_u$, their quotient $\nu_u$ and the set $S_0$ at tuple $u$. The half difference of the source ranks is $e$ at the first removal and $e+1$ at the second. [Grid signatures][mix-sig] · [Stage characters and ranks][mix-stage] · [Two oriented recurrences][mix-rec]

Write $\widehat f=f_t/f_0$, where $0=\mu_0$. For the comparison with a given $t$, choose one fitting base per removal for $\{t,0\}$. Let $\mathfrak b_\beta,\mathfrak c_\beta,\mathfrak b_\alpha,\mathfrak c_\alpha$ denote the four adapted plane ideals. Both recurrences use the finite function $L_u^{S_0}(e+1,\nu_u)$. This Euler product is absolutely convergent and nonzero because $e+1>1$. In the beta recurrence it divides the lower model constant. In the alpha recurrence it multiplies the bottom constant. Consequently these values cancel for each $u$, regardless of differences in stage markings. The same conclusion follows with the actual primitive finite functions, since the places over $S_0$ are already ramified. No separate arithmetic hypothesis on this critical ratio enters the cancellation. [Characters attached to the original square][mix-fit] · [Finite Euler-product convention](ROW_FIRST_BETA_STEP.md#scalar-for-this-step)

There is also exact cancellation of the marked Gauss factors even if the two fitting bases differ. Indeed $B.\psi=W.\psi$ and $T_t\subset B.P$ for each such base. Over every $v\in T_t$, the prime $w$ selected by its split embedding lies in $\operatorname{locPl}(G,t)$. Projection of this set to the places of $F$ is injective, so both bases select the same $w$ for the given $v$. The definition of $\mathrm{chrW}$ uses only this prime and its canonical completion map. The prescribed uniformizer depends only on $v$. Thus the conductor, the residue representatives used in the Gauss sum and the additive character in that sum all agree. The filter restricts the product exactly to $T_u$ for $u=t,0$, since $T_0=S_0\subset T_t$. Extra places in a fitting base contribute nothing. The fixed local sign on both standard vectors is $+1$, and the two epsilon arguments are $-e$ and $1-(e+1)=-e$. Write $G_u$ for this common nonzero product. With the actual ideal signs the two finite factors are
$$
 D_u^\beta=\nu_u(\mathfrak b_\beta\mathfrak c_\beta)^{-1}G_u,
 \qquad D_u^\alpha=\nu_u(\mathfrak b_\alpha\mathfrak c_\alpha)G_u .
$$
Their quotient is $D_u^\alpha/D_u^\beta=\nu_u(\mathfrak A)$, with $\mathfrak A=\mathfrak b_\alpha\mathfrak c_\alpha\mathfrak b_\beta\mathfrak c_\beta$. Hence its normalized quotient equals $\xi_t(\mathfrak A)$, where $\xi_t=\nu_t/\nu_0$ and the ideal value is evaluated by the fixed finite uniformizer ideles. This is a root of unity in the given coefficient field $K$. Both ideal signs become positive upon composing the recurrences. Norm powers of the individual ideals and archimedean comparison constants are fixed on each pair $\{t,0\}$ and drop out upon normalization. [Fitting conditions][mix-fit] · [Unique marked prime over each place][mix-inj] · [Local character and epsilon normalization][mix-eps] · [Sets at the reference tuple][mix-ref] · [Ideal signs][mix-ideals] · [Beta sign][mix-bsign] · [Alpha sign][mix-asign] · [Root of unity for ideal ratios][mix-idealroot]

To track a possible change of presentation on the middle stage, number the original, middle and bottom stages $2,1,0$ and call their standard factors $\ell_{r,u}$. Denote by $M_{r,u}^\delta$ the model constant at stage $r$ in the adapted presentation for removal $\delta$. There are four changes of marking. With fixed model Haar measures each gives
$$
 \widehat M_r^\delta=\xi_t(k_{\delta,r})\widehat\ell_r,
 \qquad (\delta,r)=(\beta,2),(\beta,1),(\alpha,1),(\alpha,0).
$$
Here each $k_{\delta,r}$ records the comparison with that stage's *own* standard reading. This follows by evaluating on the standard finite pair and a nonzero common real image. Changing the three split bases multiplies the finite comparison by the quotient character on the product of their determinants. Any extra scalar from the real intertwiner or Haar measures is common to $t,0$. In particular the two readings of the middle stage satisfy $\widehat M_1^\beta/\widehat M_1^\alpha=\xi_t(k_{\beta,1}/k_{\alpha,1})$. Equal adapted middle markings are unnecessary. For each removal the two markings being compared share the fixed archimedean embeddings. The fitting bases can differ between removals, and the construction above permits one common base for both. [Beta comparison with two standard stages][mix-adaptb] · [Two alpha markings and ratios][mix-adapta] · [Full normalized grid transport](ROW_FIRST_BETA_GRID_RATIOS.md#from-the-fixed-model-to-the-prescribed-grid)

Let $g_u$ denote the actual nonzero regularized grid constant. Its finite flip and fixed-Haar calibration give $\widehat g=\xi_t(y/x^{(t)})\widehat\ell_2$ as on the preceding column. Here $y$ is a determinant-root idele for the common finite target translation. The idele $x^{(t)}$ records the units in the standard zeta comparison on the marked branches for $t$ and has component one elsewhere. The two standard bottom ranks $\mathrm{botBig},\mathrm{botSmall}$ for the original square are precisely $a-2,n-2$. Choose the standard bottom $X$ with the common-period monomial sums supplied by $\mathrm{BottomIn}(i,j)$, using existence of a standard triangle first. Write $b_u$ for the constants witnessed there. Comparison with the actual stage-zero triangle gives $\widehat b=\zeta_t\widehat\ell_0$, $\zeta_t\in\mu(K)$, by the lattice and frame transport and the injectivity on the definite finite tests. Standardness of the triangle obtained by splitting is unnecessary. [Bottom ranks][mix-br] · [Existence of a standard candidate][mix-bex] · [Scope of the arithmetic bottom][mix-bscope] · [Transport between definite bottom triangles](ROW_BETA_RATIONAL_PLANES.md#the-descended-bottom-and-the-specified-standard-bottom)

Multiplying the beta and alpha recurrences and the markings gives
$$
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 \frac{g_t}{g_0}=
 \zeta_t^{-1}\,\xi_t\!\left(
 \frac{y\,k_{\beta,1}\,k_{\alpha,0}}
 {x^{(t)}k_{\beta,2}\,k_{\alpha,1}}\right)
 \,\xi_t(\mathfrak A)\,\frac{b_t}{b_0}.}
$$
The factor preceding $b_t/b_0$ lies in $\mu(K)$, and the bottom linear system gives $b_t/b_0\in K^\times$. Thus the normalized mixed-square constant and the normalized arithmetic bottom constant agree up to a root of unity in this embedding. The formula compares each $t$ with the reference on the same original square, with the reference in each removal evaluated on the same fitted data as $t$. ## Conditional cancellation throughout an original square tower

The same calculation gives the following implication for any original column. It requires the nonzero analytic comparisons, adapted markings, standard finite tests and transport at every intervening stage. The [tower induction](ROW_TOWER_INDUCTION.md) now establishes these premises on the prescribed grid under the standing inputs. Put $k=m-j$, $p=\lfloor(k-1)/2\rfloor$, $q=\lfloor k/2\rfloor$. First make the $q$ beta removals, then the $p$ alpha removals, since the alpha recurrence in the pin requires small defect zero. At successive beta removals the half-rank difference is $e,e+1,\ldots,e+q-1$, because each one reduces the small rank by two. The denominator Hecke arguments are consequently $z=e+1,\ldots,e+q$. At the alpha removals the half-rank differences are $e+q,e+q-1,\ldots,e+q-p+1$, because each reduces the big rank by two. Each is its numerator Hecke argument. Every factor at a given $z$ uses $\nu_u,S_0$ of the original square. In both directions its epsilon argument is $1-z$, and the sign is $+1$. The unique marked branches and fixed uniformizers just proved identify the ramified products $G_u(1-z)$ across independent fitted bases. All matched arguments therefore cancel both their Hecke and their Gauss products exactly at each tuple. The terminal ranks are $a-2p,n-2q$, precisely the arithmetic bottom ranks of this square.

After normalization the plane ideals from every removal occur with positive signs and contribute the finite twist on their product. The quotients of the intermediate marking factors contribute further values of the same finite twist, as the mixed-square formula illustrates. The final bottom transport contributes its normalized coefficient-field root of unity. Thus, if $b_u$ is taken on the selected standard arithmetic bottom for the original $(i,j)$, one obtains for some $\varepsilon_t\in\mu(K)$
$$
 \frac{g_t/g_0}{b_t/b_0} =
 \begin{cases}
 \varepsilon_t, & k\ \mathrm{odd},\\[3pt]
 \displaystyle \varepsilon_t\,
 \frac{G_0(-e)}{G_t(-e)}\,
 \frac{L^{S_0}_0(e+1)}{L^{S_0}_t(e+1)}, & k\ \mathrm{even}.
 \end{cases}
$$
Indeed $q-p=0$ in the first case. In the second it is one and the only unmatched beta denominator is the first, with $z=e+1$. The [finite step calculation](ROW_SQUARE_RATIOS.md) puts the surviving Gauss quotient in the prescribed field, and the [critical Hecke ratio](../galois/ROW_CRITICAL_HECKE_RATIOS.md) belongs to it under the advance field choice. The normalized grid constant therefore lies in its multiplicative group in either case.
