3. One bound for every cohomological degree

SHV1–6 proves the complete original large-degree exactness theorem.
The elementary Laurent-monomial calculation from FCD15 is written with
the missing positive-degree consequence explicit. For each exponent
vector, its negative coordinates specify which localized vertices can
contain it. If some coordinate is nonnegative, deleting that index with
the original alternating sign is a contracting homotopy. Only vectors
whose coordinates are all negative can survive, and none has positive
total degree. This calculation is valid over arbitrary coefficient
rings, including zero divisors and the zero ring.

A finite free module with basis degrees d_l is therefore exact in all
cohomological degrees above the single source bound 1 + sum |d_l|.
For a finite graded module over Noetherian R, choose nu+1 successive
graded free surjections, where nu is the number of variables. Their
kernels are finite by Hilbert's basis theorem. The finite list and the
maximum of its free-module bounds are fixed before t and before the
cohomological index. An explicit cycle-lifting argument propagates
exactness downward from a degree above nu, where every localization
complex vanishes. The last kernel need not be projective or zero;
finite global dimension is not assumed.

SHV9 transports this exactness through the original nonempty-vertex
cube isomorphisms. It proves positive-degree exactness of the actual
section complex on the prescribed affine cover. Augmented degree two
is ordinary Cech degree one. This chosen-cover result is then upgraded,
rather than being renamed as the full all-cover predicate.

5. Why the polynomial conjunct follows from the same work

SHV18 proves the full original mem_span_of_saturating_of_le. Its
ambient graded module need not be finite. A finite homogeneous span
which saturates chartwise has the same ordinary localization complex;
large-degree augmented exactness for that finite span gives a global
homogeneous representative. The original no-common-chart-power-torsion
hypothesis makes its difference from the given element zero.

Actual graded sections satisfy that torsion hypothesis: restrict to
D(s_i), cancel only the unit coordinate of s_i there, and use the
original cover. SHV20 proves the generic cyclic dehomogenization
saturation lemma, even for an arbitrary polynomial variable set and
arbitrary submodule containing the degree-zero generator. It homogenizes
a polynomial lift of the chart coefficient, then uses the homogeneous
power-kernel property. No extra surjectivity assumption on epsilon is
introduced.

For SHV21 the finite span is the cyclic module generated by the actual
constant section 1 in the trivial-bundle graded section module. Its
dehomogenization is the constant 1 on every original chart, so all
chart sections are its coefficient multiples. Saturation and the
high-degree argument apply. Projection to degree t gives a homogeneous
