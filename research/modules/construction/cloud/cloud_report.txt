0. WHAT IS ACTUALLY PROVED HERE

The source's CloudStep is not merely a statement about a large family. Its
quantifiers demand a bounded integral dual test for EVERY coefficient array
having a unit coordinate, and the test must vanish to a prescribed order at
every other incoming edge. Testing each displayed vector separately would not
be enough. This note explains how the source meets that stronger condition.

We reconstruct the complementary family, its exact count and Weyl comparison,
its preservation of primitivity modulo w, the contraction/detection argument,
the logarithmic cost arithmetic, the passage from a structured flat test to a
full dual test, and the explicit source-compatible constant 54. We also give
a different short proof of the characteristic-zero operator-word bound.

The remaining inputs are real representation/structured-polynomial results:
the integral standard-monomial basis, the Weyl/dual-Weyl sandwich with its
explicit exponent, and production of a constrained signed-chain test. Their
locations and exact interfaces are stated in Section 9. Their names are not
counted as human proofs. In particular this note does not prove the entire
arithmetic window theorem or RH/GRH on its own.

2. THE FAMILY, COUNT, AND PRIMITIVITY [CL1–CL3]

2.1. The exact complement.

For each effective remainder weight subtract its last entry to obtain a
partition nu. The integral standard-monomial input supplies polynomials b_e,
monic for column-lexicographic order, whose leading exponent arrays e(a,t)
are nonnegative integers with column sums nu_t. Their interlacing says

  sum_{b<=a} e(b,j) <= sum_{b<a} e(b,l),  l<j.

Array indices in this section are 0,...,k-1. At a selected even twist retain
e(0,0)=nu_0. The column sum then gives e(a,0)=0 for a>0, while interlacing
gives e(0,t)=0 for t>0. Deleting the first row and column gives the
interlaced (k-1)-square array of shape (nu_1,...,nu_{k-1}). Conversely insert
nu_0 in the top-left corner and zeros elsewhere in that row and column.
Interlacing across the inserted column follows from nu_t<=nu_0.

At a selected odd twist retain e(0,t)=0 for every t. The normalized odd
partition has last entry zero, so its last column is zero. Delete its first
row and last column, and invert by reinserting those zero row/column. This
gives shape (nu_0,...,nu_{k-2}). Because the effective weight was the negative
reverse, this shape is lambda^0_2 minus the reverse of the original tail.
Adding a constant to all weights and taking dual reversal leave Weyl
dimension unchanged. Thus both parity cases have count

  D_{k-1}(lambda^0_2,...,lambda^0_k).

At unselected twists no array restriction is imposed. Taking independent
products in the disjoint blocks gives the exact n2 in (CL.1). The equality
between the number of interlaced arrays and Weyl's product is part of the
standard tableau/Gelfand–Tsetlin counting input, not proved anew here.

2.2. The coordinate transport is compatible with multiplication.

For an edge basis M_s the model identifies R_e(lambda) with the coefficient
lattice by applying

  C_lambda(v)=w^{A_lambda} (prod_s det(M_s)^{lambda_{s,k}})
               blockAct(M_s)v.

This is an isomorphism onto the integral tensor polynomial lattice; its
inverse constructs h_i from the selected products b_i. It is valid for
signed determinant powers. Weight additivity gives

  C_{lambda+mu}(uv)=C_lambda(u) C_mu(v).

The flat-coordinate identity is
C_flat(Theta F)=Theta_0(subst F), where Theta_0 replaces the flat variables
by a first-column entry (even twist) or signed maximal minor (odd twist).
These are precisely tr_theta and transStep in the source. The identity is
a matrix substitution identity, with the powers of w and determinants
cancelled by A_{lambda+mu}=A_lambda+A_mu. The EL polynomial lattice model
justifies the assertion that C_lambda(v) is integral when v lies in R_e.

2.3. Why the product map is injective modulo w.

At an even selected twist the flat generators are X_{c,0}. Thus the first
column of the leading exponent of X^alpha b_e is alpha plus the fixed
column (nu_0,0,...,0). It recovers alpha, and then e.

At an odd selected twist the flat generator N_c is, up to sign, the maximal
minor in the first k-1 columns with row c omitted. Its leading monomial has
a 1 at (t,t) precisely when t<c, for t<k-1. The zero first row of e and
interlacing imply inductively e(a,t)=0 for a<=t. Therefore the diagonal of
the product's leading exponent recovers the tails sum_{c>t} alpha_c. These
tails and the fixed total sum_c alpha_c=d_s recover every alpha_c; subtract
the flat exponent to recover e. At an unselected twist d_s=0, so there is
nothing to recover. All leading coefficients are units, including the
minor signs, over every residue characteristic.

Distinct leading monomials prove independence over the residue field: a
nontrivial finite relation has a greatest leading monomial among its
nonzero terms, which cannot cancel. Independent blocks tensor together.
Equivalently, for multihomogeneous Q_i of degree d,

  sum_i Theta_0(Q_i)b_i=0  implies every Q_i=0.

Now suppose span_O(B_b) is a direct summand of the symbol coefficient ring,
the B_b are independent, and subst(F_b)=B_b P after renaming variables,
where P is integral with nonzero reduction. If U=sum c_{b,i}Theta(F_b)h_i
were in wR_e, transport and reduce to get

  sum_i Theta_0(Pbar sum_b cbar_{b,i} Bbar_b) bbar_i=0.

Injectivity makes each parenthesized polynomial zero. Since the residue
polynomial ring is a domain and Pbar!=0, sum_b c_{b,i}B_b lies in w times
the ambient coefficient ring for every i. Saturation gives
span(B) intersect w Ambient = w span(B). Independence of the B_b then
forces every c_{b,i} in wO. This contradicts a unit coordinate. This proof
works for EVERY primitive array, not just basis vectors. It also explains
why merely independent symbols over K would be insufficient.

2.4. The dimension inequality.

Shift the selected tail to a dominant (k-1)-tuple mu in [0,c0]. If d>=a,
Weyl's product factors as

  D_k(d+mu_0,mu)=D_{k-1}(mu)
                prod_{j=1}^{k-1}(d+mu_0-mu_{j-1}+j)/j.

Each factor is at most (1+c0/a)(d+j)/j. For x=c0/a in [0,1/k],
(1-kx)(1+x)^{k-1}<=1: on [0,1/k) the logarithmic derivative is
-k/(1-kx)+(k-1)/(1+x)<0. This gives (CL.2), and then (CL.3) from the
separate symbol-size bound. If 1-C_rNr^2k/Y<0, the final count is already
automatic; multiplying a lower bound by that negative factor would be
invalid. The proof separates those two cases.

3. A SHORT PROOF OF THE OPERATOR-WORD BOUND [CL4]

This is a derived simplification. It proves the source's OpBound through
characteristic-zero highest weights rather than reproducing its quadric
vanishing implementation. It invokes the standard complete-reducibility
theorem for finite-dimensional rational representations of GL_k in
characteristic zero. It does NOT assert complete reducibility over O or
over the residue field. All coefficient bounds below are field statements.

Let H_d be the homogeneous degree-d polynomials in k>=2 variables over a
characteristic-zero field F, and let E_ab=X_a partial_b act on H_d. Put
U_c=span_F{products of at most c operators E_ab}. Claim: if a subspace
W of End(H_d) is closed under commutators with every E_ab and is spanned
by simultaneous diagonal-weight vectors whose every weight coordinate
is <=c, then W is contained in U_c.

It suffices to prove this after algebraic closure; membership in a finite
dimensional linear span descends by solving linear equations over F.
The stated diagonal-weight spanning makes W stable under the diagonal
torus. Commutator stability makes it stable under the exponentials of
the nilpotent off-diagonal E_ab. These elementary unipotents and the
torus generate GL_k, so W is a rational GL_k-submodule to which complete
reducibility applies.
Identify End(H_d), as a GL_k-module, with the bidegree-(d,d) polynomials
in X and Y, with infinitesimal action

  E_ab acts as X_a partial_{X_b} - Y_b partial_{Y_a}.

This is the natural Sym^d(F^k) tensor Sym^d((F^k)^*) identification. Its
highest vectors are exactly

  X_1^j Y_k^j Q^{d-j},  0<=j<=d,  Q=sum_i X_iY_i.

Here is a direct proof, including multiplicities. A highest vector is
annihilated by E_ab for a<b. Exponentiating these locally nilpotent
derivations says it is invariant under
X_b -> X_b+tX_a, Y_a -> Y_a-tY_b. Invert X_1Y_k. For b>1 choose
t=-X_b/X_1 in the (1,b) transformation, killing every X_b and changing
only Y_1. The remaining X is (X_1,0,...,0). For 1<a<k choose the (a,k)
transformation to kill Y_a; this does not disturb X, since X_a=0. The
remaining Y is (Q/X_1,0,...,0,Y_k). A bihomogeneous polynomial evaluated
there is a linear combination of
X_1^d(Q/X_1)^bY_k^{d-b}=Q^b X_1^{d-b}Y_k^{d-b}, 0<=b<=d.
These are polynomials before localization, whose injection into the
localized ring proves the same identity globally. Conversely each is
annihilated by all upper E_ab. Their diagonal weights j(e_1-e_k) are
distinct, so every highest-weight space has dimension exactly one.

Complete reducibility now says End(H_d) is the multiplicity-free direct
sum of irreducible modules of highest weights j(e_1-e_k), 0<=j<=d.
The space U_c is adjoint-stable: commuting E_ab past a product replaces
one factor by a linear combination of E_ij and does not increase length.
For j<=d the operator E_{1k}^j=X_1^j partial_k^j is nonzero, has highest
weight j(e_1-e_k), and lies in U_c when j<=c. Thus U_c contains all
irreducible summands with j<=c. Every summand in W has highest weight
coordinate j<=c by the assumed coordinate-weight bound. Hence W<=U_c.
The case k=1 is immediate: End(H_d) consists of scalars and the empty
word is the identity. The case d=0 is also covered by j=0.

Application to Fock contractions.

At one twist, define A_{beta,s} on H_d by

  <Theta(A_{beta,s}P),Theta Q>_F
       = <Theta P . s,Theta Q . beta>_F.

The restricted flat Fock form is nondegenerate, so this operator exists
uniquely. For a standard rational basis this nondegeneracy also follows
by positivity of the real coefficient Fock form; the determinant is a
nonzero rational number and stays nonzero over every characteristic-zero
field. The explicit Theta image is the first-row or first-column-minor
flat representation, as in theta_Hd.

Let R_ab=sum_t X_{a,t} partial_{b,t} be the row derivation. Fock adjointness
is <R_ab P,Q>=<P,R_ba Q>, checked on monomials. The row rule under Theta
is E_ab in the even case and d delta_ab-E_ba in the odd case. For the
odd case this follows by differentiating cofactors, or from
adj(gX)=adj(X)adj(g) on invertible matrices and then polynomial identity.
The product rule and adjointness give

  [T_ab,A_{beta,s}]=A_{beta,R_ab s}-A_{R_ba beta,s},

where T_ab is the appropriate row rule. Therefore the span of these
contractions is adjoint-stable under the E_ab in either parity.

Every bideterminant of shape nu has exactly nu_0 minor factors, and each
minor uses a given row at most once. It is a row-weight vector with all
coordinates between 0 and nu_0. Such bideterminants span the remainder
module. Thus A_{beta,s}, for row-weight vectors beta and s, has coordinate
weights mu_s-mu_beta in the even case and the reverse in the odd case.
Every coordinate is <=nu_0<=c0. The operator-word lemma gives length <=c0.
Expand tensor products of beta and s: Fock factorizes across disjoint
twists, so each term uses <=c0 letters at EACH selected twist, and none
at unselected twists. A linear change of the flat variables preserves
this filtration, because it conjugates each X_i partial_j to a linear
combination of the same operators. Scalars introduced by the field and
Fock normalizations are harmless for the K-linear operator span.

The short proof uses integral standard-monomial spanning only to obtain
the finite row-weight bound; all subsequent operator coefficients may
have denominators. The later primitive normalization is legitimate
because SgOp is a K-linear subspace. No integral coefficient conclusion
has been silently extracted from complete reducibility.

4. CARTAN MULTIPLICATION AND THE INTEGRAL SANDWICH [CL5–CL6]

Cartan multiplication spans the integral dual-Weyl module of weight
lambda+mu by products from weights lambda and mu. Given standard-monomial
spanning by products of initial-column minors, this is elementary: a
shape lambda+mu has the sum of the prescribed numbers of minors of each
height. Partition the factors of each height into the lambda and mu
groups. Each group is an allowed bideterminant of its shape. Tensoring
over twists preserves spanning. Determinant twists and the w^{-A}
normalization multiply because the weights add. This proves the precise
Cartan-onto property used in RhoAtGOfPair; it is stronger than surjectivity
only after tensoring with K.

The remaining integral representation fact is the following one-position
sandwich, with normalized Fock duality:

  p^{binom(k,2) floor(log_p(h+k-1))} nabla_nu
       <= Delta_nu <= nabla_nu,  h=nu_0,
  Delta_nu and nabla_nu are mutually dual for normalized Fock pairing.

The zero-spread case has exponent zero. Equivariance transports these
lattices to arbitrary integral bases and opposite edges; tensoring sums
the exponents. Consequently Psi=the inverse normalized-Fock map satisfies
p^kappa Rbar <= Psi(R') <= Rbar, both at the edge and at vertices. This
is the content of FormCoreSKXOf.formCoreGX_of and JetMatrixAlgebra. It is
an integral assertion and is retained independently of Section 3.

Work first on T alone. For i=0 (the selected full weight) and i=f (the
flat weight), let kappa_i be the sum of these sandwich exponents. The
source bounds each spread h by h+k<=a^2. Hence

  floor(log_p(h+k-1)) <= 2 log_p a,
  binom(k,2) <= k^2,
  kappa_0+kappa_f <= 4 N k^2 log_p a = 4L.

These bounds are deliberately the source's bounds. Replacing binom(k,2)
by a tighter elementary estimate might improve 4 but is not used here.
The precise selected full spread is aT_s+bT_s, the flat spread is d_s,
and the source's numerical inequalities give the asserted a^2 bound.

5. DETECTION AND PRIMITIVE NORMALIZATION [CL5]

Let R_0,R_f and their normalized duals have perfect integral pairings
beta_0,beta_f. On selected twists, write the opposite-edge lattices
Rbar_0,Rbar_f,Rbar_rem. Assume the two Psi sandwiches from Section 4
and integral Cartan spanning Rbar_0=span{y beta}. For beta in Rbar_rem put

  Lambda_beta=p^{kappa_0} Psi_0^{-1} M_beta Psi_f,
  beta_f(rho_beta u,T)=beta_0(u,Lambda_beta T).

The sandwich and Cartan multiplication imply Lambda_beta R'_f<=R'_0;
perfect duality then gives rho_beta R_0<=R_f. Suppose u in R_0 and every
rho_beta u lies in w^{2(kappa_0+kappa_f)+1}R_f. For Z in R'_0, write
Psi_0 Z=sum y_j beta_j. Set T_j=Psi_f^{-1}(p^{kappa_f}y_j), which lies in
R'_f. Then

  beta_f(rho_beta_j u,T_j)
    =p^{kappa_0+kappa_f} beta_0(u,Psi_0^{-1}(y_j beta_j))

is divisible by w^{2(kappa_0+kappa_f)+1}. Cancel p^{kappa_0+kappa_f} and
sum to conclude beta_0(u,Z) in wO for all Z. Perfectness gives u in wR_0.

Reinsert unselected tensor factors using the normalized integral dual
basis of their edge lattice. If all contractions of a tensor are in w
times the selected lattice, its basis expansion shows the tensor itself
is divisible by w. Thus these factors do not increase kappa. At any
other edge with the same target, their dual basis is bounded by
w^{-M}H(target)^vee, M<=2rn'c''. Selected remainder vectors cost at most
rNc0. This accounts for ell=rNc0+2rn'c''. The source's RhoAt hypotheses
are exactly

  rho R_0(e)<=R_f(e),
  rho R_0(e')<=w^{-ell}R_f(e') for all incoming e',
  rho H_0(target)<=p^{A_rem}w^{-ell}H_f(target),
  rho(Theta F . s)=Theta F' with F' in the c0-word closure,
  all rho u in w^{2kappa+1}R_f implies u in wR_0.

The height comparisons follow from the maximal representation weight
spread; no positivity of A_rem is needed. Source declarations rho_peel,
ordMax_top and ordMax_spec justify the stated constants.

For primitive U select rho U not in w^{2kappa+1}R_f. Since R_f is finite
free, write rho U=w^nu y with 0<=nu<=2kappa and primitive y in R_f. Its flat
preimage F1 remains in the K-linear word closure after division by w^nu.
The normalized adjoint is

  Lambda=w^{2(A_f-A_0)}rho^*=p^{-A_rem}rho^*.

Indeed beta_0(U,Lambda T)=beta_f(rho U,T). Multiplying by
c=w^{2kappa-nu} gives c beta_0(U,Lambda T)=p^kappa beta_f(y,T).

6. THE FACTORIAL AND PROJECTION COSTS [CL7–CL8]

From here onward v is normalized by v(p)=1, so v(w)=1/2. The misleadingly
named source definition twiceValuationFactorialProduct is actually
v_p(prod_s d_s!), with NO extra factor 2. Our formulas use its definition.

For coefficient Fock pairing, <X^alpha,X^beta>=alpha! delta_{alpha,beta}.
Put f=prod_s d_s! and kappa_fl=sum_s k floor(log_p d_s), with log_p(0)
defined as 0 for this integer bound. Legendre's formula gives

  v_p(d!)-sum_i v_p(alpha_i!)
    =sum_{j>=1}(floor(d/p^j)-sum_i floor(alpha_i/p^j)).

Each summand is in [0,k-1], and is zero for p^j>d. Thus it is at most
k floor(log_p d). Summing by blocks proves p^{kappa_fl}alpha!/f integral.
Consequently, if G is integral of multidegree d and H has coefficient
valuation >=t, then

  v(<G,H>) >= t+v_p(f)-kappa_fl.

This proves the normalized Fock integrality used in the final value bound;
it does not require an invariant subspace or a positive form.

The normal-variable projector cost is explicit. With true-letter set Z,
normal dimension Kperp, and the nested/disjoint cut sets Z_i, it is

  D_no=sum_i binom(Kperp,2)
        floor(log_p(2 sum_{x in Z_i} d_no(x)+Kperp-1)).

The source has |Z|<=N and at most |Z|+J<=2N cut indices, since r>=1.
The block decomposition gives d_no(x)<=2a. Under 4N<=a and k<=a,
2 sum_Z d_no + Kperp-1 <=4Na+k<=a^2+a<=a^3. Each summand is at most
3k^2 log_p a, hence D_no<=6L. For plane or normal monomial exponents
with their prescribed block degrees, the factorial deficit is at most
3Nr log_p a or 3N Kperp log_p a respectively (the same Legendre argument,
using d<=2a<=a^3). Half of each deficit plus D_no is <=9L. This is the
source's SizeAt constant 9, with its actual coarse estimates.

Here is the selection argument below the PickAt wrapper. Define the
weighted coefficient valuation

  b(F)=min_alpha [v(F_alpha)+v_p(alpha!)/2].

It satisfies b(F+G)>=min(b(F),b(G)). A finite complete family of
projectors has F=sum_mu P_mu F, so some component has b(P_mu F)<=b(F).
Choose one component successively at each cut. The projectors commute
for the source's nested/disjoint cuts, so their product is a joint
projector, self-adjoint for Fock, without any loss in this selected
inequality. If each projector is integral after multiplication by
p^{D_i} on the weighted coefficient lattice, the product is integral
after p^{sum D_i}. The existence, commutation and individual integral
exponents of those isotypic projectors are representation inputs; the
selection and additive cost are proved by the displayed argument.

For completeness, the cancellation that produces the scalar 9L bound
can be written without the source's packaging. Start with a unit
coefficient of G. Its digit/block decomposition has plane and normal
exponents alpha_P,alpha_N and a factorial factorization

  alpha! = C alpha_P! alpha_N! gamma_P gamma_N,

where gamma_P,gamma_N are p-adic units. Write the valuations of the four
factorials as V_total,V_C,V_P,V_N, so V_total=V_C+V_P+V_N<=v_p(f).
Project the normal coefficient polynomial, select a coefficient indexed
by nu attaining b, and then take its plane coefficient polynomial P_c.
If U=v_p(nu!) and q2 is the selected coefficient valuation, then

  q2+U/2 <= V_N/2,
  b(P_c)=q3 <= q2+V_P/2.

Choose z with v(z)=q3 (the source uses a coefficient times
w^{v_p(alpha'!)} attaining the minimum). Then F2=z^{-1}P_c has b(F2)=0.
The normal-test approximation input supplies an integral N0 and scalar

  v(cY)<=Nfact/2+D_no-U/2,

where Nfact=v_p(prod_x d_no(x)!). Define k0=C cY nu! z. If

  v(k0) <= v_p(f)-Pfact/2
             +D_no+(Pfact-V_P)/2+(Nfact-V_N)/2
         <= v_p(f)-n0+9L,  n0=Pfact/2.

This is the substantive cancellation in pick_core/gram_exponent_arithmetic.
It prevents the large factorial values from becoming the final loss.
The approximation N0, the unit digit factors, and the structured

dual test T_q with the same order bound, edge integrality, off-edge
divisibility w^{c_P}, and

  beta_f(Theta F1,sum_q T_q)=y+w^{c_P}z, z in O.

This realization is the exact seam with the companion localizer section;
we do not count it again as proved here. If yb=p^{D_X} and 2D_X<c_P,
(y+w^{c_P}z)b=p^{D_X}(1+w^{c_P-2D_X}zb). The final factor is 1 modulo
the maximal ideal, hence a unit. The realized flat value y' therefore
still divides p^{D_X}, without increasing its exponent.

Push T=sum T_q through the normalized adjoint Lambda of Section 5. It
lies in R'_0 at the chosen edge, has target height <=tF+ell, and at
every other incoming edge lies in w^{c_P-ell}R'_0. Since c_P=c_*+ell,
this is exactly the requested off-edge order. The full pairing x is
integral. Its identity c x=p^kappa y' and y'b'=p^{D_X} give

  x(cb')=p^{kappa+D_X}.

No division by c is needed; c need not be a unit. Consequently
D1=kappa+D_X<=4L+50L=54L. The same tF, D_X and kappa bounds apply to
every primitive coefficient array, although rho, F1 and T may depend on
that array. Source and target order bounds also do not depend on it.

The extraction of 54 follows explicit witness choices:
  SizeStepAlt.sizeStep' uses C_Dn=9;
  LeOfOneLeMul.restNum uses C_Dm=20;
  proposition5_at_of_three_parts gives ceil((9+2*20)L)<=50L;
  XiStepN_of_P, xiAt_of and restFlat_of preserve that exponent;
  LogSizeBoundLe.restSize uses C_K=4;
  test_bound_of_two_halves adds C_K and C_Df.
This is marked a derived simplification because the top source theorem
only exports existence of a constant, not the numeral 54. Increasing the
unspecified global threshold C is necessary; no claim is made that 54 is
the threshold for every other numerical inequality. With C>=54 and the
original source thresholds, D1<=D_p and c_*>=8(D_p+1) imply all required
strict inequalities, including 2D_X<c_P and c_P>69L.

9. PRECISE REMAINING INPUTS AND NONCLAIMS [CL12]

(a) Integral standard monomials. We use a monic basis indexed by the
specified interlaced arrays, its integral bideterminant spanning, and
the tableau count equal to Weyl dimension. These are sSBasis,
standardMonomialSpanDomain and dGT_weylDim. We prove the selected-array
bijections and the distinct-leading-term argument, not the entire
straightening theorem. The EL section establishes the full coefficient
lattice and its elementary weight bounds without needing this basis;
that does not by itself supply the particular complementary family.

(b) Integral normalized Fock duality and the explicit Weyl sandwich.
The exact retained statement is in Section 4. Its source is
ThetaLogCoordinates/SandwichModelDomains/FinalW.sandwichLocal and the
Weyl-pairing/dual-inclusion results feeding FormCoreSKXOf. The source's
construction of Psi and the scalar exponent estimates are unpacked;
the integral sandwich theorem is not. Characteristic-zero complete
reducibility used in Section 3 cannot replace this integral input.

(c) Structured flat testing. The remaining construction is considerably
more specific than 'a nonzero pairing exists'. It needs the following:
  - MemAt for the c0-word closure of the actual symbols B*SPR: after
    digit splitting and normal isotypic projection, every plane
    coefficient belongs to the signed-chain space XX.
  - Commuting isotypic projectors at the nested/disjoint normal cut sets,
    self-adjoint for Fock and integral on the weighted coefficient
    lattice after p^{D_no}; the explicit costs are those in Section 6.
  - GaAt: digit factorial ratios gamma_P,gamma_N are units and their
    diagonal actions and inverses admit binomial-polynomial
    approximations modulo p^j of degree <=6j.
  - NoAt: an integral normal polynomial N0 of its prescribed isotypic
    weight approximates diag(gamma_N^{-1})(cY e(X^nu)) modulo p^{c_P},
    with the exact cY bound of Section 6.
  - ProjectionDerivativeEstimate(26,2,24,60): for every signed-chain
    F2 in XX with b(F2)=0, and Hyp(26), c>4(D_m+D_l+3k_p), there are
    Gp,R satisfying coefficient integrality, YY(24,60), the error
    bound c-E_E, multidegree, the weighted lower bound, and the
    pairing upper bound in Section 7. Bform is coefficient Fock with
    multiplication of the coefficient at alpha by the unit ga(alpha).
  - MembAt: the assembled H=asm(phi(Gp),N0) is a finite sum of the
    exact OneSidedLemma4Element symbols, so it is covered by the
    localizer realization. The numeric bounds include
    D_m+D_l+3k_p<=20L and kappa_fl<=20L; the factorial part is proved
    here, while the frame-chain geometric estimate is retained.

These are supplied by the pinned Lean development, but their substantive
polynomial/representation proofs are not fully reproduced here. Precise
source targets are PickDefs, PickCore, GramExponentArithmetic,
TwistedSubstitution/JointEIntList, Proposition5Gram,
Lemma5aAt.ProjectionDerivativeEstimate, and the closed DiagonalSupport
and PlaneGram modules. Merely invoking restNS or restFlat is not counted
as closing them. Our valuation/core proof starts from their explicit
quantitative outputs and shows exactly why the final cost is logarithmic.

(d) Symbol construction and dimension. The companion SY section treats
SPtrue, the primitive polynomial P and the localizer algebra. Its
remaining path-generator/span and dimension estimate are shared inputs
to (CL.3), not an extra proof in this note. The finite-place lattice
comparisons and perfect duality used above are in the companion EL
section. JR algebra and the Smith tail-to-index proof from v0.3 are not
rederived here.

The common use of these local ingredients in the RH and Dirichlet-GRH
