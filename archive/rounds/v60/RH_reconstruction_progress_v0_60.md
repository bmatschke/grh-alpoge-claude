## Why the host Selmer group vanishes

The original counting argument fixes an arithmetic character, a level, a coefficient ring, a numerical witness and a frame. A Selmer class on this frame measures a possible global deformation subject to local conditions. In weight −2 the question has a particularly explicit form. A cocycle is matrix-valued, but only its bottom-left entry can be nonzero. The local conditions on that entry force the entire cocycle to be a coboundary. The following steps prove this for every permitted choice in the original definition. [Exact host contract](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/UnitaryThreeDeformations/HostNumber.lean#L14-L17)

1. **Construct the two characters required by the earlier vanishing theorem.** The theorem BR7 takes a pair of primitive Dirichlet characters. HXD1–3 start with the character already attached to the host. Its residual ℓ-power part disappears. The odd part and the Teichmüller lift give the first primitive character. Squaring the component of conductor $2^t$ and twisting by the odd character modulo four gives the second, of exact conductor $2^{t-1}$. The numerical witness supplied by the host gives the conductor inequality. Evaluating on the same absolute Galois element identifies the inverse product of the two residual characters with the one that acts on the host line. [Character construction](host_xpp_v60/input.tex)
2. **Identify the matrix entry and both local conditions.** The three diagonal weights are $1,0,-1$, hence the weight −2 space is $kE_{20}$, with indices starting at zero. The diagonal action contributes $\bar\Psi^{-2}$. Negative reflected transpose fixes the position of this entry and changes its sign, contributing the quadratic character $\eta$. Write $\lambda=\bar\Psi^{-2}\eta$. The subgroup $K=\ker(\eta,\bar\Psi)$ has index dividing $2(|k|-1)$, prime to the odd residue characteristic ℓ. Away from ℓ the host conditions give the required vanishing on inertia intersected with $K$, including at primes outside the host set. At ℓ the cocycle is locally a coboundary, so it vanishes on inertia intersected with $\ker\lambda$. Conjugating the chosen valuations gives the test at every valuation required by BR7. [HXP1–3](host_xpp_v60/main.tex)
3. **Return the global witness to the counting frame.** BR7 gives one scalar $m\in k$ for which the pulled-back cocycle is $g\mapsto\lambda(g)m-m$. Surjectivity of the absolute Galois group onto the original quotient gives this formula on the host itself. The matrix $mE_{20}$ now witnesses that the given cocycle is a global coboundary. Every class in the actual Selmer quotient is therefore zero. In the source call to the counting theorem this supplies the required argument on the already chosen frame, with its marked element and level. ## From the period identity to a family of spectral settings

The previous edition proved a Gram expansion for a pair of words in a setting with compatible arithmetic data and comparison isometries. Applying it across a family requires two further steps. First one needs the analytic and arithmetic data for the particular setting chosen at each family member. Then all chosen members must satisfy the hypotheses at one family threshold. An existence theorem for a convenient Bessel function at a single place would leave the first step open. [Previous reading guide](RH_reconstruction_progress_v0_59.md)
