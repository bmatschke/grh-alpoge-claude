CONVENTIONS THAT AFFECT THE PROOF

Coordinates are 0,...,n−1 and positive roots are e_a−e_b with a<b. Compact
roots have equal signs in epsilon; rho_c and rho_n are the half-sums of the
compact and noncompact positive roots. Their sum has coordinates
rho_i=(n−1)/2−i. A regular integral parameter has strictly decreasing lambda
and lambda_i−(n−1)/2 integral. The compact weight is
Lambda=lambda+rho_n−rho_c and the principal-minor exponent is mu=lambda+rho.

Inner products are conjugate linear in the first slot. The kernel's formula
contains Phi evaluated on an inverse MATRIX, not the reciprocal of Phi.
The leading-minor function is Phi_mu(z)=product_j Delta_j(z)^{−(mu_{j−1}−mu_j)}.
This convention is required both by the compact integration-by-parts
calculation and by the signs of the central differential operators.

The literal Capelli operator is the column determinant
  C(u)=sum_sigma sign(sigma) product_{j=0}^{n−1}
       (E_{sigma(j),j}+delta_{sigma(j),j}(u−j)),
with factors composed in increasing j from left to right, so the rightmost
factor acts first. At u=Y+(n−1)/2 its scalar is product_i(Y+lambda_i).
Rank one gives Y+lambda; the trivial compact rank-two representation gives
(Y+1/2)(Y−1/2). The manuscript records these tests explicitly.

If p and q are the sign-block sizes and q0=min(p,q)>0, the sufficient
constructive bound is
  h>max(npq+1,16nq0+8 sqrt(2q0)).
Here h bounds only the opposite-sign parameter differences; compact gaps
need not be large. The smaller condition h>npq+1 suffices for rigidity once
the model exists. Compact signatures, rank zero, and rank one are handled
separately, so no division by q0 occurs in those cases.

DC1 — THE EXACT COMPACT TYPE

dominance difference is lambda_a−lambda_b+r−1, hence nonnegative. The
construction uses the finite-dimensional Young-symmetrizer representation
of the partition obtained by subtracting the last weight, followed by the

DC2 — THE PRINCIPAL-MINOR AVERAGE

For the exact compact representation T and highest-line projection P_w,
define P(z)=dim(T) integral_K Phi_mu(k^{-1}zk) T(k)P_wT(k)^{-1} dk, where
K has probability Haar. Positive definite matrices have all leading minors
positive. Compactness supplies a common complex neighborhood on which the

compact subsets; differentiation under the integral is justified there.
Triangular multiplication supplies the exact diagonal weight. Compact

exp(−X)=J exp(X) J and diagonal conjugation preserves every leading minor.
Thus P(exp(−X))=P(exp(X)), establishing the inverse-matrix convention.

DC3 — THE DIFFERENTIAL IDENTITIES AND THE ACTUAL GLOBAL KERNEL

The essential calculation is compact integration by parts with
B=k^{-1}Yk. Lower-minus-upper differentiation moves the variable triangular
direction to the two compact representation factors. The divergence of
the variable coefficient is −4 sum_i rho_c,i B_ii. Together with the
principal-minor weight −2 sum_i mu_i B_ii, this gives the exact shift
mu=Lambda+2rho_c. Adding the conjugation differential identity yields
  dP_z(zY)=−dT(Y)P(z),  dP_z(Yz)=−P(z)dT(Y).
Holomorphic continuation extends the identities from the positive cone.

The unique polar decomposition g=k exp(X) defines Psi(g)=T(k)P(exp(X)).
The two differential identities show local independence along a short
compact logarithm path and give Psi(g)=T(k0)P(g^{-1}k0) near any fixed
g0=k0 exp(X0). This is a genuine ambient holomorphic formula. It proves
the source's local ambient smoothness, not merely smoothness of a formal
expression on polar coordinates.

DC4 — THE FULL CENTRAL PARAMETER

The quadratic Casimir has scalar ||lambda||²−||rho||². The proof uses the
local formula from DC3, differentiation under the compact integral, and

unit past a lower one produces E_aa−E_bb. The analogous column-determinant
calculation gives the complete shifted Capelli polynomial, with the literal

remaining diagonal commutators give the column shifts. Induction in the
number of columns verifies the calculation in every rank.

The coefficients generate the center: their leading PBW symbols are the

DC5 — AN EXPLICIT LOGARITHMIC MINOR ESTIMATE

The elementary exponential-moment estimate uses e^u>=1+u+u²/3 on |u|<=1,
log(1+t)>=3t/4 for 0<=t<=1/3, and Jensen for a concave power. For mean-zero
exponents bounded by R it gives log(sum w_j exp(y_j))>=
(1/4)min(1,1/R) sum w_j y_j². Cauchy–Binet expresses each leading minor of
exp(Y) as just such a positive exponential sum. Differentiating that
determinant at zero identifies its mean and variance exactly: the mean is
the trace of the principal block and the variance is the squared norm of
the cross block. Since rank(Y)<=2q0, the exponent bound is sqrt(2q0)||Y||.
This supplies the numerical constant 1/4 and all rank dependence used next.
