# From a nonzero core period to a Bessel period at the original level

Fix the grid and its Haar data. Let $\Psi_0,\ldots,\Psi_m$ be an admissible descending core chain and $u$ a bottom function with rapidly decreasing partial row lifts. Suppose $\int_{[U(T)]}\Psi_0(\iota h)u(h)\,dh\ne0$ for the prescribed embedding of the bottom group. We reconstruct its ascent to a nonzero Bessel test. The finite datum at each new arrow is a coset from the given row catalogue. The proof uses the mixed-model and depth-lattice properties of its standard frame, identified below. [Rung statements][rungs] · [Original level and test class][loc] · [Even-length endpoint][asc]

## Detecting a vector at the given depth

Write $H(w)$ for the Heisenberg operators and $v_0$ for any nonzero depth vacuum. The relevant annihilator $A_\Gamma$ consists of $w$ such that $\psi(B(w,2a))=1$ for every $a\in\Gamma$ and $\psi(B(w,a))=1$ for every $a$ in each split half of $\Gamma$. If $f$ is another depth vector then $\langle f,H(w)v_0\rangle=0$ off $A_\Gamma$. Indeed, translate both entries by a testing lattice shift. Their depth eigencharacters cancel and the commutator multiplies the coefficient by a character different from one. Moyal's formula with additive rational covolume one reads, in the convention linear in the first argument,
$$
 \|v_0\|^2 f(x)=\int \langle f,H(w)v_0\rangle H(w)v_0(x)\,dw.
$$
It follows by Fourier inversion in the modulation coordinate and integration in the position coordinate. In the rational lattice realization the corresponding proof unfolds the rational translates and applies character orthogonality on the compact quotient. The coefficient in this integral is absolutely integrable. The period functionals used here are tame. In particular they commute with this integrable superposition. One can apply Fubini to their defining integrals by bounding the kernel by polynomial height growth and using the rapid automorphic factors. A functional killing every $H(w)v_0$ for $w\in A_\Gamma$ consequently vanishes on the entire depth space. This is a test for the actual smooth period functional, with its original level. [Lattice conditions and tameness][tam] · [Moyal formula on this model][moyal] · [Application to this fixed space][irr] · [Period bounds][tameper]

For the first-column row catalogue, decompose a finite annihilator shift into the chosen marked split half, its conjugate half, and the complement. The first translates a coset indicator to another coset in the given quotient. The second multiplies by a character constant on its supporting coset. The third acts by the lattice-vacuum scalar. Their composition, including the Heisenberg factor, is a scalar multiple of another original coset vector. For a general $w\in A_\Gamma$ its real and finite parts have zero symplectic cross term, and its finite part is still in $A_\Gamma$, since all tested lattice shifts are finite. At infinity, the Taylor coefficients of the Gaussian-normalized coherent state are the Fock creation monomials. More precisely, the Gaussian integrals against conjugate monomials of the coefficient of a real translate give the evaluations of the polynomial vectors, up to nonzero normalization factors. If all those evaluations vanish, the Bargmann reproducing formula gives vanishing on all real translates. The Gaussian bound justifies the moment integrals and integration against the reproducing kernel. Apply this argument to $f\mapsto\overline{\ell(\bar f)}$ when conjugating the source polarization. Thus a tame row functional nonzero on a depth vector detects some $\overline{\mathrm{Fock}(v_\kappa,P)}$ with $\kappa$ in the original quotient and $P$ a polynomial. ## The bottom seesaw

The bottom core space splits as $X=T\perp L$ for the prescribed line $L$. Write $S$ for the next core space, $\phi=\overline{\Psi_1}$, and $u'=\bar u$. In the initial Jacobi flag, of Witt rank zero, the unipotent group is trivial and its probability integral is evaluation at the identity. Form
$$
 \mathcal L(F)=\int_{[U(S)]\times[U(T)]}
     \phi(g)u'(h)\,[\omega_X(g,h\oplus1)F]\,(0)\,dg\,dh .
$$
On the given core vector $\Xi$ this is the complex conjugate of the assumed nonzero core period by the chain equation. The height of $h\oplus1$ is bounded by a power of the height of $h$. The theta kernel has polynomial height growth and both automorphic factors decrease rapidly, so the integral is absolutely convergent in either order. For $F=\Phi\boxtimes\overline{\Phi_L}$ the Weil kernel at zero equals
$$
       [\omega_T(g,h)\Phi]\,(0)\,
                  \overline{[\omega_{-L}(g,1)\Phi_L]\,(0)} .
$$
Conjugation reverses the line character. Integrating first in $h$ gives the rank-zero Fourier–Jacobi period of the row lift of $u'$ with the specified line test. [Tensor rule, conjugation and Haar order][base] · [Actual characters and first core evaluation][baseapp]

Why does some admissible product work? The row projection preserves the bottom core depth lattice. Subtracting this projection gives the line projection. Extension by zero identifies the row summand. Hence the core lattice decomposes into the row and line components, also on their split halves. Negating the line changes the commutator character to its inverse and preserves its kernel. Take nonzero vacua on the two components. Their product is nonzero since the two arguments vary independently. Every core-annihilator translate splits as a product of permitted translates on the factors. If all such product periods vanished, Moyal's identity would imply $\mathcal L(\Xi)=0$. A successful product therefore exists, and the preceding test-generation argument replaces its row factor by an allowed polynomial and coset, keeping its line factor. [Bottom depth projections and actual vacua][baselat] · [Transfer of the annihilator translates][baseann]

## What Fourier inversion isolates at an interior step

Let $x_0$ be the full-column-rank rational tuple in the target $W$, $U_\rho$ the upper unitriangular group on its $\rho$ columns, and
$$
 R=\{r:rx_0=x_0u\text{ for some }u\in U_\rho(\mathbb A_E)\}.
$$
In the Jacobi-to-Bessel step the two partial-transform coordinates are $(x_{\rm zero},y_{\rm zero}(\zeta))$. In the reverse step they are $(x_{\rm last}(\zeta),0)$. The first coordinate called $x_{\rm zero}$ is the tuple of Witt flag vectors; its columns are independent. The group $R$ is the Jacobi group for the first tuple and the Bessel group for the second. [Partial Fourier transforms and the two input statements][loc] · [Orbit and stabilizer][orb]

Expand the mixed theta series in its rational matrix coordinate $x$. The integral over the unipotent center kills its term unless $x^*Hx=x_0^*Hx_0$, by character orthogonality on the compact rational quotient. If the columns of $x$ are dependent, use a relation with last nonzero coefficient one. The corresponding transvection fixes the mixed term while translation multiplies the period character by a scalar different from one. Its integral is therefore zero. The surviving independent tuples of this Gram matrix form one rational orbit by Witt extension. Unfolding that orbit leaves integration over $(R\cap U(W)(F))\backslash U(W)(\mathbb A_F)$. Integration in the remaining unipotent and auxiliary variables gives a function $f(h)$ on the target group. In the completely unfolded integrand the matrix read by a modulation is $h^{-1}x_0u$, with $u\in U_\rho(\mathbb A_E)$. The sum of absolute mixed theta terms is bounded by a polynomial in the quotient height on the compact unipotent fundamental set. The rapidly decreasing automorphic factor absorbs this power. In the additional integration variable the same bound is multiplied by its integrable weighted envelope. Thus Fubini applies also after restricting $h$ to a measurable set. [Center and dependent-column tests][orb] · [Unfolding at the surviving orbit][unf] · [Absolute convergence and restricted integrals][family]

Suppose every next period vanished. Replace a localized test by its Heisenberg modulation in an arbitrary *real* dual matrix $c$. This preserves its finite datum and multiplies the unfolded density by $\psi_\infty(B((h^{-1}x_0u)_\infty,c))$. Push the integrable complex density forward by $(h,u,\ldots)\mapsto(h^{-1}x_0u)_\infty$. The result is a finite complex measure with zero Fourier transform on the whole real dual by nondegeneracy of the trace pairing. Fourier uniqueness gives the zero measure. In particular, the integral of $f$ over each strip $R C\,U(W)(\mathbb A_f)$ in its rational fundamental set vanishes for compact $C\subset U(W)_\infty$. Indeed, take a left inverse $Y$ of $x_0$. Membership in this strip can be read from the real matrix $z=(h^{-1}x_0u)_\infty$: some $c_0\in C$ has $x_0Yc_0z=c_0z$ and $Yc_0z$ upper unitriangular. These equations give a closed condition after projection over the compact set $C$. [Modulation identity and real Fourier uniqueness][mod] · [Measurability and identification of the strip][strip]

Let $K$ be the *prescribed* finite subgroup at this rung. It fixes the localized tests and the incoming partial row. The support of a nonzero flat transform at $h$ forces $h\in R\,U(W)_\infty K$. The relative mean $T(h)=\int_{R(F)\backslash R} f(rh)\,dr$ is consequently left $R$-invariant, right $K$-invariant, supported in this set and continuous. If $T(1)\ne0$, choose a compact real identity neighborhood $C$ so small that $\operatorname{Re}(T(c)/T(1))>1/2$ for $c\in C$. On this strip each nonzero value of $T$ equals some $T(c)$, since the real and finite parts of $R$ split. The strip contains the open image of $\operatorname{int}(C)K$ in the quotient, of positive measure. The real part of the normalized quotient integral on it is positive, contradicting strip vanishing. The Haar measure on $R$ here is the semidirect-product measure inducing the given period. This proves $T(1)=0$ at the original finite level. ## Testing the relative coset at the prescribed finite level

In the mixed realization take two bumps on the finite lattice cosets of the designated pair of coordinates and a nonzero core vacuum. Take the first bump $b$ nonnegative and equal to one at $x_0$. At infinity choose its support so that the real part of the unitriangular character stays positive wherever $b(x_0u)\ne0$. The generic finite character is trivial on the prescribed lattice shift. Since $x_0$ admits a left inverse, the preimage of the compact bump support under $u\mapsto x_0u$ is compact. The second bump equals one at the second coordinate. Invert the two partial Fourier models. The resulting test has flat value equal to the core vacuum times $\int_{U_\rho} b(x_0u)\overline{\psi_U(u)}\,du$. Its real part as a scalar is positive by the contribution of an identity neighborhood. The mutual annihilator relations on the two finite lattices give the required translation and modulation conditions. Embedded shifts from the core annihilator commute with the outer tests, so the class also contains all shifts used in Moyal's formula. [Nonzero flat value on the localized class][bump] · [Closure under the permitted shifts][stab]

At a marked split place the adapted Witt frame prescribes scaled columns $e_{i_p}$ and rows $e_{j_q}^t$. An allowed change at depth ideal $I$ gives columns $\xi_p\equiv e_{i_p}$ and rows $\eta_q\equiv e_{j_q}^t\bmod I$. Preservation of the Gram matrix yields $\eta_q\xi_p=\delta_{j_q,i_p}$. In the case of a shared final coordinate, the last row of each unitriangular factor is the last coordinate functional, and the pairing remains the same. Form $D_1$ from the identity by replacing columns $i_p$ by $\xi_p$. Form $D_2$ from the identity by replacing rows $j_q$ by $\eta_qD_1$. The pairing equations show that $D_2$ fixes every $e_{i_p}$. Both matrices have unit determinant in the sense of invertibility over the local ring, since they reduce to the identity modulo $I$. Then $k=D_1D_2^{-1}\equiv1\bmod I$ satisfies $ke_{i_p}=\xi_p$ and $\eta_qk=e_{j_q}^t$ simultaneously. The conjugate split factor is determined by the unitary adjoint. This gives the actual unitary move inside the original principal congruence. The remainder stabilizes the flag up to its unitriangular action and belongs to $R$. [Integral completion][completion] · [Scaled split coordinates and pairing][split] · [Marked-place application][bad]

At an unmarked place use the Witt Iwasawa decomposition. In the Jacobi-stabilizer case the point-support condition gives integrality of the inverse torus diagonal entries. The shift-character condition gives integrality of the forward diagonal entries up to the last, which follows from the isotropic character and primitivity of the designated vector. The torus part therefore stabilizes the lattice, and the unipotent and core factors belong to $R$. This argument tests the support after removing the Iwasawa compact factor on the right. It is legitimate precisely because that factor fixes the original test. In the Bessel-stabilizer case the extra column imposes the integral line condition on the core element. The transported self-dual lattice contains the self-dual designated line. Splitting it off leaves a self-dual lattice on the orthogonal complement. The local single-orbit theorem for self-dual lattices on that complement at the host's tame places gives the remaining factor in the complementary unitary group times the lattice stabilizer. Thus in either orientation $h_v\in R_vK_v$. At almost all places take the $R_v$ factor to be one because $h_v\in K_v$. Assembling the local factors gives the finite support assertion used above. At marked places the splitting characters are trivial on determinants congruent to one at the prescribed depth. Away from them the unramified action fixes the lattice vacua. Thus the finite-level action has the required normalization as well as the required support. [Jacobi-stabilizer factorization][goodj] · [Bessel-stabilizer factorization][goodb] · [Weil scalar and original level][fix] · [Assembly at all finite places][collect]

## The alternating witnesses

In the first direction the mean at the identity is the Jacobi functional on the flat test. Thus total Bessel vanishing kills this functional on the full core-annihilator orbit of a nonzero flat vector. Moyal gives its vanishing on every core depth vector. But on the product of the preceding core test and the line test the orthogonal factorization gives the preceding nonzero Jacobi period. In identifying the lines, the source uses the rational map taking the one chosen line vector to the negative of the other and the squared-norm factor $\xi_0\bar\xi_0$. Its inverse brings the already selected line test into the product. In the reverse direction the relative mean is the Bessel functional after contraction in the line variable. On a product of the core test with the line vector used for the contraction, its value is the preceding Bessel period times the norm square of the nonzero line vector. This follows by Fubini and invariance of the Hermitian pairing on the line oscillator. Both product evaluations contradict total vanishing. [Jacobi-to-Bessel product][prod1] · [Bessel-to-Jacobi product and positive norm][prod2]

At each step the resulting localized vector belongs to the original row depth space. The test-generation argument gives a successful polynomial and coset. Update their coordinate at this arrow only. The previous partial row depends only on the earlier coordinates. Alternate Jacobi periods at indices $2k+1$ with Bessel periods at $2k+2$. For $m=2r\ge2$ the last Bessel index is $2(r-1)+2=m$. The final rescaled flag has the prescribed character after conjugation, and both positive Haar scale factors preserve nonvanishing. This proves the ascent implication on the stated core chain and standard frame. [Update of one coordinate][steps] · [Final character and measure normalization][end] · [Even-length induction][asc]

## The finite lattice calculation on the given grid

Write $\mathfrak a$ for the host ideal, $M$ for the modulus on the chosen halves of the marked split places, $\bar M$ for its conjugate, and $e$ for the projector to the chosen halves. Put
$$
 A=\mathfrak a/\bar M,\qquad B=\bar{\mathfrak a}/M,\qquad
 \Delta=M/\bar M,\qquad \mathfrak d=(\xi_0)\mathfrak a\bar{\mathfrak a}.
$$
The last ideal is the trace-dual of $\mathcal O_E$ for the standard finite additive character. Locally $s\in\mathfrak d_v$ is equivalent to $\psi_{E,v}(os)=1$ for every $o\in\mathcal O_{E,v}$. The unshifted oscillator lattice is $L=\mathfrak a\,\operatorname{Hom}(\Lambda_S,\Lambda_T)$ with finite completion understood. The depth lattice $\Gamma$ imposes in addition $eu\in M\operatorname{Hom}(\Lambda_S,\Lambda_T)$. The second test lattice is $L'=\mathfrak a\,\operatorname{Hom}(\Lambda_S,\Delta\Lambda_T)$. Indeed $\Gamma\subset L'$ place by place. At a chosen half $M$ gives the prescribed divisibility, and $\mathfrak a$ contains a local unit by the host and marked-place conditions. At its opposite half $\Delta=\bar M^{-1}$ enlarges the original target. Everywhere else $\Delta$ is a unit ideal. Since $\Delta\bar\Delta=1$, the shifted target remains self-dual. The trace matrix pairing hence makes $L'$ self-annihilating. In integral dual bases this follows by testing each matrix coefficient against a matrix unit with arbitrary coefficient in $\mathfrak a_v$ and using the trace-dual ideal above. The same argument works for $L$. [Depth condition][fa-depth] · [The shifted target][fa-shift] · [Trace conductor][fa-cond] · [Hom-lattice duality][fa-hom]

The actual frame has staircase coefficient ideals $J_{c,p}=A^{c-p}B^{-(p+1)}$ for $0\le p<c$. The $X$ columns have scalars $(-\xi_0)^{p+1}$ and the $Y$ columns use the reversed indices. Each Witt projector is the identity on its selected auxiliary columns and zero on the complementary columns and the orthogonal core. In the composite of projection and inclusion the two scalings cancel by the dual-basis identities. Thus every projector preserves the staircase lattice, even when the individual scalars are nonunits. It preserves a scalar multiple of this lattice as well. For endomorphisms of the source or target preserving the indicated lattices, the corresponding precomposition or postcomposition preserves the Hom and depth conditions, since both commute with $e$. Apply the three source projectors to $u\in\Gamma$. Within its source-core part apply the three target projectors. This gives five summands of $u$, with the original depth condition on each. The first is in $A_1$ by definition. The second is in $A_2=A_1^\perp$ because both entries to be paired lie in the unshifted self-annihilator $L$. The two off-core target summands are in the pullbacks $B_1,B_2$ of $L'$, and the last belongs to the pullback $\Gamma_0$ of $\Gamma$. The lattice $A_1$ is compact open in its coordinate subspace (the embedding has a continuous left inverse), so additive biduality recovers it from $A_2$. For the two $B$ pieces the only nonzero pairing of an $X$ block with any of the five blocks is against the opposite $Y$ block. Both projectors preserve $L'$, so annihilating that opposite component is equivalent to annihilating all of $L'$ for the embedded block, hence to belonging to $L'$. This proves both mutual annihilator identities. The core depth lattice lies in the unshifted self-dual Hom lattice of the two core factors by inclusion and projection. Its standard lattice vacuum supplies the nonzero core vector. [Five pieces and their required phases][fa-pieces] · [Witt coordinate projections][fa-proj] · [Block pairing][fa-dual] · [Core vacuum][fa-vac]

For primitivity of the good-place superdiagonal character set $I_{c,p}=(-\xi_0)^{-(p+1)}J_{c,p}$, the coefficient ideal in the scaled $X$ basis. A scalar $s$ takes the $(p+1)$-st line lattice into the $p$-th exactly when
$$
 s\in I_{c,p}/I_{c,p+1}=(\xi_0)AB=\mathfrak d/(M\bar M).
$$
At an unmarked place the right side equals $\mathfrak d_v$. Thus the character tests exactly the required lattice of adjacent-line maps in both frames. The column comparison across successive frames uses $J_{c+1,p}=A J_{c,p}$, including the final line column because $\zeta_{p+1}=(-\xi_0)^{p+1}\zeta_0$. At marked places compare the two Hom bounds. With the Bessel source, applying the ordinary and shifted bounds to the same superdiagonal entry gives membership in $\mathfrak d/M$ and $\mathfrak d/\bar M$. Their intersection there is $\mathfrak d$ since $M,\bar M$ have disjoint support. With the Jacobi source the two adjacent-row line quotients give $s\in\mathfrak d/M$ and $es\in(\xi_0)\bar{\mathfrak a}=\mathfrak d/\mathfrak a$. The first gives the conductor off the chosen half, the second gives it on that half since $\mathcal O_{E,w}\subset\mathfrak a_w$. Summing the entries gives the asserted trivial character on allowed finite shifts, also for a flag of rank zero or one where the sum is empty. [Bessel primitivity][fa-prim] · [Jacobi primitivity][fa-prj] · [Successive columns][fa-cols] · [Bessel marked comparison][fa-badB] · [Jacobi marked comparison][fa-badJ]

The marked split frame explains why the point condition gives congruences on *both* its columns and its rows. Let $d$ be the depth at $v=w\bar w$ and $c$ the number of raw target columns, so the source has $c+1$. At this auxiliary place the valuations of both host ideals vanish. Since $M_w=(\varpi^d)$ and $M_{\bar w}=(1)$, the definition of $J$ gives
$$
 \operatorname{ord}_{w} J_{c,p}=d(p+1),\qquad
 \operatorname{ord}_{\bar w} J_{c,p}=-d(c-p).
$$
Compare a perturbation in $A_1$ with the base tuple in the integral bases. Its $p$-th column is the image of the corresponding source column under its extension in $\Gamma$. The factors $(-\xi_0)^{p+1}$ in the base and source columns cancel in the normalized comparison, also on the last-line column. Multiplying that raw source column by a generator of the *target* ideal introduces the factor $J_{c,p}/J_{c+1,p}$. At $w$ this is a unit ideal and the explicit $e$-depth contributes $\varpi^d$. At $\bar w$ the quotient itself is $(\varpi^d)$ and the ordinary Hom bound suffices. In the split Hermitian pairing an integral basis for the opposite lattice is the dual basis, so its vector congruence is precisely the row congruence used in the completion argument. The anti-diagonal Gram matrix puts the chosen column indices in forward order and the opposite row indices in reverse order. They are disjoint for the isotropic tuple. For the extra-line tuple their only possible overlap is the final column. [Marked valuations and integral frame][fa-markframe] · [Chosen-half column bound][fa-markleft] · [Opposite-half row bound][fa-markright]

The two base-point phases in the five-piece identity only need be trivial on the **projections of $\Gamma$**. For the Bessel source the first is the trace of $\xi_0$ times the conjugated sum of diagonal entries of $p_Yu j_Y$. On each such entry the ordinary bound gives $\mathfrak a B$ and the depth bound on the $e$ component gives $\bar{\mathfrak a}$. The first bound equals $\mathfrak a\bar{\mathfrak a}$ away from $M$, and the second is contained in $\mathfrak a\bar{\mathfrak a}$ on the chosen half. Conjugation preserves this product. For the second phase only the final functional survives. The coefficient in a plain Hom map from its middle source column to the last selected target row belongs to $(\xi_0)B$. In fact the target-to-source staircase quotient is $J_{2\rho,\rho-1}/J_{2\rho+1,\rho}=B$, and the two frame scalings supply one $\xi_0$ up to sign. The ordinary and depth conditions therefore bound the coefficient by $\mathfrak d/M$ and its $e$ component by $\mathfrak d/\mathfrak a$, respectively. Both phases are one. If $\rho=0$ the last functional is zero. [Diagonal phase][fa-bx] · [The two diagonal coefficient ideals][fa-diag] · [Last-row phase][fa-by]

For the Jacobi source $y=0$. In the $x$ phase the scalings cancel within the pairing, leaving the $p$-th raw target column paired with the image of the reversed $p$-th raw source column. Set $c=2\sigma+1$ and $q=c-p$. The two coefficient ideals are $J_{c,p}$ on the target and $J_{c+1,q}$ on the source. Direct multiplication gives $J_{c,p}\overline{J_{c+1,q}}=A^{-1}$. Self-duality on the target therefore bounds the raw pairing by $\bar{\mathfrak a}A=\mathfrak a\bar{\mathfrak a}/\bar M$ using the ordinary Hom condition. At an unmarked place this is already sufficient. At a marked place, the row maps the source lattice into $\mathfrak a M$ times the target lattice locally: this follows on the chosen half from the depth condition and $\mathcal O_{E,w}\subset\mathfrak a_w$, and on the opposite half from the ordinary condition. This gives an extra $\bar M$ in the pairing bound because the image enters in the conjugate-linear variable. The raw pairing belongs to $\mathfrak a\bar{\mathfrak a}$ and its $\xi_0$ multiple to the conductor. This proves the remaining phase for every $p$, including the final column. [Cancelling column scalings][fa-fx] · [Coefficient-ideal product][fa-fxp] · [Marked depth and paired ideal][fa-fxm]

For the remaining local orbit assertion, the host has $E/F$ unramified at every finite place and split above two. At a split place a self-dual lattice is a pair consisting of a lattice and its ordinary dual under the two-factor pairing. A basis change in the first determines the inverse-adjoint change in the second, proving transitivity. At a nonsplit finite place the residue characteristic is odd. Reduction of a unimodular Hermitian lattice gives a nondegenerate Hermitian space over the quadratic residue extension. In positive rank it has a vector of nonzero norm by polarization. Lift it to a vector with unit norm, and split off its integral orthogonal line by the projection formula. Normalize the generator to norm one: the residue-field norm is surjective and Hensel lifting by the surjective residue trace lifts norms of principal units. Induction supplies an orthonormal integral basis. Any two such lattices in the same Hermitian space are carried to one another by mapping these bases. This applies to the complementary space in the good-place argument, including dimension zero. Thus the finite calculations use the established hypotheses of the chosen standard grid and add no further orbit or primitive-character hypothesis to this ascent.
