# From cuspidal multiplicities to the lower row

**Proposition.** Work on the common eventual range for the pinned columns. Suppose that on every such column, for each stage $Z$, each field witness and each place set, the two marked-group comparisons, the exact model comparisons (`ExAt`), and the geometric realization (`InGeo`) imply the equality `MultEq` of cuspidal multiplicities. Then these same hypotheses on the **given datum** imply all seventeen lower-row laws `HoldsL`. The [individual multiplicity comparison](LOWER_ROW_MULTIPLICITY.md) supplies this hypothesis on the common range. The standing theta and representation-theoretic results supporting the implication have exact source statements and producers below. The source also gives a route that uses the complementary-space residue on the doubling tests and requires no independent assumption of the universal regularized first-term package. ## Two consequences of the geometric datum

Write $K_s$ for the small rationality field and $L$ for the row coefficient field. For each row $i$, the geometric frame identifies the $K_s$-space of cusp sections with the stored rational subspace. Its prescribed basis is still a basis after extension to $L$. Under this identification the stored integral module consists exactly of the sections integral on the frame tests. The geometric fullness condition gives finite generation over $\mathcal O_{K_s}$ and spanning over $K_s$. The pinned action preserves the small-field sections and their integral frame conditions, so both the subspace and the integral module are stable. This proves `LIntegral`, including its field-of-definition clause. [Precise lattice assertions][latdef] · [Identification of the full lattice][lat] · [Stability and assembly][stab]

For every embedding $\sigma:L\hookrightarrow\mathbf C$ the row realization carries an $L$-linearly independent list to a complex-linearly independent list, and its image spans the complex row. The geometric uniformization and the frame identification give both assertions. To check the action, evaluate the same section on a translated frame. The pins identify its finite-group action and its level character. For a good restriction of $\sigma$ use the given equivariant evaluations. At the other restriction use the conjugate evaluation and the conjugate form of the same identity. Injectivity on the evaluated forms gives equivariance on the whole space, with scalar $\sigma(\chi_i(g))$. This is `LUnif` on the original row. Both deductions apply to each qualifying datum. The three analytic arguments listed in the two older theorem signatures are unused in their proofs. ## The analytic results used in the assembly

For a number field $K$, a finite set $T$ of finite places, and a unitary Hecke character $\chi$ outside the family $|\cdot|^{it}$, the incomplete Hecke $L$-function $L^T(s,\chi)$ extends to an entire function and is nonzero on $\operatorname{Re}s\ge1$. Also $(s-1)\zeta_K^T(s)$ extends to an entire function nonzero on the same closed half-plane. To see the finite-set qualification, start with Hecke's entire continuation and nonvanishing at one for non-norm characters. Twisting by $|\cdot|^{it}$ gives the boundary value at $1+it$ and preserves the non-norm condition. Absolute convergence of the logarithm gives nonvanishing to the right. For $\zeta_K$, its simple pole has positive residue. At $1+it$ with $t\ne0$, the Euler-product inequality $\zeta_K(\sigma)^3|\zeta_K(\sigma+it)|^4|\zeta_K(\sigma+2it)|\ge1$ for $\sigma>1$ follows from $3+4\cos u+\cos(2u)\ge0$. A zero at $1+it$ would make the left side tend to zero as $\sigma\downarrow1$. Finally multiply by the finitely many deleted inverse Euler factors. An unramified factor $1-\chi(\varpi_v)q_v^{-s}$ is entire and nonzero on $\operatorname{Re}s>0$, and a ramified factor is one. This proves the precise `HeckeLPartialContinuation` used here. [Hecke, *Eine neue Art von Zetafunktionen*, II](https://geodesic.mathdoc.fr/item/MZ_1920__6_167556/) · [Unramified factors, norm twists and the pole-removed zeta function][hl]

## The lift onto the prescribed space

The source's earlier route invoked a regularized first-term formula quantified over general complementary spaces, regularizers, tests and measures. The version used here proves the needed vanishing of a particular residue pairing. Let $A$ be the definite source, $W$ the prescribed lower target, $r=\dim W$ and $n=\dim A=r+t$. Put $B=W\perp\mathbb H^t$. A scalar component $u$ of the upper form gives a doubling integral. Its Satake string supplies the simple pole, and the local test calculation makes its residue nonzero. If every downward test vanished on $u$, all the corresponding pairings with doubled theta kernels on $W$ would vanish. The complementary spaces have $m'=r>0$, $m=r+2t$, $m+m'=2n$, and the Siegel–Weil point is $s_*=t/2$. Gan–Qiu–Takeda, Theorem 7.3(i), identifies the coefficient $A_{-1}$ for $B$ with a nonzero constant times the corresponding theta coefficient on $W$, evaluated after compact averaging on $B$ and the Ikeda map. The additive character is the same in the two models. For anisotropic $W$ this is an ordinary theta integral. For positive Witt index it is the residue of the regularized theta family. The compact source pairing commutes with extraction of the coefficient. In the isotropic case the regularized test has rapid decay, which allows the source pairing to be moved inside on the half-plane of convergence before analytic continuation. This would kill the nonzero residue. Thus some downward test is nonzero for **each** required scalar component. [Pole and remainder](continuation_v86/pole.tex) · [Local tests, polarizations and residue proof](continuation_v87/residue.tex#L264-L344) · [Gan–Qiu–Takeda, Theorem 7.3(i)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1207.4709#page=34) · [Precise compact-pairing implication][rescore] · [Its use in downward detection][ruse]

The test just obtained can depend on $u$. The fixed-test argument uses the finite- and infinite-place generation statements, preservation of the good type, exclusion of level characters, and compatibility of unramified Hecke eigenvalues. These turn detection with varying tests into injectivity on the required source eigenspaces. In particular one must test every vector in a repeated eigenspace. The upward map has the complementary injectivity from its inner-product identity and Schur orthogonality. Applying the eigenspace comparison with the rational Hecke adjoints and the given Hecke-intertwining condition gives the required surjection `liftOnto` for each allowed action. ## The uniform choice and the return to the original datum

Fix $\varepsilon>0$. The existence of good definite forms gives an $x_0(\varepsilon)$ before the choice of any row or datum in this implication. Intersect its range with the existing size, host-frame, coefficient-field and Weber-level conditions. On a stage admitting the size parameters, the marked types give the finite Green-series projectors. The geometric frames, extension of the reference operators, one-form comparisons on three levels and the Weber field give the geometric ties, with the phases read in the prescribed models. [Uniform bound and good forms][tied] · [Two sets of ties on a stage][ties]

On such a tied stage `MultEq` compares the multiplicity spaces. Multiplicity equality transfers eigensystem parts at the three levels using the cuspidal decomposition and Schur orthogonality. The models supply unitary group actions, and the character and exactness comparisons give equivariance. The theta-integrability statement gives the block condition. Core compatibility supplies the rational lift tests, including the target basis tests at the indicated level, and the modular lattice. The uniform good forms and Schur orthogonality give nonempty rows. The scalar identity and matching adjoints imply positivity on the positive-dimensional carried row part. The spanning and level comparisons then give the parts and Hecke laws. The previous section supplies surjectivity, while the normalized frames supply conjugation and the exact level subspaces. Together with integrality these are the fields of `HoldsL`, including the type separation. [Construction of the seventeen fields][tied] · [Definition of the record][laws]

For an arbitrary permitted $Z$, create an auxiliary stage with the same marked group data, with $\eta_F=6x^{-C_F}$ and with every $A_j$ zero. These last conditions supply the common size parameters and $8fr+4\le n_V$ on the auxiliary stage. Its host frames are orthonormal and span the prescribed lattice integrally. Transport the **given** datum to this stage along the same-group identification. Exactness, `LUnif`, `LIntegral` and the given multiplicity equality transport. The ties and preceding argument prove `HoldsL` on the copy. Transport its seventeen fields back to $Z$. The vanishing of the auxiliary operators is used only to choose these size parameters. ## What the first pass now gives

Apply the [initial fine construction](THETA_PINNED_COLUMNS.md), the [old-to-new fine construction](THETA_FINE_EXTENSION.md) and the [finite auxiliary-prime and saturation argument](UNTWISTED_AUXILIARY_PRIMES.md) in their stated common range. Green's theorem is already among the standing results. On a pinned untwisted column, the initial fine datum meets the antecedents above. It therefore provides the unrestricted seed with its lower-row laws. The bounded seed is chosen with its **whole** coefficient field inside the new fixed field. Extending this seed produces a fine datum on the requested column for which $W_1$ lies above the allowed primes, the twists are trivial off it, and $W_1$ is a union of complete fibers over the fixed field. Consequently, under the stated inputs, one can use this saturated fine datum on the actual first-pass column. [Exact stage and support conclusion][sat]

To continue to a bare construction state one needs the full row law at the characteristic-supported weights. The next comparison adds `RowTest` for the bottom theta operator on each of the required data. It asks for complex linear forms separating the bottom target and taking values in the chosen embedded field on **every** rational source image. The input `LiftTest` to `HoldsL` is defined on the individual lifted targets. The source's full weight-law implication specifies both `MultEq` and `RowTest`, and the construction of a bare state uses that weight law together with its remaining geometric and division inputs. Finally the actual third-stage record `Has3` requires the successive same-Galois-level, rationality, cuspidality, forms, series, moduli, acyclicity and finiteness data in the third-stage construction.
