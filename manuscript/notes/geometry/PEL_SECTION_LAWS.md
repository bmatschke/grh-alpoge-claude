# Theta sections, integral frames and the laws used by the marked core

The arithmetic content of a frame is simple. It chooses a basis in each eigenspace of the tangent space and a generator of the one-dimensional space of theta sections. An integral frame chooses these from the corresponding lattices over a discrete valuation ring. This description gives the following proof of the section laws after the marked PEL scheme and its complex period charts have been supplied.

The proof of `exists_coreJ` uses well-defined evaluation, independence of integral-frame valuations, existence of integral frames at each finite place, extension of a generic frame up to triangular change, multiplication of cusp forms, finite dimensionality and definiteness of the supremum norm. The stronger theorem quoted there also contains `AllTest` and the valuation-transfer theorem `regular₂`. The former is useful for constructing the frames themselves. The projections in the construction of the core skip the latter. [Core, lines 75–125][core] · [All nine conclusions][package]

## The global section theorem in the required generality

Let $p:A\to S$ be an abelian scheme of relative dimension $g$, with a rigidified symmetric ample line $L$ inducing a principal polarization. For $q\geq1$ and a section $c$ after any base change $T\to S$, consider the line
$$
M=L_T^{q-1}\otimes t_c^*L_T
  =L_T^q\otimes(t_c^*L_T\otimes L_T^{-1}).
$$
On every geometric fiber, $M$ is a translate of $L^q$. Indeed the factor in parentheses belongs to the dual abelian variety and $\phi_{L^q}=q\phi_L$ is surjective. The standard vanishing theorem for ample lines on abelian varieties gives $H^{i}(M)=0$ for $i>0$. In this symmetric case one can also pull $L^q$ back by multiplication by an arbitrarily large integer $m$ prime to the residue characteristic. The theorem of the cube gives $[m]^*L^q\simeq L^{qm^2}$, Serre vanishing applies, and the trace divided by $m^{2g}$ splits pullback on cohomology. The degree formula $\chi(L^q)^2=\deg(q\phi_L)=q^{2g}$ and positivity give $h^0(M)=q^g$. [Mumford, *Abelian Varieties*, §16](https://books.google.com/books/about/Abelian_Varieties.html?id=J81jPwAACAAJ) · [Milne, Theorem 13.3](https://www.jmilne.org/math/articles/1986b.pdf)

The morphism $p_T$ is proper, flat and finitely presented, and the line $M$ is relatively perfect. Consequently $Rp_{T*}M$ is perfect and commutes with arbitrary derived base change. Its fibers are concentrated in degree zero with dimension $q^g$, so locally it is a vector bundle of that rank in degree zero. For example, over a local base the invertible entries in the differentials of a finite free complex split off contractible pairs. In the remaining complex the residue differentials vanish, so only the $q^g$ degree-zero terms remain. A local basis before base change therefore gives a basis after every affine test map. Over a local ring every section of the residue fiber lifts by lifting its coefficients in that basis. Taking $c=0$ proves the pure-power law for all positive $q$. This gives both of the source's section base-change laws and its full twisted-section law, including non-Noetherian test rings. The objects of these three statements are polarized **abelian** schemes. Boundary semiabelian families enter below through a different argument. [Stacks Project, Tag 0DJT](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0DJT) · [Pure powers and twists][twist] · [Earlier GSL23–26 construction and its retained hypotheses](../../../research/modules/geometry/global_section_laws_v52/main.tex#L1113-L1329)

In particular $p_*L$ is a line. The scheme of its generators and of bases of the tangent eigenspaces represents frames on the marked family. On reduced finite-type charts a natural evaluation rule is the regular function obtained by evaluating it at the universal frame. These functions agree on overlaps by naturality. Their pullbacks give the same value in an arbitrary target algebra whenever two presentations give the same frame, including a target with nilpotents. Over a reduced coefficient algebra every framed point descends to a finitely generated reduced coefficient subalgebra. These are `EvalWD` and `AllTest` with the section theorem just proved supplying the assumption of the existing detailed arguments. [TDR7–13](../../../research/modules/geometry/test_ring_descent_v50/main.tex#L365-L673) · [NFL11](../../../research/modules/construction/normalized_frame_laws_v51/main.tex#L630-L703)

## Integral frames from semistable reduction

Fix a complete discrete valuation ring $V$ with fraction field $K$ containing the coefficient field $F$ and finite residue field. Start with **any** marked generic point and any of its specified frames. By Grothendieck’s semistable reduction theorem, after a finite extension $K'/K$ the identity component $G$ of the Néron model of the underlying abelian variety is semiabelian over the integral closure $V'$ of $V$. This is a complete DVR with finite residue field. The Néron mapping property extends the entire CM action and its relations, and group endomorphisms preserve the identity component. In the source the required integral object is a semiabelian group with action and cubical line. The torsion sections belong to the given generic moduli point. The definition of `Integral` compares its generic polarization and action to those of $G$ and imposes its frame conditions on the Lie eigenspaces and the theta generator. It requires no torsion sections on $G$. [SGA 7 I, Exposé IX, Théorème 3.6](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/BFb0068688) · [Integral frame and its generic-fiber identifications][integral]

At the levels used for the core, Raynaud's criterion gives a stronger conclusion **over the original DVR**. Let $p$ be its residue characteristic and take $n=M/p^{v_p(M)}$. The good-level hypothesis gives $n\geq3$. The idempotents of the CM order split $A[n]=C_n\oplus C'_n$ into two mutually dual summands. The half-marking, restricted from $M$ to $n$, labels every point of $C_n$ over $K$. Inertia preserves $C'_n$ since the CM action is defined over $K$, and acts trivially on $\mu_n$ since $p\nmid n$. Perfectness of the Weil pairing between the two summands then makes its action on $C'_n$ trivial as well. Thus all $n$-torsion is unramified. Raynaud's theorem gives semistable reduction over $V$, so in the following argument one may take $K'=K$ and $V'=V$. The more general finite-extension argument above also works when this level hypothesis is omitted. [Half-marking and duality](PEL_MARKED_LEVEL.md) · [Good-level hypothesis][good] · [Silverberg–Zarhin, Theorem 3.5, p. 406](https://www.numdam.org/article/AIF_1995__45_2_403_0.pdf#page=5)

Here is why the line and all its identities extend. $G$ and every finite power of $G$ over $V'$ are regular integral schemes. Their special fibers are smooth and geometrically connected algebraic groups, hence geometrically integral. In each power the only vertical prime divisor is the special fiber, of multiplicity one in the divisor of a uniformizer $\pi$. Extend the generic theta line by closing a divisor representing it. Regularity makes its closure Cartier. Tensor by the inverse of the pullback of its restriction at the identity to obtain a rigidification compatible with the original one. For any two such rigidified lines on a power of $G$, a generic isomorphism that preserves rigidification extends uniquely. Its rational section of the quotient line has divisor $m$ times the special fiber. After dividing by $\pi^m$ it is everywhere invertible by normality, and evaluation at the identity shows $m=0$. Apply this to the normalized symmetry, to both sides of the adjoint and graph identities, and to the normalized generic trivialization on $G^3$ defining the cube. The face, permutation and cocycle equalities extend by density on the corresponding powers. This produces exactly the required integral cubical line. [Source polarization and cubical identities][line]

The generic section space is a $K'$-line. A nonzero generator has no horizontal poles. Divide by the uniformizer to the power of its order at the generic point of the special fiber. The resulting section is regular everywhere by normality and has vertical order zero. Its multiples that are regular are exactly its $V'$-multiples. Thus the full section module on the possibly nonproper $G$ is free of rank one, and its generator differs from the given generic theta vector by one scalar in $K'^\times$.

For an embedding $\tau:E\hookrightarrow F$ (using the fixed labeling in $F$), intersect the generic tangent eigenspace with $\operatorname{Lie}(G/V')$. This is exactly the module of integral eigenvectors, since the eigenvalues of $\mathcal O_E$ are integral and equality can be checked in $K'$. Intersect this lattice in turn with the spans of the successive initial segments of the given generic eigenbasis. Each intersection is saturated, each consecutive quotient is free of rank one, and lifting generators of these quotients gives an integral basis adapted to the original flag. Its $i$th vector is a combination of the first $i$ original vectors, with nonzero diagonal coefficient. This is the direction of triangular change in `FrBor`. The construction works separately for every embedding, even when their integral eigenspaces do not split the Lie module at a ramified prime. Together with the scalar on the theta vector it proves the required triangular change. The new generic frame descends from a test ring by `AllTest`. Pullback of the original frame gives `FrMap`, using ordinary base change on its tangent spaces and theta line. [Frame change][frame] · [Exact extension conclusion][intall]

If the starting valuation lies over $\mathfrak l\subset\mathcal O_F$ with ramification index $e$, the extension has index $e(K'/K)e$ there and sends $V$ into $V'$. Thus `IntFrExt`, starting just with the specified generic point, uses the inclusion of fields, the positive ramification index $r=e(K'/K)$, and the identity $v'(z)=r v(z)$ on the original field (with the source's zero convention), and its integral frame lies over the pullback of that same point. At good level these choices are the identity and $r=1$. Starting with the prescribed generic frame and using its flag as above gives `IntAll` with the original prime and all its quantifiers. [Extension at a specified point][ext]

For `IntFrNe`, choose a closed point of the nonempty marked finite-type $F$-scheme, for example one of the CM points of the marked period charts. Its residue field is a finite extension of $F$. Complete it above any specified $\mathfrak l$, choose a generic frame there, and apply the construction. The condition called `IsAlg` in this conclusion says precisely that the residue field of the DVR is finite. For CM points the pre-existing everywhere-good model of ICL4–6 gives the same conclusion directly. Its good reduction argument applies to those points; semistable reduction above treats all generic points in `IntAll`. [Two different quantifiers][two] · [CM point and its integral eigenlattices](../../../research/modules/geometry/integral_cusp_lattices_v46/main.tex#L171-L328)

For two integral frames on one generic point, the generic identification of their semiabelian groups extends uniquely over the DVR. This is the extension theorem for semiabelian schemes over normal Noetherian bases, applied also to the inverse. Their normalized line isomorphism then extends by the vertical-divisor argument. Consequently an integral triangular change acts by a unit on each rank-one quotient in the flag of eigenspaces and by a unit on the theta generator. The weight character, a product of integer powers of those units, has valuation zero. This proves `UnitIndep` for all original weights. [Lan, Proposition 3.3.1.5, after Faltings–Chai I.2.7](https://www.kwlan.org/articles/cpt-PEL-type-thesis-revision-dense.pdf) · [Integral-frame independence][ind]

## Cusp forms at a fixed weight

For a supplied marked uniformization, multiplication of sections multiplies the evaluations and adds the algebraic weights. The two theta exponents are even, so their half-exponents add in the analytic weight as well. The product of a bounded holomorphic cusp form with another is a cusp form. Along each cusp their Fourier series have support in the allowable positive cone and convolution preserves strict positivity. Together with the transformation rule this proves `CuspMul`. A zero supremum norm forces all readings to vanish on every domain because their invariant norm factors are positive. The covering by the marked period charts then forces vanishing at every complex frame by triangular covariance. The frame scheme is reduced of finite type over a number field, so regular functions vanishing at all such points vanish. This gives `SupDef` at every good embedding. [Multiplication at the exact weights][mul] · [Positive norm and vanishing][sup]

For finite dimensionality, fix **one** good embedding $F\hookrightarrow\mathbb C$ and one dominant weight. Finitely many $F$-independent rules of that weight have $\mathbb C$-independent readings. Indeed, restriction to a finite affine cover of the frame scheme injects their span into a finite product of coordinate rings. Tensor this injection with $\mathbb C$ and use reducedness of the base-changed charts (the ground field is perfect). The period covering and triangular covariance detect their values there. On each component the bounded holomorphic readings transform under an arithmetic subgroup with the fixed weight and finite character. They lie in a finite-dimensional complex space. On unitary frames the compact flag fiber gives the prescribed finite-dimensional weight representation by Borel–Weil, and holomorphy kills the antiholomorphic tangent operators on the extremal vector. Thus the central character of the enveloping algebra is fixed by the weight, by the highest-weight formula. The bounded invariant norm gives moderate growth. Harish-Chandra’s finiteness theorem for a fixed compact type and infinitesimal character applies, passing to the finite-index kernel of the finite character if needed and fixing the central character on central torus factors. The source also gives a direct analytic proof. On each component it constructs a finite set of frames whose evaluations detect every bounded holomorphic cusp section of that weight. The components are finite in number. Either bound on the complex dimension bounds the size of every $F$-independent family. This proves `CuspFD` over the **given** coefficient field for each dominant weight. [Harish-Chandra, *Automorphic Forms on Semisimple Lie Groups*](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/BFb0098434) · [Component evaluations][detect] · [Passage to the coefficient field][fd]

The [direct semiabelian specialization](PEL_SEMIABELIAN_SECTION_TRANSFER.md) supplies the remaining regularity clauses for all integral frames of this given marked datum. For a coefficient-trivial frame and a finite list, a cofinite set of possible horizontal places suffices. The [marked-chart comparison](PEL_SIMULTANEOUS_SECTION_VALUATIONS.md) proves the stronger conclusion at any prescribed horizontal place under its geometric centre hypothesis.
