NORMALIZATION CONVENTIONS

The setting is a literal DefPair: either top t j or the rightmost chi i m.
The latter reverses source/target vertex order, negates the source
Hermitian space, and puts complex conjugation outside the period-jet
formula. Mu and core pairs are excluded. The fixed orthonormal rational
frames, normal points u, period delta, un-killed half-point, original
oddE0, actual Weil action and c0 are preserved throughout. c0 is nonzero;
it is not assumed to have modulus one. The period-normalized e0 is not
silently replaced by an L2-normalized vector.

Iop always denotes the positive square-root-normalized finite lattice
sum. CA5's separate identification with signed normalization requires
both ranks even and is NOT applied in this component, whose literal
DefPair setting need not have both ranks even. QD5 proves only the
finite-order composition phase needed here; the original NormSum.Inp
eighth-power assertion remains available under the retained source
inputs, but we do not claim to have newly proved its exact order.

EqAt q means exact integer-denominator inclusions prime to q, in both
directions. It is neither rational span equality nor merely a rank
statement. The output local-unit scalar is the same scalar in every jet,
for every good q, on the same period lattice. The Fourier coefficient
bound uses f=[F:Q], not [E:Q], and includes ramification multiplicities.

CLAIM-BY-CLAIM PROOF NOTES

QD1 — Positive powers and arithmetic embeddings

If z^m=u and m>0, algebraicity of u makes z algebraic. Under every field
embedding into C or C_q the positive real norm satisfies |z|^m=|u|.
Thus norm one is inherited from the positive power. This elementary
step is repeatedly separated from the distinguished complex norm.
For roots of unity the monic polynomial X^m-1 gives integrality over Z.
The source's lamC totalization is harmless after algebraicity has been
proved: the algebraic branch is the actual mapped value.

QD2 — The stronger arithmetic in a finite Hecke value

WA7 already proved qualitative algebraicity. The stronger statement is
obtained by preserving the class-group relation, rather than discarding
its arithmetic structure. Let z be a finite idele. A positive class
number power gives z^h=w b at finite places, where b lies in E^times
and w is a finite integral unit. The continuous character on the compact
profinite unit group has finite image. Indeed a small open subgroup
maps into a no-subgroup arc of the circle, so that subgroup maps to one;
compactness gives finite index. Kill the value of w by a positive power.

At infinity, the actual prescribed ResEta tested against HF9 implies
triviality on positive real factors. A continuous circle character is
an integer power, yielding the source's integer phase exponents m_tau.
Squaring the class relation removes all absolute values:

 chi(z)^(2hM) = product_tau (conj(tau(b))/tau(b))^(M m_tau).

Every embedding of E respects the CM involution as complex conjugation;
hence all complex conjugates of this right side have modulus one. If
z is one at all P above q, valuation in the class relation makes b a unit
at those P. Both b and its inverse then have global integer denominators
prime to q. Every q-adic embedding has norms at most one for both, hence
exactly one. QD1 concludes the required all-embedding assertions. This
does not rely on arbitrary embeddings commuting with analytic limits.

QD3 — Determinant units really require the integral lattice witnesses

For a rational unitary U, det(U) conj(det(U))=1; every complex embedding
has the same equality. For q-adic units, combine the two EqAt comparisons
M~std and UM~std to obtain prime-to-q integer multiples of both U and
U^{-1} with integral entries. Determinants then give two norm inequalities
whose product is one. This argument works for every embedding, including
the CM-conjugated embeddings arising in the restriction-of-scalars
determinant. Conversely, local equalities above q imply the literal EqAt
witnesses by clearing denominators at finitely many other primes. No
free global basis of a projective lattice is needed.

QD4 — An elementary finite Fourier proof of Gauss phase

The source supplies a general finite quadratic phase theorem. The
human proof gives a shorter finite-dimensional argument. After removing
the radical, the bicharacter is perfect. For the normalized Fourier
matrix F and diagonal quadratic-value matrix T, translation of the
Gauss sum gives FTF=(S/sqrt(N))T^{-1}F^{-1}T^{-1}. Therefore
(FT)^3=(S/sqrt(N))I. F^4=I and T has root-of-unity diagonal entries.
Taking determinants makes the unit-modulus scalar S/sqrt(N) a root
of unity. If the quadratic character on the radical is nontrivial the
sum is zero; otherwise reduce to the quotient. No odd-prime restriction,
classification of 2-primary quadratic modules or unproved Galois
isometry assertion is needed.

QD5 — Positive intertwiner composition

All lattices and their semicharacters are represented in one finite
Heisenberg quotient after choosing a small common even sublattice.
Positive-normalized sums are nonzero isometries between the theta lines
by VI5. Their composition scalar has absolute value one. Expand the
trace of three lattice projectors in the finite module: the nonzero
Heisenberg traces are exactly the terms with x+y+z=0. Their coefficient
is the product of the three actual semicharacters and the two actual
Heisenberg cocycles. On that kernel this is a root-valued quadratic
function. QD4 makes its nonzero phase finite order. Positive index and
trace normalizations do not change phase. This proves exactly what
QD6/QD7/QD11 require. The stronger fixed exponent eight is not an
additional conclusion of this shorter proof.

QD6 — Basis scalar square equals root of unity times determinant

The same product theta functions for two rational orthonormal bases
span their respective theta lines, have the same positive mean square,
and are compared by an isometric positive Iop. Thus their comparison
scalar c has distinguished modulus one. This is only the start.

If det(1-G*) is nonzero, expand the reproducing theta kernel and unfold
over a common sublattice. Completing the positive complex Gaussian gives
the exact factor 1/det(1-u) times a positive Haar scale and a finite
quadratic sum. Positivity of Re H((1-u)z,z)=||z-u z||^2/2 justifies
absolute convergence. The quadratic phase is
alpha(x) exp(-pi i Im H((1-u)^{-1}x,x)), on the finite quotient by
(1-u) times the common sublattice. Its polarization is a bicharacter.

On the theta-line side, finite translate orthogonality leaves only the
zero coset, with positive volume. Therefore c det(1-G*) has finite-order
phase. The exact determinant identity conj(d)=(-1)^r det(G)d converts
that fact to c^2=zeta det(G). For possible fixed vectors insert an
orthonormal basis z b with z a rational norm-one element of K avoiding
finitely many eigenvalues. The two determinant-safe comparisons compose
using QD5, and their determinants multiply to det(G). Zero dimension is
handled by the unit scalar. This reconstructs the source's substantive

the norm-one assertion.
