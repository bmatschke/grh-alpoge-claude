# The adelic unitary measures and the split-column comparison

Let $l$ be the fourth-stage column for the chosen first-pass extension and write $D=w.D(Z)$. The host CM datum gives a family of Haar measures on all its adelic Hermitian unitary groups satisfying the required one-vector quotient formula. On the same $D$, the full column of $l$ gives the seven assertions of $\mathrm{ColLay}$ for its own level $l.\mathrm{col.full}.1.\mathrm{base.lev0}$ and its own place data. Both conclusions hold for each such $l$. The integral kernel and its entry on the chosen divisor are additional fields of the built object. ## The measure of a vector of prescribed length

Write $E/F$ for the quadratic CM extension, $\sigma$ for its involution, $V=E^r$ and $G=U(J)$ for a nondegenerate Hermitian matrix. The measure problem ranges over **all** such rational matrices. A step is required when $r\ge3$, $x\in V(F)$ has length $\beta\in F^\times$, and $B$ is a rational frame for $x^\perp$ with Gram matrix $J'=B^*JB$. The stabilizer of $x$ is identified, through $B$, with $H=U(J')$. Put $q(y)=\tfrac12\operatorname{Tr}_{E/F}h(y,y)$ and $B_J(y,z)=\operatorname{Tr}_{E/F}h(y,z)$. The measure on $q^{-1}(\beta)$ uses self-dual measures on $V_v$ for $\psi_v B_J$ and on $F_v$ for $\psi_v(st)$, for every nontrivial continuous unitary character $\psi$ of $\mathbb A_F/F$. [Pairings][pair] · [Products and quotient integrals][fiber] · [CM hypotheses furnished by the host][gg]

Here is a direct simultaneous choice of the group measures. Fix one such additive character $\psi_0$ and write $dt_v$ for its self-dual measure on $F_v$. For a nonzero $F$-rational left-invariant top differential $\omega_G$, form the local measure $|\omega_G|_{dt_v}$ in analytic coordinates. At finite unramified places multiply this by the same factor in every positive rank,
$$
 c_v=(1-\eta_v q_v^{-1})^{-1},
$$
where $q_v$ is the residue cardinality and $\eta_v=1$ at a split place and $-1$ at an inert place. Take $c_v=1$ elsewhere. For fixed $G$, at almost every finite place the rank-$r$ model is smooth with hyperspecial integral points, $\omega_G$ generates its integral top differentials and $dt_v(\mathcal O_v)=1$. Reduction modulo $v$ gives the unscaled integral subgroup mass $q_v^{-r^2}\#G(k_v)=\prod_{i=1}^r(1-\eta_v^i q_v^{-i})$. These are the orders of $\mathrm{GL}_r(k_v)$ and of the finite unitary group, respectively. Thus the scaled masses differ from one by $O_r(q_v^{-2})$. Their product converges absolutely to a positive number. The resulting restricted product of local Haar measures is a Haar measure $dg_J$. Choose the point mass at rank zero. Assign arbitrary measures to degenerate matrices, which carry no gauge condition. Multiplying a top differential by $a\in F^\times$ multiplies the adelic measure by $\prod_v|a|_v=1$. In particular conjugation by a rational change of basis identifies the chosen measures. Feigon–Lapid–Offen give the local gauge-form and quotient conventions, with the full product of local factors. Retaining the first factor suffices for convergence and permits that same factor to cancel at every step. [Feigon–Lapid–Offen, §1.2, p. 195](https://numdam.org/item/10.1007/s10240-012-0040-z.pdf#page=11)

To identify the quotient measure, choose rational linear coordinates $y$ on $V$ and let $A_J$ be the Gram matrix of $B_J$ in these $2r$ coordinates. Write $\psi=\psi_0(a\,\cdot)$ with $a\in F^\times$ by adelic additive duality. Fourier inversion gives the source measure $|a^{2r}\det A_J|_v^{1/2}dt_v^{2r}$ and the line measure $|a|_v^{1/2}dt_v$. The local quotient along the regular fibre therefore differs from the measure of the rational relative form $dy/dq$ by $|a^{2r-1}\det A_J|_v^{1/2}$. Its adelic product is one. The relative form means the form obtained in submersion coordinates by dividing the ambient volume by $dq$. Its integral on a compactly supported continuous test varies continuously near every nonzero value. It agrees there with every disintegration having the continuity required in the definition, by testing against compactly supported functions of the base variable. For existence of a global local-field disintegration one can use the charts away from the singular point and give the exceptional point measure zero in the ambient integral. For the product of the real completions, take the product of these local disintegrations. Thus the differential form determines the actual pointwise fibre density used in the statement. [Uniqueness on regular fibres][reg] · [Self-dual comparison for all characters and Gram matrices][sd]

The map $G\to q^{-1}(\beta)$, $g\mapsto gx$, identifies the latter with $G/H$ as a homogeneous variety. Witt extension gives transitivity at each completion. At almost all finite places the model and fibre are smooth and integral, $\beta$ is a unit, and there is a single orbit on integral points. Indeed finite-field Witt extension gives transitivity after reduction, and smooth lifting of the stabilizer torsor gives integral transport. Consequently the adelic orbit is the full restricted product of these local fibres. Equivalently the pinned orbit result proves its complement null for the product measure. The relative form $dy/dq$ is $G$-invariant, since the $F$-determinant of a unitary change on $V$ is the norm of its $E$-determinant and equals one. Through the quotient, pairing that form with $\omega_H$ gives $\omega_G$ up to a scalar in $F^\times$. Integration in local submersion coordinates now disintegrates $dg_J$ into $dg_{J'}$ and the fibre measure $d\nu_\beta$. The factors $c_v$ cancel and the rational scalar has global modulus one. At almost all places the fibre's integral mass is the ratio of the two hyperspecial masses and equals $1-\eta_v^r q_v^{-r}$. This gives the convergent tail on pure tensors. Compactly supported product tests determine the fibre measure: compact rectangles at finitely many places and lattice indicators outside them generate its Borel sets, and larger compact tests bound its mass on compacts. The definition thus forces this measure for every admissible choice. Finite products followed by monotone convergence on the restricted product give, for nonnegative measurable $f$,
$$
 \int_{G(\mathbb A_F)} f(g)\,dg_J
   =\int_{q^{-1}(\beta)(\mathbb A_F)}
      \int_{H(\mathbb A_F)} f(s(y)h)\,dg_{J'}(h)\,d\nu_\beta(y).
$$
The inner integral is independent of the section by Haar invariance. A Borel section computes it, so the equality also holds for every section in the definition. The stabilizer map satisfying $gB=B g'$ is uniquely determined by this identity. This proves the required compatibility for every permitted frame and character. The pin also constructs the same kind of family by correcting arbitrary Haar measures by basis-invariant scalars and a rank-dependent product of self-dual normalization factors. Both arguments leave the absolute scaling of the group family free to the extent allowed by the quotient law. ## The column at its recorded level

The two type generators $\pi_{g_i}$ come from the base column at level zero. The generator $\pi_T(u)$ and the place below each level label $v$ come from the same word's full Hermitian column. At $g_i$, the first component of the conjugate of $\pi_{g_i}$ in the designated splitting has positive valuation. At $T=T(u)$, $\pi_T(u)$ annihilates the finite level module. These are the level ties in the column. The flag $\mathrm{seenP}(w)$ at $w\mid T$ is equivalent to positive valuation of every recorded embedding of $\pi_T(u)$. Indeed the embeddings in question fix its distinguished quadratic field $K$, and the selected and conjugate primes give complementary choices on $\pi_T$. Its norm is $T$ and $T$ is unramified in $K$ by the defining congruence, which excludes $2$ and the possible ramified prime $d$. On integers of $F$, conjugation is the identity, so a selected prime of $E$ and its conjugate have the same contraction to $F$. This proves the recorded test for membership in the prime below $v$ using exactly $\tau_{w,v}$. The fields are unchanged in the passage from the tied column to its full copy, as the two Hermitian readings agree on the actual object. [Meaning of all four assertions][errdef] · [Generators, seen flag and contracting prime][err] · [Transport to the full column][cc]

Every word and tuple uses the fixed $D_\Gamma(E,R)$, the least common multiple of the orders of automorphism groups of the prescribed unimodular lattices for the standard positive-definite form in the listed ranks. Each such finite group acts faithfully on a rational space of dimension $[E:\mathbb Q]n=2fn\le 2fN(m)$. Adding an identity block embeds it in $\mathrm{GL}_{2fN(m)}(\mathbb Q)$. Hence every integer divisible by the order of every finite subgroup of this general linear group is divisible by $D_\Gamma$. This supplies the common denominator and Minkowski divisibility assertions with the base column's actual value. [Ranks and least common multiple][ranks] · [Rational dimension bound][mink] · [Shared value on all columns][cc]

It remains to compare the two lattices at a coefficient place $w\mid T$. For each level place $v$ of a word, the upward operator $U_v$ sums along the level neighbours with the transported polynomial action. In the split lattice model a neighbour incidence at a reduced functional $g$ is $g(z)=1$ in the finite residue vector space $W$. Right diamond translates permute these incidences, giving commutation with each diamond and preservation of the common kernels of the diamond norms. These joint kernels inside the finite-dimensional forms space are the ramified space $M_{\rm ram}$. At the type primes, residue additive characters have order dividing $g_i$. The fixed coefficient field already contains a primitive $g_i(g_i-1)$-st root and hence all their values. Each of the rational changes of polynomial frame and its level character coefficient therefore preserves the rational polynomial span. Summing over the finite neighbours proves $U_v$ preserves the recorded rational ramified space. [Roots in the recorded field][roots] · [Incidence and diamond commutation][up] · [Rational polynomial span][rat]

Here is the injectivity argument. If $U_v\phi=0$, evaluate near a fixed level representative, indexing the neighbouring pairs by their residue vectors. Set the value to zero outside the image of the pairs. The local level description gives a function $P:W\to M$ with $\sum_{g(z)=1}P(z)=0$ for every nonzero linear functional $g$, by the upward equation. The diamond norm condition gives $\sum_{a\in k^\times}P(az)=0$ for each $z$. Scaling $g$ gives all affine slices $g=t$ for $t\ne0$. Averaging the diamond norm condition on each scalar orbit inside $\ker g$ gives the vanishing sum on the slice $g=0$, and the same average gives $\sum_zP(z)=0$. For fixed $z_0$ sum over all $g\in W^*$ the vanishing equations on $g(z)=g(z_0)$. There are $|W^*|$ functionals contributing to $P(z_0)$ and $|W^*|/|k|$ contributing to each other $P(z)$. Subtract the latter multiple of the total sum. What remains is the positive multiple $|W^*|(1-|k|^{-1})P(z_0)$, so $P(z_0)=0$ over $\mathbb C$. This works at every representative and proves injectivity on $M_{\rm ram}$. [Reduction of pairs to residue vectors][pairs] · [Finite incidence count][radon] · [Application to adelic forms][inj]

Let $A=M_{\rm ram,L}(u)$ be the finitely generated source lattice and identify its rational space with the ramified subspace using $\nu_{\rm in}$. The definition of $N_\lambda(u,w)$ takes the image of the localized level-zero lattice in that subspace under the prescribed product $P_w$ of scaled $U_v$ operators, in its recorded order, raised to $m$. The scaling factors are nonzero powers of $T$, hence $P_w$ is a rational isomorphism. Clear denominators for $P_w\nu_{\rm in}$ and $P_w^{-1}\nu_{\rm in}$ on generators of $A$ inside the full integral level-zero lattice. Their two clearing integers have a positive common multiple $n$. The inverse comparison gives $\nu_{\rm in}(nA)\subseteq N_\lambda(u,w)$. In the forward comparison express any preimage from the localized lattice by clearing a scalar outside $w$. The integral pullback of the level-zero lattice is $A$. Applying the forward bound and cancelling the scalar in the localization gives $n x\in A_w$ whenever $\nu_{\rm in}(x)\in N_\lambda(u,w)$. This is the required two-sided statement. Its integer may depend on the word and coefficient place.
