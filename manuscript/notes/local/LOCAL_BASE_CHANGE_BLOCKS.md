# Reading the lengths of the base-change blocks from split primes

At each finite prime of a CM field $E$ above a split prime of $F=E^+$, a row $\sigma$ prescribes a local parameter of rank $r$. If the row comes from a global parameter, how much of its decomposition can these split primes determine? They determine every cuspidal constituent and the length of its string, including repetitions. This proves the block uniqueness used in the original local construction. The argument uses temperedness over CM fields and strong multiplicity one for general linear groups. [The precise uniqueness statement](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Definitions/AutomorphicMembership/BlocksUnique.lean#L118-L124)

## The parameter one can hear at the split primes

A block $(\pi,d)$ consists of a unitary cuspidal representation $\pi$ of $\mathrm{GL}_a(\mathbb A_E)$ and a positive integer $d$. In Arthur's notation it is $\pi\boxtimes\operatorname{Sym}^{d-1}(\mathbb C^2)$. The weights of the second factor give the centered string
$$
H_d=\{(d-1)/2,(d-3)/2,\ldots,-(d-1)/2\}.
$$
At an unramified place $w$ of norm $q_w$, a root $\alpha$ in the unitary Satake parameter of $\pi_w$ contributes $q_w^h\alpha$ for each $h\in H_d$, in the source's arithmetic Frobenius convention. A list of blocks contributes the multiset union of these roots, of total cardinality $\sum ad=r$. The local construction uses the multiset of pairs $(a,d)$, called the *shape*. [Definition of the strings and their rank](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/LevelLetterCocycles/DataN.lean#L1239-L1250) · [Frobenius convention](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/LevelLetterCocycles/DataN.lean#L546-L552)

**Split-prime rigidity.** Suppose two such lists have the same local parameter at almost all split primes. Assume each $\pi$ is conjugate self-dual and that at every embedding $\tau$ its infinitesimal character consists of distinct weights in $\mathbb Z+(r-d)/2$. Use the CM field and character frame of the original construction. Then the lists agree up to permutation and isomorphism of their cuspidal representations.

First, all their underlying cusp forms are tempered at unramified finite primes. Here the half-integral shift matters. For $\mathrm{GL}_a$ the infinitesimal character of an algebraic coefficient representation has weights in $\mathbb Z+(a-1)/2$. If this differs from the given coset, twist $\pi$ by the frame's unitary character $\eta\circ\det$. At a conjugate pair of embeddings its angular weight one adds $1/2$ and $-1/2$, which both change the required parity and preserve distinctness. Its restriction to $\mathbb A_F^\times$ is the quadratic character of $E/F$, hence $\eta(x)\eta(cx)=\eta(N_{E/F}x)=1$. Thus the twisted cuspidal representation is still conjugate self-dual. In either parity the resulting representation has regular algebraic infinitesimal character. For cuspidal $\mathrm{GL}_a$ over a CM field this gives cohomology with algebraic coefficients by Clozel's archimedean criterion. Caraiani's Theorem 1.2 now gives finite-place temperedness for $a\ge2$. An inverse unitary twist preserves it, and $a=1$ follows directly from unitarity. [The fixed character](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/UnitaryNorms/CharHost.lean#L26-L34) · [Its restriction on finite and infinite components](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/UnitaryNorms/IsUnitaryNormOf.lean#L187-L205) · [Clozel's criterion, as stated by Grobner–Raghuram, Theorem 6.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1102.1872) · [Caraiani, *Local-global compatibility and the action of monodromy on nearby cycles*, Theorem 1.2](https://www.ma.imperial.ac.uk/~acaraian/papers/lgc1.pdf#page=2)

Temperedness gives $|\alpha|=1$. For any $h\in\tfrac12\mathbb Z_{\ge0}$, retain precisely those roots of the full parameter with absolute value $q_w^h$ and divide them by $q_w^h$. The resulting roots, with multiplicity, are the Satake parameter of
$$
\Pi_h=\mathop{\boxplus}_{(\pi,d)\, :\, h\in H_d}\pi .
$$
The symbol $\boxplus$ denotes isobaric sum. If this level is empty, the common root multiset shows it is empty for both lists. Otherwise the two sums have equal rank and isomorphic local components at all but finitely many split primes. Such primes of $E$ exhaust the primes of relative residue degree one over $F$, up to the finite ramification set. Ramakrishnan's Theorem A applies since $E/F$ is quadratic and each $\Pi_h$ is an isobaric sum of unitary cuspidals. It identifies the two global sums, and uniqueness of their cuspidal decompositions gives the multiplicity $m_\pi(h)$ of every $\pi$ at height $h$. [Ramakrishnan, *A Theorem on GL(n) à la Tchebotarev*, Theorem A](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.08429) · [Jacquet and Shalika, *On Euler Products and the Classification of Automorphic Forms II*](https://doi.org/10.2307/2374050)

In this tempered case there is also a short direct justification for using only split primes. Pair $\Pi_h$ with the contragredient of any cuspidal constituent $\pi$ of either list. The pole order at $s=1$ of the incomplete Rankin–Selberg product $L^S(s,\Pi_h\times\pi^\vee)$ is $m_\pi(h)$, where $S$ contains all archimedean and ramified places. The two lists give identical Euler factors at the split primes outside $S$. At an inert prime $w$ over $v$, $q_w=q_v^2$, and temperedness bounds the absolute logarithm series of its Euler factor by $C\sum_{k\ge1}q_v^{-2k\operatorname{Re}s}/k$ for a constant depending only on the ranks. Summing over $v$ converges locally uniformly for $\operatorname{Re}s>1/2$. The omitted inert factors therefore have holomorphic nonvanishing product near $1$. The two pole orders agree. [Rankin–Selberg pole theorem in the required isobaric normalization, Ramakrishnan, *A mild Tchebotarev theorem for GL(n)*, Theorem 1.1](https://www.its.caltech.edu/~dinakar/papers/mildTcheb-JNT.pdf#page=4)

For $d\ge1$, $m_\pi((d-1)/2)$ counts all blocks of $\pi$ whose lengths are $d,d+2,d+4,\ldots$. Consequently the number of blocks of length exactly $d$ is
$$
m_\pi\big((d-1)/2\big)-m_\pi\big((d+1)/2\big).
$$
This proves the assertion, including nested strings and repeated blocks. For instance, lengths $1$ and $3$ for the same $\pi$ give multiplicities $2,1,0$ at heights $0,1,2$. Successive differences recover one copy of each length.

## What this proves for the original construction

For a fixed row, take the shape of any list of the stated rank and infinity type realizing its prescribed local parameters at every split prime. The theorem proves that the answer is independent of the choice. For a row with no such list, the source assigns the singleton $\{(r,1)\}$. Both assignments give positive entries and total weighted rank $r$, since the construction has $r>0$. Whenever a local coefficient factor supplies an actual list, its split parameter agrees with the original row by the automorphic comparison. This list therefore realizes the first case and has the assigned shape. This is exactly the universal implication called `BcTie`. A singleton obtained by the default rule carries no witness for a cuspidal lift. Two representations play different roles here. On the rank-$n_V$ unitary group, `SideA.au` already identifies the ambient labels with isomorphism classes of actual unitary-group automorphic representations and records their weights, multiplicities and local comparisons. The specialization sends each representation selected by the given level and type into this ambient set. Global transfer of every such representation is a separate existence question. On the rank-$r$ row, the block argument above applies to the attached $\mathrm{GL}_a$ labels because `CGTrue` identifies them with genuine unitary cuspidal isomorphism classes and preserves the infinitesimal characters, local parameters and conjugate duality. The actual call that produces the original localized data supplies both this identification and the character frame. Thus the block implication is discharged there without an additional existence theorem for packets. The pinned source reaches the same uniqueness theorem by a different use of the literature. After a common shift $(1-r)/2$ it realizes the characteristic polynomials of the expanded strings in $\ell$-adic representations of regular algebraic polarized cuspidals. Split Frobenius density and continuity extend equality to almost all finite primes. Jacquet–Shalika rigidity then identifies the expanded global cuspidal twists. Unitarity singles out the real twist exponent, and the same count recovers the lengths. Both arguments identify the complete list up to permutation before taking its shape.
