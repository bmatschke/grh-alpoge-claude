RS1. DATA, PERIODIC GEOMETRY AND THE TWO-OCCURRENCE BOUND

Write k=r+K, N=|TT|, and f_{j,i}=fr(j,i), 0<=i<r. Put
z_j=f_{j,r-1}, s_j=f_{j-1,0}, D_j=z_j-s_j. The frames obey
f_{j+J,i}=f_{j,i}+n', increase strictly within each frame, and satisfy
z_j<f_{j+1,0}. Their entries are even selected letters. There are no
other selected letters between successive entries of a frame.

The segment letters are TT lifted to [s_j,z_j]. Its old frame W_j is
the frame j-1. Its late letters are the selected lifts in (z_{j-1},z_j];
these split disjointly into LL_j, the selected lifts in
(z_{j-1},f_{j,0}), and the current frame f_{j,i}.
The late intervals for j=0,...,J-1 tile an interval of length n', so
reduction modulo n' identifies their union bijectively with TT. Hence
products over TT equal products over all those late letters, J<=N,
and every selected residue has a unique late occurrence in one period.

A full segment lies in (z_{j-2},z_j], the union of two consecutive
late intervals. Each of the two families of such intervals tiles a
period. Consequently a fixed residue occurs at most twice in the full
segment-letter sets, counting occurrences, over j=0,...,J-1. This proof
does not assume that every entire segment is shorter than a period.

The literal Hyp further supplies p>=13 prime, even n', r>=2,
a<=deg_t<=2a on TT, |letters_j|<=Ns, Ns<=N1, Ns<=2N,
Ns+r<=K, p^dist(j,x)<=Q, Y>=1, E'_t<=E0, and
  Ck Ns^2 log a <= k,
  128 k^2 Ns Y^2 <= p^(L1-1),
  32 p Y k^2 Ns Q^2 (E0+mStar+1) <= a.
Frame validity also contains the gap and cardinal conditions stated in
SegRing.lean; they are preserved in the theorem, even where this
summation proof uses only the preceding geometric subset.
Nonemptiness of a frame gives Ns>=1. Thus k>=2r+Ns>=5, r<=k, Q>=1,
and a>=32. These elementary consequences will be used below.

RS2. COST OF THE DETERMINANT MULTIPLIER AND THE REMAINING DEGREE

For a late letter x, let y_0 be the oldest of its last r even
predecessors and y_i the other rows of its determinant. The multiplier
exponent is
  sg(j,x)=floor(deg(pos y_0)/(p^dist(j,y_0) k^2 Y)).
As y_i>=y_0, dist(j,y_i)<=dist(j,y_0). Therefore
  sg(j,x) p^dist(j,y_i) <= 2a/(k^2Y).
Its contribution to the physical degree of residue t is zero unless
pos(y_i)=t. The rows y_i are distinct segment letters. For a fixed
segment, each determinant's contributions to t are thus bounded by
2a/(k^2Y) times the number of occurrences of t in that segment.
There are at most Ns late letters per segment. RS1's two-occurrence
bound gives
  Et_t <= 4 Ns a/(k^2Y).                                      (RS2.1)
Off TT every term is zero.

Since a>=32, log a>=1. For Ck>=32, the first size inequality gives
k>=32 Ns^2 log a>=32 Ns; k>=5 and Y>=1 imply
k^2Y>=160 Ns. Hence Et_t<=a/40. The last size inequality implies
E0<=a/32. On TT, the ordinary integers
  d_t=deg_t-E'_t,       a'_t=d_t-Et_t
are nonnegative and agree with the source's natural subtractions.
Indeed E'_t+Et_t< a/10<=deg_t/10, so
  a'_t >= (9/10)deg_t >= (9/10)a,       Et_t<=d_t.             (RS2.2)
Off TT both d_t and Et_t vanish. This proves the complete
TopCountLowerBounds interface, including its all-residue clause.

We also need the sharper Y-dependent total loss. Put
U=8Yk^2Ns. From the last size inequality and Q>=1,
  E0 kY <= a.
From (RS2.1) and Ns<=k,
  Et_t kY <= 4a.
Consequently
  deg_t-a'_t=E'_t+Et_t <= 5a/(kY) <= 5deg_t/(kY).            (RS2.3)

RS3. COARSE SCALES AND ALL REQUIRED NUMERICAL MARGINS

Define T_j=a/(U p^D_j), R_j=log_p(floor(T_j)),
P_j(x)=p^(dist(j,x)+R_j), and
Q_j=p^((z_{j+1}-z_j+R_{j+1}-R_j)_+).
Here log_p is the natural integer logarithm used in the source.
The twist bound p^D_j<=Q and the last size inequality give
  T_j >= 4p(E0+mStar+1)>=1.
Thus the defining floor and integer logarithm give
  p^R_j <= T_j < p^(R_j+1),
  p^R_j > 4(E0+mStar+1) > mStar.                            (RS3.1)
For every letter y in segment j, dist(j,y)<=D_j, so
  U P_j(y) <= a,
  a'_(pos y)/P_j(y) >= (36/5)Yk^2Ns.                       (RS3.2)
In particular kP_j(y)<=a'_(pos y), and a>=32Y.

The scale exponents have a compact exact description. Put
H=log_p(floor(a/U)). Since D_j is a nonnegative integer,
floor((a/U)/p^D_j)=floor(floor(a/U)/p^D_j). If H>=D_j, the
largest power of p below that quotient has exponent H-D_j;
if H<D_j, the quotient is zero and its natural logarithm is zero.
Therefore R_j=(H-D_j)_+, and
  A_j:=z_j+R_j=max(z_j,H+s_j).
Both z_j and s_j increase strictly, so A_{j+1}>A_j. For x<=z_j,
  P_j(x)=p^(A_j-x),       Q_j=p^(A_{j+1}-A_j),
  P_j(x)Q_j=P_{j+1}(x).                                  (RS3.3)
Periodicity gives A_{j+J}=A_j+n', hence
  product_{j=0}^{J-1} Q_j=p^n'=q.                         (RS3.4)
The same geometry gives periodicity of R_j,Q_j and of the old-frame
degrees below. Equal-residue late occurrences differ by simultaneous
shifts j->j+mJ, x->x+mn', so their P_j(x) agree.
This proves the source's uniqP, prodQ and perW clauses, including
removal of the positive-part ambiguity in the exponent of Q_j.

For x=f_{j,i}, apply (RS3.2) in segment j+1 and (RS3.3):
  a'_(pos x)/(P_j(x)Q_j) >= (36/5)Yk^2Ns >= k+r+1.          (RS3.5)
This is the exact margin used by LA to recover all multidegrees.

RS4. ALL DEGREE FAMILIES ARE ADMISSIBLE (RESTPAD)

An admissible label at a selected residue t is a vector
lambda_t=(lambda_{t,1},...,lambda_{t,k}) with
0<=lambda_{t,c}<P, L_t=sum_c lambda_{t,c}<=a'_t, and
L_t congruent a'_t modulo P. RS3 makes P independent of the chosen
late representative of t. Put n_j(x)=(a'_(pos x)-L_(pos x))/P_j(x),
an exact nonnegative integer. Then
  a'_t/P_j(x)-k < n_j(x) <= a'_t/P_j(x).                   (RS4.1)
These are exactly the source's AdmLL inequalities for x in LL_j.

For each current-frame position choose 0<=e_{j,i}<rQ_j with
e_{j,i} congruent n_j(f_{j,i}) modulo Q_j. The actual degree family is
  b_j(x)=n_j(x)                      for x in LL_j;
  b_j(f_{j,i})=e_{j,i};
  b_{j+1}(f_{j,i})=(n_j(f_{j,i})-e_{j,i})_+/Q_j;
and zero off the segment. Define
  Bw_j(y)=floor(a'_(pos y)/P_j(y))-(k+r+1).

Here is a shorter floor lemma than the source's arithmetic proof.
For n,s,e>=0, P,Q>=1, s<kP and e<rQ, set
  A=floor(n/(PQ)), U0=floor((n-s)_+/P),
  W0=floor((U0-e)_+/Q).
Then U0>=AQ-k, whence
  W0 >= A-r-ceil(k/Q) >= A-r-k.
For the first inequality, floor((n-s)/P)>=floor(n/P)-k
and truncating a negative numerator can only increase the result.
Apply the same observation at scale Q and use
floor((AQ-k)/Q)=A-ceil(k/Q). This proves in particular the source's
slightly weaker W0>=A-(k+r+1).

Apply it with n=a'_t, s=L_t, P=P_j(f_{j,i}), Q=Q_j and
e=e_{j,i}; equation (RS3.3) identifies PQ with P_{j+1}(f_{j,i}).
Thus b_{j+1}(f_{j,i})>=Bw_{j+1}(f_{j,i}). Together with
(RS4.1) this is RestPad for every admissible lambda,e and every
integer segment index (the indices are extended periodically).

For clarity, there is no hidden cancellation of truncated subtraction:
by (RS3.5) and L_t<kP,
  n_j(f_{j,i})>Q_j(k+r+1)-k>=rQ_j>e_{j,i}.
Consequently n-e>=0 and the defining congruence gives the exact
identity e+Q_j*((n-e)/Q_j)=n. This also supplies LA's degree step.

RS5. DIGIT SUMS AND THEIR EXACT COMBINATORIAL MEANING

Let B_k(n)=binom(n+k-1,k-1). It counts k-tuples of nonnegative
integers with sum n. The unique decomposition w_c=lambda_c+Pv_c
gives the exact identity
  B_k(n)=sum_{0<=lambda_c<P, L<=n, L congruent n mod P}
             B_k((n-L)/P).                               (RS5.1)
Thus any larger admissible label set gives the corresponding lower
bound. When kP<=n, every residue vector satisfying the sum congruence
automatically has sum<n. There are exactly P^(k-1) such vectors,
because any k-1 coordinates determine the last modulo P.

For the source's label set at t, all late representatives impose the
same P by RS3. Its global label set LA is a product over t. Define
  H_t(lambda_t)=product_{j,x in LL_j, pos x=t}
                     B_k((a'_t-L_t)/P_j(x)),
  S_t=sum_{lambda_t admissible} H_t(lambda_t).
Every factor is >=1. If t has an LL occurrence, (RS5.1) gives
S_t>=B_k(a'_t). If t is a frame residue represented by f_{j,i},
the label count and (RS3.2) give S_t>=P_j(f_{j,i})^(k-1).
If t is not selected, the zero label shows S_t>=1.
Finite distributivity gives exactly
  sum_lambda product_{j,x in LL_j} B_k(n_j(x))=product_t S_t. (RS5.2)
The source uses inequalities to tolerate extraneous allowed labels;
the unique late representative explains why the basic digit identity
is itself an equality. No equidistribution or primality is needed here.

RS6. THE TWO BINOMIAL COMPARISONS

The general elementary estimate is as follows. For nonnegative integers
x,y,M, if 0<=theta<=1, s>=1 and
s(x+1+l)>=(y+1+l)(1-theta) for 0<=l<M, then
  s^M binom(x+M,M)>=binom(y+M,M)(1-M theta).               (RS6.1)
Multiply the M inequalities, divide by M!, and apply
(1-theta)^M>=1-M theta. This is the source's factorial/product argument.

Take M=k-1. First put x=a'_t, y=deg_t, s=1 and
theta=5/(kY). RS2.3 gives a'_t>=deg_t(1-theta), so the
individual inequalities follow; the added positive l+1 only helps.
Since M theta<=5/Y,
  B_k(a'_t)>=B_k(deg_t)(1-5/Y).                           (RS6.2)
This remains valid when the right side is nonpositive.

For the frame comparison, let n=a'_t, P'=P_j(f_{j,i})Q_j,
m0=k Ns+1, and b=(floor(n/P')-(k+r+1)-m0)_+.
The floor estimate and k>=5,r<=k,Ns>=1 give
  P'b >= n-4kNs P',
because k+r+3+kNs<=4kNs. By RS3.2 in segment j+1,
n/P'>=(36/5)Yk^2Ns>=4Yk^2Ns. Thus the deficit is at most
n/(kY). Apply (RS6.1) with x=b,y=n,s=P',theta=1/(kY):
  (P')^(k-1)B_k(b)>=B_k(a'_t)(1-1/Y).                    (RS6.3)
These are the full compA and compW clauses of SumFacts.

RS7. SUMMING THE ACTUAL SEGMENT FAMILIES

Let alpha=1-a^(-Ns^2), tau=max(0,1-r/(2Y)), and
  W_j=product_{i<r} B_k((Bw_j(f_{j-1,i})-m0)_+),
  T_j^*= [Q_j^(r-1) tau]^r.
The source's segment lower bound is
  segBound_j(b)=alpha W_j T_j^* product_{x in LL_j} B_k(b(x)).

Assume functions G_j(b) are nonnegative, depend only on the degrees
on the late letters, and their sum over every permitted terminal-frame
residue class is at least segBound_j(b) when b satisfies AdmLL.
These are exactly the hypotheses of RestSum; no dimension statement
is being assumed in this paragraph.

For a fixed lambda, the EE choices factor independently over j.
On late letters b_j agrees with n_j on LL_j and has terminal values
e_j, so the late-degree dependence of G_j gives
  sum_e product_j G_j(b_j(lambda,e))
    =product_j sum_{e_j} G_j(setTau_j(n_j,e_j))
    >=product_j segBound_j(n_j).                          (RS7.1)
All factors are nonnegative once the final target is positive, as
checked in RS8. RS4 provides AdmLL. Sum (RS7.1) over lambda and use
(RS5.2), obtaining
  total >= alpha^J product_j W_j T_j^* product_t S_t.      (RS7.2)

The frame periodicity permits replacing product_j W_j by
product_j W_(j+1). Use RS1's disjoint tiling and RS5's lower bounds
for S_t. Multiplication by q^(rK), with q=product_j Q_j, assigns
a factor Q_j^K to each of the r frame entries of segment j.
At a frame residue t=pos f_{j,i}, the assembled contribution is
  B_k((Bw_(j+1)(f_{j,i})-m0)_+) * Q_j^(r-1)tau
      * P_j(f_{j,i})^(k-1) * Q_j^K
  =tau (P_j(f_{j,i})Q_j)^(k-1)
      B_k((Bw_(j+1)(f_{j,i})-m0)_+),
since (r-1)+K=k-1. Equations RS6.2–RS6.3 bound this below by
  (1-5/Y)(1-1/Y)tau B_k(deg_t).
At each LL residue the same bound follows from RS6.2, because the
other two factors lie in [0,1]. Therefore, putting
eta=(1-5/Y)(1-1/Y)(1-r/(2Y)),
  q^(rK) total >= alpha^J eta^N product_{t in TT} B_k(deg_t). (RS7.3)
This explains the q^(-rK) loss: it is the exact exponent needed to
complete the frame dimension factors, not an unproved heuristic count.

RS8. FINAL LOSS, FULL RESTSUM AND ITS DEPENDENCY BOUNDARY

If 1-Nr^2k/Y<=0, the target is nonpositive and total>=0 proves
RestSum immediately. Suppose it is positive. Then Y>Nr^2k,
N>=J>=1,r>=2,k>=5, so every factor of eta lies in [0,1],
tau=1-r/(2Y), and alpha lies in [0,1]. RS3 gives a>=32Y;
as Ns>=1, alpha>=1-1/(32Y).

The elementary loss estimate already proved as SD7 now applies with
all its actual hypotheses discharged:
  alpha^J eta^N >= 1-Nr^2k/Y.
For completeness, product(1-x)>=1-sum x gives
alpha eta>=1-(7+r/2)/Y; alpha^J>=alpha^N as J<=N.
Bernoulli then gives (alpha eta)^N>=1-N(7+r/2)/Y,
which is at least 1-Nr^2k/Y. The weaker coefficient 7 absorbs
the exact loss 6+1/32. Combining with RS7.3 proves
  total >= q^(-rK)(1-Nr^2k/Y) product_{t in TT}B_k(deg_t).

This is RestSum 1088 1. Together with RS4's RestPad, an actual
