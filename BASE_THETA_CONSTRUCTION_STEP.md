# Choosing the level and the real representations for a theta family

The first step towards constructing the base theta lifts is to put their finite level, coefficient representations and archimedean discrete series on one arithmetic datum. Fixing them together matters. For example, the projector selecting the good automorphic representations has to act on the finite residue groups defined by the very lattices on which the Hecke operators act. At this first step one can make all these choices before constructing a single theta integral.

**Fixed-stage theorem.** Take stages and a column satisfying the field and column laws of the original construction. Fix a finite conjugation-stable set of coefficient places and the specified twist family whose set of characters agrees with the column's. Assume the prescribed choice of halves at the ramified twist primes, the marked-group identification and projector conditions, and the original coefficient values and roots of unity for the twists. Retain the host hypothesis, the standard lattices for the spaces $Y_j,V_j,W_j,X'$, in their specified index ranges, and the definiteness assumption on the $V_j$. Fix a number field $K_s'$ inside the large coefficient field. Its image must contain the specified small-field elements, the image of the CM field, and the values of every admissible choice of the untwisted level characters on ideles with trivial infinite component. For the local real representations use the Harish-Chandra existence theorem in the convention verified in v0.90. Then the stage admits the kept preliminary real datum $R_0$ of `exists_realDat_keep`. In particular the two Hecke realizations, the selected tuples, the marked finite characters and projectors, and the archimedean frames all agree on this one datum. ## Fix the level before choosing the forms

Use the tuple's character from the column, the column's finite support at split primes, and the untwisted character at the base tuple. A finite-order idele character is unitary, and a tame character on local units descends to the residue units. Thus both the unitary characters and their finite level characters are defined on these very places. For a tuple with chosen set of primes $Q$, the ramification set is $Q$ together with its complex conjugate. Among all chosen primes over any one rational prime, their contractions to the imaginary quadratic field are the same. This gives the common choice of a prime in that field required in the level construction. The exclusion hypothesis on the twist primes and the host identity $\mathfrak c_K=(1)$ give the required coprimality with the fixed bad factors. Assign to each designated coefficient place its given twist and assign the base tuple elsewhere. Injectivity of the column's character assignment determines its tuple uniquely. Conjugate coefficient places have the same character, and each used tuple other than the base is attained at a designated place. Take the entire image of the designated coefficient places $W_t$ as the finite twist set. The prescribed tuple is the base off this set. At every place in the set, the original twist family supplies its auxiliary character and local prime data. [Level characters][level] · [Tuple map and its local identities][tupl] · [Lattice and support construction][frames]

Keep the coordinate lattice and identity frame on $X'$ and every $V_j$. On the $Y_j,W_j$, use the simultaneous normal frames if they exist, and otherwise take the coordinate lattices and identity frames. Standardness of the given lattices ensures the self-duality required in both cases. The extra conclusion about normal frames is conditional on their existence, exactly as required. [Choice of frames][frames]

Let $G$ be the product of the groups $\mathrm{GL}_r(k_P)$ at the marked primes. The marked-group hypothesis identifies the prime indices place by place. At a prime of norm $g_k$ the residue is the prime field $\mathbb F_{g_k}$, so this gives the requisite group isomorphism by reindexing and coefficient reduction. Pull the designated central idempotent $e$ back to $\mathbb C[G]$. On each irreducible representation it acts by $0$ or $1$. Take $X_g$ to be the irreducible characters on which it acts by $1$. With $[g]$ denoting the group basis, character orthogonality gives the exact projector
$$
 e=\sum_{\chi\in X_g}\frac{\chi(1)}{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}\chi(g^{-1})[g].
$$
The small-field data contain a primitive $|G|$th root of unity. Brauer's splitting-field theorem therefore realizes each selected complex character by an absolutely irreducible representation over this cyclotomic subfield. This gives the character set required over the coefficient field. Each $\chi(g)$ is the sum of roots of unity of order dividing $|G|$, so all the coefficients in this formula have the prescribed small-field values. The base unipotent character is a product of residue additive characters and its values likewise belong to the small field, which contains the roots of the marked prime orders. At the first level the local subgroup and character are transported factor by factor. At the symmetric level the change of block order conjugates the corresponding subgroups. The same permutation carries each entry in the defining character sum to its designated position. The product of local characters therefore agrees on both presentations of each level. This identifies the actual idempotent, both level subgroups and their characters in the same group coordinates. [R. Brauer, *Amer. J. Math.* 67 (1945), 461–471](https://www.jstor.org/stable/2371973) · [Finite groups][groups] · [Character expansion and field][projector] · [Two level characters][levels] · [Generic character compatibility][psip]

## The weights give all the discrete series at once

At each embedding, write $b_i,e_i,v_a$ for the column's weights and $t$ for the odd string length. At step $0\le j\le m$, set $s_j=-1/2$ for odd $j$ and $s_j=0$ for even $j$. Before taking the dual parameter the coordinates are
$$
\begin{aligned}
v_a+s_j &&(0\le a< k-1),\\
-b_i+s_j &&(0\le i<m),\\
-e_i+(t-1)/2-h+s_j &&(0\le i<j,\ 0\le h<t).
\end{aligned}
$$
There are $r+jt$ coordinates. The gaps between $b_i$ and $e_i$ and between $e_i$ and the next $b_{i+1}$ are at least $(t+3)/2$. Thus each string lies strictly between the neighboring $-b$ coordinates wherever both neighbors occur, distinct strings are disjoint, and the inequalities on $v_a$ keep those entries separate. The $v_a$ themselves are strictly ordered. Since $r,t$ are odd, the shifted entries lie in $\mathbb Z+(r+jt-1)/2$, as the unitary-group convention requires. Only a $b$ entry can carry a negative sign, at indices $i>j$ with $i-j$ odd; there are $\lfloor(m-j)/2\rfloor$ such indices. This is the prescribed negative signature. Passing to the contragredient preserves admissibility. At $j=m$ the form is definite and the same parameter works on $X'$. The Harish-Chandra classification therefore supplies all the $Y_j$ models and the terminal model. The source also indexes $Y$ by arbitrary natural numbers, using the stage $\min(j,m)$ at index $j$. [Weight calculation and signatures][weights] · [Application to the terminal space][terminal] · [Classification in the source convention](continuation_v90/discrete.tex#L132-L221) · [H. He, *On the Gan–Gross–Prasad conjecture for $U(p,q)$*, §1](https://www.math.lsu.edu/~hongyu/pub/ggp_hhe_final.pdf)

There is a separate field issue for $W_i$. At an embedding $\sigma$ the relevant parameter is a prescribed holomorphic parameter or its dual, according to the CM type. Its coordinates satisfy the same regularity, parity and signature tests for $W_i$. Choose one model for each resulting parameter on $W_i$. Embeddings agreeing on $K_s'$ agree on the embedded CM field, so they yield the same labels and parameter, and hence literally the same model and frame matrix under this choice. At any embedding with the prescribed CM labels, the positive members of the holomorphic parameter come first. The distinguished embedding has these labels. This proves the required sign order and the equality of the frame matrices over embeddings agreeing on $K_s'$. [Model choice by parameter][wframe] · [The two precise frame assertions][wfield]

## Completing the preliminary datum

If $a$ is an infinite adele of the totally real field and $x$ is in the CM field, send $a\otimes x$ to $a_v\tau(x)$ at the real place $v$ under the chosen CM embedding $\tau$. These evaluations give the continuous archimedean maps specifying the coefficient family. On the definite spaces use the scalar extensions of the column's algebraic highest-weight coefficient modules in the given frames. Their Gram pairings are induced by the Hermitian forms, with the sign chosen to make the definite form positive. At $X'$ the highest weight is contragredient to the given terminal parameter, so there is a nonzero invariant evaluation pairing with the terminal discrete series. Its Hermitian square is a positive scalar multiple of the coefficient inner product by irreducibility. Divide the pairing by the positive square root of that scalar. This fixes its required norm and leaves the rational coefficient maps as chosen. In these coordinates H78K1–6 give both readings by evaluation on the marked lattices. They intertwine the specified split and inert Hecke operators and the finite flag action. Use the standard additive character of sign $-1$. In the same group coordinates its restriction gives the prescribed character at both marked primes. Install these models, characters, frames, readings and projector on a single datum, using the identity on functions of the inert Hecke data as the initial trace assignment at each chain index. The preliminary conditions allow zero for the later theta maps and unit phase. The discrete-series and coefficient models and their terminal pairing already have their prescribed nontrivial realizations. The level, all-place identifications, tuple and Hecke conditions hold by construction. The retained conditions consist of the character and its signs, the conditional frame normalization, the identity frames on the definite spaces, the normalized terminal pairing, and the group and level comparisons above. Their remaining field conditions are the assumed values of the untwisted characters, the common $W$ frames, and the small-field values of the projector and finite characters. This accounts for every conclusion of the fixed-stage theorem. [Construction of the preliminary object][seed] · [Complete list of retained conditions][keep] · [Final assembly][assemble]

**Where the proof continues.** This construction applies to each *given* stage satisfying the displayed hypotheses. The [coefficient-enlargement proof](THETA_COEFFICIENT_EXTENSION.md) starts with a stage already carrying the earlier analytic data and constructs compatible choices over an enlarged coefficient field. It must preserve the additive character, lattices and finite level, the framed $W_i$ representations at every extending embedding, the evaluations and integral lattices on corresponding coefficient vectors, and the reading of the base tuple. The source gives the full conditions in [the definition of `RSideW`](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/IwasawaDecomposition/CrdOver.lean#L25-L73). To obtain the final geometric record one still needs the weak analytic kernel construction, the interior intertwiner before each twist, and the theta multiplicity maps for that same intertwiner. The two-pass family construction must produce the original stages in the required order. These are the steps at which existence and uniformity enter the two open theta obligations.
