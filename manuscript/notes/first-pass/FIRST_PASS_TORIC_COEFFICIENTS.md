# The toric coefficients at the first series extension

Fix the intermediate row, its character grid $G$, and the chosen twist rings. At residue characteristic $\ell$ the Galois character takes values in the units of the ring of integers $O_0$ of the prescribed finite local extension. The normalized toric sums take values in the fraction field of the larger prescribed complete discrete valuation ring $O$. The input named `hratio` proves closure under valuation-bounded division for the local ring. The toric reciprocity input is `hSer.ratio` on the following line. ## The characters in the fixed ring

Take $j,a\in\{0,\ldots,m-1\}$, $1\le s\le N_{\rm fun}+1$, and a choice $b$ of complex reading. The character and the orders of differentiation are
$$
\lambda=\mu^0_j\,\delta_\ell\,\chi_a^{-1},\qquad
k_\tau=\lfloor e_j(\tau)\rfloor+c_a(\tau)-s .
$$
Here $\delta_\ell$ uses the distinguished ramification prime different from $\ell$. The strict grid bound and integrality of $e_j$ give $k_\tau\ge1$. The conjugates of the selected host embeddings form a CM type and restrict to one embedding of the imaginary quadratic field $K$. The character is unitary and trivial on the real subfield. The base order conductor is $\mathfrak f=(g_0g_1p_\delta p'_\delta)^2\,\mathfrak p_{w_0}$, with $\mathfrak p_{w_0}$ the prime below $w_0$. The local conductor bounds and the choice of the reference prime place all primes over $\mathfrak f$ in the excluded set $S_0(i)$. Choose $\kappa:\operatorname{Frac}(O_0)\hookrightarrow\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell$ over $\mathbf Q_\ell$. The ring of integers embeds over $\mathbf Z_\ell$, and the embedding extends to fractions. Choose a field isomorphism $\iota:\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell\simeq\mathbf C$ giving the prescribed complex reading on $L_0$ through $\kappa$. This is possible by extending an isomorphism of countable subfields by transcendence bases. Use $\iota$ on the true side and its complex conjugate on the false side. Outside $S_0(i)$ the frame supplies values of $\mu^0_j$ and $\delta_\ell$ in the image of $L_0$, and the field condition on the grid supplies those of $\chi_a$. The algebraic Hecke character has a continuous $\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell^\times$-valued Galois character by Artin reciprocity. Under the selected reading its unramified Frobenius values are the inverses of the Hecke values. They lie in the closed subgroup $\kappa(\operatorname{Frac}(O_0)^\times)$ away from finitely many primes. The quotient character is trivial by Frobenius determination. ## The Galois action on normalized sums

For an admissible set $Q$, let $C$ be the order class group for $\mathfrak f_Q=\mathfrak f\prod_{v\in\operatorname{below}(Q)}v$. Write $T_q$ for the analytic class terms. The trivial-character toric sum $T_0$ at the base conductor equals $L(s,\lambda)$ in the unitary normalization. It is nonzero by the Euler product for $s>1$ and Hecke nonvanishing at $s=1$, the nonzero infinity type supplying the entire Hecke function used at that endpoint. For $j_K:\overline K\hookrightarrow\mathbf C$ compatible with the CM type, the ratio law asserts the existence of algebraic numbers $\rho(\psi)$ satisfying
$$
j_K(\rho(\psi))=T_0^{-1}\sum_{q\in C}j_K(\psi(q))^{-1}T_q .
$$
Let $g\in G_K$ fix all images of the CM embeddings of $E$ and all values $\lambda_{\rm fin}(y)|y|^s$. Take $t$ with $\operatorname{Art}_K(t)=(g^{-1})^{\rm ab}$ and let $c_t$ be the class of its extension to $E$. Then
$$
g\rho(\psi)=(g\psi)(c_t)^{-1}\rho(g\psi).
$$
The automorphism can permute the finite characters in this identity. To see the origin of the scalar, normalize a toric sum by $A_\Omega(T)=C_0(E,d,2s,k)T/\Omega^{2s|\Phi|+2\sum_\tau k_\tau}$. Take the elliptic lattice of $K$ with $\Omega^{12}$ equal to its discriminant. Its normalized lattice evaluations are algebraic. Elliptic CM transports its period and torsion coordinates to a second lattice with period $\Omega'$. The translated summand for $q$ belongs to $q c_t^{-1}$. Changing representatives preserves its finite-character normalization. Reindexing the sum for $T_\psi=\sum_q\psi(q)^{-1}T_q$ in the complex reading gives
$$
g A_\Omega(T_\psi)=A_{\Omega'}\!\left(
\frac{|t|^{-\sum_\tau k_\tau}}{\lambda_{\rm fin}(\operatorname{up}t)|\operatorname{up}t|^s}
(g\psi)(c_t)^{-1}T_{g\psi}\right).
$$
The calculation at the base conductor with the trivial character has the identical scalar multiplying $T_0$, except that the class-character factor is one. Division cancels the common scalar, periods and $C_0$. Uniqueness identifies the sums with their prescribed analytic representatives. Extend $g$ from $\overline K$ to a field automorphism of $\mathbf C$ and then restrict the identity back, using algebraicity of the normalized ratios and of the data required to be fixed. ### The precise partial-zeta theorem needed here

For the totally real field $F$ of degree $n$ and absolute discriminant $D_F$, both lattice arguments use rationality of $\zeta_C(1-2s)$ for every ordinary ideal class $C$ and every relevant integer $s\ge1$. Siegel proves, for $k=2s$, that
$$
 A_C=\pi^{-kn}D_F^{k-1/2}\zeta_C(k)\in\mathbf Q .
$$
In the project's convention the class paired with $C$ in the functional equation is $C'=[\mathfrak d_F]C^{-1}$. With $\Gamma_{\mathbf R}(u)=\pi^{-u/2}\Gamma(u/2)$, the factorial formula for the gamma function gives
$$
 \frac{\Gamma_{\mathbf R}(2s)}{\Gamma_{\mathbf R}(1-2s)}
 =\pi^{-2s}\,\frac{2(2s-1)!}{(-4)^s},
 \qquad
 \zeta_{C'}(1-2s)=
 \left(\frac{2(2s-1)!}{(-4)^s}\right)^n A_C .
$$
The involution $C\mapsto C'$ covers all ordinary ideal classes. This proves exactly the rationality input used for these toric ratios. Deligne and Ribet also give congruences that control denominators elsewhere in the construction. The local Fourier inversion below uses only the rationality just proved and the stated CM transport. ## Descent to the prescribed fraction field

Put $M=\operatorname{Frac}(O)$ and $\Delta=\mathrm{RCG}(i,Q)$. Global reciprocity gives $\pi:C\to\Delta$, killing principals and order units. Characters of $\Delta$ are unramified above $\ell$ since $Q$ avoids $S_0(i)$. Hence $\pi$ kills finite ideles supported on units above $\ell$. Put
$$
P_d=T_0^{-1}\sum_{\pi(q^{-1})=d}T_q,\qquad
z_d=\frac{1}{|\widehat\Delta|}\sum_{r\in\widehat\Delta}r(d)^{-1}\rho(r\circ\pi).
$$
Then $j_K(z_d)=P_d$. The coefficient of $T_q/T_0$ is the character average at $d^{-1}\pi(q^{-1})$, equal to one precisely on the displayed fibre.
