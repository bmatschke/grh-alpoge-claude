# From two member constructions to the original design window

The functional and covolume implications invoked below are now supplied on this extension. See [the completed common-family argument](../geometry/COMMON_FAMILY_AND_PERIOD_HEIGHT.md#supplying-the-joint-construction).

The design obligation asks for one family above a specified host request, with its original selector and all translated points. The RH Lean Roadmap has a theorem in exactly this form. Its Theorem B8.07 provides the two member constructions under its automorphic hypotheses and its separating rational test theorem. Its Theorem B8.05 then constructs the family with the required parameter order. Here we identify the output of that construction with the individual clauses of the original design request, including the extra smoothness condition and the constant for the word primes. This gives a route to the common-family obligation without comparing a family produced in one exposition with a family chosen independently in another. ## The two passes and the one selector

Use the strong origin extension with the extra predicate on the base, frame and twist. Its norm-choice extension records the original grid, its depth, primitive roots, signed characters and a lift from the same starting state. The extra predicate must hold eventually on bases in the specified order. Write $Q$ for constructed membership, $Q'$ for good membership and $L_k$ for the corresponding point-to-bundle relation. The field in the base condition is the specified embedded compositum, and the terminal map preserves the individual frame vectors. These are the choices of the original two-pass construction. [Extension parameters and dependency of requests][params] · [Two-pass construction in this repository](../../../archive/rounds/v78/continuation_v78/producer.tex#L219-L275)

Four steps suffice on this extension. Choose a preliminary base and frame before the two finite field requests. On an eligible untwisted base with the required primes, construct a $Q$-member. Export the norm and theta data from this very member. Enlarge the same frame and prime set and construct a $Q,Q'$-member there with the specified twists. In the fourth step, first unite the new avoidances and impose these on the ray primes. For each residue characteristic impose the bounds for all permitted signed powers on the same ray character. Only after its character set has been chosen do we evaluate the request for the final field, which is allowed to depend on those characters. Adjoin this request and the transport coefficients together. Pulling back the places along the enlargement keeps the selected rational primes and their avoidance. For every newly selected place the two readings on the finite cyclic root group differ by a unit power, already covered by the choice of character. [Four requirements][four] · [Order of ray and coefficient choices][ray] · [Integral orders on a supplied localization](../second-pass/SECOND_PASS_GALOIS_ORDERS.md)

For the required bundle properties let $Q\Rightarrow_{KP}R$ express that all constructed members over eligible base frames satisfy $R$ beyond the applicable thresholds. A finite collection of such strengthenings can be met by intersecting them. Take each maximum of thresholds after fixing the earlier parameters on which it depends. Unite the finite field requests and prime sets at the argument where each is evaluated. Intersect the strengthening needed for bundle existence with the one needed for the member rows. A point satisfying $Q^*=Q'\land R$ now has a linked bundle at every real size, and the row conclusion applies to that bundle. For a linked parameter $\kappa$ the construction class asks for this property for each size at every indexed point. Choose one bundle at the family's size constant. Its witness has all the row predicates in the intersection. This identifies the selector used on the target subclass with the restriction of the original selector. ## Keeping the host and the parameter thresholds

Fix the host request $G$, window exponent $\lambda>1$ and an arbitrary requested threshold record $\mathcal K$. Take sufficiently small $\varepsilon$ in the host and construction ranges. The host constants and the unbounded set of windows precede the choice of individual hosts. The word-prime parameter in the final family is
$$
 C_{Tu}=c_T(\varepsilon,9)=\max(11,16+3\max(5,10))=46 .
$$
The preliminary outer list has $C_{YL}=9$ and $C_{Tu}=25$. Evaluate the **requested** middle- and last-threshold functions after replacing the latter entry by $46$. Combine this modified request with the construction and weight requirements at the preliminary parameters. Choose the middle entries, including $C_\Gamma$ and $C_F=C_H$, from the resulting middle bound. Choose the final entries with that entire middle list in hand. Realizing the model with the preliminary list and then changing its $C_{Tu}$ field to $46$ gives the original requested bounds by construction. The change preserves the scale, the index set, the size, the objects and the selected bundle predicate. This substitution also explains why increasing a level constant only after testing an arbitrary threshold function would lack justification. [Choice of the lists][lists] · [Final substitution][reset]

At each sufficiently large window scale use a single host, pair of weight functions and injective word code for all translates. Construct each translated point on its prescribed levels by the two passes. The construction cutoff precedes the choices of these weights and translates. The top set, raw host, conductor and arithmetic rank are the ones at which $G$ was tested. Choose the family cutoff above the host, point and numerical cutoffs, and a reference scale above it. The same family then contains arbitrarily late full windows and satisfies $G$ throughout its indexed points beyond that cutoff. Intersecting the class with this target condition preserves the original selector. [Construction of the individual points][points] · [Assembly of the translates][windows]

## What the member construction uses before selection

B8.07 is a member-existence theorem under $P$, B8.18, the weak local correspondence and the eventual base condition on its extension. The native rationality input has the form “for every stage satisfying these guards, there are separating rational tests.” B8.18 is used precisely in this direction. Its native application constructs the universal implication from separation, the two-model theta identity, local matching and the background representation results. An inhabited stage is needed at a different step, when constructing an actual member. [Native universal test][guard] · [Conversion and use of the test][use] · [Preselection tests and stage hypotheses][tests] · [Roadmap's construction of a member][advance]

On the admissible hosts, Jin gives this intermediate existence separately. The actual type and word norms fix the tuple of norm generators at the marked level. Start there with no active localization, $\eta_{j,v}=0$ and $\eta_F=6x^{-C_F}$. The original measures, finite group projector and integral lattices give the bare copy of the starting row. Before the supported row is chosen, request the finite coefficient field for the tests and retain the same reference tuple and measures through the comparison. The ranks in the auxiliary tower are $r+1+i(r+5)$ and the relevant Witt index is at most $\lfloor(m-i)/2\rfloor<r+5$. The convergent Siegel–Weil formula with probability Haar applies there. The real tests are chosen on that grid before the tuple and the input. Their joint kernel is zero and their normalized theta values lie in the embedded coefficient field at every allowed tuple. These steps take place on the actual preliminary data under Jin’s stated trace, theta, Galois and geometric inputs. Postselection rational tests are unnecessary for them. [Jin's first state][jstart] · [Realization of its tuples][jnorms] · [Bare-level test on both readings][jbare] · [Convergent integral and order of choices][jtests]

The row strengthening in B8.05 is a separate dependency. The application in B8.04 first fixes the counting constants and then intersects the 26 rows of B8.06 with the word-prime row. It uses the same conjunction and bundle size as X87S4, so the argument above applies to those witnesses themselves. In this application B8.06 takes the functional comparison of B8.23 and the geometric covolume comparison of B8.34 on the augmented extension *before* constructing the class. The [functional and geometric comparison](../geometry/COMMON_FAMILY_AND_PERIOD_HEIGHT.md) supplies these rows on that extension. With those rows and the other inputs in B8.06 verified there, B8.07 and B8.05 produce the original class and selector. The simpler application of B8.07 alone already supplies members and all-size bundles at the prescribed hosts under its hypotheses. Applying a period estimate obtained only after selecting a family would reverse the order of the stronger construction.
