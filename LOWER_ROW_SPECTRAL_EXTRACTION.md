# Extracting lower-row multiplicities from matched cuspidal traces

Fix the actual lower row $V=Y_0$, its target $V'=W_i$, an irreducible admissible good finite-adelic representation $\sigma$, and the two archimedean models $M,M'$ in the original datum. Use the [marked-type construction](LOWER_ROW_MARKED_TYPE.md) for $V$, and the finite adelic identification $\Phi_0$ induced by the lattice-compatible local isometries $j_v$. The proposition below concerns an individual finite representation and an individual member of the archimedean packet, including the possibility of zero multiplicity. [Source reduction and weights](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/TraceEqXOfGeomExpansionX.lean#L493-L553)

**Spectral extraction.** Assume equality of the two **cuspidal** traces for the corresponding archimedean pseudo-coefficients and every matched finite test specified below, with the finite Haar factors pushed forward under $j_v$. Then
$$
m_{\rm cusp}(\sigma,M;V)=m_{\rm cusp}(\sigma\circ\Phi_0^{-1},M';V').
$$
The same conclusion follows from equality of traces on the full automorphic spaces when the two test operators have the cuspidal trace expansions there. The original `SpecTraceX` gives precisely these expansions. On the actual hosts the [simple trace formula](LOWER_ROW_SIMPLE_TRACE.md) and [comparison of its elliptic terms](LOWER_ROW_ELLIPTIC_TRANSFER.md) supply the matched cuspidal traces assumed here. ## What the archimedean test detects

At a real place the group is $U(p,q)$ of odd rank $r=p+q$. In decreasing order the common Harish-Chandra parameter is $\lambda=(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_r)$ with integer entries and adjacent gaps at least $100r^4$. For $\rho=((r-1)/2,(r-3)/2,\ldots,(1-r)/2)$ the algebraic weight $\lambda-\rho$ is integral and strictly dominant. Indeed its adjacent gaps are $\lambda_j-\lambda_{j+1}-1$. This verifies the regular **highest weight** hypothesis needed for spectral isolation. Both rows have these same sorted coordinates. Their signatures and their specific discrete-series models specify which real representation to select on each row. [Source gap and ranks](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Definitions/Divisors/RangeShape.lean#L19-L39) · [Verification on the actual lower weights and both models](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/TraceEqXOfGeomExpansionX.lean#L501-L533)

Choose the compactly supported smooth bi-$K$-finite pseudo-coefficient of the particular discrete series $D$. The defining tempered trace property makes its trace one on $D$ and zero on the other tempered irreducibles. Dalal's regular discrete-series trace lemma gives the same delta function on irreducible **unitary** representations in the range just verified. The real group $U(p,q)$ has compact center, so its connected real split central factor is trivial. In the usual proof, nonzero trace requires the infinitesimal character of $D$. Salamanca-Riba's strongly regular classification then gives a cohomologically induced module $A_{\mathfrak q}(\eta)$, with $\eta$ a character of its Levi. For a unitary group, in a Levi block of length $b$, the infinitesimal-character coordinates are a common shift of $((b-1)/2,\ldots,(1-b)/2)$. The inducing character and the half-sum of roots in the nilradical are constant on that block. A block of length at least two would supply a pair of coordinates differing by one. The gap excludes it, so the Levi is a compact Cartan and the representation is discrete series. The tempered property now singles out $D$ within its packet. This explains where unitarity and the gap enter. The elementary tempered trace property alone would leave the nontempered unitary summands untreated. Take products of the specified local pseudo-coefficients over the real places, normalized for the chosen measures. On a cuspidal automorphic summand the trace is the product of the archimedean traces and the finite operator trace. The product at infinity is one exactly for $M$ on the first row, and exactly for $M'$ on the second. Thus the surviving term for a finite part $\tau$ is $m_{\rm cusp}(\tau,M)\operatorname{tr}\tau(f)$. This calculation concerns the spectral traces of the chosen functions. Their elliptic orbital integrals, required on the geometric side, impose additional normalization conditions in the source. ## The finite list on which to compare traces

Write $S$ for the marked places, and choose an auxiliary split place $u\notin S$ with Iwahori subgroup $I$. At a marked place the finite test belongs to $e_{\tau_v}*\mathcal H(G_v)*e_{\tau_v}$, where $e_{\tau_v}$ projects onto the inflated type. At $u$ the test is $1_{ItI}$ for $t=\operatorname{diag}(\varpi^{a_1},\ldots,\varpi^{a_r})$ with $a_1>\cdots>a_r$. At every other place use a spherical test, equal to the unit at almost all places. All these operators are left invariant by one compact open $K$ independent of $f$. Take the principal reduction kernel at each marked place, $I$ at $u$, and the frame-lattice stabilizers elsewhere. If $\tau(f)$ is nonzero then its image supplies a nonzero $K$-fixed vector. At fixed open compact level and fixed $M$, the cuspidal spectrum supplies finitely many such finite parts, each with finite multiplicity. Indeed each gives a nonzero contribution to the space with that level, the lowest compact type of $M$, and its central infinitesimal character. Harish-Chandra finiteness makes the latter space finite dimensional. The same applies at $\Phi_0(K)$ with $M'$. Pull back the second list, take the union, add the particular representation $\sigma$ even if it occurs on neither row, and retain one representative $\tau_j$ per equivalence class. Write $a_j$ for its first multiplicity minus its transported second multiplicity. For every allowed $f$, transport of the finite measures gives the same finite trace on both rows. The matched trace equality therefore gives
$$
 \sum_j a_j\operatorname{tr}\tau_j(f)=0.
$$
The finite list was fixed before $f$ varied. ## Why the restricted finite tests actually separate

At $u$ the stipulated tests range only over strict diagonal translations. To justify their use, fix the factors of $f$ away from $u$. Factorization of each of the finitely many traces makes the relation at $u$ a linear relation on local characters with constant weights. Group equivalent local representations, adding their weights. Those without $I$-fixed vectors contribute zero to every left-$I$-invariant test. For the others, the Borel–Casselman comparison with the unnormalized Borel Jacquet module gives
$$
\operatorname{tr}\pi(1_{ItI})
=\operatorname{vol}(I)\,\delta_B(t)^{-1}
  \sum_{\chi} m_\pi(\chi)\,\chi(t),
$$
where $m_\pi(\chi)$ counts the exponents on the torus-integer invariants of that Jacquet module. The nonzero factor in front is common to all $\pi$. These unramified torus characters are linearly independent on the strict cone. Indeed the cone contains a translate of the monoid of weakly decreasing integer tuples. That monoid generates $\mathbf Z^r$ as a group, so distinct characters remain distinct there. Translation multiplies each character by a nonzero scalar, and independence of characters of a monoid follows by the standard subtraction argument. Thus every coefficient of every exponent in the relation vanishes. [Borel, *Admissible representations … with vectors fixed under an Iwahori subgroup*](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01390139) · [Iwahori trace and normalization](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/BlockTheory/TwistedDiscreteSeries/IwahoriDoubleCosets.lean#L89-L106) · [Strict-cone calculation](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/TwistedOperators/UnramifiedCharSumsStrictConeVanishEach.lean#L50-L105)

One also needs the independence of the **multiplicity lists** $\chi\mapsto m_\pi(\chi)$ of the inequivalent Iwahori-spherical irreducibles. Here is why the source obtains it for split $\mathrm{GL}_r$. Normalize the Jacquet module, multiplying its exponents by the same fixed modulus twist in every list. Record an ordered torus character by the word of its $r$ values on a uniformizer. In the unramified principal-series block, Zelevinsky's Grothendieck ring has its segment classes as polynomial generators. A segment of length $l$ on an unramified line contributes the single word $(z,zQ^{-1},\ldots,zQ^{1-l})$ in the source orientation, where $Q$ is the residue cardinality. Distinct segments give distinct words. The geometric lemma makes parabolic induction the shuffle product on their Jacquet words, with multiplicities. Order letters by decreasing absolute value, breaking ties by argument. Each segment word is strictly increasing in this order, hence is a Lyndon word. The largest word in a shuffle of such words is their concatenation in decreasing lexicographic order, with nonzero coefficient. Unique Lyndon factorization distinguishes the concatenations for different segment monomials. This proves independence of the monomials, hence injectivity of the word map on this block. Jordan–Hölder makes the irreducible classes independent, so their Jacquet multiplicity lists are linearly independent too. This deals with overlapping exponents between different representations. Consequently the grouped local relation holds for every left-$I$-invariant test at $u$. [Bernstein–Zelevinsky, geometric lemma](https://www.numdam.org/item/ASENS_1977_4_10_4_441_0/) · [Zelevinsky, §3 and Corollary 7.5](https://www.numdam.org/item/ASENS_1980_4_13_2_165_0/) · [Prior segment conventions](continuation_v72/local.tex#L82-L102) · [Word computation and independence](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/TwistedOperators/LengthFilterFinRange.lean#L337-L447)

Finally take the product idempotent $e$ consisting of $e_{\tau_v}$ at the marked places, the normalized Iwahori indicator at $u$, and normalized stabilizer indicators elsewhere. Each $P_j=\tau_j(e)$ is a finite-rank idempotent by admissibility. It fixes a vector of the prescribed marked type, since that vector is fixed by the whole stabilizer at $u$. For any finite adele $g$, the compressed translate $e*\delta_g*e$ is a pure tensor. Its factors at the marked and ordinary places have the permitted form, and at $u$ it is left-$I$-invariant. The local calculation therefore extends the vanishing relation to
$$
\sum_j a_j\operatorname{tr}(P_j\tau_j(g)P_j)=0 \qquad\text{for every }g.
$$
Extend linearly to the group algebra. For any $i$ with $P_i\ne0$, simultaneous density on the inequivalent simple group-algebra modules gives an element acting as the identity on a basis of $\operatorname{im}P_i$ and zero on bases of all other images. Evaluating the identity gives $a_i\dim\operatorname{im}P_i=0$, hence $a_i=0$. In particular the coefficient of $\sigma$ vanishes. This proves the claimed individual multiplicity equality. The original adelic isomorphism differs from $\Phi_0$ by an inner conjugation, which intertwines the transported representations and gives the same conclusion for that isomorphism.
