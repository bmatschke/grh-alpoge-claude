# The first labelled state on the original family

Fix the saturated Hida state $x$ in the chosen pre-stage $P$, with empty index set of auxiliary local records. Write $D=D(x)$, and retain the coefficient embedding $j_E:E\hookrightarrow\overline{\mathbf Q}$ furnished with the ray primes. Use the [row-depth, Green and tame labels](FIRST_PASS_ROW_LABELS.md), the [ray class field on this host](FIRST_PASS_RAY_CLASS_FIELD.md) and the [de Rham and filtered-dimension comparison](FIRST_PASS_FILTERED_DIMENSIONS.md), with their stated inputs. Then the labelled state required by the first pass exists over the **same** $P$. The construction applies to each state in the eventual family satisfying the emptiness condition. ## Which local correspondence is used

The standard statement `SA` starts with full local Langlands for general linear groups. The third-place series uses `SameGaloisLevelExists` and the separately chosen `RecTExists`. This gives a map on **all** irreducible split local classes with specified laws on the eligible support. Two assertions from the row-label calculation suffice. In the ambient unitary family every automorphic class is represented. At a split place with local component $u$, put $T_w^{-1}\operatorname{rec}_T(u)$ in its parameter slot. Uniqueness of the local component on isomorphic representatives makes this well defined. The spherical fixed-vector hypotheses in the polynomial comparison supply the local spherical case of the restricted laws. At an inert place the separate Satake assertion is conditional on existence of a Satake parameter. In the general-linear cuspidal family the indexing and the surjectivity assertion restrict to classes satisfying `GoodAll`. These quantifiers allow ambient local components outside the eligible support. The alternate comparison does not need a theorem putting all such components in that support. ## How the finite type operators survive the change of stage

The agreement of the frame places and weights at $x$ gives the type hypothesis for its read rows. Install the row Weil–Deligne dictionary, the restricted cuspidal records and the ambient records. On the resulting ray layer the tame generator, Teichmüller and Green series data agree with the prepared frame. Thus the equations for labels, conjugate labels and character values still hold there. The resulting layer $L$ supplies its two row ties and its character tie, equality of its split type-place indices and their actual places with the original ones, and agreement of **both** centre actions. These are the hypotheses needed to refit the central action. Let $G_1,G_0$ be the products of the finite general linear groups on the new and old type places. The actual place bijection gives $e:G_1\simeq G_0$, which sends the component at $v$ to the component with the same prime. For the induced group-algebra isomorphism $\phi$, we have $[eg]\phi(z)=[g]z$. The type-idempotent coefficient formula identifies $\phi(e_{1})=e_{0}$, so the new element is idempotent and central. For an arbitrary central element $z$ of the new group algebra, $\phi(z)$ is central by surjectivity of $\phi$. Compose any proposed standard identification of the row group with $e$ and use the old fitted action for $\phi(z)$. The transported coefficients agree term by term in the finite action sums. After identifying the places, the two given centre maps agree on the resulting element, on each ambient space and separately on every multiplicity space indexed by a tuple and a row. This proves the centre identities as operator identities for every standard identification.
