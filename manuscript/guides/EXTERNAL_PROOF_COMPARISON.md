## Effective orders and the quantitative size of the orbit

## The square-root length and the normalization of the period

An additional comparison is Tokuhiro Eto's *Reading Alpöge's proof of the Riemann hypothesis*, §§2.4 and 6.3–6.4. $$

### The signed wave identity in the residual tower

The new comparison also gives an explicit test for the derivative estimates in the spectral obligation. On its regular compact residual class Chen's word calculation identifies four operators and gives, at a nonzero frequency $s$,
$$
 s\,\beta(f_sF,F')=\beta(f_s\Theta_1F,\Theta_0F')-\beta(f_s\Theta_3F,\Theta_2F').
$$
Here $f_s$ is the first-coordinate unitary wave and $\beta$ the Hilbert-space pairing in a fixed tower. Cancellation in each subsequent site reduces the difference to the first-coordinate commutator $[P_0,f_s]=s f_s c_0$. The flat-area change absorbs the residual measure once, through the square of $G^{(k-1)/2}$ times the norm-preserving frame map. Each of the four lifted differential operators has order at most $n$ and polynomial coefficient degree at most $8Rn$ in the notation of that construction. These are concrete identities for lifting a word through the period. [Four operators and wave commutator](https://github.com/yyyyychen/RH-Natural-Language-Proof/blob/d4dfb1a282dbf857288e73eb1f546dbfcc5899b8/tex/word-pairing.tex#L415-L466) · [Lift order, degree, and change of measure](https://github.com/yyyyychen/RH-Natural-Language-Proof/blob/d4dfb1a282dbf857288e73eb1f546dbfcc5899b8/tex/word-pairing.tex#L1340-L1385)

Only a sum of squared derivative norms is required at each length. Suppose that on the reachable first-argument spaces at depth $j$ one has $\|\Theta_1 v\|^2+\|\Theta_3 v\|^2\le A_j^2\|v\|^2$, and on the corresponding second-argument spaces one has $\|\Theta_0 v'\|^2+\|\Theta_2 v'\|^2\le B_j^2\|v'\|^2$. If $|\beta(f_s v,v')|\le M\|v\|\|v'\|$ uniformly at the leaves, then on these data
$$
 |\beta(f_s F,F')|\le M |s|^{-N}
       \left(\prod_{j=0}^{N-1} A_j B_j\right)\|F\|\|F'\|.
$$
Expand the signed identity $N$ times. Each binary path gives a pair of vectors. Cauchy–Schwarz on the path index costs the product of the two square roots of summed squared norms. Iterating the respective depth bounds gives exactly the product displayed. The estimate is uniform in the size of the binary expansion once those paired bounds are supplied. Chen's lift result explicitly allows individual constants to depend on the prefix, shell and cutoffs. Applying it to the [point-to-section estimate](../notes/spectral/SPECTRAL_ORDINARY_SQUARED_AMPLITUDE.md) requires product bounds at the selected depth uniform in those choices, with the original physical energy. Differential order by itself does not give the needed constants.

### Why the shortened proof helps with the endpoint but needs a transfer theorem for an original word

Sernau’s construction uses one target of dimension $d>0$. Its translate set $S$ satisfies $|S|\dim V_{\rm ram}\le 5d\log(1+2b^2)$ and provides $n$ independent vectors spanning at least $(1-4/b^2)d$ dimensions. If $M$ is the common main amount, $R$ the prime remainder, $L=fa(\log b)^4$ and $I=10\log(1+2b^2)fa(\log b)^3$, its three inputs give
$$
 d(M-3L)\le n(M-mR+L)+2dI .
$$
The lower estimate on the left uses a determinant on the same independent family whose lifted norms bound the right by Hadamard. Since $M+L\ge0$ and $n\le d$, one obtains $nmR\le d(4L+2I)$. The large-parameter range gives $n\ge d/2$ and $2(4L+2I)\le fa(\log b)^5$. This proves the shorter main inequality from its three inputs. [Sernau, exact bounds and cancellation](https://github.com/lsernau/grh-lean/blob/72fead102f663d5c7a407964a13dc0cf9d977207/grh/src/New/Reduction/MainInequality.lean#L93-L184)

There are two specific obstacles to using those conclusions verbatim here. In the shorter seesaw, the order of choice is a fixed linear form and real point, then one nonzero scalar, then for every twist a *choice* of chain datum which works on the whole ramified source. In the original period problem the chart operations of each tuple have already been specified. In the shorter spanning theorem an almost-full span suffices, whereas our original mixed spectral assertion asks for each pair's squared Gram norm at its own dimension scale. Even a collection of unit vectors spanning a $d$-dimensional space can contain two copies of the same vector. Their squared pairing is one, against the rank-one target $(1+\eta)/d$. This example isolates the failure of an implication from the spanning conclusion alone. The shorter proof's underlying moment estimates remain candidates for transfer once the operators, measures and allowed errors have been identified. ## Special choices with a verifiable benefit

* **Choose the primes in two sets.** The first set carries prescribed Frobenius in a common finite quotient and gives units at the trivial theta character. The second set supplies independent ray coordinates and detects Galois minors. At a toric label the residual order per second prime is zero or two. At an automorphic row of Hecke rank $r$ on $b$ basis columns it is at most $rb$. Both bounds persist at $\ell=2$. [Toric calculation](../notes/first-pass/FIRST_PASS_TORIC_DISTRIBUTION.md) · [Automorphic calculation](../notes/first-pass/FIRST_PASS_AUTOMORPHIC_FACTORS.md)
* **Use the cyclotomic image to decide whether the cofactor argument is necessary.** At every odd coefficient prime unramified in the fitted CM host the first-order calculation already gives a unit coefficient. At two the allowed loss is exactly controlled by the largest group of $2$-power roots of unity contained in that host. This can be tested on the field already chosen. [Proof and exact criterion](../notes/first-pass/FIRST_PASS_GALOIS_PRIMES.md#when-the-simpler-galois-construction-applies)
* **Take one set of interpolation points before the long Frobenius orbits are known.** In Hexagon 2611.00839v1, Theorem 2.1, $0<\varepsilon<1/52$, the finite abelian group has order comparable to $x^{2+8\varepsilon}$ and its distinguished subgroup has bounded index $g$. Its nontrivial quotient characters consequently have unweighted mean $1/(3g+1)$. This offers a simplification for that combinatorial input. Subsequent derivative weights vary.
