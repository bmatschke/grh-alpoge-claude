# The reciprocity datum exists before the choice of bundle size

To construct the common family, one first produces a good arithmetic point, with its prescribed rows and readings. One then equips that point with a linked bundle at the size required by the family construction. The step between these operations involves a concrete question in class field theory. The character data refer to an abelian extension specified by its Frobenius elements, whereas the geometric data give a field generated by values on a complex elliptic curve. The two fields must agree in the chosen embedding. **Proposition.** Fix the local Langlands input, depth and geometric condition of S77C2, and the additional condition on the underlying twist when using its strengthened parameter. For either choice of parameter and every prescribed good point $\beta$ of S77C3, one can choose a weighted point $q$ and its compatible reciprocity datum $R$ **before any real bundle size $c$ is specified**. The equality of the full pair consisting of the datum and its object identifies $q$ with $\beta$. The chosen $q$ has the required kernel certificate, and $R$ satisfies the original compatibility with its columns, theta maps, and kernel. This proves the existence assertion of S77C4 at every such point. ### The field of the specified Weber values

Use the fixed embedding of the imaginary quadratic field $K$ into $\mathbb C$. Its discriminant is less than $-4$, and the lattice $\Lambda$ in the assertion is the image of $\mathcal O_K$. Put $E=\mathbb C/\Lambda$ and $\Delta=g_2^3-27g_3^2$. The reconstruction adjoins to $K$ the numbers

$$
  \frac{g_2^3}{\Delta},\qquad
  \frac{g_2g_3}{\Delta}\wp_\Lambda(z/n)
  \quad(z\in\Lambda,\ z/n\notin\Lambda).
$$

The first number is $j(E)/1728$. Multiplication by the nonzero rational number $1728$ gives the same field. The arguments of the second expression represent precisely the nonzero points of $E[n]$. The factor multiplying $\wp$ matters. It is the normalization in equation (6-14) of Cohen and Stevenhagen, invariant under simultaneous homothety of the lattice and the argument. The discriminant condition excludes the two elliptic curves with additional automorphisms, for which the Weber function takes a different form. By the first main theorem of complex multiplication, $K(j(E))$ is the Hilbert class field. By the second, adjoining these Weber values gives the ray class field modulo $n\mathcal O_K$. This works also for $n=1$, where the set of nonzero torsion points is empty. It identifies the actual subfield generated inside $\mathbb C$ and supplies its finite abelian Galois structure. ### Identifying the ray field by its characters

For the separate ray-field assertion, let $F$ be any number field and $\mathfrak m$ any nonzero integral ideal. Take the modulus with finite part $\mathfrak m$ and **all real places**. Global class field theory gives a finite abelian ray extension $H/F$, unramified away from the modulus, with Artin isomorphism from the ray class group. Its Galois group is generated by the Frobenius elements of the primes away from $\mathfrak m$, since the prime-to-modulus fractional ideals are generated by these primes. For an integral ideal $I$ prime to $\mathfrak m$, the source's ideal character is the product of its Frobenius character values with the exponents in the factorization of $I$. Thus it is $\chi(\operatorname{Art}(I))$ in the arithmetic Frobenius convention. All such values are one exactly when $I$ is trivial in the ray class group, since characters separate elements of a finite abelian group. [Global ray-class existence and Artin isomorphism, Cohen–Stevenhagen, §2](https://library.slmath.org/books/Book44/files/15cohenpsh.pdf) · [Ideal character and the real sign condition](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Definitions/GlobalTwisting/SmulCommClassGalIntegers.lean#L89-L101)

In that case $I=(a)$ with $a$ totally positive and $a\equiv1\bmod\mathfrak m$. The generator is integral because $I$ is integral. Taking $(\alpha,\beta)=(a,1)$ gives the pair in the original definition. Conversely such a pair gives $I=(\alpha/\beta)$. Both entries are units at every prime of $\mathfrak m$, their difference lies in $\mathfrak m$, and they have the same sign at every real embedding. Hence $\alpha/\beta$ has the required ray congruence and positivity. This proves the three defining assertions for the general ray field and verifies the same assertions for the Weber field. ### Reciprocity and the common choice of size

For the idelic map in S77C4 take geometric reciprocity, the inverse of arithmetic reciprocity. On the idèle class group of a number field it is onto the absolute abelian Galois group. The kernel is the closure of the image of the connected archimedean factors. Pulling back to the idèles gives the closure of their product with the principal idèles. Openness follows because congruence subgroups at the finite places, together with the connected archimedean components, map to open subgroups fixing the corresponding ray fields. These congruence subgroups can be chosen inside any prescribed identity neighborhood at the finite places, and the archimedean identity component maps trivially. Local units map onto the image of inertia, and the class of a uniformizer modulo inertia gives inverse arithmetic Frobenius. The closure of the image of the local multiplicative group is the image of the decomposition group. These statements specify the orientation and the topology of the original Artin map at every prolongation of the place. [Sharifi, *Algebraic Number Theory*, §10.4, especially Remark 10.4.12](https://math.ucla.edu/~sharifi/notes/algnum-ch10.html) · [Local reciprocity and Frobenius convention](https://kskedlaya.org/cft/sec_localrecip.html) · [Pinned properties of the idelic map](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/PublishedTheorems/AutomorphicForms₂/ExistsUniqueHcharOfFiniteOrder.lean#L19-L41)

These three class field theorems supply the arithmetic inputs to S77C4. Editions 0.89–0.90 established its five general analytic inputs concerning discrete series, Harish-Chandra modules, globalization, finiteness and cuspidal decay. S77C3 first constructs $q$ on the prescribed point's division lattice. The good point fixes the level primes, and its row comparison gives the zeta identity by the division-pairing theorem using the five analytic inputs. The transported kernel certificate of S77C3 is on the same lattice. S77C4 can therefore install the reciprocity and kernel data on this very $q$ to give $R$. This proves the proposition.
