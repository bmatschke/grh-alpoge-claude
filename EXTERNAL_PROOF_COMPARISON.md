The roots-of-unity chapter gives a directly reusable local lemma. Its proof chooses the radius by a harmonic-series bound and then moves the coordinates one at a time. Three new exercises check edge cases. For example, a primitive ninth root fails for $m=3,\ell=3,F(X)=\Phi_3(1+X)$ because at the third power the value vanishes. Sernau's arithmetic application still takes the descent construction of the evaluating polynomials as input. The original fitted first pass already constructs low-coefficient Galois polynomials and toric and Hecke determinant polynomials. Its [exceptional-order argument](FIRST_PASS_COPRIME_SPECIALIZATION.md) now supplies the same tolerance for every power with bounded $\ell$-adic order on one chosen tuple. ### Why the shortened proof helps with the endpoint but needs a transfer theorem for an original word

Sernau’s construction uses one target of dimension $d>0$. Its translate set $S$ satisfies $|S|\dim V_{\rm ram}\le 5d\log(1+2b^2)$ and provides $n$ independent vectors spanning at least $(1-4/b^2)d$ dimensions. If $M$ is the common main amount, $R$ the prime remainder, $L=fa(\log b)^4$ and $I=10\log(1+2b^2)fa(\log b)^3$, its three inputs give
$$
 d(M-3L)\le n(M-mR+L)+2dI .
$$
The lower estimate on the left uses a determinant on the same independent family whose lifted norms bound the right by Hadamard. Since $M+L\ge0$ and $n\le d$, one obtains $nmR\le d(4L+2I)$. The large-parameter range gives $n\ge d/2$ and $2(4L+2I)\le fa(\log b)^5$. This proves the shorter main inequality from its three inputs. [Sernau, exact bounds and cancellation](https://github.com/lsernau/grh-lean/blob/72fead102f663d5c7a407964a13dc0cf9d977207/grh/src/New/Reduction/MainInequality.lean#L93-L184)

There are two specific obstacles to using those conclusions verbatim here. In the shorter seesaw, the order of choice is a fixed linear form and real point, then one nonzero scalar, then for every twist a *choice* of chain datum which works on the whole ramified source. In the original period problem the chart operations of each tuple have already been specified. In the shorter spanning theorem an almost-full span suffices, whereas our original mixed spectral assertion asks for each pair's squared Gram norm at its own dimension scale. Even a collection of unit vectors spanning a $d$-dimensional space can contain two copies of the same vector. Their squared pairing is one, against the rank-one target $(1+\eta)/d$. This example isolates the failure of an implication from the spanning conclusion alone. The shorter proof's underlying moment estimates remain candidates for transfer once the operators, measures and allowed errors have been identified. [Short seesaw with its quantifiers](https://github.com/lsernau/grh-lean/blob/72fead102f663d5c7a407964a13dc0cf9d977207/grh/src/New/UpperBound/Seesaw/SeesawIdentity.lean#L612-L630) · [Original spectral inequality](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/GlobalTheoremInputs.lean#L142-L157)

* **Use the cyclotomic image to decide whether the cofactor argument is necessary.** At every odd coefficient prime unramified in the fitted CM host the first-order calculation already gives a unit coefficient. [Proof and exact criterion](FIRST_PASS_GALOIS_PRIMES.md#when-the-simpler-galois-construction-applies)
* **Take one set of interpolation points before the long Frobenius orbits are known.** In Hexagon 2611.00839v1, Theorem 2.1, $0<\varepsilon<1/52$, the finite abelian group has order comparable to $x^{2+8\varepsilon}$ and its distinguished subgroup has bounded index $g$. The construction gives exact coset counts in the ratio $4:3:\cdots:3$ and spacing on every orbit of order at least $x^{\varepsilon/4}$ from every origin. Its nontrivial quotient characters consequently have unweighted mean $1/(3g+1)$. This offers a simplification for that combinatorial input. Subsequent derivative weights vary. Corollary 4.1 of the same paper selects the two outer host primes $q,d$. The inner conductor $Q$ also has independent ray coordinates, determinant requirements, and local Selmer conditions. ## How the determinant fits Alpöge’s remarks

Items 17 and 18 distinguish two costs of twisting. Surviving cohomology contributes through its total length, while congruences change the index of a lattice of forms. On the fitted column the square Selmer determinant measures the first cost and the determinant of the projected theta columns bounds the second. Choosing them at the same conductor is what makes the two estimates usable for the same twist. Items 15 and 16 raise a further question about the positions of several period images in one target lattice. The first-pass determinants concern individual rows and leave those positions to the period and spectral estimates. ## Other literature at the boundary

Poitou–Tate and the local and global Euler characteristic formulas provide the count of obstructions to the square Selmer presentation. Bloch–Kato's local dimension formula applies to the actual de Rham representations once their conjugate jumps and rational invariant vanishings are checked. The integral finite cocycle lattice must still commute with specialization of coefficients. We give that step explicitly. Gross's formulation of Landherr's local-global theorem identifies the residual rational Hermitian space for the flag classification after the finite local lattices and all real signatures are compared. [Square matrix](FIRST_PASS_SELMER_PRESENTATION.md) · [Bloch–Kato, Corollary 3.8.4][bk] · [Milne, *Arithmetic Duality Theorems*][milne] · [Gross, Theorem 3.1][gross]

Waterloo v2 supplies the analytic host-prime selection and a detailed zeta specialization, with its main-inequality dependencies set out in Part III. Naskręcki's three digests address a different Selmer group, the locally trivial negative Tate twists on a cyclotomic tower. Those results are valuable on the window branch. Adriazola's proposed shorter K1 argument in §17.4.3 bounds a global second cohomology group over a field containing the requisite roots of unity. None of these results asserts the inner twisted row determinant for every coprime power on its installed theta conductor. [Waterloo v2][waterloo] · [Digest comparison at the formal pin](continuation_v83/nasqret.md) · [Proposed K1 bound][kone]

Two alternative geometric approaches merit a specific test. Huang's quantitative equidistribution and arithmetic Hilbert–Samuel inputs are stated as hypotheses. The Roadmap's simplification notes suggest using definite Siegel–Weil positivity to estimate the integral theta image. The remaining quantity is the **covolume of that integral image**. Surjectivity on the fraction-field spectral block allows an arbitrarily large integral index. The proposed single-embedding determinant shortcut would also need the signed bound at all embeddings. These alternatives leave concrete quantities to estimate on our [original period lattices](PERIOD_COMPARISON_STEP.md). [Huang, §§1, 6][huang] · [Roadmap simplification notes][simple]
