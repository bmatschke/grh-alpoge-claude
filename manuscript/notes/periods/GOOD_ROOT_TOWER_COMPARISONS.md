# Comparing the integral flag models at two cutoffs

The volume argument uses a family of integral flag models indexed by a component $Z$ of a deep cover and a prime cutoff $N$. At a given cutoff it uses the de Rham lattice away from a finite set and Hodge–Tate lattices at the primes in that set. Comparing the resulting intersection numbers requires putting the two flags on the same model. We give this comparison, identify which local calculations it requires, and prove the standard flag-height law that enters every component. The six laws in B8.26r also require geometric and metric estimates particular to the unitary variety. [Definition B8.26r][rt-laws] · [Use in the volume calculation](PERIOD_VOLUME_BEFORE_SELECTION.md)

## The reference flag height

<!--
% BEGIN GRT1
-->

<span id="claim:GRT1"></span>Use the product of complete flags of the standard rank-$n$ bundle over $\mathbb Z$, one at each of the $f$ labels $\tau$. Its relative dimension is $d=f\binom n2$. The variable $X_{\tau,i}$ represents the first Chern class of the dual of the $i$th graded quotient, with its subquotient metric. The tensor product over one row is the dual determinant of the standard bundle with its unit metric, since in an orthonormal frame adapted to the flag the modulus of the determinant is one. The associated arithmetic first Chern class vanishes. Thus $h_{\rm fl}((\sum_i X_{\tau,i})Q)=0$ for every homogeneous $Q$ of degree $d$.

Let $H_{\tau,j}=\sum_{i\ge j} X_{\tau,i}$ for $1\le j<n$. It is the pullback of the Plücker hyperplane with its Fubini–Study metric from the Grassmannian of $(n-j)$-planes. Writing $L_v=-\sum v_{\tau,i}X_{\tau,i}$ and removing the trivial row determinants gives
$$
 L_v=\sum_{\tau}\sum_{1\le j<n}(v_{\tau,j-1}-v_{\tau,j})H_{\tau,j}.
$$
For a rational dominant weight clear denominators. The resulting line has semipositive curvature, is nef on finite fibres and is generated by products of integral Plücker coordinates of sup norm at most one. Its top arithmetic intersection is nonnegative. One can see this from the arithmetic divisor recursion by choosing, on each horizontal component, a generating small section nonzero at its generic point. Its effective divisor has nonnegative multiplicities, the Green term $-\log\|s\|$ integrates nonnegatively against the semipositive Chern measure, and the finite-fibre terminal degrees are nonnegative. Induction on the dimension proves the assertion. The height polynomial extends it to real dominant weights. If an adjacent gap is zero, the metrized line pulls back from the partial flag omitting that step. Its fibres include the projective line of intermediate subspaces and the base's relative dimension is $d-1$. The projection formula gives zero for a product of $d+1$ pulled-back arithmetic classes. Clearing denominators and continuity on the wall give the assertion for real weights there. This proves all three requirements of law 2.

For comparison the geometric coefficient functional $D$ alternates the coefficients of $\prod X_{\tau,i}^{\sigma_\tau(i)}$. The multinomial expansion and the Vandermonde determinant give
$$
 D(L_v^d)=d!\prod_\tau
 \frac{\prod_{a<b}(v_{\tau,a}-v_{\tau,b})}{\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}j!}.
$$
The signs from $L_v$ and from reversing each Vandermonde cancel. This fixes the reference normalization at a regular weight. GRT8–9 compare the normalized geometric degrees on a common cover and prove equality of the component volumes at the selected principal level. [Reference flag, wall and degree calculation][rt-flag]

## Local lattices on a common model

<!--
% BEGIN GRT2
-->

<span id="claim:GRT2"></span>Let $F_c$ be the coefficient field, $M'$ the level modulus and $E$ the CM host. At every cutoff use
$$
 \mathcal B=\{\ell\text{ prime}:\ell\mid2\,|\operatorname{disc}E|M'\},
 \qquad S_N=\{\ell\text{ prime}:\ell\le N\}\cup\mathcal B .
$$
On the generic abelian locus full $\ell^4$-torsion becomes rational over a finite extension of its function field because the group scheme is finite étale in characteristic zero. For the finitely many $\ell\in S_N$ take the compositum of the splitting fields. This chooses the same depth four at each prime, including $2$. Normalizing a projective model in that field puts all the generic data on one model. GRT10–11 construct the unlabelled integral lattice on the boundary charts. GRT13–14 construct the labelled quotients on a common refinement, including at ramified places.

Here is the patching step once those frames have been constructed. On a quasi-compact noetherian flat model over $\mathcal O_{F_c}$ let $\mathcal D$ be a de Rham vector lattice in its generic bundle. At each $\ell\in S_N$ suppose a locally free lattice $\mathcal H_\ell$ over the model localized over $\mathbb Z_{(\ell)}$ agrees with $\mathcal D$ after inverting $\ell$, via the specified generic identification. On a finite affine cover, clear the powers of $\ell$ in both transition matrices. Taking the maximum gives $c_\ell$ with
$$
 \ell^{c_\ell}\mathcal D_{(\ell)}
 \ \subseteq\ \mathcal H_\ell\ \subseteq\
 \ell^{-c_\ell}\mathcal D_{(\ell)}.
$$
Set $m=\prod_{\ell\in S_N}\ell^{c_\ell}$. Work in $m^{-1}\mathcal D/m\mathcal D$, killed by $m^2$. The Chinese remainder idempotents split it as the direct sum of its $\ell$-primary parts. Each such part is canonically its own localization at $\ell$, since the other rational primes act invertibly. In that part take the image of $\mathcal H_\ell/m\mathcal D_{(\ell)}$. Take the inverse image of their direct sum in $m^{-1}\mathcal D$. This coherent lattice restricts to $\mathcal H_\ell$ at $\ell$ and to $\mathcal D$ elsewhere. It is uniquely determined, because equality of subsheaves can be tested on stalks. Its stalks are free by the local hypotheses. Refining the cover or enlarging the denominators therefore gives the same vector bundle. Several primes of $F_c$ above one $\ell$ are included in the same localized calculation.

Apply this to each labelled eigenlattice and each step of the prescribed generic flag on a common model where subbundles and their successive quotients are locally free at the designated places. Inclusions and exactness of each flag sequence are stalkwise, so the resulting root lines are the local quotient lines at all the primes simultaneously. This specifies the gluing required by the cutoff. The arithmetic problem is to construct local charts with these freeness and subbundle properties, particularly at bad primes. <span id="claim:GRT3"></span>For two cutoffs $N\le M$ on one component, let $F$ be the reference flag function field. Embed the finite cutoff fields $F_N,F_M$ over $F$ in one algebraic closure and take their compositum $\Omega$. A common projective model is obtained by closing the image of the common generic point in the product of the two projective models over the reference base, then normalizing. The function field of that closure is $\Omega$ because functions from the two factors generate their compositum. Normalization is finite here for schemes of finite type over rings of integers. The projections are proper and generically finite and may be composed with a common refinement carrying the specified flags. If $d_j=[F_j:F]$, $b_j=[\Omega:F_j]$ and $\delta_j=[F_c:\mathbb Q]d_j$, the tower law gives
$$
 b_N\delta_N=b_M\delta_M=[F_c:\mathbb Q][\Omega:F].
$$
Consequently either normalized intersection can be computed on the common model by dividing by this one number.

The two pulled-back flags agree generically through the fixed reference flag. The comparison of the two patched root lines is therefore an isomorphism away from $S_M\setminus S_N$. Its divisor is vertical and supported at good primes $N<\ell\le M$. Likewise, when the smoothed metrics come from the same reference potentials, the generic identification is an isometry at infinity, so this vertical divisor has zero Green term. ## The change contributed by one good prime

<!--
% BEGIN GRT4

<span id="claim:GRT4"></span>Fix one of the new good primes $\ell$, a complementary monomial $Q_0$, and a Newton power $k\ge1$. Write $r$ for the left rank and $s=n-r$ for the right rank. For a signed label $\epsilon$ over $\sigma$ let $B_{\epsilon,k-1}$ be $\sum_{i<r}X_{\sigma,i}^{k-1}$ on the left and $\sum_{i\ge r}(-X_{\sigma,i})^{k-1}$ on the right. Let $A_\epsilon$ denote $k$ times the local intersection of this class and $Q_0$ with $\operatorname{div}(\ell)$. The local inputs are the two comparison identities for the Hodge–Tate and de Rham chart lattices on a finite label cover. In the notation of B8.26u these read
$$
 \Delta_{e(t)}=H(t)-\frac{A_{e(t)}}{\ell-1},\qquad
 \ell^kH(t)=H(\phi' t)+A_{e(\phi't)} .
$$
Here $e\phi'=\phi_\ell e$, where $\phi_\ell$ is arithmetic Frobenius on signed labels. The first identity records the uniformizer correction because $(\zeta_\ell-1)^{\ell-1}$ is a unit times $\ell$. The second records $\ell$th powers of the successive character lines. The finite cover makes $H$ bounded. Iteration gives
$$
 H(t)=\sum_{a=1}^b\ell^{-ka}A_{\phi_\ell^a e(t)}
                 +\ell^{-kb}H((\phi')^b t).
$$
Let $b$ tend to infinity. The series is absolutely convergent and depends only on $e(t)$. If the orbit length of this label is $m$, its value is the finite sum $\sum_{a=1}^m\ell^{k(m-a)}A_{\phi_\ell^a e(t)}/(\ell^{km}-1)$. In particular the first term uses the first iterate. At a right-hand label the factor is $(-1)^{k-1}$.

To use this identity in a mixed intersection, write $U,V$ for the root classes before and after the change. Expand the difference of $p_{\tau,k}Q_0$ into the difference of the powers times $Q_0(U)$ and $p_{\tau,k}(V)(Q_0(U)-Q_0(V))$, where $p_{\tau,k}=\sum_i X_{\tau,i}^k$. The second factor in parentheses is a sum of root-line differences times mixed monomials by telescoping. On the supporting fibre the second flag is a full flag of a trivial bundle, a local geometric input. All its positive Newton sums vanish in the Chow ring by multiplicativity of the total Chern class and the Newton identities. Thus the second term has zero intersection. For the first term use $(U^k-V^k)=(U-V)\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}U^jV^{k-1-j}$ separately on each line. The chart comparison above evaluates the resulting divisor term. The geometric functional on the first flag is $V_ZD$ at the normalization of law 1. Intersecting with the total fibre over $\ell$ contributes $\log\ell$ times this normalized generic degree by flatness and the arithmetic divisor formula. Summing the disjoint vertical contributions of GRT3 gives
$$
 (P_{N,Z}-P_{M,Z})(p_{\tau,k}Q_0)=
 V_Z\!\!\sum_{\ell\in S_M\setminus S_N}\!\!
 k\log\ell\left(\sum_{a\ge1}\ell^{-ka}D(B_{\phi_\ell^a(+\tau),k-1}Q_0)
             -\frac{D(B_{+\tau,k-1}Q_0)}{\ell-1}\right).
$$
The fixed smooth comparisons have a finite prime sum, so their common cutoff-metric limit preserves this equality. Linearity extends it to homogeneous $Q_0$ of degree $d+1-k$.

As a sign and index check take $k=1$. If Frobenius preserves the two sides all iterates have $B=r$ and the correction is zero. If it exchanges them, the series starts with $s/\ell$, followed by $r/\ell^2$. Subtracting $r/(\ell-1)$ leaves $(s-r)\ell/(\ell^2-1)$, to be multiplied by $\log\ell\,D(Q_0)$. This is the determinant correction of law 4 on a single component. The side is common to the labels for the induced CM type of this host. <span id="claim:GRT5"></span>There is also a local-to-global step at infinity. Fix one cutoff component of the Grassmannian base of dimension $frs$. Write $h_0$ for a smooth reference metric on the determinant line and $q_j$ for its local weights. The corrected weight in B8.26u is the Hodge weight plus $U/2$, with $U$ the nonnegative cusp function. Suppose its relative weight has local smooth regularizations $u_{j,t}$ on charts $V_j$, with $dd^c(q_j+u_{j,t})\ge-\delta(t)\omega$ and $|u_{j,t}-u_{k,t}|\le e(t)$ on overlaps. Here $\omega$ is a fixed positive form on the compact smooth complex model and $e(t),\delta(t)\to0$. We give the gluing with the error needed for $b\mu+aA$. The same argument applies to the original truncated Hodge weights when their own overlap and curvature estimates hold. Only finitely many charts are involved at the given component.

Choose smaller sets $V'_j$ covering the model with closure in $V_j$, and smooth functions $\rho_j\in[-1,0]$, zero on $V'_j$ and $-1$ near the edge of $V_j$. Put $\eta=\max(e(t),t)$ and $f_j=u_{j,t}+4\eta\rho_j$. Near the edge of chart $j$ some inner chart $k$ has $f_k\ge f_j+3\eta$. Smooth the maximum of the $f_j$ by averaging $\max_j(f_j+h_j)$ over independent shifts with a smooth even probability density supported in $(-\eta,\eta)$ in each variable. The result is smooth, convex, increasing in its arguments and translates by $c$ when all arguments translate by $c$. If one entry lies more than $2\eta$ below another it drops out, including from the integral. Thus the formulas glue smoothly when a chart disappears. Adding $q_j$ locally to all entries computes the ordinary local weight, by translation invariance. The differences of reference potentials between holomorphic frames are pluriharmonic. The Hessian of this regularized maximum is a convex combination of the input Hessians plus a positive semidefinite form. Since the finitely many $\rho_j$ have bounded second derivatives, the global weight has curvature at least $-(\delta(t)+C\eta)\omega$. On an inner chart the result is at least $u_{j,t}$ by convexity and the zero means of the shifts. In particular an upper regularization there still dominates the corrected weight. Conjugate charts may be included in pairs. Averaging the final metrics' logarithmic weights under conjugation, using a conjugation-invariant reference form, preserves the curvature bound and dominance for a conjugation-invariant original weight.

If the auxiliary metric has curvature at least $c\omega$ for $c>0$, choose $t$ with $b(\delta(t)+C\eta)<ac$. The smoothed $b\mu+aA$ then has semipositive curvature. This choice is allowed to depend on the component, cutoff and ratio $a/b$. The proof uses an overlap error tending to zero for the *actual* chart regularizations. For the truncated Hodge metric the height of truncation must also dominate its $\log\log(1/t)$ singularity. Constructing the cusp correction with these overlap estimates on that model, verifying positivity on finite fibres, and computing its mean in the top intersection are distinct requirements of law 6. ## The finite torsion at a boundary valuation

<!--
% BEGIN GRT6
-->

<span id="claim:GRT6"></span>There is a way to obtain the cokernel bound used in patching directly at depth four. Work over the complete valuation ring $V=\mathcal O_C$ of an algebraically closed field of mixed characteristic and normalize $v_p(p)=1$. Let $G/V$ be the given smooth semiabelian model with abelian generic fibre, and let $T=G_C[p^4](C)$ with the perfect generic Weil pairing from the given principal polarization. The boundary group scheme $G$ is separated and of finite type. We first construct the finite part of its torsion. Multiplication by $p^4$ is flat and quasi-finite on $G$ by Bosch–Lütkebohmert–Raynaud, §7.3, Lemma 2(a), which allows an arbitrary base and semiabelian fibres of varying toric rank. Pullback by the identity section gives the same properties for $Y=G[p^4]\to\operatorname{Spec}V$. By Zariski's main theorem the separated scheme $Y$ embeds as an open in a finite $V$-scheme $\overline Y$. The finite algebra of $\overline Y$ splits over the Henselian ring $V$ as a product of local rings. Take the union $U$ of those factors whose closed point lies in $Y$. Each such factor lies entirely in $Y$, since every open of a local spectrum containing its closed point is the whole spectrum. Thus $U$ is finite, open and closed in $Y$, flat over $V$, and contains the whole special fibre of $Y$. [Flatness of multiplication on a semiabelian scheme][rt-blr] · [Zariski's main theorem][rt-zmt] · [Henselian finite algebras][rt-hensel]

The identity belongs to $U$. The inverse image of $Y\setminus U$ under addition on $U\times_V U$ is closed in a finite $V$-scheme and misses its special fibre. Its image is therefore closed and empty over the local base. The same argument for inversion makes $U$ a subgroup. Its generic points $H\subset T$ are precisely the torsion points extending to $V$-sections of $Y$. Properness of $U$ extends every point of $H$, and any section of $Y$ factors through $U$ because its closed point lies there. This construction uses only the semiabelian model at the valuation. Applying its differential estimate to a coherent boundary chart will also require agreement with that chart's level-four coordinates.

Write $B_U\subset V^H$ for the coordinate ring of $U$ and form the ring of functions $F:T\to V$ whose restriction $h\mapsto F(t+h)$ lies in $B_U$ for every $t$. Each $h\in H$ extends to a $V$-section of $U$ by properness, so translating an element of $B_U$ by $h$ preserves $B_U$. After choosing coset representatives this ring is $\prod_{T/H}B_U$. Its spectrum $\mathcal G_4$ is finite flat. To add two cosets, add on their copies of $U$ and translate by the section whose generic point is the sum of their representatives minus the representative of the sum. This gives the group law. Changing representatives amounts to translation on those copies. All group identities hold on the generic fibre and hence everywhere by flatness. Its identity neighbourhood is the one in $U$. Consequently
$$
  \omega_{\mathcal G_4}=\omega_U=\omega_{G[p^4]}=\omega_G/p^4\omega_G .
$$
The last equality follows from the conormal sequence of the kernel of $[p^4]$ at the identity, since pullback on invariant differentials is multiplication by $p^4$.

Apply Fargues's finite flat theorem to $\mathcal G_4^\vee$. Its Hodge–Tate map takes points of the Cartier dual to their logarithmic differentials on $\mathcal G_4$. Over $V$, every generic character extends uniquely to a $V$-point of that dual by properness. The perfect pairing identifies all those characters with $T$. Restricting their differentials to $U$ computes precisely the finite-level differentials of the corresponding generic characters there, since the extensions agree by separatedness. The theorem says that the cokernel is killed by every $\kappa$ with $v_p(\kappa)\ge 1/(p-1)$. For odd $p$ take $\kappa=\zeta_p-1$, since the product of its $p-1$ unit multiples is $p$ up to a unit. At $2$ the prescribed $\kappa=4$ qualifies. Thus the integral chart lattice $\mathcal H\subset\omega_G$ generated by lifts of these differentials satisfies $\kappa\omega_G\subset\mathcal H+p^4\omega_G$. On $D=\omega_G/\mathcal H$ this says $\kappa D=p^4D$. The finitely generated module $\kappa D$ equals $(p^4/\kappa)(\kappa D)$, and $p^4/\kappa$ is in the maximal ideal. Nakayama gives $\kappa D=0$. This removes the finite-level error and gives $\kappa\omega_G\subset\mathcal H\subset\omega_G$. The argument uses the finite flat theorem even when $G[p^4]$ itself has extra generic points outside its finite flat open. [Fargues, §5.4, Theorem 3][rt-fargues] · [Depth-four chart construction][rt-ht]

On a common normal chart, coherent generically full lattices and all finitely many flag steps can be made locally free at once in the following sense. Take a presentation by a vector bundle surjecting onto each coherent sheaf (on a projective model twist to obtain generators). On the open where the sheaves have their specified ranks take the graph of the corresponding sections of their relative Grassmannians of quotients. Normalize its closure in the product. The original relations vanish generically and hence in the locally free quotient. The kernel of the resulting surjection is precisely the torsion, by equality of ranks and torsion freeness of the target. Thus the universal quotient is the pullback modulo torsion. For inclusions use the flag Grassmannian of the generic steps in this quotient. The tautological flag on the graph closure gives subbundles and locally free graded pieces. This operation preserves the original flags where they already have these properties. To identify the new lattices with the prescribed chart lattices one needs compatibility of the chart construction with this pullback and with the valued points, as in the local model.

For such lattices the sandwich on a height-one valuation implies the inclusions on the chart. Indeed a normal noetherian domain is the intersection of its height-one DVRs in its fraction field. Apply this to matrix entries on a trivializing open. A valuation of an algebraically closed extension centered on a height-one prime detects negative orders there. At the other primes the chart uses the common de Rham comparison. Applying the same eigenprojection to both sides preserves the inclusions for its image lattices, and taking duals of these free lattices reverses the two bounds, giving $\kappa\mathcal H^\vee\subset\omega_G^\vee\subset\mathcal H^\vee$. Here is the check, including the places over $2$. Put $I=\xi^{-1}\mathfrak D_{E/\mathbb Q}^{-1}$. Trace duality and stability under $\mathcal O_E$ identify $L_i$ with $\{x:h_i(x,L_i)\subset I\}$. At a finite place of $F$ it has a generator in $F_v^\times$, because the relative algebra is split or unramified. Divide the form by that generator. The resulting integral Hermitian module is unimodular. At a split place it is a pair of lattices in perfect duality, and dual bases identify any two pairs of the same rank. At an inert place the residue field extension is quadratic separable. Some vector has nonzero norm in the residue Hermitian form. If all diagonal values vanished, evaluating on $x+c y$ for all residue scalars $c$ would make every cross pairing zero by nondegeneracy of the field trace. Lift the vector. Its norm is a unit and the line it spans splits off orthogonally. Every unit of the base is a norm of a unit. First solve modulo the uniformizer using the norm of the finite fields. A multiplicative correction $1+\pi^j t$ changes the norm at order $j$ by the residue trace of $t$. Its surjectivity and completeness lift the solution, also in residue characteristic two. The signatures give isometry at the infinite places. Landherr's local-global theorem for Hermitian spaces over a quadratic extension places the two rational spaces in one space $V$. After this identification the two integral lattices agree away from finitely many places. The integral isometries at the remaining places give $g\in U(V)(\mathbb A_{F,f})$ carrying one lattice to the other. The stabilizer of a full principal marking consists of automorphisms congruent to the identity on the marked lattice, independently of its chosen frame. The transport is $E$-linear, so it respects the two complementary ideals at $3,5$ as well. Hence conjugation by $g$ carries the full local level groups to the other ones. <span id="claim:GRT15"></span>At the boundary valuation of GRT6 write $g$ for the generic abelian dimension and $a,t$ for the abelian dimension and torus rank of the special fibre, so $g=a+t$. For each $j\ge1$ let $U_j$ be the finite open subgroup containing the whole closed fibre of $G[p^j]$. Its rank is $p^{j(2a+t)}$ by the torus and abelian multiplication sequences on that fibre. The full generic torsion is $T_j$ of order $p^{2gj}$, and $H_j=U_j(V)=U_j(C)$ by properness. Both the natural inclusion and multiplication by $p$ preserve the finite parts at their respective levels. In fact the inverse image of the complement of the target open is closed in the finite source and has empty closed fibre, so has empty image over the local base.

The map $[p]:U_{j+1}\to U_j$ is finite faithfully flat. On the closed fibre it has constant finite flat degree $d=p^{2a+t}$ by the isogeny sequences there. If the two finite free $V$-algebras are $B,B'$, then $B'/\mathfrak m_V B'$ is free of rank $d$ over $B/\mathfrak m_V B$ since the latter is a product of Artinian local rings. Lift its basis. The resulting $B^d\to B'$ has equal finite $V$-ranks and invertible determinant modulo $\mathfrak m_V$, hence is an isomorphism. It follows also that $H_{j+1}\to H_j$ is onto. The inclusion $U_j\to U_{j+1}$ is closed by the surjection of coordinate algebras on the closed fibre and Nakayama over $V$. Moreover $H_{j+1}\cap T_j=H_j$. An extending point killed generically by $p^j$ factors through $G[p^j]$ by separatedness and then through its finite open, since its closed point lies there.

Use copies of $U_j$ indexed by $T_j/H_j$ to form $\mathcal G_j$ as in GRT6. For each residue class $\beta\bmod NX$, a degree-$N$ Fourier coefficient is a section of $L_B^N$ twisted by the character line of $\beta$ on the abelian quotient $B$. The period equation transports this section from $\beta$ to $\beta+Nz$. In the integral abelian frames the order increases by
$$
 \frac{Q(\beta+Nz)-Q(\beta)}{N}
   =Q(\beta+z)-Q(\beta)-Q(z)+NQ(z).
$$
The first three terms are the valuation of the bilinear period pairing, and the last is the valuation of the multiplier to power $N$. Translation on the good-reduction abelian part transports its lattice of sections isomorphically. Thus extracting the $Q(\beta)/N$ factor gives one free integral coefficient lattice per class.
