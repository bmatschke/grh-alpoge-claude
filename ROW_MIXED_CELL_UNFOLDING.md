# The mixed-cell expansion on the prescribed opposite path

Fix a sufficiently deep choice in the stated Hida setting. Write $\Phi_a$ for its prescribed finite datum and $S_{t,a}$ for the set of contributing families of finite double cosets. For every finite combination $\tau_\infty$ of real polynomial pairs on the opposite path there are a finite combination $\sigma$ on the two definite paths and character values $z'_t\in\langle\xi_{t,w}(x)\rangle$ such that
$$
 B^+(\phi_a,\operatorname{lift}_{\rm opp}(\tau_\infty,\tau;F_{t,y}))
 =
 (z'_t)^{-1}s_{AA}
 \sum_{\beta\in S_{t,a}}
 \left(\prod_w c_{w,\beta}\right)
 \operatorname{defTot}(M_\beta,\sigma,\tau_\beta;\phi_a,F_{t,y}).
$$
Here $\tau$ is **any** finite tensor realizing the flipped product on the indicated split halves. The incoming measure $M$ is any measure normalized for $t$. Each $M_\beta$ is independently normalized for its term levels, and $\tau_\beta$ is any expansion of the prescribed local term product. The same $\sigma$ works for all these choices and all $t,a,y$. The character factor can be chosen before $\tau_\infty$. This is the mixed-cell identity, preceding evaluation by jets. Its inputs are the row's listed cuspidal lift theorem, cusp rapid decay, the finite lattice and local mixed-model calculations described below, and the compact real branching law. The pin derives the required cuspidal theorem from its discrete-series, Harish-Chandra, cusp-decay and Casselman–Wallach inputs. Application on the two prescribed grid paths also uses existence of the required unitary discrete series. [Right-edge specialization][right-ds] · [Down-edge specialization][down-ds]

The finite-sum producer uses the particular Hida level and disjointness of the auxiliary primes, even though its signature accepts the broader published-input package. [Quantifiers and definitions][statement] · [Cuspidal input][cusp] · [Finite-sum producer][sum] · [Separate dependencies of unfolding][join]

## Absolute integrability in the prescribed order

Work first with one incoming real polynomial pair $P$ and one summand of $\tau$. The split-half support condition gives the localized Weil vectors on all the edges of this summand. The listed cusp condition persists under the row lift and each remaining down-lift. In particular the entering function $G$ at the noncompact source of each unfolding satisfies, for all $A$,
$$
 |G(h)|\,\operatorname{ht}_{[H]}(h)^A\le B_A.
$$
In the mixed model the unitary flag character selects the orbit of the designated rational frame $x_0$. The bound on the sum of the absolute theta terms, extended from this orbit to all rational matrices, is
$$
 \sum_x |T_x(ng,h)|\le C\operatorname{ht}(g)^A\operatorname{ht}_{[H]}(h)^A.
$$
It holds uniformly for the flag-unipotent variable used in the integral. Multiply by $|G(h)|$ and integrate over the flag quotient of mass one and the source quotient of finite volume. Unfolding the resulting positive orbit sum bounds the integral of the single frame term over its rational stabilizer quotient by $C'\operatorname{ht}(g)^A$. The absolute value of the flat transform is bounded by the corresponding integral of the absolute mixed kernel. This proves convergence on the unfolded stabilizer quotient even when its volume is infinite. For the remaining definite group the factor is continuous on a compact quotient, and the same polynomial bound is uniform on compact sets of representatives. This supplies Fubini for the Bessel-to-Fourier–Jacobi step. The reverse step uses the Fourier–Jacobi character integral and the corresponding absolute estimate. [Absolute rational-orbit estimate][bound] · [Unfolded stabilizer bound][stab] · [Bessel step and compact-quotient Fubini][bf] · [Fourier–Jacobi step][fb]

At a pair of successive edges the outer stabilizer integral is absolutely convergent because the function entering it is already a rapidly decreasing lift. For each fixed outer point the inner integral is absolutely convergent by the reverse one-edge bound applied to the original cusp form and the translated test. This justifies the ordered pair. Each one-edge use of Fubini exchanges the stabilizer variable with a compact quotient variable. [Two-step integrability in this order][paired-L1]

Apply the two identities successively along the down path, ending in the definite period. The nontrivial row variables have indices $j=1,\ldots,m-2$, in the prescribed quotient-iteration order. For $m=2$ this list is empty. At each step one obtains an integrable quotient function after the inner integrations have been performed. Summing over the finitely many original pure terms preserves integrability and linearity. This establishes precisely the ordered iterates needed below, with common Haar measures on the integrating row groups. At those vertices the normalizing compact levels agree across tuples, so their Haar measures agree. The associated stabilizer measures use these same definite Haar measures and the fixed mass-one unipotent measures. [Agreement of incoming Haar measures][row-haar] · [Stabilizer quotient measures][row-stab] The proof uses only the indicated exchanges, so it also applies without an absolute majorant for the product of *all* noncompact row variables at once. [Row indices][indices] · [Alternating iteration][chain] · [Actual finite tensors and common measures][iter]

## Which finite cells contribute

Write $q_P(h)$ for the resulting mixed kernel with its finite summands already combined. For active $j$ let $R_j$ denote the Bessel or Jacobi stabilizer and $K_j$ the prescribed finite lattice group. The period is left $R_j$-invariant. For right $K_j$-invariance move the level element onto the two adjacent factors using the Weil action. The resulting simultaneous action fixes the specified finite product function. Multilinearity then gives invariance of $q_P$. The definition of a flipped realization imposes this product identity on the sum, so imposing invariance on each decomposition summand would add a hypothesis. [Moving the level elements and summing][invariance]

The [finite support proof](ROW_MIXED_CELL_SUPPORT.md) starts with a nonzero paired contraction, transports the lattice vacua along the unmarked edges, and reads the marked double cosets after the prescribed tilt. This gives a unique candidate label. If the label lies outside $S_{t,a}$, the local data admit the zero expansion, and multilinearity of the definite period forces $q_P$ to vanish there. The finite set is independent of the real polynomial and the input vector. On the restricted untwisted column even the union over tuples and finite tests has at most one label. A surviving cell can have zero period. [Finite support and expansion on the given datum][finite]

## Value on a cell and the common real functional

Fix the finite Haar measures used to disintegrate the row quotients. Let $w_\beta$ be the product of the finite quotient masses of the component cells. For a real row argument $p$ and incoming pair $P$ there is a finite real polynomial combination $\sigma_{p,P}$ such that on every contributing cell,
$$
 w_\beta q_P(p b_\beta)
  =(z'_t)^{-1}\left(\prod_w c_{w,\beta}\right)
    \operatorname{defTot}(M_\beta,\sigma_{p,P},\tau_\beta;\phi_a,F_{t,y}).
$$
The order of construction matters. At a marked place use the compactly supported split matrix kernels in the chosen frame. Group the $m$ arrows in adjacent pairs. At every interior pair the successive Bessel and Jacobi flat transforms give the pair's linear expansion against the two outgoing functions. The last pair has its own terminal formula. Multiply these identities and use the defining equality of finite product functions for $\tau_\beta$. This is an identity on all the matrix arguments, so it survives any admissible choice of expansion. The computation in the standard character convention takes the common tilt sign $-1$. On the contributing labels the staircase condition fixes the phase. In the pin's notation the flat phase is $z_1(t)(\prod_{k<m/2}z_2(t,2k))^{-1}$, with $z_2(t,2k)$ supplied by comparison with the base vacuum. The scalar common to the local products can be inserted once, at index zero. [Paired transforms on the original local data][pairs] · [Staircase on support][stair] · [Flat phase and the single common scalar][phase]

On the definite column, averaging the paired real kernel over the incoming compact center against the appropriate character leaves the lift unchanged, by Haar translation and the central transformation law of the incoming form. A second central average at the middle group fixes the character on the other factor. For a definite oscillator with its fixed lattice vacuum, only finitely many Fock monomials have any prescribed central weight. Orthogonal projection onto their span leaves a vector orthogonal to those monomials by construction and to all others by the different unitary central characters. Totality of the Fock polynomials makes that remainder zero. Applying this to the two factors gives a finite sum of polynomial pairs. Equality of the real Weil actions across tuples follows from the equality of their two splitting-character exponents at every real place. Thus each two-edge reduction, including its next central character, works simultaneously for all tuples. The starting column has the same central character for every catalogue index by Schur's lemma on its coefficient module. [Two averages and the finite polynomial pairs][cell-favg] · [Bounded Fock degrees and totality][cell-fdeg] · [Common real actions][cell-feq] · [Iteration from the catalogue column][cell-find]

One more normalization identifies this expansion with the actual term on each finite cell. In an allowed expansion the moved column vector on edge $j$ is $\kappa_j z_{t,j}$ times the specified Heisenberg combination of the common column vacuum. The moved row vector on edge $i$ is $\kappa'_i$ times its specified combination. Here $\kappa_j,\kappa'_i\in\mathbf C^\times$ are independent of the tuple and the expansion, and $z_{t,j}\in\langle\xi_{t,w}(x)\rangle$. These statements use the finite moved-vacuum identity on the actual test vectors, applied term by term using the vector clause of `SumOK`. Multiply the column polynomial combination by $(\prod_j\kappa_j\prod_i\kappa'_i)^{-1}$ and pair it with the original row polynomial. Multilinearity cancels those two products and leaves precisely $(\prod_j z_{t,j})^{-1}$ multiplying the definite sum. Continuity and rational invariance of the remaining row lift follow by integration on the definite quotients. This gives the Fock comparison phase and a polynomial combination independent of the measures, finite expansion and tuple. [Moved vectors of both orientations][cell-fmv] · [Product cancellation and continuity of the row lift][cell-fprod]

For the ordered integral, change the definite quotients to the term measures $M_\beta$. The [Haar ratio computation](ROW_PERIOD_JET_FORMULA.md#the-cell-mass-on-the-mixed-side) identifies the product of these ratios and $w_\beta$ with $\prod_w c_{w,\beta}$ times a scalar common to all the configurations. Absorb that scalar in the polynomial combination. Together the finite flat and moved-vacuum comparisons give the factor $(z'_t)^{-1}$. This yields the cell identity with $\sigma_{p,P}$ independent of $t,a,\beta,\tau,M,M_\beta,\tau_\beta,y$. [Relative vacuum factors at the same real points][relative] · [Assembly of the kernel and Haar comparisons][value]

To obtain the common evaluation for branching, allow both endpoint covectors to vary in the definite period as well as every Fock polynomial. The two paths have continuous, rationally invariant integrands and terminate on a compact quotient, so these generalized periods converge and are multilinear. Transport the two lists of polynomials into their fixed compact Fock modules and identify the endpoint covectors with the two coefficient representations, using the dual at the prescribed endpoint. The resulting evaluation $e(Q)$ is the tensor of the transported polynomials with the fixed pair of coefficient vectors. In the source these last factors are $A_\psi^{-1}\xi$ and $B_f^{-1}(\operatorname{pr}_X^{\mathrm{flip}}(v))$. All factors in this evaluation map are fixed before the tuple and cell are chosen. Changing variables by the compact groups at the vertices proves invariance of each generalized period functional on this tensor product. The two actions at every internal vertex cancel, and the two endpoint changes are absorbed by the equivariant coefficient identifications. Thus every specialized definite period equals $L(e(Q))$ for some invariant functional $L$ on the **same** representation. The compact branching calculation gives this invariant space dimension one, so each $L$ is a multiple of a fixed generator $L_0$, and $Q\mapsto L_0(e(Q))$ is the required common function of the polynomial data. The proof uses the prescribed endpoint coefficient identifications and convergence for all endpoint covectors, as well as compact multiplicity one. [Generalized definite integrals and convergence][cell-igen] · [Common tensor and invariant functional][cell-icom] · [Endpoint coefficient identifications][cell-iend] · [Final interlacing and dimension one][cell-ibr]

Denote the common functional on polynomial combinations by $\ell$. If the evaluation on this line is identically zero, every definite period vanishes and any nonzero $\ell$ will do. Choose a polynomial combination $\sigma_*$ with $\ell(\sigma_*)=1$. [Passage through the invariant line][line] · [Zero case and normalization][unit]

For fixed $P$ let $i$ index all admissible configurations including measures and expansions. Denote the original ordered integral by $L_i$ and the finite sum on the right by $G_i$, evaluated at $\sigma_*$, including $(z'_t)^{-1}$ and omitting $s_{AA}$. After integrating the finite cells at fixed real $p$, the preceding identity gives $\ell(\sigma_{p,P})G_i$. Consider two configurations and any relation $uG_i+vG_j=0$. The corresponding weighted kernel on the finite union of both sets of cells has zero finite integral at every real argument. Integrating successively over the remaining quotients gives $uL_i+vL_j=0$. Indeed each stage of the ordered integral is linear on the known integrable kernels, the real and finite components of each row group split, and disintegration in that row gives zero whenever all its inner finite projections vanish. This argument avoids having to choose a measurable family of polynomial representatives or integrate its coefficients separately. [Linear relations passed through the quotient iterates][relations]

If some $G_i\ne0$, set $c_P=L_i/G_i$. The two-term relations give $L_j=c_PG_j$ for every $j$. If every $G_i=0$, the same argument gives $L_i=0$ for every $i$ and one takes $c_P=0$. Finally write $\tau_\infty=\sum_P b_P[P]$ and take $\sigma=s_{AA}^{-1}(\sum_P b_Pc_P)\sigma_*$. The auxiliary-vacuum factor $s_{AA}$ is nonzero by its normalized conductor calculation. This proves the asserted identity even if the period vanishes on all prescribed finite tests. Nonvanishing of the resulting common period scalar, and arithmetic descent across twists, are separate questions. [Recombination with the prescribed normalization][final]
