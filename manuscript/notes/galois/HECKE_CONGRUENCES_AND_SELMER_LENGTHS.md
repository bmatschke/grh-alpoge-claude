# Why a Selmer length bounds a denominator in the theta tower

At a coefficient place fix the discrete valuation ring $O$, its fraction field $K$, a uniformizer $\varpi$, and its residue characteristic $\ell$. Write $v(\varpi)=1$. The local Hecke algebra $T$ acts on a finite set $X$ of characteristic-zero eigensystems, and its eigenvalues are integral. Let $Z\subset X$ consist of the systems occurring in the theta lift. For one selected good system $\pi\in Z$, the ideal
$$
 \mathfrak c_\pi=\{h(\pi):h\in T,\ h(x)=0\ \text{for every }x\in X\setminus Z\}\ \subseteq O
$$
measures the loss from isolating the theta part at $\pi$. If it equals $\varpi^hO$, there is an integral operator zero on the unwanted systems and acting on $\pi$ by $\varpi^h$ times a unit. On a lattice in a space of dimension $D$, multiplication by that scalar has cokernel of length $Dh$. This explains why a denominator valuation naturally occurs multiplied by a rank. For example, take $X$ to consist of two points and $T=\{(a,b)\in O^2:a-b\in\varpi^hO\}$. The projector to the first coordinate is defined over $K$, and its least integral multiple has scalar ideal exactly $\varpi^hO$. In the tower $Z$ can contain many systems. Off the type primes, multiplying by the operator that cuts out the good systems costs an additional $v_\ell(M_0)$ on each retained system. The quotient length is bounded by summing the two losses with their eigenspace multiplicities. ## Bounding the ideal by determinants of relations

Here is the local algebra behind that bound. At $\pi$ the representation splits into blocks $M_j$ with chosen integral lattices of ranks $d_j$. In the lifted matrix algebra $\mathcal A$ the map to each $M_{d_j}(O)$ is surjective. On the higher-rank block this uses absolute irreducibility in the residue field, the matrix-algebra theorem for an absolutely irreducible representation, and Nakayama's lemma. Let $g$ generators present the column module, and let $R\subseteq \mathcal A^g$ be the left module of relations among them. A solution at block $j$ assigns values in $(K/O)^{d_j}$ to all $g$ generators, subject to every relation. Write $S_j$ for this module of solutions. The following proof applies when all its lengths are finite. [Matrix-algebra and support conditions on this datum][hc-relate]

Form the row lattice $L_j\subseteq O^{g d_j}$ generated by all scalar rows obtained by evaluating all the relations in block $j$. Its annihilator under evaluation on $(K/O)^{g d_j}$ is exactly $S_j$. In Smith normal form, an elementary divisor $\varpi^a$ contributes the length $a$ to $S_j$, since $\varpi^{-a}O/O$ is the corresponding solution module. A missing pivot would contribute a copy of $K/O$, which finite length excludes. Thus $L_j$ has full rank and a basis whose square determinant has valuation $\operatorname{length}_O S_j$. This basis arises from an array of actual relations. Multiplying a relation on the left by a lift of a matrix unit puts any specified scalar row into any designated slot and makes its other rows zero. Taking sums realizes any chosen row of $L_j$ in that slot. Group the $g d_j$ basis rows into $g$ lists of $d_j$ rows each. Filling the slots in this manner gives a $g$-by-$g$ relation array attaining the required determinant valuation in block $j$. Denote that array by $A_j$. [Elementary divisors and matrix-unit lifting][hc-smith]

Different blocks can require different arrays. Introduce an indeterminate $z_j$ for each, and consider the single array $A(\mathbf z)=\sum_j z_j A_j$. Write $P_j(\mathbf z)$ for its scalar determinant on block $j$ and $P=\prod_j P_j$. The support theorem for these relations says that at any unwanted characteristic-zero system there is a nonzero simultaneous solution. Hence the full determinant is identically zero at that system, coefficient by coefficient. At the ordered-root level the coefficient algebra is finite free over $T$ and generically étale. Vanishing on every ordering over an unwanted system makes every coordinate in a $T$-basis vanish there. Evaluating at the selected ordered point shows that every coefficient of $P$ lies in $\mathfrak c_\pi$. The existence of the unwanted-system solution and this support property are the arithmetic inputs to the elementary calculation. [Construction of the array and descent of its coefficients][hc-relate]

One can see the source of the common kernel by counting local equations. Fix an unwanted point, write $V$ for its representation, and put $n=\dim V$. At a column place $u$, the admissible local cocycles form the inverse image of the Bloch–Kato finite space in $Z^1(E_u,V)$. The coboundaries have dimension $n-h_u^0$, where $h_u^0=\dim H^0(E_u,V)$. The crystalline dimension formula gives the finite cohomology dimension $h_u^0+j_u$, with $j_u$ fixed by the common Hodge–Tate jumps. Thus the local equations take values in a space of dimension $n+j_u$. The unknowns consist of one global vector on the rank-one projector line and, for each $u$, a vector in a hyperplane, $j_u$ line coordinates, and one more line coordinate. There are precisely $1+\sum_u(n+j_u)$ unknowns and at most $\sum_u(n+j_u)$ equations. A nonzero solution gives a nonzero assignment to the generators. If its global vector is nonzero, absence of invariants off the theta locus makes some global generator value nonzero. Otherwise a local generator already has a nonzero value. [The column equations and their dimensions][hc-cocycle]

For any nonzero polynomial over $O$ let $c(P)$ be the least valuation of its coefficients. Reduction of primitive polynomials modulo $\varpi$ proves $c(P_1P_2)=c(P_1)+c(P_2)$, even over a finite residue field. At the vector $\mathbf e_j$ with only $z_j=1$, $P_j$ specializes to the determinant attaining the length of $S_j$. Consequently
$$
 \operatorname{length}_O(O/\mathfrak c_\pi)
 \le c(P)=\sum_j c(P_j)
 \le \sum_j v(P_j(\mathbf e_j))=\sum_j\operatorname{length}_O S_j .
$$
For a small example of why the coefficients matter, $XY(X-Y)$ has a unit coefficient over $\mathbb Z_2$, while every evaluation at $X,Y\in\mathbb Z_2$ is even. A requirement that all blocks attain their least valuation at the same scalar choice could lose a factor of two in this example. ## From pointwise finiteness to a height estimate

For every positive twist and every finite character the strict Selmer group is finite. On a generic orbit, set $F$ equal to the fixed field of its common kernel. The full sum of normalized strict lengths at $\ell$ equals the $\mathbf Z_\ell$-length of the primitive part of $K_{2e}(\mathcal O_F)$ at $\ell$. Forming the least integer with the required valuations isolates the remaining size inequality in the height argument. The congruence quotient used in the separate ordinary-family proof must still have the automorphic support required there. ## Which solutions contribute a global cohomology length?

In the split congruence calculation, the blocks at the selected point, after twisting by the inverse of a common character $\chi$, consist of a rank-$r$ lattice $W$ and the characters $O(-e)$ for $0\le e<t$. The column relations contain the elements
$$
 y(g)=(\chi(g)^{-1}\widetilde r(g)-1)E_0,
$$
where $E_0$ is an integral multiple of the projector to the first character in the character block. Multiplication gives $y(gh)=y(g)+\chi(g)^{-1}\widetilde r(g)y(h)$. A solution of the relations, viewed as an equivariant homomorphism into its torsion block, therefore sends $y(g)$ to a Galois cocycle. Although the displayed formula takes a difference in the ambient algebra, the solution is defined on the column module. Its value on $E_0$ need not be defined. This is how the cocycle can represent a nonzero class. [Scaled projector and column relation][hc-cocycle]

At a column place $u$ the local relation on the negative-twist block, for $1\le e<t$, reads
$$
 \phi(y(g))=-(\chi(g)^{-1}\widetilde r(g)-1)\phi(x_u^{(0)}),
 \qquad g\in G_{E_u}.
$$
The other terms in the relation vanish on this block. The restriction is a coboundary. Away from the column places the proof charges the lengths of the allowed nonzero local images and the exceptional inertia terms. The classes left over on $O(-e)$ are locally trivial at the required places and unramified outside $\ell$. They are measured by the strict global kernel $\operatorname{Sha}_{\ell,e}(E)$. The trivial block instead contributes the $\ell$-part of the class number by reciprocity, and $W$ contributes its own Selmer group with its prescribed finite local conditions. Kernels of the map from solutions to cocycles, coboundaries, and the costs of imposing the other local conditions have their own terms in the estimate. [Cocycles, local images, and the three different global groups][hc-global]

One of these local costs can be read directly. Interpolating the first character eigenvalue $\alpha_0$ among the ordered eigenvalues uses the scaled projector $E_0$ with scalar $\Delta=\prod_{1\le e<t}(\alpha_0-\alpha_e)$. At the chosen point the factors differ by cyclotomic powers of a unit $u$, with $v_\ell(u-1)=s_0$, where $v_\ell(\ell)=1$. In the depth range used for this formula ($s_0\ge2$ at $2$ and $s_0\ge1$ at odd $\ell$), the binomial theorem gives $v_\ell(u^e-1)=s_0+v_\ell(e)$. Raising a principal unit to a power prime to $\ell$ preserves the first nonzero valuation and raising it to the $\ell$th power increases that valuation by one. Thus
$$
 v_\ell(\Delta)=(t-1)s_0+v_\ell((t-1)!).
$$
The empty product has valuation zero at $t=1$. This calculation accounts for the marked projector in the split congruence estimate. Throughout this passage ordinary $O$-lengths are divided by the ramification index $v(\ell)$ to obtain the normalized lengths used in the global sum. [Valuation at cyclotomic depth][hc-cyc] · [Projection and the signed range of twists][hc-split]

At a fixed completed host set $s_{\ell,e}=\operatorname{length}_{\mathbb Z_\ell}\operatorname{Sha}_{\ell,e}(E)$. These lengths are finite and nonnegative. Extending a finite $\mathbb Z_\ell$-module $A$ to $O$ gives $\operatorname{length}_O(O\otimes A)=v(\ell)\operatorname{length}_{\mathbb Z_\ell}A$, by filtering $A$ with quotients $\mathbb F_\ell$. Finite free scalar extension commutes with the continuous cochain complexes and the restriction kernels defining the strict group. Thus the normalized length at the coefficient place recovers $s_{\ell,e}$ exactly. [Definition of the strict group and host estimate](https://github.com/rh-lean-roadmap/rh-lean-roadmap/blob/c7755c1086f05d0918ee8496d95912e4f35e22f9/docs/src/book/main.tex#L11774-L11786) · [Local length normalization](https://github.com/rh-lean-roadmap/rh-lean-roadmap/blob/c7755c1086f05d0918ee8496d95912e4f35e22f9/docs/src/book/main.tex#L22031-L22046)

The host bounds every finite sum of $\log\ell\sum_{1\le e<t}s_{\ell,e}$ by $\sqrt b$. Each prime making a nonzero contribution adds at least $\log 2$, hence only finitely many can contribute at that host. In particular
$$
 D_{\rm Sha}=\prod_\ell\ell^{\sum_{1\le e<t}s_{\ell,e}}
     =\prod_{\ell,\,1\le e<t}\#\operatorname{Sha}_{\ell,e}(E),
 \qquad \log D_{\rm Sha}\le\sqrt b .
$$
The host may change with $x$. What is uniform is the inequality. After the remaining blocks and the local costs have been summed, the denominator theorem bounds the weighted cut length in each eligible layer by $D_0\{C(fmbL^2+(\log x)^2N_c)+\sqrt b\}$, with $D_0$ the actual reference rank. This recovers the input used for the period heights.
