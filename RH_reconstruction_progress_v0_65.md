## Why one component forces all the others

Let a residual point lie on several minimal components of the deformation space. Assume the potential modularity ideal $J$ vanishes on one component through the point. The source theorem asks for vanishing on every such component, for the same space, ideal and point, when the tower degree satisfies $d\ge 30(C+1)^2$. The remaining case in the earlier proof involved a residual flag with a nonzero entry in position $(1,0)$. [Component argument](continuation_v65/component.tex) · [Source statement](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/ModularityArgument/ThresholdArguments/DenseThresholdCases/PromodularityCaseThree.lean#L26-L32)

For eight possible reducible supports, a degeneration retaining precisely that flag coordinate bounds the dimensions of the unwanted loci by $d+20C+1$. The ninth support preserves the two symplectic planes spanned by $e_0,e_3$ and by $e_1,e_2$. The first flag vector projects nontrivially onto both. Its eigencharacter therefore occurs on both planes, which forces an equality of local characters on the entire decomposition group. There is a second saving in dimension. Projectors onto the four marked eigenlines turn traces of products into products of opposite matrix entries. One chosen entry in each plane suffices to recover every matrix entry over the trace field with the marked eigenvalues adjoined. With four flag coordinates per place this gives the required extra dimension bound $2+4s$ for $s$ marked places. [Two-plane argument](continuation_v65/planes.tex)

These estimates produce a one-dimensional prime quotient in the intersection of adjacent components whose generic representation is absolutely irreducible and whose local characters are distinct in the required sense. The given potential modularity hypothesis then carries containment of $J$ across that intersection. The connectedness bound supplies a chain between any two minimal components through the residual point. [Dimension estimates and the propagation argument](continuation_v65/component.tex)

## What remains in the integral seed construction

The seed must begin with one split prime and one residual pair on the initial tower, chosen before all later towers. At each later acceptable tower on which the auxiliary character dies, an integral seed over a finite coefficient extension must recover that initial data. The new proof constructs the correcting scalar twist, verifies the integral and residual tensor matrices with their local flags, produces the two required nonzero entries, and computes the four ordinary weights. It proves irreducibility on the full cyclotomic kernel and identifies the exact cusp and twist supplied by the arithmetic hypotheses. The proof returns to the dihedral construction at that selected exponent over the same coefficient ring, producing the integral seed on every later allowable tower.
