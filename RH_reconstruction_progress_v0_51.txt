4. WHY THE SIMPLIFICATIONS HELP

Holomorphy without a continuous square root. The normalized theta scalar satisfies kappa_b^2=C det(h_b), with a separate choice at every frame. Every allowed section weight n is even: writing n=2m, its reading is (C det(h_b))^m times the reading with theta scalar one. The latter is holomorphic by the actual algebraic frame chart and the universal evaluation identity. Thus no continuous square-root choice is needed. For an arbitrary coefficient ring, locally only finitely many coefficients and nonvanishing denominators occur; their evaluated inverses are holomorphic after shrinking the neighborhood. This avoids analytifying an arbitrary Spec(R). NFL15 also retains the full arbitrary normed-space and within-set differentiability scope of the natural-rule theorem. See NFL13–NFL17.

The untwisted cohomology bound from CM units. LFC11–LFC12 use the appropriate Kummer eigenspace over F(mu_ell). The valuation contribution is separated integrally before reduction modulo ell. The CM-unit anti-invariants contribute only the root-of-unity term, and the class-group term uses the existing C0 through prime-to-ell restriction. This proves the required h1 bound without adding a Leopoldt premise. The subsequent Euler estimate keeps the actual local subspaces, continuous primitives and inflation defect before applying the exact retained P input. All 28 conditions are assembled on the same frame, with the same C0 and Big, in LFC21–LFC23.

Derivatives preserve the decay exponent. A compact conjugation-averaged smooth kernel acts by a nonzero scalar on the unique finite lowest compact type. Normalization makes it reproduce that type exactly. Right derivatives in the coefficient formula then move onto the compactly supported kernel, whose derivatives remain bounded on a fixed compact set. The original decay exponent survives every derivative word. This gives both literal tensor maps and their smooth L2 outputs. See BST1–BST5.

Ordered minors compute the actual periods. The Capelli entries do not commute. BST6–BST8 prove alternation using exterior variables and prove row-column equality by summing the commutator difference: each replacement minor occurs equally often with opposite signs. The resulting expansions keep the operator order. Ordinary determinant identities are used only for scalar change-of-frame matrices. For Q=g0^(-1) g P^(-1), d=2r+k, and j*=0 when r>0 or d when r=0, the witnesses are y=Q^(-1)e_j* and alpha=e_d^t Q. For r>0 the two periods equal kappa product_j(u_j-lambda_s-1/2) times the old period and kappa product_j(u_j-lambda_s+1/2) times the old period, where kappa=-T_z product_a |c_a|^2 / 4^r. For r=0 the corresponding prefactors are -sqrt(T_z) and +sqrt(T_z). All are nonzero under the original exclusions, with the original Haar pair, character and free translates unchanged. See BSE3–BSE8.

Build the integral complement after the rational splitting. DFD1–DFD7 first separate the prescribed character summands over the fraction field. For the complementary rational subspace W they take Lambda=W intersect O^N. This intersection is saturated, so over the original DVR both it and its quotient lattice are free. An integral basis b and retraction q give rho(g)=q R(g)b, providing integral continuous matrices. The construction does not assume an integral character splitting or continuity of a complex coefficient embedding.
