3. ONE NOETHERIAN MODEL FOR ALL THE ALGEBRAIC CHARTS

FCD1-FCD18 complete the original algebraic Noetherian section-cube model. Finite polynomial and matrix identities record both formal smoothness of the chart algebra and the projective splitting of its module. All their coefficients descend together to one finitely generated subring that works for every homogeneous chart. The chapter proves completion flatness through Artin-Rees and a formal-smooth retract, finite graded presentation up to chart-power torsion, and finite Cech cohomology by an explicit Laurent-monomial contraction and descending dimension shifting. Homogeneous localization, all restriction maps and arbitrary scalar extension give the original R0,C0,theta of exists_noetherian_model_grCube. Actual geometric sections and their localization/gluing comparison remain the next supplier; no global section-law closure is inferred.

The central efficiency gain is to descend finite certificates, rather than search among possible coefficient rings. For each homogeneous chart, the original smooth/projective data give finite matrices witnessing formal smoothness and a projective splitting. Put all coefficients of these certificates, for every chart, into one finitely generated subring R0 of the original ring R. The same polynomial and matrix identities remain true over R0, because the inclusion into R is injective. Hilbert basis makes R0 Noetherian. This is an actual construction of one common ring, and every later coefficient extension uses it.

Flatness is proved rather than assumed to descend. The formal-smoothness certificate supplies compatible lifts to the powers of the relation ideal, hence a section into the completed polynomial ring. The text proves the required Artin–Rees inclusion from the finite Rees module, deduces completion exactness on finite modules, and applies the ideal flatness criterion. The chart quotient is therefore a retract of a flat module. A second explicit retract handles the presented module. These proofs retain arbitrary original R and impose no flatness on R0→R.

The model also needs finite cohomology. For a shifted free graded module, the augmented localized Cech complex decomposes into Laurent-monomial complexes. For an exponent vector with at least one nonnegative coordinate, deleting that coordinate gives a signed contracting homotopy. Only exponent vectors negative in every coordinate can contribute at the top; there are finitely many at each fixed total degree. A finite graded presentation and descending dimension shifting then prove the result for all finite graded modules over R0. The ordinary degree-zero Cech group is handled separately, so the proof does not silently confuse augmented and ordinary complexes.

FCD18 assembles the full original exists_noetherian_model_grCube: one R0, one C0, and the literal scalar-extension comparison theta, with flat nonempty vertices, finite Cech cohomology and bijective comparisons on every nonempty vertex. GSL8 can now consume this exact model. Actual affine line-bundle projectivity and chart smoothness are also still required. The independent polarization, H1, rank and square-law suppliers remain.

4. THE FULL FINITE-POINT IMPLICATION AND ABSTRACT PROPAGATION

ACP1-ACP19 complete the full original FinPtOf implication at every IsCD test ring and the original abstract propagation principles. For the actual diagonal-residual IsLiftA points, C=k+m_A is a complete Noetherian local coefficient ring with residue k; finite residue degree is supplied by IsCD. The point-realization shortcut is specific and preserves the source scope. Let A be any original IsCD test ring, with its prescribed O→k→A scalar tower. IsCD says that A is a complete discrete valuation domain with finite residue field k_A. The field k therefore embeds into k_A with finite degree. Define C=k+m_A inside A. This ring has residue field k and maximal ideal m_A. For N≥1 its Nth maximal-ideal power equals m_A^N as a subset of A: t^N a is the product of N−1 copies of a uniformizer t and the element ta. Thus the inherited adic topology is exactly the required one, and C is complete.

Noetherianity is also written out. Lift a k-basis b_1,...,b_d of k_A. Successive residue extraction gives every a∈A as sum_i f_i(t)b_i, with f_i∈k[[t]]. Therefore A is finite over k[[t]], and C, which contains k[[t]], is a submodule of this finite module. It is finite over a Noetherian ring and hence Noetherian. This verifies every original IsCO requirement.

The actual finite-point consumer assumes literal diagonal residual reduction, through IsLiftA. All representation entries, the entries of inverse matrices, and the full ordinary-character values and their inverses consequently lie in C. The prescribed universality of Space realizes the point over this ring. The larger formal point_loc theorem permits merely triangular residual reduction and uses a more general construction; that theorem is neither assumed nor falsely declared proved here. The smaller argument suffices for the full FinPtOf consumer.

The same OffFin ideal is fixed before A and the representation. ACP11 therefore proves the whole original OffFin→FinPt implication, at every permitted test point. The analytic obstacle can be separated into compatible measure construction and arithmetic volume identities. The construction in GMR directly supplies the former. It chooses the standard adelic character once. Local Fourier inversion fixes each self-dual additive Haar measure. On the independent tuple locus, the Gram map gamma(X)=X*JX is a submersion even when its value is singular: if X*JW=1, the tangent vector WS/2 maps to any prescribed Hermitian S. Analytic coordinates therefore turn gamma into a projection. Their vertical Jacobians define the fiber measures, and overlap changes preserve them.

The original fiber-family condition requires continuity at every Hermitian parameter. The proof covers the compact support by finitely many smaller charts and applies dominated convergence. Two such families agree almost everywhere by disintegration, then everywhere by continuity and full support of additive Haar measure. The argument works for every fixed pair of additive Haar measures, which is needed by the broad intermediate local theorem. It does not assert ambient local finiteness near the dependent boundary of a singular fiber.

For nondegenerate rational T at a good finite place, integral frame counting and smooth lifting give the exact mass a_v=product_{j=n-a+1}^n(1−epsilon_v^j q_v^(−j)), where epsilon_v is 1 in the split case and −1 in the unramified quadratic case. Both finite-field counts are proved in the chapter. In the original range 1≤a and 2a<n, s=n−a+1≥3 and |log a_v|≤4q_v^(−s). The sum over finite places converges, so the product is finite and strictly positive. This proves nonvanishing of the normalization rather than inferring it from convergence alone.

Normalize each compact good-place fiber to mass one, form its probability product, and restore the explicit positive scalar. This produces the actual adelic fiber measure and every original pure-factor test identity. Local isometry extension and integral lifting identify this fiber with the actual adelic unitary orbit. The quotient formula then fixes the stabilizer Haar measure relative to the supplied ambient Haar. The local singular frame fiber is treated on its independent locus. An explicit upper-unitriangular section gives the actual S/S0 quotient.
