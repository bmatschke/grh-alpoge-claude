CONVENTIONS AND BACKGROUND

The readable notation E denotes the source's K. Its involution is complex conjugation at every embedding. F is the fixed field and f=[F:Q]. All Hermitian forms are conjugate linear in their first variable. An integral lattice is a full O_E-module with integral pairings; global freeness is never assumed. A genus is in one fixed rational Hermitian space. Its mass is the finite sum of reciprocal orders of the actual integral isometry groups, using the finiteness and positivity already reconstructed in IG9.

At a fixed-field finite prime p, R is the completed local integer ring, q is its residue cardinality, and B=O_E tensor R is either R×R or the unramified quadratic extension. Both members of a split prime pair are included in B. The symbol pi in local arguments denotes a uniformizer of R. It is not replaced by the rational residue characteristic, whose valuation need not be one.

Ordinary number-field and Dedekind-domain facts, completions and valuations, finite fields, weak approximation, finite-dimensional real and nonarchimedean linear algebra, Haar integration, elementary local analytic change of variables, Riemann sums, dominated convergence and absolutely convergent Euler products are background. Where a nonarchimedean implicit-function statement is needed, the text gives its convergent correction argument and bounds the denominators; in particular no division by 2 in an integral dyadic ring is assumed. Prior DR1 supplies the explicit trace/norm lifting argument, LP1–LP3 supply finite-support lattice intersection and patching, CR4 supplies finiteness of norm-one integral units, and IG9 supplies finite positive genus mass. QW1 supplies the already proved elementary Euler-product convergence and source normalization.

MF1 — FIXED FIELD, DIFFERENT AND EULER NORMALIZATION

The literal IsCMUnram predicate is stronger than a label saying “unramified.” At each prime it supplies an integral theta whose difference from its conjugate is a unit. The formulas expressing an element in the basis 1,theta show that the complete quadratic algebra has that integral basis. Its discriminant is a unit, so it is finite étale; this includes dyadic places. The trace pairing is perfect. The relative different is therefore the unit ideal.

The decomposition E=F+Fw for a nonzero skew element w proves [E:F]=2 and identifies the archimedean embeddings. Determinants of trace matrices give |disc E|=(disc F)^2. With v=|disc E|^(1/(2f))/2, this is disc F=(2v)^f. Ordinary Lebesgue measure on one complex coordinate for each conjugate pair gives covol(O_E)=2^(-f)sqrt(|disc E|)=v^f; the real Minkowski covolume of O_F is sqrt(disc F). These are the two different covolumes that later enter the ball count and the norm-value count.

At p put eta=1 for a split algebra and eta=-1 otherwise. A_m is the product over j=1,...,m of 1-eta^j q^(-j); e_m is its last factor. The source's Euler factors are indexed by primes of E and contain half powers. For a split pair the two equal factors multiply; an inert prime has E-norm q^2. In either case the resulting fixed-field factor is e_m^(-1). Thus the final L_m is exactly lValEta, not a second normalization differing by a square root.

MF2 — LOCAL CLASSIFICATION WITHOUT A DYADIC EXCEPTION

For the unramified quadratic extension, residue norm surjectivity is followed by successive corrections of the form 1+pi^j t. Modulo pi^(j+1), the correction changes the norm by 1+pi^j Tr(t). Residue trace is onto even in characteristic 2; no inverse of 2 is used. The corrections converge and retain the chosen nonzero residue. In the split algebra the norm is simply multiplication.

To diagonalize an integral Hermitian lattice, choose a pairing with minimal valuation. If a diagonal entry does not attain it, add a suitable multiple of the second vector to the first. The leading cross term is a nonzero residue trace for a suitable multiplier, so the new diagonal entry does attain the minimum. The orthogonal projection has integral coefficients because this minimum divides all pairings. Induction and norm surjectivity leave diagonal entries pi^a. In the split algebra the statement is Smith reduction of the perfect pairing between the two component modules. The exponents are the elementary divisors of the map to the dual module, so their multiset is invariant.

Over the fraction field, scaling coordinates reduces all exponents modulo two. A pair pi,pi represents 1 because a unit t of norm pi-1 exists, and the vector (pi^(-1),pi^(-1)t) has norm 1. Its complement has determinant of even valuation and hence can also be normalized. This gives a lattice of determinant valuation at most one in every local Hermitian space. In that minimal range the integral isometry type is determined by rank and determinant valuation. Keeping a supplied norm-one vector first gives the exact reference lattice used to pin the local scalar.

MF3 — RATIONAL LOCAL ORBITS, NOT JUST COMPLETED ORBITS

The reflection formula z -> z-(x-y) h(x-y,x)^(-1) h(x-y,z) carries x to y whenever their common norm is nonzero and the denominator is nonzero. Expanding the pairing proves isometry because the denominator plus its conjugate is h(x-y,x-y). If it vanishes, pass through -y, where the denominator is twice the common nonzero norm. This is a field calculation, so dyadic integrality is irrelevant. For a positive definite global space, the vanishing case is just x=y.

The source's local genus and fiber definitions use rational isometries with prescribed localized lattice action. A statement only about completed unitary groups would be insufficient. The Cayley map on the F-vector space of skew-adjoint matrices proves the needed weak approximation. Its domain maps onto the open chart where 1+g is invertible. A norm-one scalar arbitrarily near one moves any finitely prescribed local targets into that chart while avoiding finitely many forbidden eigenvalues. Such scalars themselves are obtained with the one-dimensional Cayley map; weak approximation makes the choice rational and simultaneous. Weak approximation in the remaining matrix coordinates then gives rational unitary approximations. Since lattice stabilizers are open, approximation gives exactly the required lattice action. This is a weak-approximation argument for this rational group; it makes no strong-approximation claim.

MF4 — THE ACTUAL BINARY HERMITIAN REPRESENTATION SUPPLIER

Let a,b,n be totally positive elements of F. At a split finite place the norm represents every field element. At a nonsplit unramified place, coefficients may be reduced by norms to valuation parity zero or one. If the parities of a,b differ, one coordinate has the target's parity and its unit part is supplied by the norm map. If their parities agree, their ratio differs from -1 by a norm and the binary form becomes a scalar multiple of a difference of two norms. The elementary difference-of-squares identity represents every target. This takes place over a fraction field and imposes no integral division by 2.

Choose w with bar(w)=-w and delta=w^2. The five-variable quadratic form a(X1^2-delta X2^2)+b(X3^2-delta X4^2)-n X5^2 is isotropic at every finite place by these explicit representations, and at every real place by its signature (4,1). Hasse–Minkowski supplies a global zero. Its last coordinate is nonzero, since otherwise positive definiteness at a real place forces all coordinates to vanish. Division by that coordinate gives a s bar(s)+b t bar(t)=n. This is exactly HermRepUnram. Diagonalizing a positive Hermitian space then gives the required representation of every totally positive n in every rank at least two.

MF5 — THE MATRIX DENSITY IS A SPECIFIED HAAR VOLUME

For a local lattice with Gram matrix H, d=ord(det H) equals the source vd: determinants of Gram matrices of arbitrary m-tuples generate the determinant ideal. The literal alphaL counts all endomorphism matrices X modulo pi^k satisfying X*HX=H modulo pi^k and divides by q^(k m^2). Once k>d, taking determinants forces N(det X) to be a unit, so these matrices are automorphisms. In the split case the two determinant valuations are nonnegative and have sum zero.

The ambient matrix space has F-dimension 2m^2 and the Hermitian target space has dimension m^2. At the identity the derivative of X*HX-H is Z*H+HZ. For a Hermitian target, choose its upper triangular entries and a trace-one coefficient on the diagonal to write it as Y+Y*. Multiplication by H^(-1) solves the derivative equation with fixed bounded denominators. On sufficiently small balls, successively correcting the linear error makes the quadratic error strictly smaller; the corrections converge. Thus the map has projection coordinates on those balls. A finite cover of the compact stabilizer works uniformly, and all sufficiently accurate approximate zeros lie in that cover by compactness. Counting inverse images of pi^k times the target integer lattice is therefore exactly the fiber measure times q^(-k m^2) for sufficiently large k. This proves stabilization and positivity of alphaL.

Under a basis change C, conjugation has Jacobian one on the full matrix space. The target change Y -> C*YC has Jacobian |N(det C)|^m on the Hermitian space: check diagonal C coordinate by coordinate, then elementary matrices. If t=ord N(det C), d changes to d+t and the fiber density changes by q^(m t). Consequently nu(L)=q^(-m d) alphaL is basis independent. Unitary translation has norm determinant one, so this defines the same Haar measure on U(V) for every lattice, and vol(K_L)=nu(L). Taking the volume of K_A intersect K_B gives the exact orientation nu(A) orb(B,A)=nu(B) orb(A,B), where orb(A,B)=[K_A:K_A intersect K_B]. This is LocalDensityRatio with its literal powers of q.

MF6 — FINITE UNITARY COUNTS AND AN EXACT DIVISOR FORMULA

The finite norm-vector count is computed directly. In the split case select a nonzero first component, then solve one linear scalar-product equation for the second. In the quadratic residue field, the one-coordinate norm has one zero preimage and q+1 preimages of each nonzero value; convolution gives the higher-rank counts. A nonzero residue vector is smooth for the norm map by trace perfection. This proves both the exact number of norm-one vectors and their q^(2m-1) lift count.

Choosing orthonormal columns yields the finite unitary-group order, and Z+Z*=Y lifts residue isometries with q^(m^2) choices at each step. Hence alphaL=A_m at every unimodular place. This fixes the density normalization independently of the later mass induction.

For e=ord(n), let rho_e=alphaN(n)/e_m. Primitive zero-residue vectors contribute 1+beta, where beta=eta^m q^(1-m). Vectors divisible by pi contribute beta^2 rho_(e-2). Thus rho_0=1, rho_1=1+beta and rho_e=sum_(j=0)^e beta^j. This exact formula is stronger than the source's later difference estimate. It produces the multiplicative divisor expansion used in MF16 and works without excluding dyadic places.

MF7 — GENERAL NORM DENSITIES AND THEIR MEAN

For a primitive vector x, the derivative of its norm has image pi^t R with 0<=t<=d. If all its coefficients were divisible by pi^(d+1), perfectness of the trace pairing followed by the adjugate identity would make x divisible by pi. Partition primitive vectors into balls modulo pi^(d+1). On each ball choose a coordinate attaining t. The same convergent correction argument makes the norm uniformly distributed on one coset modulo pi^(d+1+t), with the other 2m-1 coordinates free. The finite sum of these contributions is a bounded locally constant primitive density P.

Every nonzero vector is pi^r times a primitive one. Its contribution to the pushforward density is q^(-(2m-2)r) P(n/pi^(2r)), when pi^(2r) divides n. For n nonzero the sum is finite; for m>=2 it converges uniformly even at zero. The unresolved final ball in a finite congruence count has contribution at most q^(k-2m ceil(k/2))<=1. This proves uniform bounds in both n and k, local constancy away from zero, and stabilization for nonzero n. The conservative source bound k>=2d+2ord(n)+2 follows from the displayed primitive balls after scaling.

At each finite level, summing the congruence count over all norm residues counts every vector exactly once. Its normalized mean is one. Uniform domination, or directly the geometric tail just proved, passes that identity to the limit. Thus the local alphaN density integrates to one on R. No assertion about the singular value zero is inferred by cancelling a derivative there.

MF8 — FINITE LOCAL CUT FIBERS AND THE RELATIVE IDENTITY

Fix x0 of nonzero norm n and its orthogonal complement W. Every lattice Lambda of the required genus containing x0 and cutting W in T lies between Bx0+T and n^(-1)Bx0+T-dual. The quotient is finite. Its projection onto the x0-line has length at most 2ord(n). The determinant-index identity then bounds d_T by d_L+ord(n). MF2 gives only finitely many possible Jordan types. These statements give the literal finite fiber sets and local representative sets; they do not merely assert that a formal sum is finite.

Give V the basis-independent measure which is |det H_L| times coordinate measure. Its volume on L is q^(-d_L). Dividing by the norm-coordinate measure yields a measure sigma_n on the smooth norm-n fiber, whose value on L is q^(-d_L) alphaN_L(n). Transitivity identifies this fiber with U(W)\U(V). The quotient of the fixed Haar measures of MF5 is a fixed positive multiple kappa of sigma_n, independent of L.

A K_L orbit on the norm fiber has quotient volume nu(L)/vol(stabilizer). On fixing the cut T and regrouping its finite extension set under K_T, ordinary orbit–stabilizer for compact open groups gives sum 1/vol(stabilizer)=f_L(T)/nu(T). Multiplying by A_(m-1) gives Psi_p=A_(m-1) kappa q^((m-1)d_L) alphaN_L(n)/alphaL. This is the relative identity LIRatio. Empty fibers yield zero on both sides, so no division by a vanishing representation density is used.

MF9 — PINNING THE SCALAR AND ALL OF LISTAT

For norm one, choose the minimal lattice Bx0 orthogonal T0 from MF2, with d<=1. Any integral lattice of that type containing x0 splits integrally along x0. Its cut has the same rank and determinant valuation, hence the same integral isometry type. For a fixed cut, the extension is unique. Therefore Psi equals lambda(T0).

For k>d, the finite isometry group acts transitively on norm-one vectors modulo pi^k. Each such vector lifts to exact norm one while retaining its residue, since its norm derivative is surjective; its complement has the unique minimal type. The stabilizer of the first vector is exactly the finite isometry group of the cut: the first row and column are forced by the unit norm. Thus a_L(k)=n_L(k,1)a_T(k). The exponents match because m^2=(2m-1)+(m-1)^2, so alphaL=alphaN_L(1) alphaT. Together with positive matrix densities this pins kappa=1.

For general n, replace h by h0=h/n and perform the scaling comparison on a reference lattice integral for h0. The original h-integral L need not be h0-integral. The scalar kappa of MF8 is independent of the integral lattice for a fixed form and norm; computing it on this reference lattice, whose representation density is nonzero, then transfers the result to the original L by LIRatio. Scaling a rank-s Gram matrix by n changes d by s ord(n), alphaL by q^(s^2 ord(n)), and the norm density at n by q^ord(n). These factors follow by comparing counts at levels k and k-ord(n): free matrix lifts contribute q^(2s^2 ord(n)) and free vector lifts q^(2s ord(n)), before division by the prescribed normalizing powers. Lambda factors are unchanged. The relative expression in MF8 scales by q^(-(m-1)ord(n)), so kappa=q^((m-1)ord(n)).

The resulting exact identity is Psi_p=q^((m-1)(ord(n)+d_L)) A_(m-1) alphaN_L(n)/alphaL. To obtain the literal global reference lattice, approximate the chosen local complement basis by rational vectors and clear all other denominators by an F-integer which is a unit at p. The norm-one vector is kept fixed. This supplies the pin lattice in the same rational space. Rank one has a zero-dimensional complement, with all empty products, densities and group orders equal to one. Thus the proof covers every LIStat quantifier.

MF10 — A COMMON POSITIVE RANK-ONE MASS

A Hermitian line is a bar(x)y and an arbitrary lattice is a fractional ideal I. Its rational unitary group is E^1, independent of a and I. Its local stabilizer is the norm-one unit subgroup, also independent of them. A relative ideal JI^(-1) belongs to the genus exactly when its exponents are zero at inert primes and opposite in each split pair. MF3 converts the completed description to the source's rational local one. Thus the class set is one fixed quotient of balanced ideals by principal norm-one ideals for every line and lattice. The automorphism group is always the finite norm-one unit group. IG9 gives finiteness and positivity of the corresponding genus mass c1.

In rank one, the matrix congruence for determinant valuation d is the norm-one congruence at level k-d. Counting the 2d free digits gives alphaL=q^d(1-eta/q), hence lambda_p=1. Therefore all positive integral rank-one lattices satisfy MassConst(1,c1). Computing the absolute value of c1 would require an unnecessary extra formula; it cancels from the adjacent-rank ratio.

MF11 — ARCHIMEDEAN LATTICE COUNT AND ITS DETERMINANT

For an arbitrary projective lattice L define D(L)=sqrt(N_E discrIdeal(L)). Local Hermitian determinant valuations at conjugate split primes agree, while the inert residue norm is q^2. Hence D(L)=product q^d_L. Choose an orthogonal E-basis inside L; its free span A has finite index I in L. In these coordinates covol(L)=v^(fm)/I. Determinants of the finite-index inclusion give D(L)I=product_tau det H_tau. This also proves genus invariance of D.

The region h_tau(x,x)<=T at every real place is a product of complex ellipsoids, of volume T^(fm)(pi^m/m!)^f divided by product_tau det H_tau. Tiling by a fixed fundamental parallelotope leaves only a bounded-width boundary error, negligible after scaling. Therefore its lattice count divided by T^(fm) tends to (pi^m/m!)^f/(v^(fm)D(L)). The source uses T=j^2; this gives exactly its j^(2fm) denominator. This constant is derived directly, without a global mass or volume formula.

MF12 — RECIPROCAL STABILIZER WEIGHTS

For totally positive n, sum the number of norm-n vectors in each genus representative divided by its actual automorphism order. Positivity makes each vector set finite. MF4 supplies one rational vector x0 of norm n. First apply finite orbit–stabilizer inside each lattice, then move each vector to x0 by MF3. The resulting objects are genus lattices containing x0, modulo the rational unitary group of its complement.

For a fixed cut T, the global extension set is finite by the same lattice sandwich as locally. Aut(T) acts on it, and its stabilizer is exactly Aut(Lambda,x0), because an isometry of the complement extends uniquely by fixing x0. A second orbit–stabilizer identity yields R_L(n)=sum_[T] |F_T|/|Aut(T)|. Keeping these reciprocal weights is essential: unweighted numbers of complement classes do not give the mass formula.

MF13 — PATCHING FIBERS AND COMPLEMENT GENERA

The finite global extension set is the product of its actual local extension sets. A finite family patches by intersection between the explicit lower and upper global lattices; Chinese remaindering gives the exact localization at every prime. Containing x0, integrality and cutting W in T are local conditions. Rational local isometry follows from MF3. Outside the finite set dividing the norm, lattice determinant and one reference cut determinant, a unit-norm vector in a unimodular lattice splits integrally, and its fiber is a singleton. Thus the product is genuinely finite.

Under MassConst in rank m-1, regroup the weighted complement sum by genus. Fiber sizes and local factors are constant on a complement genus. Every finite tuple of allowed local cut types occurs: the representatives are lattices in the already fixed W_p, and they patch to a lattice in the same rational W. No assertion about an arbitrary collection of rational Hermitian-space invariants is made. Distributing the finite sums now yields R_L(n)=c_(m-1) product_p Psi_p(L,n). This reconstructs the actual representation-mass identity rather than importing it.

MF14 — ALL GLOBAL FACTORS BEFORE A LIMIT

Define lambda_p=A_m q^(m d)/alphaL, lambda=product lambda_p, and the singular series S_L(n)=product alphaN_L(n)/e_m. Both products have finite support for n nonzero, by the unimodular and unit-norm computations. Substitute LIStat, use A_m=A_(m-1)e_m, and collect the prime powers. Product q^d is D(L), and product q^ord(n) is |N_F/Q(n)|. This gives the exact finite identity

  R_L(n)=c_(m-1) lambda(L) D(L)^(-1) |N(n)|^(m-1) S_L(n).

No interchange of an infinite product with a global sum has yet occurred.

MF15 — WEIGHTED IDEAL BOXES AND THE SINGULAR RESIDUE

In the real Minkowski lattice of F consider 0<tau(n)<=T at every embedding. For a fixed ideal a and a fixed congruence class modulo c coprime to a, Riemann sums give the weighted count divided by T^(fm) tending to m^(-f)/(sqrt(disc F) N(a)N(c)). The integral of the rescaled weight product x_tau^(m-1) over the unit box is m^(-f). This is a fixed-ideal limit; no unproved uniform lattice error for all ideals is required.

Uniform domination follows by a different argument. Distinct points of a cannot occupy the same half-open cube of side N(a)^(1/f), since the norm of their difference would be smaller than N(a), contrary to (difference) being contained in a. If the positive T-box contains a point of a, N(a)<=T^f. The number of cubes is at most 2^f T^f/N(a), and hence the normalized weighted sum is at most 2^f/N(a).

At finitely many bad places, the product g(n) of local norm densities divided by e_m is bounded by MF7. Away from p^j, it is constant on sufficiently fine residue classes. Replacing it there by a step function leaves an error supported on p^j. For ideals a prime to p, the uniform bound applied to a p^j makes the error at most C/(N(a)q^j). First take the fixed-step limit, then let j increase. The local mean-one identities give the exact finite-place average, including the exceptional zero residue, and a uniform bound C/N(a).

MF16 — AN ABSOLUTELY DOMINATED SINGULAR-SERIES AVERAGE

The good-prime formula is an exact finite geometric sum. For a fixed bad set S it gives

  S_L(n)=g(n) sum_{a divides (n), (a,S)=1} chi_m(a)/N(a)^(m-1),

where chi_m(a)=product eta_p^(m ord_p(a)). The normalized weighted contribution of one ideal a is bounded by C/N(a)^m, by MF15. This is summable for m>=2: at most f prime ideals lie over a rational prime and each has norm at least that prime, so the Euler logarithm is absolutely convergent; expanding the product gives the summable ideal series.

Dominated convergence therefore passes the limit through the divisor sum. The finite bad-place average contributes product_(p in S) e_m^(-1); the good-prime ideal series contributes the complementary Euler product. The limit is exactly m^(-f) L_m/sqrt(disc F). This treats m=2 as well. The proof does not rely on a heuristic exchange of an infinite product and an average and does not assume prime-counting estimates.

MF17 — THE SUCCESSOR MASS CONSTANT

Count the same lattice vectors in two ways, retaining the zero vector separately. Summing each ball count over genus representatives with reciprocal automorphism weights equals mass(L)+sum_(n in the positive norm box) R_L(n), before any limit. Divide by T^(fm). MF11 gives the left limit and MF14–MF16 give the right limit. The isolated zero-vector term tends to zero.

Cancelling the positive determinant, volume and factorial factors gives

  mass(L)=c_(m-1) R_m lambda(L),
  R_m=(((m-1)! (v/pi)^m)^f / sqrt((2v)^f)) L_m.

The m^(-f) in the norm-value average cancels exactly the extra m^f from m!=m(m-1)!. The real discriminant covolume leaves sqrt((2v)^f), while the complex vector-lattice covolume leaves v^(fm). This audit fixes all powers of 2, pi, v, factorials and residue norms in the literal source formula.

MF18 — INDUCTION OVER ALL INTEGRAL LATTICES

Let c_m=c1 product_(j=2)^m R_j. Every factor is positive. MF10 establishes rank one, and MF17 supplies each successor. Thus for every positive definite rational Hermitian space of rank m and every full integral lattice in that space, mass(L)=c_m lambda(L). Bad local types remain in lambda(L), and globally projective modules are included. The induction is the mathematical content of massConst_all; it uses no mass theorem as an input.

MF19 — THE TWO LITERAL MASS-RATIO INTERFACES

For an IsOrthoLattice the displayed basis identifies both the form and lattice with the standard identity form and O_E^m. Every local determinant valuation is zero and every lambda factor is one.
