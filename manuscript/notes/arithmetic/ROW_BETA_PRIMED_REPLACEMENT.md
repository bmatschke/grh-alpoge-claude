# Preserving full rank after evaluation in the first beta step

Let the small intermediate space have rank $n=a'$ and the target rank $d=a+n$. Remove the target hyperbolic plane $H'$ and write $d_1=d-2$. The small source is unchanged in this **ordinary evaluation at zero**, used to compute the alpha part of the small constant term. For every polynomial upper test $\Phi$ already primed at the designated split place $v_1$, there is another primed polynomial test $\widetilde\Phi$ such that
$$
 I_s(\widetilde\Phi)=I_s(\Phi),\qquad E'_0\widetilde\Phi
                  \text{ is primed for the pair of ranks }(n,d_1).
$$
Here primed means that the barred local matrix test is supported on matrices of full row rank. The equality of integrals holds as functions on the whole upper target. We prove this on the actual rank inequality $2(n-1)<d_1$, equivalent to $n<a$. [Required replacement][move] · [Where it enters the first constant term][alpha]

Write $k=F_{v_1}$, use the prescribed split embedding and choose target coordinates adapted to $W_1$ and to the two isotropic lines of $H'$. In the barred matrix chart an upper test is a locally constant compactly supported function $\phi(x,Z,y)$ on the matrices with $x,y\in k^n$ and $Z\in M_{n,d_1}(k)$. Orient the two line coordinates so that the local ordinary evaluation is, up to a fixed nonzero scalar,
$$
       (e\phi)(Z)=\int_{k^n}\phi(0,Z,y)\,dy .
$$
Indeed at fixed $Z$ the mixed Heisenberg evaluation is supported on the affine fiber $(0,Z,y)$: modulation by a character in the eliminated coordinates vanishes on the functional unless those coordinates have the specified values. Local constancy lets one apply this observation on disjoint compact open pieces. Translations in the free coordinate $y$ leave the functional invariant. On its fiber a translation-invariant functional on Schwartz–Bruhat functions is a multiple of Haar integration. Translations in $Z$ intertwine the lower and upper evaluations, so this multiple is independent of $Z$. It is nonzero by evaluation of the vacuum. Equivalently this is partial Fourier transformation in one line and evaluation at zero in the two mixed coordinates. Changing the adapted column basis does not affect rank. [Fiber support and translation laws][fiber] · [Nonzero vacuum and equivariance][cov]

The source acts on upper matrices by $\phi(Y)\mapsto\nu(\det A)|\det A|^{d/2}\phi(AY)$ in the convention for the barred action. In the lower action the exponent is $d_1/2$. The change of variable $y\mapsto Ay$ in the integral gives exactly this change in exponent, so $e\omega(A)=\omega_1(A)e$ with the same character on both sides. [Source action through evaluation][cov]

Decompose the given full-rank local function into a finite sum of constant multiples of indicators of boxes $C_x\times C_Z\times C_y$ contained in its support. Choose matrix balls $C_Z=Z_0+\varpi^lM_{n,d_1}(\mathcal O)$ by refining the boxes. Their ordinary evaluations are constant multiples of $1_{C_Z}$, including zero when $0\notin C_x$. For each nonzero evaluation apply the [row sieve for a matrix ball](../construction/ROW_DEFINITE_LOCAL_QUOTIENTS.md#full-rank-representatives-at-the-prescribed-split-place) to $C_Z$ with $d_1$ columns. More explicitly, row and column Smith operations first make $r$ rows independent modulo $\varpi$ and the other rows unrestricted. The finite combination $z$ of source translations enforcing independence of the remaining rows has coefficient sum
$$
        D_r=\prod_{j=r}^{n-1}\bigl(1-\nu(\varpi)q^{j-d_1/2}\bigr),
        \qquad q=\#(\mathcal O/\varpi).
$$
Undoing the row-coordinate change conjugates these source translations. Its paired column-coordinate change commutes with them. Since $j\le n-1<d_1/2$, $D_r\ne0$. Dividing the combination by $D_r$ gives coefficient sum one and sends $1_{C_Z}$ to a lower test of full row rank. The proof uses the actual oscillator multiplier in the lower chart; using the upper exponent in $D_r$ would give an incorrect normalization.

Apply **that same combination of group elements and coefficients to the original upper box**, using the upper Weil action. Its evaluation is the sieved lower function by equivariance. Its upper support remains in full-row-rank matrices because every summand is the pullback of the original box by an invertible row operation. For a box of zero evaluation keep the original box. Sum all the replacements. In each box the difference from its replacement is a linear combination of $\omega(A)\phi-\phi$ with $\phi$ itself primed. This proves the local assertion in the required span of source-translation differences. It imposes no condition on the phase of $\nu(\varpi)$ beyond unitarity. [Local formulation and source augmentation][local]

On a finite tensor expansion one can arrange that each local factor at $v_1$ is primed. To see this, tensor the finite factor at that place with all remaining variables and use linear independence of a basis for the finite span of the remaining tensors. A sum in the primed subspace has each corresponding local coefficient in that subspace. All real factors stay in the finite span of the original polynomials. Apply the construction to these local coefficients. In each pure summand the global difference is a sum of source-translation differences of primed tensors, all of whose integrals converge by the full-rank estimate. Right invariance of the source quotient measure gives zero upon integrating each difference. The ordinary evaluation factors into its local and real mixed evaluations, so the evaluated sum has the required lower support. This proves the two assertions simultaneously. [Integration of the local replacement][global] · [Absolute convergence and the estimate in the exterior variable](../construction/ROW_SQUARE_PATH_FLIP.md#integrating-one-square-against-a-cusp-form)

For $j=m-2$ the source of the lowered pair has signature $(1,n-1)$ and its target is definite. At any real place the actual splitting adds the same central shift to both source diagonal generators. Before this shift the polynomial weights on a positive row are $N+d_1/2$ and on a negative row $-N'-d_1/2$, with $N,N'\ge0$. They cannot both equal the value required for source invariance. The lower theta functional on polynomial tests with primed finite factors is invariant under the source by absolute convergence, and hence kills every polynomial. The projective-line correction for the ordinary evaluation also vanishes on the lower primed support. Applying the replacement after each finite and real compact target translation, and using Iwasawa and the two Levi transformation laws, gives vanishing of the alpha coefficient on its whole domain. The beta coefficient is the [separate scalar-line integral](../construction/ROW_FIRST_BETA_STEP.md).
