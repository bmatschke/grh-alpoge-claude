1. Objects and conventions
==========================

Write L=D.what₂.LL and L0=D.LL0. D7 supplies that L is a number field,
Galois over Q, its complex embedding, its conjugation, its genuine prime
indexing, and the additive valuation equality. L0 is an intermediate
field. Its being Galois over Q is NOT automatic and is separately
retained when conjugating readings or making an orbit-consistent
character family.

The additive valuation is nonnegative on the valuation ring, positive on
its maximal ideal, and +infinity at zero. The multiplicative valuation
in the prime-ideal library has the equivalent conditions <=1 and <1.
The Reading record asserts equivalence of valuation rings, not equality
of numerical valuations after passage to a ramified DVR extension.

For a side b, the base-field embedding is emb0 on true and emb0b on false.
The character convention psiAt is psi on true and psi^{-1} on false.
It is an involution at a fixed side. These two conventions are checked
separately in their literal source definitions.

The nonreference support is Wtw={w | muAt(w) != mu0}. Every local index
a:D.nd.Loc has a specified w(a) in Wtw. This fact is part of the supplied
local record; NC does not create new such indices.

2. NC1: the complete DVR extension is proved, not assumed
=======================================================

The source's generic integral-closure theorem is more general than needed.
Our coefficient fraction field contains the number field L0, so it has
characteristic zero. NC1 proves precisely that specialization.

Let A be a complete DVR and K'/Frac(A) finite. Separability provides a
primitive element alpha. Multiplication by a sufficiently divisible
nonzero element of A makes alpha integral: after writing a monic minimal
polynomial over Frac(A), multiplying alpha by a common denominator makes
every transformed coefficient integral. The algebra C=A[alpha] has the
power basis up to the field degree.

For the integral closure B, Tr(xC) is in A for all x in B. To justify
the trace assertion, every conjugate of an integral element is integral,
so their sum is integral; the trace lies in Frac(A), and A is integrally
closed. Nondegeneracy of the separable trace matrix identifies the trace
dual C^vee with a finite free A-lattice. Thus
                  C subset B subset C^vee.
Over a DVR, submodules of a finite free module are finite free. This
establishes finiteness of B and Frac(B)=K'. It is a concrete trace-dual
bound, not an appeal to an unexplained excellence theorem.

For locality, the finite A-algebra B/pi B is Artinian. Two or more local
factors would give a nontrivial idempotent modulo pi. The Newton iteration
 e -> e - (e^2-e)/(2e-1)
doubles the pi-adic error. The denominator is a unit because its square
is 1+4(e^2-e), and 1+pi b has a geometric-series inverse. This argument
also works at residue characteristic two: it never divides by 2.
The finite free A-module B is complete, so the idempotent lifts. Since
B is a domain, the lift must be 0 or 1, a contradiction.

B is therefore a normal Noetherian local domain of dimension one. The
text proves the DVR criterion rather than leaving it as a black box:
choose a nonzero socle element u modulo a nonzero a in the maximal ideal,
put t=u/a, and observe t*m subset B. If t*m subset m, the determinant
trick makes t integral, impossible. Hence t*m=B and m is principal.
The pi-adic and m-adic filtrations are cofinal, giving both separatedness
and completeness. Their cofinality follows from the Artinian quotient,
not from an assumed unramified extension.

Classical algebra used here is explicitly limited to finite separable
primitive elements, trace nondegeneracy, the PID submodule theorem,
finite-dimensional Artinian algebras, and the determinant trick. The
application does not require a finite residue field or local compactness.
In particular NTw.O(i), whose residue field is algebraically closed,
is allowed.

3. NC2–NC3: selecting the exact place and preserving the base field
=================================================================

Every number-field integer maps into the integrally closed DVR inside
the target fraction field: the image of Z lies in that DVR, so the same
monic polynomial over Z proves integrality.
This produces the unique integral map on O_L.

Suppose its maximal ideal contracts to a prescribed prime p of O_L.
Localization then proves the full valuation-ring equivalence. A fraction
a/s with s outside p has integral image. If x has negative valuation,
its inverse is a fraction a/s with a in p and s outside p; its image is
in the DVR maximal ideal, so x cannot also have integral image.
This treats all elements of L rather than merely its global integers.

To make the contraction equal the requested prime, start with any
extension of L0 -> Frac(A) to L in an algebraic closure. It lands in a
finite extension because L/L0 is finite; one root of the mapped
minimal polynomial suffices. NC1 gives a finite complete integral DVR
extension B. The initial embedding contracts m_B to a prime q above the
correct prime of L0, but it need not yet read the requested p.

The proof of Galois transitivity on primes is given explicitly. If q is
outside the orbit of p, the Chinese remainder theorem produces an
integer a which is 0 modulo q and 1 modulo every conjugate of p.
Its Galois norm lies in the contracted prime and hence in p, while
every factor avoids p. This contradicts primality. Choose sigma with
sigma^{-1}(q)=p and replace e1 by e1 composed with sigma.
This orientation is essential: the prime pulled back by e1*sigma is
sigma^{-1}(q). Sigma fixes L0, so the specified base-field embedding
is unchanged.

The resulting denominator identity is stronger than the later NChTie
field: if i(x)z=y in Frac(A), then e(x)j(z)=j(y), even when z=0.
This is compatibility with the actual coefficient maps, not just an
abstract isomorphism of fraction fields.

The source invokes a finite-extension constructor which also adjoins a
square root, using a=1 in this application. NC explains why it can be
omitted here. No square-root existence or extra sign choice is hidden.

4. NC4: all Reading fields and residue-characteristic equality
============================================================

NTw supplies O0(i), O(i), Ok(i,k), their injections, completeness of O(i),
and finiteness of Ok(i,k) over O(i). Its O_l field places ell(i) in the
maximal ideal of O(i).

The map O0(i)->O(i) is local for a substantive reason. If a nonunit
became a unit, a uniformizer of O0(i) would become a unit. The nonzero
integer ell(i), a unit times a uniformizer power, would then become a
unit, contradicting O_l. The injection O(i)->Ok(i,k) is finite integral
between local domains and is local. The composite phiK is therefore
injective and local.

Ok(i,k) is finite torsion free, hence free over O(i), so complete for
the base-adic topology. Its unique maximal ideal has a power contained
in the extended base ideal; therefore its own adic topology is the same.
NC3 applies to the composite side embedding and constructs Reading:

  Oe                         the integral closure B;
  cr, dom, dvr               its commutative ring, domain and DVR data;
  alg                        the actual Ok-algebra map;
  inj, fin, loc              injective, module finite and local;
  emb                        the aligned embedding of L;
  emb_w                      nonnegative valuation iff membership in B;
  emb_side                   the exact denominator identity through phiK.

The reading prime contains ell(i), so ell(i)=resChar(w). Distinct rational
primes cannot both belong to a proper ideal, by Bezout. NC also proves
TwCover -> ReadElsewhere by contracting each w to L0. It does not provide
the actual family of coefficient indices required for TwCover itself.

5. NC5–NC8: actual integral matching of the Mt spaces
===================================================

The global source lattice is OLat, a finite set of complex generators
spanning the complex space. It should not be silently replaced by an
arbitrary rational basis. The exact left-hand set is
        S={x in MtL(mu,j) | jMt(x) in MtLat(mu,j).gen}.

NC5 proves that S is finite and spans over L. The supplied jMt_basis
turns an L-basis into a complex basis, so the basis is finite and jMt
is injective. The pullback of a finite generator set is therefore
finite. If S failed to span, choose an L-linear functional annihilating
it and extend that functional to the complex basis. The extension
annihilates every complex lattice generator, hence the whole complex
space; injectivity of L->C contradicts the original nonzero functional.
This is a full rational-span proof using the actual MtLat_sub premise.

NC6 constructs the local lattice after coefficient extension:
  V'=Frac(B) tensor_A V,
  Lambda'=span_B {1 tensor lambda | lambda in Lambda}.
The injection A->B extends to the fraction fields. Localizing the
tensor product identifies V' with Frac(B) tensor_Frac(A) V, which
preserves dimension. Images of any finite generating set of Lambda
generate Lambda'. Clearing denominators proves it spans V': if d*v
lies in Lambda, a tensor v=(a/d)*(1 tensor d*v). No flatness assumption
is being used without proof; field extension and this tensor identity
are enough.

NC7 supplies the comparison map. Pick an L-basis and embed its
coordinates into Frac(B)^r. The B-span of the embedded S is a full
finite lattice. Match a B-basis of that lattice to a B-basis of
Lambda', then extend linearly over Frac(B). Composing gives the desired
semilinear map f. It carries every L-basis to a Frac(B)-basis because
an invertible change-of-basis matrix stays invertible under a field
embedding. The integral span of f(S) is exactly Lambda', by construction.

The text gives the elementary least-valuation proof of the free-module
fact: choose a minimal finite generating family, take a nontrivial
relation, divide by a coefficient with least valuation, cancel by
torsion freeness, and eliminate the generator with unit coefficient.
This contradicts minimality.

NC8 combines these arguments and preserves the source's proposition c.
If c is false, the zero map suffices. If c is true, the supplied rank
equality and NC5–NC7 produce both basis and integral-span conclusions.
For NChoice, c is exactly w nonreference and 1<=j<=m. No comparison
property is claimed at other indices.

Scope of the map:
  - The scalar extension of f is a vector-space isomorphism.
  - f maps the specified generator span exactly to the local lattice.
  - f need not be surjective as a set map over the smaller field L.
  - No Hecke equivariance, metric preservation, compatibility among
    different j, or canonical choice is asserted.
These are limits of the literal record, not additional assumptions.

7. NC10–NC11: the common character and conjugation
================================================

If L contains a primitive nth root and a K'-valued character has exponent
n, every value lies in the image of L. The powers of the embedded
primitive root are n distinct roots of X^n-1, hence exhaust its roots.
The unique lifts preserve multiplication because the embedding is
injective. This proves actual character descent.

ValuesIn is exactly the needed primitive-root and exponent hypothesis.
It applies to the side-normalized character too. NC4 and this descent
give HasReading for the chosen packet.

For the conjugate reading, retain L0/Q Galois. Conjugation then preserves
L0 and its integers, so the given relation between emb0 and emb0b is
applicable at every base-field element. Keep Oe and compose emb with
conjugation. The prescribed valuation relation gives emb_w, and the
side-embedding relation gives emb_side.

Every character value pulled from the finite ring-class group is a root
of unity. Complex conjugation sends it to its inverse. The opposite
side inverts the local character, so the conjugate reading pulls the
same Galois character psi0. In particular the coherence condition is
psi0(conj w)=psi0(w), not psi0(conj w)=conjugate(psi0(w)).

Choose one representative of each conjugation orbit. Descend once on
each twisted representative and use the conjugate reading on its
partner. Fixed places require no side switch. Set psi0=1 on untwisted
orbits. This proves exists_isCharOf under the precise hypotheses.
It does not prove LocsCarry for any separately prescribed local family.

9. NC14–NC16: the literal TupTied supplier
========================================

TwDat is supplied. Its character set is
 {1,delta,delta'} union range(w -> delta_at_resChar(w)*T.psi(w)).
The IsDelta and IsTwist clauses imply each character has finite order,
is trivial on F, and is tame everywhere. Products preserve those
properties, using the product of two positive exponents.

Every displayed twisted character is nontrivial. The distinguished
delta at p has nontrivial inertia of exact exponent 15 above p.
T.psi(w) is unramified at every prime above p, because otherwise
IsTwist would force its rational norm prime p outside offSet, although
offSet contains both distinguished primes. A product with an unramified
character cannot cancel delta on local units.

The existence of a prime above p is supplied by the elementary finite
quotient O_E/p: it is nonzero because the rational number 1/p is not
an algebraic integer, and every nonzero finite ring has a maximal
ideal. This replaces an opaque call to an isolated prime-above theorem.
Finiteness of places over the finite rational-prime set T.pl follows
from the same finite quotient. Outside that set the twisting character
is 1, so the complete displayed character set is finite. Conjugation
preserves resChar, and TwDat.conj preserves psi and k.

MuIsW remains a ten-field hypothesis on the same X,f,T,W,xi. Its on/off
fields and the nontriviality just proved give
        muAt(w) != mu0 iff w in W.
Its sub field and prime finiteness give W finite. The supplied row
muAt_conj field gives conjugation stability. The set W is not enlarged
to all places above T.pl.

For any NChoice C with C.Q(w)=T.Q(w) on W, choose TupTied.xi=xi and
TupTied.psi=T.psi. Its twelve fields are:
  xi, xi_inj, xi_fin, xi_triv, xi_tame, psi;
  trueColumnChoices_heckeChar_reference_eq_one;
  xi_star, xi_star', xi_tw, psi_is, psi_un.
Finite/trivial/tame follow from the range and NC14. The four named

No coefficient-index equality k'=T.k is required for this endpoint.
