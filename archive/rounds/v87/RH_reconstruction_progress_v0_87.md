## Why an entire eigenspace is detected

Fix a good upper Hecke point and any nonzero vector $f$ in its eigenspace. A repeated Hecke character can occur on several independent vectors. Proving that one of them lifts would leave room for a nonzero linear combination in the kernel. The argument begins with $f$ itself. Its scalar component and a polynomial in the archimedean Weil representation can be chosen so that the real factor of the doubling integral is a positive multiple of the integral of a squared absolute value on a compact unitary group. The matrix coefficient is nonzero at the identity. [Real occurrence and scalar forms](continuation_v87/definite.tex) · [Original Hecke point](continuation_v87/return.tex)

Write $r$ for the lower rank, $r+t$ for the upper rank, and $t$ for the length of the character string in the unramified parameter. Adding $t$ hyperbolic planes gives complementary target dimensions $r$ and $r+2t$. At the level primes a full-column-rank test cuts out the prescribed principal group. Spherical averaging at the remaining places gives the Euler product for this very scalar component. The string yields a simple pole with nonzero residue at $s=t/2$. The complementary-space first term identity of Gan, Qiu and Takeda would make the residue vanish if every downward theta integral vanished. Hence some Schwartz function detects $f$. The proof checks the splitting character, the additive character, the regularized case and the determinant twist used in this comparison. [Weil actions](continuation_v87/normalization.tex) · [Residue and complementary space](continuation_v87/residue.tex)

The project prescribes one Schwartz test in its downward map. If that map killed $f$, the good projector and real polynomial coefficients would propagate the vanishing to every test with the same finite annihilator. At each finite place the relevant projected functions are generated by translates of the prescribed local factor. Separate arguments handle ordinary split and nonsplit places, the two type primes and the twist primes. This would make every downward integral vanish, contradicting the residue argument. Adjointness now gives the whole upper eigenspace as the image of the lower point space. This proves **what₂** and **z** simultaneously. $$
\mathrm{etaSum}(L,j)\le dZ_0(j)
\left[C\left(fm b L_{\rm sc}^2+(\log x)^2N_c\right)
+\sum_\ell \mathrm{vvTerm}(D,\ell)\right].

Here $dZ_0(j)$ is the original lift rank, and $L_{\rm sc}$ is the member's scale parameter. The cohomological lengths in the sum belong to this same $D$. The original finiteness hypothesis includes the prime 2 and the summability hypothesis applies to this series.
