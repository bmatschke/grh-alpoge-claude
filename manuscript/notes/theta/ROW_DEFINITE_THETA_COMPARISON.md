# Kernel and Petersson form of the two definite theta lifts

Fix a realized triple with $p=q=0$, intermediate groups $A_b=U(V)$ and $A_s=U(V')$, and target $G=U(W)$. All three groups are compact at infinity, and their arithmetic quotients are compact. Let $[\mathcal G]=\mathcal G(F)\backslash\mathcal G(\mathbb A_F)$. On side $j=b,s$ the actual theta map is
$$
 J_j(\Phi)(h)=\int_{[A_j]}\theta_j(\Phi;g,h)\,dg .
$$
Write $q_{j,v}$ and $q_{j,\infty}$ for the prescribed projections of the local Schwartz–Bruhat and real polynomial oscillator spaces. The small and big projections have a common nonzero irreducible target $\Pi_v$ at each finite place and $\Pi_\infty$ at infinity. Their vacuum images outside the ramified finite set agree and are nonzero. The [local construction](../construction/ROW_DEFINITE_LOCAL_QUOTIENTS.md) identifies these common targets on the two actual Weil representations, including their splitting characters and vacuum normalizations. The [definite nonvanishing argument](../construction/ROW_MATCHED_SQUARE_TESTS.md#nonzero-integrated-kernels-at-the-definite-bottom) gives a nonzero global lift on each side. ## Descent on each local factor

The theta sum is invariant under rational translations in either group. Absolute convergence and right invariance of the quotient measure give
$$
 J_j(\omega_j(r,k)\Phi)(h)=J_j(\Phi)(hk)
 \quad(r\in A_j(\mathbb A_F),\ k\in G(\mathbb A_F)).
$$
In particular, with all factors except $v$ frozen, the map $T_v$ from the local oscillator space into $L^2([G])$ is invariant under $A_j(F_v)$ and equivariant under $G(F_v)$. Its functions are continuous. Compactness gives square integrability and pointwise equality whenever two such functions agree in $L^2$. This applies to every polynomial Schwartz test on both definite sides, including unrestricted replacements at the distinguished split place. [Linearity and right translation][dq-actions] · [Good-place coinvariants and compact integration][dq-so]

Here is a way to pass from this equivariance to the **kernel of the prescribed projection**. At a finite place, the image $D_v$ of $T_v$ is a smooth, unitary and admissible representation of $G(F_v)$, where unitary means it carries the positive definite invariant inner product induced by $L^2([G])$. Indeed the frozen test has a fixed open compact level away from $v$ and finitely many real compact types. For a compact open $K_v$, there are finitely many adelic double classes at the resulting finite level. On each class the real variable runs in a compact Lie group, and the functions in any fixed finite list of real types form a finite-dimensional space by Peter–Weyl. This proves $\dim D_v^{K_v}<\infty$. At infinity the analogous assertion needed below follows from compact finiteness of each polynomial.

Every submodule $N$ of $D_v$ has an invariant algebraic orthogonal complement. If $u$ is fixed by $K_v$, take its orthogonal projection $u_0$ onto the finite-dimensional space $N^{K_v}$. For $z\in N$, its normalized compact average $e_{K_v}z$ belongs to $N^{K_v}$ by smoothness. Thus $(u-u_0,z)=(u-u_0,e_{K_v}z)=0$. This splits off $N$ inside the smooth space. Hence $D_v$ is semisimple as an algebraic representation, and an irreducible quotient detects any nonzero element. For an archimedean slice the compact-type decomposition gives the same conclusion on the polynomial vectors.

Apply local Howe duality to the actual pair $(A_j(F_v),G(F_v))$. For the trivial representation of the first group, the maximal semisimple quotient of its oscillator coinvariants is either zero or a single irreducible representation with multiplicity one. The map $q_{j,v}$ is onto a nonzero irreducible module, kills intermediate-group translates minus their originals, and intertwines the target action. Therefore it identifies that unique quotient. Every composite of $T_v$ with an irreducible quotient of its image kills $\ker q_{j,v}$. Those quotients separate points of $D_v$, so $\ker q_{j,v}\subseteq\ker T_v$. The same argument uses the product of the compact real Howe correspondences at infinity. At a split finite place this is the type-II pair $(\mathrm{GL}_n,\mathrm{GL}_{a+a'})$. At a nonsplit finite place it is the Hermitian–skew-Hermitian unitary pair over a characteristic-zero local field. Twisting the oscillator splitting by the specified determinant characters transports the quotient and uniqueness statements. This argument allows the frozen test to be arbitrary or to give the zero map. ## Every prescribed finite test

Include the finite exceptional set, the distinguished good place, and the exceptional factors of any given finite sum of tests in one finite set $S$. Multilinearity and the preceding kernel calculation show that the integral on each side descends to
$$
 \Pi_S=\Pi_\infty\otimes\bigotimes_{v\in S}\Pi_v .
$$
Indeed, the kernel of the tensor product of surjections over $\mathbb C$ is the sum of the subspaces with a kernel vector in at least one slot. Each such subspace is killed by the integral. Enlarging $S$ tensors with the common nonzero vacuum images, so the maps agree on the resulting algebraic restricted tensor product $\Pi$. A linear combination of tests is represented by the **sum** of their tensor classes. At the good place $v_1$, the primed full-rank tests map onto $\Pi_{v_1}$ and one can replace an arbitrary primed factor by such a representative without changing its class or its definite integral. [Finite-sum descent][dq-linear] · [Full-rank replacement][dq-so]

The restricted tensor product is irreducible for the target group on these algebraic vectors. To see this, take a nonzero vector and any desired vector at a common finite stage. On each local simple module the commutant is scalar. A commuting endomorphism has an eigenvector on a nonzero finite-dimensional compact-fixed subspace, or a finite-dimensional real module, and the eigenspace is invariant. For independent $u_1,\ldots,u_r$ in one local module $U$, target group-algebra operators can prescribe their images simultaneously. Otherwise the submodule generated by $(u_1,\ldots,u_r)$ in the semisimple module $U^r$ would be killed by a nonzero homomorphism $U^r\to U$. By the scalar commutant this gives a nontrivial linear relation among the $u_i$. Applying this observation in each slot of a finite tensor separates one pure term and then generates all pure tensors. Passing to larger finite stages proves the assertion. Thus the kernel of each of the two descended global maps is zero, since each has the independent nonzero seed. For a prescribed polynomial pure test the actual lift is nonzero as a function **if and only if** its real and all its finite quotient factors are nonzero. The point of evaluation can depend on the test. For a finite sum the criterion is nonvanishing of its sum of tensor classes. ## What the invariant Gram form determines

Use the Petersson product $(f,g)=\int_{[G]} f(h)\overline{g(h)}\,dh$, linear in its first variable. Put $H(x,y)=(J_s x,J_s y)$. This is positive definite on $\Pi$. For any two linear combinations $J,J'$ of $J_b,J_s$, the form $B(x,y)=(Jx,J'y)$ is invariant under the simultaneous action of the target group. Every such form is $\lambda H$ for a single scalar. To verify this, fix $K=G_\infty K_f$ with $K_f$ open compact. Vectors are $K$-finite. Each $K$-type has finite multiplicity in the realization $J_s(\Pi)$: impose the kernel of its finite-place type as level and apply the double-class argument above. Invariance makes $B$ vanish between inequivalent $K$-types. On each finite-dimensional isotypic component define $A$ by $B(x,y)=H(Ax,y)$, and assemble the operators on their algebraic direct sum. Invariance under the entire adelic group and nondegeneracy of $H$ imply $A$ commutes with that group. Any eigenvalue on a nonzero $K$-isotypic component gives an invariant eigenspace, hence $A=\lambda$ by irreducibility. This also works for a mixed form which need not be Hermitian. [Invariant forms on the actual compact quotient][dq-gram]

In particular choose $c$ such that $(J_b x,J_s y)=cH(x,y)$. Put $D=J_b-cJ_s$. Then $(Dx,J_s y)=0$ for all $x,y$, and $(Dx,Dy)=\lambda H(x,y)$ with $\lambda\ge0$. To prove $D=0$ one must still prove $\lambda=0$. Two distinct orthogonal copies of one irreducible module would satisfy the Gram-form calculation with $\lambda>0$.

## Removing the orthogonal part at the definite bottom

For a primed polynomial test $\Psi$ and any of the maps $J$, define the reverse lift
$$
 T_\Psi(Jx)(g)=\int_{[G]} Jx(h)\,\overline{\theta_s(\Psi;g,h)}\,dh .
$$
The [split-place and determinant calculation](ROW_DEFINITE_REVERSE_LIFT.md) proves this function constant, for every linear combination of the two incoming maps. Its prerequisites are now available. The two maps kill tests of zero tensor class by local descent, and polynomial tests have partners with the required support by the [full-rank construction](../construction/ROW_DEFINITE_LOCAL_QUOTIENTS.md#full-rank-representatives-at-the-prescribed-split-place).

The other input is the **anisotropic doubled Siegel–Weil identity and Eisenstein continuation on the primed side**. For polynomial $\Psi$ it transfers the equality of the Gram forms of $D$ and $J_s$ to
$$
 \|T_\Psi(Dx)\|^2_{[A_s]}=\lambda\|T_\Psi(J_sx)\|^2_{[A_s]}.
$$
In the doubled group of $G$ take the box section of $\Psi\otimes\overline\Psi$ with the primed splitting character $\alpha$. In the far right half-plane the unfolded global zeta pairing integrates the matrix coefficients $(R(h)Dx,Dx)$, respectively $(R(h)J_sx,J_sx)$, against the same section and measure. Equivariance shows the first coefficient is $\lambda$ times the second for every $h$. Continue this equality to the Siegel–Weil value $(a'-(a+a'))/2=-a/2$. Here the compact primed theta integral gives twice its normalized average, and the doubling restriction is the product of a theta kernel and its conjugate. Fubini on the compact quotients gives $2/\operatorname{vol}([A_s])$ times the corresponding squared reverse-lift norm on each side. Cancelling the common nonzero factor gives the displayed equality. In the notation of Gan–Qiu–Takeda the anisotropic integrating space has dimension $a'$, and the maximal isotropic space of the doubled target has dimension $d$. For a unitary pair their $d(n)$ equals $n$. Theorem 7.1 gives holomorphy of the Siegel–Weil section at $(a'-d)/2$ and coefficient two there. Their §3.1 uses probability measure on the anisotropic quotient. Writing the same average with the project's Haar measure gives precisely the reciprocal volume above. The box section and the compact pullback here use the prescribed character and the same normalization for both vectors. [Gan–Qiu–Takeda, §§3.1 and 7.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1207.4709#page=33) · [Box section, unfolding, continuation and the factor two][dq-coef]

Choose $\Psi$ with $J_s\Psi\ne0$ from the definite seed, and put $x_0=[\Psi]\in\Pi$. Constancy and Fubini give
$$
 T_\Psi(Jx_0)(g)
   =\frac{(Jx_0,J_s x_0)}{\operatorname{vol}([A_s])}.
$$
For $J=D$ the numerator is zero. For $J=J_s$ it is strictly positive. The preceding norm equality forces $\lambda=0$. Thus $J_b=cJ_s$ on every matched polynomial test with the required primed support, as an equality of functions at every target point. The big-side seed gives $c\ne0$, and the small-side seed determines $c$ uniquely. [Final orthogonality and nonzero-scalar argument][dq-finish]

For a general matched Schwartz pair the match supplies simultaneous polynomial approximants in fixed finite components. On compact sets of representatives the theta sum is continuous in the real Schwartz tests. The finite support bounds the denominators in the rational sum, and a real Schwartz bound of degree larger than the lattice dimension gives a summable majorant uniform on these compact sets. Termwise convergence and compact quotient integration extend the equality to the matched pair at each target point. The primed test from the seed detects uniqueness. Thus on the specified local and doubled inputs this gives the unique nonzero comparison scalar for the entire definite model.
