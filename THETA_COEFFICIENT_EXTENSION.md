# What changes when the theta family's coefficient field grows?

The Hermitian spaces in the theta construction live over a CM extension $E/F$. The fields $L$ and $L'$ being compared serve a different purpose. They hold coefficients of automorphic forms on those spaces. Enlarging $L$ gives new linear combinations of the same vectors. It should preserve the original vectors, their norms, and the normalized functions one obtains by evaluating them. This chapter proves that comparison for the preliminary datum. It also shows how a specified theta operator at the reference tuple can be carried to the new field when it is already given on the original stage. For every admissible enlarged base and pinned enlarged column in that range, consider any of the old columns, comparison data and compatible enlarged core in the statement of `seed_at_enl`. The enlarged stages then admit the preliminary datum of the fixed-stage theorem, for the entire set $W'_t$ of coefficient primes whose residue characteristic belongs to $T'.\mathrm{pl}$. The conclusion at this step concerns the enlarged datum itself. The later comparison specifies how it must agree with the old one. For the host bounds put $N=N(m)$ and fix $0<\epsilon<1/52$. Eventually $N\le x^{402}$, $r\le x^5$, and $1\le n\le2x^3$. Taking $c_D,c_h\ge 4\cdot402+2$ makes each separation parameter $h,\Delta$ at least $256N^4$ for all sufficiently large $x$. The thinnest window has width $w''(n)=\exp(x^\epsilon-x^{\epsilon/3})/n$. For large $x$ its numerator is at least $x^{24}$, so $w''(n)\ge100r^4+1$. The three window prescriptions imply $b_0\ge w''(n)-1$ at every embedding, including the possible rounding loss in the top window. On every row the two alternating node-to-endpoint gaps equal $h$. Consequently the rank-$r$ coordinate gaps are $4\Delta$ in the upper segment, $b_0$ at the join, and $2h$ in the lower segment. The signed interlacing follows from the original weight inequalities. The two stronger node bounds follow from $r,t\le N$ and the common gap $2h$. These are precisely the separation and interlacing hypotheses of the host. Its prescribed diagonal Gram entries $\pm1$ give self-dual coordinate lattices in the prescribed ranges, and each $V_i$ used in the chain is positive definite. This establishes a common range for every allowed stage above the context. ## The common arithmetic spaces

Keep the same underlying arithmetic data, frame and prime indices as required by the enlargement. The host fields $F,E$, the Hermitian spaces and the labeled CM embeddings then agree. There is a unique $\iota:L\hookrightarrow L'$ compatible with the two distinguished complex embeddings $\sigma_*,\sigma'_*$. Existence follows from the inclusion of their images in $\mathbb C$. Uniqueness follows from injectivity of $\sigma'_*$. The small field $K$ of the identified core has the same image on the two sides, and $\iota$ commutes with its specified maps because both composites into $\mathbb C$ are the given embedding of $K$. ## The definite automorphic forms after coefficient enlargement

**Conditional reset theorem.** Let a coefficient enlargement preserve the underlying arithmetic data, frame and prime indices. Suppose the old datum has its standard readings of definite automorphic forms. On the enlarged side, take the standard coordinate lattices on each $V_i$ and on $X'$, and suppose the enlarged field contains all the specified unramified character values and a primitive root of order $|k_v|$ at each specified twist place. Given an enlarged datum satisfying `Kept`, one can choose an enlarged datum satisfying both `Kept` and the entire comparison `RSideA`, with the same enlarged character set and trace data. In particular, old and new forms at their respective reference tuples have **equal vector-valued readings** whenever their polynomial values agree over $\mathbb C$ at corresponding decorated points. [Statement and assembly](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/IwasawaDecomposition/DefiniteFormsReadingResetBaseEnlargement.lean#L274-L293)

Fix a definite row $i$. Its coefficient space $H_i$ is finite-dimensional, nonzero and irreducible. Its adelic action preserves the Hermitian product, has continuous orbit maps and is trivial on the kernel of projection to the archimedean group. These are the properties of the old coefficient representation used here. The standard polynomial coefficient map intertwines the rational unitary group and takes its polynomial basis to linearly independent vectors. Their span is nonzero and invariant under the rational group. Density at infinity, continuity and triviality on the finite-adelic kernel therefore give the whole of $H_i$. The complexified map is an isomorphism. The polynomial Hermitian form and the original Hilbert metric are invariant, so their comparison operator commutes with the rational action and is a positive scalar by irreducibility. Thus the old map is $s_i e_i\circ\sigma_*$, where $e_i$ is an equivariant complex isometry, $s_i>0$ and $\sigma_*$ applies the old embedding to the polynomial coefficients. The identity Gram matrix of this definite row gives the polynomial metric used in this argument. [Representation hypotheses](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/IndefiniteReal/LinksDefs.lean#L240-L257) · [Rational invariance](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/IwasawaDecomposition/StdSideUnique.lean#L15-L40) · [Isometry up to positive scalar](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/IwasawaDecomposition/InvTrUnitaryOne.lean#L63-L186)

For a tuple admitting a nonzero old form, choose a finite double coset on which it is nonzero and restrict the form to that coset alone. Its value satisfies the stabilizer transformation law at that coset, so this restriction is again an admissible form. The Hermitian identity of its class presentation gives a coefficient vector $v$ with nonzero image on which the old coefficient map already preserves the squared norm. Consequently $s_i^2\lVert e_i(\sigma_*v)\rVert^2=\lVert e_i(\sigma_*v)\rVert^2$, so $s_i=1$. If the space of forms at the tuple is zero, every reading there is zero and the same reading formula holds. This fixes the actual values wherever a comparison is required. [One-coset test](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/IwasawaDecomposition/ExistsIsomVec.lean#L12-L41) · [Normalized old evaluation](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/IwasawaDecomposition/EnlargedBaseCoefficientSpacesIsometricMaps.lean#L110-L175)

Use the same $H_i$, action, norm and inner product on the enlarged side. Define its coefficient map by $c'_i(v')=e_i(\sigma'_*v')$. This map intertwines the rational group and its Hermitian pairing is exactly the embedded polynomial pairing. Install the old lattices and frames, the normalized characters and the common finite-group identification. With these coefficients the definite-form construction gives an enlarged standard reading and retains the level, character and group conditions of the given enlarged datum. In particular its set of marked places is $S_b=P_g$. For a finite adelic element $g$, the decorated points $d_i(g)$ and $d'_i(g)$ at the two reference tuples correspond because their row prime sets are the same distinguished set. The resulting formulas are

$$
j'_i(\phi')(g)=e_i\bigl(\sigma'_*\phi'(d'_i(g))\bigr),
\qquad
j_i(\phi)(g)=e_i\bigl(\sigma_*\phi(d_i(g))\bigr).
$$

Here $j_i$ includes restriction to the finite class presentation. Agreement of the embedded polynomials at corresponding decorated points gives equality for each $g$, hence equality of the functions. Together with the transported frames, group and coefficient representations this proves `RSideA` on the constructed datum. ## The row model and its rational vectors

Now suppose the definite readings have been matched as above. Fix a row $i$ and write $n$ for its old coordinate dimension. For any complex embedding $\sigma':L'\hookrightarrow\mathbb C$ take the original framed discrete series, multiplicity space and finite-group action at $\sigma=\sigma'\circ\iota$. Indeed the row parameter is determined by the restriction to the embedded CM field. The old and new restrictions there agree since the two embeddings of $E$ in the large fields correspond under $\iota$. Thus all signs, weights and their order match. This construction uses the same Hilbert space and its specified norm at each such embedding. Take the old row measure and fundamental domain. At the distinguished embedding copy the old vacuum, the polynomial map on the lowest type and definite coefficient space, and the family of frame matrices on the definite space on the identified spaces. For each group element use the original action matrix with $\iota$ applied to its entries and the original character with $\iota$ applied to its value. Products of matrices and multiplication of characters commute with $\iota$. The new actions on $m(x)$ are consequently $m(\rho(g)x)$. $$

## The theta maps at the reference character

Fix an enlargement with the common arithmetic host and the row identifications just constructed. Assume the earlier realization already carries its analytic data. At the reference tuple, each new row can use the old Weil action, its distinguished test function, the Hilbert space of coefficient-valued definite forms, the two linear theta maps out of that space, the integration measure, and the chosen fundamental domain. These are the seven additional comparisons in [the definition of `RSide`][ref-seven].

Matched rows have the same adelic groups and the same space on which the test functions are defined. The preceding comparison identifies the definite coefficient space with its Hermitian structure, the framed discrete-series model, and its Hilbert multiplicity space. The two theta maps now have identified domains and targets. One produces a multilinear map from smooth archimedean vectors to adelic functions. The other takes values in the chosen Hilbert model of the multiplicity space. Use the old maps under these identifications. The same argument transports the group action, the test function, and the integration data. One can provisionally assign these choices at every tuple. The comparison proved here concerns their values at the reference tuple alone. [Transport of the seven objects][ref-copy] · [Installation in the enlarged family][ref-install]

The next analytic assertion already asks more of the chosen test function. For each row, verify that the transported action has the two prescribed splitting characters, that the transported function is the test vector prescribed by the lattices and vacuum, and that the polynomial map intertwines the prescribed archimedean actions and takes a tensor of unit lowest-type vectors and a unit coefficient vector to the prescribed polynomial $\Delta_i$. The source first identifies the reference lift characters and their finite restrictions. It then transports these three assertions using exactness and the prescribed data of the **old** realization. Further arguments identify the Hilbert model and theta lift, obtain the weak characterization with the prescribed phases and trace, and give existence of the maps required for exactness on the enlarged realization. The later all-tuple existence assertions still require a human proof on the enlarged realization.
