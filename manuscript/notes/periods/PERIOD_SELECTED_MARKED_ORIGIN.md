# The marked arithmetic origin for the nonterminal period estimate

At a nonterminal layer the root model, the rational cusp sections and the height estimate can all be attached to the bundle returned by the original selector. The core and its rational frame come directly from the selected bundle. A deep cover can simultaneously have the explicit ray-class field-size bound and the selected core's adelic frame at every good embedding. The second assertion follows by applying a frame construction to the *same covering correspondence* as the sized construction.

## From the chosen bundle to its core

Let $b=\sigma(F_m,x,a)$ be the chosen bundle, carrying its real realization $b.B$. The source's geometric compatibility supplies a comparison $t$ on $b$. More precisely it gives the point's inclusion comparison, its class-sum pin, its ties and the extra geometric condition on $(b.\mathrm{part}\,t).\mathrm{pt}$. The weaker geometric compatibility itself follows from the marked divisor and tie rows, the ray-field input and the specified extension with its law. In that deduction the class-sum conclusions apply to **every** admissible tie $t$, so one may use the tie chosen on $b$. [Geometric comparison][geocomp] · [Construction from the divisor and tie rows][geocon]

Put $p=(b.\mathrm{part}\,t).\mathrm{pt}$. Expanding the two definitions gives

$$
 p.T=b.B.T,\qquad p.R=b.B.Q.Rl,\qquad
 p.\kappa=b.B.\kappa,\qquad p.\mathrm{fw}=b.B.\mathrm{fw}.
$$

The same construction retains the column and period families, $b.R,b.S$ in the intermediate part, and the chosen vector and its evaluation data in $p$. Thus $p.T.\mathrm{toCore}$, at $j=i+1<m$, is the core on the chosen member. The reference tuple of $p$ reads the base tuple of its own row family, by injectivity of the level-character reading. [Bundle to its part][part] · [Part to its point][point] · [Reference tuple][tuple]

The family thresholds can be taken before $a,i$ and all components. On the original working class $W_3$, the seven duties give the native family hypotheses by concatenating the first five duty conclusions with the divisibility, window and size bounds of that class. On the marked primitive and divisor rows, eventual good reduction and the class-sum pin and ties above, the native row theorem gives $p.\mathrm{Rows}$ at all sufficiently large points. Its proof uses the representation-theoretic inputs for discrete series, Harish-Chandra modules, cusp decay and Casselman–Wallach globalization. The point's ties already give both parts of $\mathrm{RecTied}(D,L)$, namely the small coefficient field and the series tie. This last implication is pointwise. Simultaneous use of these properties takes the maximum of the finitely many thresholds. ## A sized cover with the prescribed frames

Fix this $p,j$. Write $K_0=\mathbf Q(\sqrt{-d})$, $F_0=p.T.Q.c.F$, $L_0=L.\mathrm{sc}.L$, $z=fnL_0$, $n=k+m-1$, and $e=\lfloor3L_0/\log 3\rfloor$. At the family threshold $3,5$ split as required for the half-level and $15$ is coprime to $g_0g_1$. The fixed field $F_0$ equals the subfield generated by the embedded coefficient order of the marked record. Indeed every generator is integral in $F_0$ by the field pin, and conversely every element of $F_0$ is a fraction of two elements of that order. The two bounds in the small-field condition, transported across this equality, give

$$
 \operatorname{size}(F_0)=\log[F_0:\mathbf Q]+
        \frac{\log|\operatorname{disc}(F_0)|}{[F_0:\mathbf Q]}
 \le 2\,\mathrm{PO}(D,L)\le217z^2 .
$$

[Equality of embedded fields][field-eq] · [Bounds and normalization][field]

Choose the half-level of depth $15^e$ and the resulting level with $M'=g_0g_1\,15^e$, auxiliary factor $A=1$. Let $R$ be the ray class field of $K_0$ at the integer modulus $M'$, embedded as in the construction, and $F=F_0R$. The Weber values supply the function-field constant condition for the moduli at this level. The compositum gives a field stable under the required conjugations. The two coefficient records and the covering correspondence $d_2$ are constructed over this $F$. The ray-class discriminant and degree estimates give

$$
 \operatorname{size}(F)\le \operatorname{size}(F_0)
          +20(\log(g_0g_1\,15^e)+\log d+1)\le391z^2.
$$

Here $e\log15\le9L_0$. The field $F$ carries the deep moduli and their sections. The ambient coefficient field of the marked row is a separate datum. [Sized ray field and fixed correspondence][ray] · [Bounds and family quantifiers][deep]

**Presentation lemma.** For every already given good cover $Y$ of $p.T.\mathrm{toCore}$ at $j$, with depth $e$ and size allowance $o$, there is a presentation $Y^*$ using exactly its coefficient records, field inclusions, moduli and covering correspondence, with

$$
 Y^*.\mathrm{Good}(e,o)\quad\text{and}\quad Y^*.\mathrm{Match}.
$$

In particular the sized construction admits both $\mathrm{GoodCov}$ and $\mathrm{Match}$ at the bound $391z^2$.

To prove this keep $Y.c',Y.\mathrm{hle},Y.d$ fixed. Its goodness supplies depth, the covering-level identities, $A=1$, the condition on constants and normality. Apply the prescribed-frame construction to these five facts, $p.T$ and its own laws $p.\mathrm{fw}.\mathrm{laws}$. It uses the lattice embedding $p.T.\iota_j$, with its rank identification, and the orthogonal starting frame $p.T.x_{0,j}(\sigma|_{F_0})$ for each good coefficient embedding $\sigma$. The context extension at that particular frame gives a uniformization, dictionary and compact subgroup $U^*,\mathrm{dict}^*,K_f^*$ with the desired pin. The initial goodness allowance is $\operatorname{size}(Y.d.c_2.F)$, bounded by $o$ for the original $Y$. Increase the allowance to $o$. At the specified family depth the extra clauses of $\mathrm{GoodCov}$ follow from the unit and model clauses of goodness. This proves simultaneous size and matching without enlarging the field. The lemma is existential in the analytic presentation. For an already prescribed presentation, the geometric and trace bounds below apply to its own $U$ under the same arithmetic inputs and its own goodness, independently of exact matching. [Goodness and exact matching][good] · [Construction with a prescribed frame][match] · [Equivalence at the specified budget][goodcov]

**A weaker hypothesis for constructing the origin.** The numerical host estimate is independent of the point. Its proof takes the maximum of two scalar thresholds before the index and ignores the row predicate. The fixed core and its laws are already $b.B.T,b.B.\mathrm{fw}.\mathrm{laws}$, independently of $t$. Moreover, in the field-size proof only the two inequalities of $\mathrm{FieldSmall}(D,L)$ enter. Consequently, given the host size estimates, this small-field condition, the ray-class field input and the core laws suffice for the sized and pinned cover. The class-sum evaluations and the complete system of row predicates enter later applications. On the source's geometrically tied members the needed field condition is already a projection of the point's ties. ## Identifying components, flags and valuations

Take either the matched presentation or any specified good presentation. The signature is $(r,s)$, with $s=m-j$ and $r+s=n$. The source's inequalities $m^2\le k$, $2\le m$, $1\le j<m$ imply $1\le s$ and $3s+1\le n$. The number of CM labels is $f=2n_0$, with $n_0=D.R.\mathrm{ord}$. The root discriminant and logarithmic derivative read the original host data. Component idempotents evaluate to one on the designated component and zero on every other component. The uniformizations and evaluation laws are obtained on $Y.d.Xo_2,Y.d.c_2$. [Good-cover component laws][laws] · [Actual invocation at this signature][deepst]

The tower's bad set is exactly $\mathcal B=\{p\text{ prime}:p\mid2\,|\operatorname{disc}(E)|M'\}$. There are at most four distinct prime divisors of $M'$, including $e=0$. For the host the number of bad primes greater than $n_0$ is at most the number of distinct level primes plus three. There are at most $n_0+1$ integers in the complementary range. Hence $\#\mathcal B\le n_0+8\le4fn^2$. At the fitted cutoff $N=\lfloor4fn^2(1+\|u\|_\infty/g)\rfloor$ this meets the required cardinality bound, with $N\ge128$. [Host count][bad] · [Original cutoff conditions][mean]

To construct the arithmetic model at this level, complete the selected moduli to a family indexed by all levels, fixing its value at the selected level to be exactly $Y.d.Xo_2$. The global model construction uses this family and $Y.d.c_2$. Its split-component property follows from the function-field constant condition. The tower construction is then invoked on this integral model over $\mathcal O_F$. It gives an origin with the local projective immersion condition and Hodge–Tate parameter $4$ at every relevant prime. The volume theorem on this origin supplies proportionality, the intersection estimate, volume equality and the uniform mean slope. [Global model and origin at the fixed level][global] · [Origin and volume hypotheses][orig]

The component bijection $\kappa$ is characterized by equality of generic points. If $Z$ is a component on the moduli and $j_0$ its map into the integral model, every generalization of $j_0(\eta_Z)$ lifts under the flat map to one of $\eta_Z$. Thus $j_0(\eta_Z)$ is maximal, and determines an origin label. Injectivity follows because $j_0$ embeds the moduli and the closure of $\eta_Z$ is $Z$. Conversely every origin label gives a point of the generic fibre. The finite union of the closures of the original generic points contains the dense open moduli and hence this point. Maximality identifies its integral generic point with one of the $j_0(\eta_Z)$. The same bijection works for every cutoff and weight. [Proof of the component bijection][components]

At $Z,N$, identify the residue field of that base generic point with $K_Z=k(\eta_Z)$. On the open flag charts of the two Hodge eigenspaces at each label there are $f[\binom r2+\binom s2]$ independent variables. Together with the base dimension $frs$ this gives $f\binom n2$. In each eigenspace, change basis over $K_Z$, take the triangular coordinates of the generic flag, and read the independent coefficients. Equivalently, elimination in the open flag chart and its inverse are rational over $K_Z$. The generic flag belongs to the intersection of these dense chart domains, so this change of coordinates preserves the generated field. The origin gives an embedding of $K_Z(\text{flag variables})$ into the model field $\Omega_{N,\kappa Z}$, of finite extension degree $d_0=\mathrm{Or.Fl.deg}$, extending the given coefficient-field embedding. The change of frame is represented by a single Borel element. Consequently there is one tuple of units $t_i$ with

$$
 \mathrm{rd}(s)=\Big(\prod_i t_i^{\operatorname{ex}_i(w)}\Big)
               \varphi(\mathrm{ev}_Z(s))
$$

for **all** algebraic weight sections $s$ of weights $w$. This is the required agreement of section evaluations before taking traces. The choices may depend on $N,Z$. [Generic field, flags and section formula][reading] · [Definition of the degree comparison][readdef]

For a strictly decreasing integral weight $u$ and a model-small section of radius $R\ge0$, division by the gauge and field trace give the component evaluation of one cusp section $s$. Its archimedean bound is $d_0R$ in all admissible frames. For every prime $p$, every coefficient place $\lambda\mid p$, and every record valuation $v$ at $\lambda$, that same section satisfies

$$
 v(s)\ge -v_\lambda(p)\mathbf1_{p\in S_N}
       \left(\frac1{p-1}+40\frac{v_p(|\operatorname{disc}E|)}{[E:\mathbf Q]}
                    +\mathbf1_{p=2}\right)\|u\|_1 .
$$

The trace count uses $\delta=[F:\mathbf Q]d_0$. The set $S_N$ equals the marked cover's set of primes up to the real cutoff together with its bad primes, by the integer floor and the equality of levels. The geometric top degree is identified with the volume of the component $\kappa Z$ times the universal flag degree. ## Scope of the transfer

The native theorem bounds the arithmetic mean of component volume slopes on this presentation, uniformly over admissible weights and gaps. Its ten named inputs are arithmetic intersection theory, Moriwaki's arithmetic Hilbert–Samuel and bigness statements, characteristic-zero resolution, semiabelian homomorphism extension, Fargues's Hodge–Tate theorem, the specified GAGA theorem for sections, the finite-flat quotient and Zarhin statements, and the nef Riemann–Roch estimate. The source derives the auxiliary large-degree line and birational projection formula within these hypotheses. The mean uses the uniform tower constant plus $120$. The evaluation at $p.T,p.\mathrm{fw}.\mathrm{laws},Y$ is explicit and uses the quotient's fitted weight $u'=\mathrm{uPrime}(u_1)$ and its gap. For the direct native height estimate, the further short-section, geometric, multiplication, degree and budget inputs yield the constant $C_s+C_m+C_b+C_0C_b$ for that same cover. The root-to-record equality above also supports the [direct trace route](PERIOD_RECORD_TRACE_TO_CHOW.md) and the [centered intersection computation](PERIOD_CENTERED_ROOT_WALL_HEIGHTS.md) on the corresponding model.
