# Why one set of formal characters controls a whole flag

A local term in the height proof measures how divisible an invariant derivative must be on integral power series. An upper bound for that depth requires an integral test with a sufficiently small valuation. At an odd unramified prime the test can be built from characters of the formal group. For each fixed chart, embedding and frame, the construction uses one ordered list for every step of that flag and every differential order. The nested choice occurs in the native proof of `exists_flag_characters`. We give an elementary matrix proof which also recovers the best minor at each step of a fixed generating list. [Native flag-character theorem][native-flag] · [Roadmap B8.28xfa and B8.28xf][statement]

### What the local arithmetic supplies

Fix a nonterminal layer and an odd prime $p$ unramified in the CM field $E$. Fix an algebraic integral frame on a marked component, its formal chart $G$, and the valuation ring $O$ of its algebraically closed complete valued extension. Normalize the additive valuation by $v(p)=1$, with $v(0)=+\infty$. At an embedding $\tau$, let $u_1,\ldots,u_s$ be the full ordered integral frame of its Lie eigenspace. The multiplicity $s=\mu_\tau$ equals $P$ on the CM type $\Psi$ and $Q$ on its conjugate. We treat $s>0$. Reduction modulo the maximal ideal identifies embeddings above the prime of $\tau$ with the residue-field embeddings. Indeed, the local extension is unramified, so its primitive residue generator has a unique lift of each specified conjugate as a root of its separable lifted polynomial. The residue Frobenius thus gives a cycle $\tau_0=\tau,\tau_1,\ldots$ with $\tau_{t+1}(a)\equiv\tau_t(a)^p$ modulo the maximal ideal. Put
$$
 R_\tau=\sum_{t\ge1}p^{-t}\mu_{\overline{\tau_t}} .
$$
The first summand uses the conjugate of the *next* embedding. [Orbit construction][orbit] · [Colength estimate on the algebraic frame][colength]

The genuinely arithmetic input is the following estimate on character differentials. Finitely many integral formal characters $\eta_b:G\to\widehat{\mathbb G}_m$ are written as multiplicative series with $\eta_b(0)=1$. They have the property that the columns $(d\eta_b(u_a))_{a=1}^s$ span a lattice $L\subset O^s$ of determinant valuation at most $R_\tau$. Here determinant valuation means the valuation of the ideal of its full minors. It can be calculated from a finite diagonal presentation over the valuation ring even when the value group is dense. To see where the bound comes from, let $a_\sigma$ be the Lie multiplicities of the abelian quotient of the semiabelian chart. The polarized Hodge–Tate calculation relates the additive determinant lengths $c_\sigma$ of its cokernels by
$$
 c_\tau=c_{\bar\tau}+\frac{a_\tau}{p-1}
                -\sum_{t\ge1}p^{-t}a_{\tau_t}.
$$
The conjugate cokernel has $a_{\bar\tau}$ generators and is killed by an element of valuation $1/(p-1)$, so $c_{\bar\tau}\le a_{\bar\tau}/(p-1)$. Polarization makes $a_\sigma+a_{\bar\sigma}$ the constant local Tate rank along this orbit. Using $\sum_{t\ge1}p^{-t}=1/(p-1)$ gives $c_\tau\le\sum_{t\ge1}p^{-t}a_{\overline{\tau_t}}$. The abelian-quotient characters and the characters lifting the torus coordinates give a surjection of this cokernel onto $O^s/L$. Its determinant length is therefore at most $c_\tau$. Finally $a_\sigma\le\mu_\sigma$ by the tangent exact sequence. The Hodge–Tate identity and the uniformization yielding these characters are used here in their stated local forms. ### Choosing optimal minors simultaneously

Let $M$ be any $s$-by-$N$ integral matrix over a valuation ring with full row rank over the fraction field. Let $h_j$ be the minimum valuation of a determinant on the first $j$ rows and any $j$ columns. There exist distinct columns $b_1,\ldots,b_s$ for which the first $j$ rows and the columns $b_1,\ldots,b_j$ attain $h_j$ for every $j$. Moreover $0=h_0\le h_1\le\cdots\le h_s$.

For the proof, choose a nonzero first-row entry of least valuation as pivot and move its column to the front. Subtract from every other column the integral multiple of the pivot column that makes its first entry zero. The multipliers are integral because the pivot divides every entry in that row. Repeat in the remaining rows and columns. At each step a nonzero pivot exists by the rank assumption. Column permutations and these subtractions preserve the ideal of all minors on the first $j$ rows, for every $j$. Multilinearity gives one inclusion of ideals, and the inverse column operations give the other. Once the first $j$ pivots have been taken, all other columns vanish on those $j$ rows. The ideal there is generated by the product of those pivots. The chosen pivot columns came from the original list, each altered only by combinations of *earlier* pivot columns. Thus the determinant of the corresponding original prefix is this same product up to sign. The difference $h_j-h_{j-1}$ is the valuation of the $j$-th pivot and is nonnegative. This proof uses operations on differentials to find the indices. The resulting tests use the original characters $\eta_{b_j}$. [Native determinant divisibility and simultaneous choice][native-flag]

Apply this to $M_{ab}=d\eta_b(u_a)$. We have $h_j\le h_s\le R_\tau$ at every flag step. Write the selected characters as $\chi_1,\ldots,\chi_s$. On $j$ copies of $G$ take the integral series $F_j=\prod_{b=1}^j\chi_b(X^{(b)})$. If $D_{u_a}^{(b)}$ is invariant differentiation on copy $b$, the identity $D_{u_a}\chi_b=d\chi_b(u_a)\chi_b$ shows that $F_j$ is an eigenfunction of the determinant of the $j$-by-$j$ matrix of derivations. Its eigenvalue is that same minor. Since $F_j(0)=1$, the $n$-th power of the determinant operator on $F_j$, evaluated at zero, has valuation $nh_j$. The infimum defining the block depth is therefore at most $nR_\tau$, with one list for all $j,n$. [Native passage to the depth][depth] · [Character evaluation on copies][copies]

For $\tau\in\Psi$ the answer is explicit. The CM type here is induced from the specified imaginary quadratic field $K$. At split $p$, Frobenius fixes the restriction to $K$, so every conjugate in the sum contributes $Q$. At inert $p$, it interchanges the two restrictions, so the contributions at odd and even $t$ are $P$ and $Q$, respectively. Hence
$$
 R_\tau=\begin{cases}
 Q/(p-1), & p\text{ split in }K,\\
 (Pp+Q)/(p^2-1), & p\text{ inert in }K .
 \end{cases}
$$
For comparison, inserting $(P,Q)=(1,0)$ gives zero and $p/(p^2-1)$ in the rank-one CM elliptic model. There the connected formal group has height one at split ordinary primes and height two at inert primes. The nonterminal estimates use their actual layer signature. At $p=3$ inert, the latter is $1/3+1/27+1/243+\cdots=3/8$. This identifies the odd powers that appear in the quadratic example of the manuscript. The inequality here is an upper bound on the depth at an algebraic integral frame. The complementary lower bound, its wider domain, and the separate treatment of ramified primes and $2$ enter the [global reference-depth sum](../periods/PERIOD_REFERENCE_DEPTH_BUDGET.md).
